There are three painters in this picture, if you count properly. Renoir, of course. And then, hanging on the wall behind the girls, two paintings by his friend Degas.

The girls are Yvonne and Christine Lerolle, the two eldest daughters of the painter and collector Henry Lerolle. When Renoir painted them, around eighteen ninety-seven, they were about eighteen and twenty. Music mattered in that house. The family held musical evenings, and one of their regular guests was Claude Debussy.

Yvonne sits at the keys on the left. Christine, on the right, turns the pages of the score. It's a quiet, ordinary moment in a family salon, and Renoir paints it like one.

Now look past them, to the wall. Those two pictures come from their father's own collection, and both are by Degas. One is a racing scene, Avant la course, Before the Race. The other shows Dancers under a Tree. So Renoir paints his friend's paintings into his own, a small salute to Degas, and a portrait of the Lerolles' taste as well as their daughters. Degas knew the sisters too. The Musée d'Orsay keeps a photograph he took of himself with Yvonne and Christine.

Here's the curious part. The Lerolles never owned this painting. Renoir never sold it to them, or gave it. It stayed in his hands, apart from a stay at his dealer's during the First World War. After his death it passed through dealers and private collections. And in nineteen thirty-seven, three years after Paul Guillaume died, his widow Domenica bought it.

Both sisters later married into the Rouart family. But in this room, they're still at the piano, and still in the house where Debussy came to visit.

Next, look for the little boy in a red clown costume. That's Renoir's son Claude, and he did not enjoy wearing it.

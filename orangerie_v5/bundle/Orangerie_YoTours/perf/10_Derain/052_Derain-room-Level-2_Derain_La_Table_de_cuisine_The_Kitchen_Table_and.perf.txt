[lightly] It's only a kitchen table, with its utensils, painted mostly in shades of brown. Yet in the nineteen thirties, critics stood in front of this picture and thought of Caravaggio.

La Table de cuisine, the Kitchen Table, was painted in the first half of the nineteen twenties. It's one of the most important of Derain's big, dark still lifes of kitchen things. From 1927 it was exhibited and reproduced, and success came quickly. [pause] What those critics saw was light pressing against dark, the drama Caravaggio was famous for.

Now look at how still it is. The whole picture is built like a piece of architecture, solid and static. After Soutine's swaying houses, it can feel like stepping into a cool, quiet room.

That calm suited Paul Guillaume.

Around it hang smaller still lifes. Pick any one and look for the black lines Derain draws around the basket, the grapes or the glass. It's the same calm, in miniature.

[amused] Then find Pears and Jug, Poires et cruche. The museum calls it a spirited sketch rather than a finished work. Here you catch Derain thinking out loud. The pears are lit with quick strokes of vibrant yellow.

[quietly] A finished table and an unfinished sketch, in the same room. Put them together and the secret is out. That stillness was never an accident. It was built, stroke by stroke, by a painter who knew exactly how fast he could go, and chose to go slowly.

Nearby, his nudes and his landscapes of Provence are waiting.

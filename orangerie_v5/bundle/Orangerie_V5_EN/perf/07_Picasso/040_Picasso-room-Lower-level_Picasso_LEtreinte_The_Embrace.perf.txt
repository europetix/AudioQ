[quietly] The woman in this embrace is pregnant. Once you've seen that, the picture changes. It stops being only about two lovers. It becomes a picture about what comes next.

Picasso made it in nineteen oh three, in Barcelona, in the depths of what we now call his Blue Period. He was working in a studio he had once shared with his friend Carlos Casagemas. Two years earlier, in Paris, Casagemas had taken his own life. Picasso was deeply affected.

[pause] This sheet belongs to a whole run of drawings of a nude couple, standing together. They led up to one large painting, La Vie, Life, now in the Cleveland Museum of Art. So what you're looking at is Picasso working his way toward a much bigger picture, about life itself.

Look at the room around them. It's a bedroom. The colours are those cold Blue Period blues, warmed here and there with pink. The man and the woman hold each other, and their faces are barely visible. Nothing here is posed for us.

[warmly] It's pastel, not paint, lent by the Louvre, and so fragile that some days it rests away from the light.

Paul Guillaume bought it in nineteen thirty, from the great dealer Ambroise Vollard. Guillaume was never Picasso's official dealer. What brought them together, the museum says, was a shared passion for so-called primitive art. The twelve Picassos in this collection run from these blue years right through to the nineteen twenties.

[warmly] Anyone who has tried to photograph a toddler knows the problem. Now imagine painting one, in oils. Renoir had a solution, and her name was Gabrielle.

Gabrielle Renard came into the Renoir household to look after the children, and became the family's faithful servant. She had a gift the painter badly needed. She could always hold the attention of a very small model.

The model here is Jean, Renoir's second son. Between eighteen ninety-five and the following year, Renoir painted the two of them many times. Together the pictures make a real album of his son's early childhood. This is one page of it.

[pause] Look at how it works, with Gabrielle holding Jean on her knee. On the table in front of them are toy farm animals, and she's keeping him busy with them. That's the trick, because while the little boy plays, the painter paints.

[amused] Now look closely at the toys, because they're barely there. Renoir brushes them in so loosely that they melt into the tabletop. As the museum's own notes admit, we can barely recognise the cow in Gabrielle's hand. Renoir didn't care about the cow. He cared about the boy, and about the woman keeping him happy.

[quietly] So this is a painting about a kind of work that's usually invisible. The nanny is doing a job, and the painter is doing a job, and the picture only exists because the two jobs fit together.

Gabrielle didn't stay in the background. She quickly became one of Renoir's favourite models, and about ten years later he painted her as a reclining nude, in the great tradition of the old masters. Look for her among Renoir's nudes, with the bathers and the long reclining figure, and the story continues there.

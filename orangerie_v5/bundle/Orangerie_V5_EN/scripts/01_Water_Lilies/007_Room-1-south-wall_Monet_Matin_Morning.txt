Here's a small riddle. Of the eight compositions in these two rooms, three are called Morning. Only one is an evening.

You've already met the evening, Setting Sun. And this is the first of the mornings. Matin, Morning, two metres high and twelve and three-quarter metres long, the same length as Clouds across the room. It hangs on the south wall, the Seine side, the side where the old orangery was all glass.

The museum numbers the four walls of this room, and Morning comes fourth. So the order you've just walked is the museum's own. A room that runs from morning to evening, and ends with morning. Which suits a man who was still painting mornings in his eighties.

Not that Monet wanted you to follow anyone's order. He planned these rooms to be entered by more than one way, and walked without a guide. Any starting point is the right one. So treat this as a beginning, too.

Give this one time. Stand far enough back to take in the whole length, if the crowd lets you. Let your eye drift along it, as the light in the water shifts from one end to the other. Then pick one small patch and go close. The reflected sky and the plants of the pond break into quick strokes of colour. Step back, and the morning comes together again.

Through the passage, the water changes company. The second room adds the branches of weeping willows at the water's edge, and the paintings there are built on the contrasts those branches make. Two more mornings are waiting, and the longest composition of all.

Walk on through the passage into the second oval room, and play its track there.

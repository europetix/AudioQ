The first room was about the sky. This one is about the trees at the water's edge, and the years in which they were painted.

The museum describes this second room very simply. Its paintings are built on contrasts, the branches of weeping willows against the light of the pond. Those willows were painted by a man who had just lost a great deal.

His wife, Alice, died in May 1911. The next year, doctors found cataracts in his eyes. Then, early in 1914, his son Jean died at forty-six. That summer the war came. Michel and Jean-Pierre, the young men of the family, were called up. At times, the guns of the front could be heard at Giverny.

Monet's answer was to work bigger than ever. In 1915 he built a third studio, about twenty-three metres by twelve, under a roof of glass. The light fell from above, as it does in here. In that studio, after years of looking down at his pond, he painted canvases like the ones around you.

So take a moment in the middle and turn slowly. Four compositions, twelve panels, and all of them about the edge of the water.

Start with the shorter composition on the end wall, around the passage you just came through. That's Reflections of Trees. Play its track there.

The light in this room comes from the sky. It falls through a glass roof and a pale stretched-fabric ceiling, and there's a little artificial light up there too. But mostly this is daylight, the way Monet wanted it. So what you see depends a little on the weather outside.

Around you are four of the eight compositions. Laid end to end, all eight together run to about ninety-one metres of painting, on twenty-two panels, every one two metres high. The height never changes, but each has its own width, cut to fit its stretch of curved wall.

And the rooms themselves have a shape worth knowing. Two ovals, joined by a short passage. From above, the museum compares them to the sign for infinity. They run east to west, in step with the path of the sun. And they sit on the great Paris axis that runs from the Louvre to the Arc de Triomphe.

This first room is about the sky and the plants, reflected in water, from morning to evening. You're not looking across a pond. You're looking into its surface, and it goes all the way around you.

Take a slow turn on the spot before you go up to any one painting. Then begin with the smallest composition in the room, a single panel six metres wide. It's called Setting Sun. Play its track there.

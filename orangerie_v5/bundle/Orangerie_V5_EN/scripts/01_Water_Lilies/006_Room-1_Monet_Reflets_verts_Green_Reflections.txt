These paintings have been through a war of their own. In August 1944, during the Liberation of Paris, a shell struck this building, and some of the panels were damaged. A series of photographs recorded the harm. Afterwards the walls were repaired, and so was the paint.

That was not the end of the worry. From the late 1940s, the people looking after the Water Lilies began to fret about the air in these rooms, and about the light. They still do. Condition reports and careful treatments carry on to this day, and during building works in 2025 the paintings were cleaned. Ninety-one metres of canvas, and every centimetre of it a patient.

Reflets verts, Green Reflections, is two metres high and eight and a half metres wide. Remember the journey the museum describes in this room, from clouds on the water to reflections where the water lilies rise. If Clouds was the airy end of that journey, the title tells you where this one sits. In the green, among the reflections of the plants.

So here's a question to take up close. Where does a lily stop and its reflection begin? Walk right up to the canvas. The strokes are quick and full of movement, and for a moment the pond falls apart into marks of colour. Now take a few steps back. Somewhere along the way, the water closes over again, and the flowers float.

That moment, when the paint turns back into water, comes at a different distance for everyone. Find yours.

To finish this room, go to Morning, the long composition on the Seine side. Play its track there.

For decades, this museum's Rousseaus numbered nine, the ones that came with Paul Guillaume and Domenica's collection. Since the end of 2024, there are eleven.

The newcomers are a pair. Two small portraits on wood, of a man and a woman, a couple, painted in 1887. They came to the museum with the support of its society of friends, and of Mathias Ary Jan. They may not always be on the wall. If you do find them, notice how small they are, and step in close. Two quiet faces, side by side, a husband and wife kept together.

The other two pictures here are harder to talk about, for an honest reason. I don't have a reliable description of either to give you, and I'd rather say too little than make something up.

Promeneurs dans un parc, Walkers in a Park, is a small canvas, about half a metre wide, painted sometime in the first decade of the twentieth century. People walking among trees. That's the promise of the title. Take a moment with it, and see whether these walkers face you the way Junier's passengers did.

La Falaise, The Cliff, is a landscape. A cliff, as the title says, and beyond that, the painting has to speak for itself. Which is not a bad way to meet Rousseau. No legend, no anecdote. Just a painter, a subject, and you.

Now head for Henri Matisse's room, and look for his painting of three sisters. Play its track there.

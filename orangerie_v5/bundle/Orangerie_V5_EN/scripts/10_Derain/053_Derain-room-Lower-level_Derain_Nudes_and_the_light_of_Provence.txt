For a modern painter, Derain kept surprisingly old company. You can see it in his nudes.

The Blond Model, Le Modèle blond, sits with her head resting in her right hand. The museum traces her back to Boucher, Fragonard and Renoir's bathers, so if you've been to the Renoir room, she may look like a relative. In The Beautiful Model, Le Beau Modèle, the drapery shifts through greys, and her face is half-hidden by her bent arm and her hair. There's just a hint of a warm smile. In the Large Reclining Nude, a pale body lies against a deliberately dark ground. And the life-size Nude on a Sofa holds a classical Renaissance pose that the museum itself calls artificial and uncomfortable.

Then the light changes. In the early nineteen thirties Derain was painting in Provence. In The Road, La Route, the village of Eygalières sits above the road to Saint-Rémy, all warm colour under an intensely blue sky. In Trees and Village, it's the same village, framed by plane trees, under a stormy sky. One place, two weathers. And in his Landscapes of Provence, a hilltop village looks down on a desolate plain, broken only by bare tree trunks.

When you've seen all you want down here, there's one more look waiting upstairs. Follow the signs back up to the Water Lilies, step into either oval room, and play its track there. If you're short of time, follow the exit signs instead. A last stop waits on the garden side, as you leave.

It's only a kitchen table, with its utensils, painted mostly in shades of brown. Yet in the nineteen thirties, critics stood in front of this picture and thought of Caravaggio.

La Table de cuisine, the Kitchen Table, was painted in the early twenties. It's one of the most important of Derain's big, dark still lifes of kitchen things. From 1927 it was exhibited and reproduced, and it quickly became a success. What the critics saw was the play of light against dark, the thing Caravaggio was famous for.

Look at how still it is. The whole picture is built like a piece of architecture, solid and static, in browns. After Soutine's houses, it can feel like stepping into a cool, quiet room.

That calm suited Paul Guillaume. The museum says his Derains reflect his taste for the classical side of the artist. And you can follow that taste around these walls.

In the Rustic Still Life, Nature morte champêtre, it's a straw hat and a basket of flowers. In the Still Life with Basket, Nature morte au panier, the dark areas, the basket and the grapes, are drawn in with black lines. In the small Still Life with a Glass of Wine, there's a plate of fruit, some bread and a glass, with the grapes and the glass picked out in black and white.

And then there's Pears and Jug, Poires et cruche. The museum calls it a spirited sketch rather than a finished work. It's a chance to catch Derain thinking out loud. Look at the pears. They're lit with quick strokes of bright, vibrant yellow.

A finished table and an unfinished sketch, side by side. Between them, you can see how carefully the calm was made.

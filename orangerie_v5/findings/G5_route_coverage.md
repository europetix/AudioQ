**Circulation, as far as the sources allow.** You enter from the west, at the Concorde end. Then come the hall and pronaos (Celmins), Room 1, the vestibule, and Room 2, which is a dead end at the east. You return through Room 1 to the hall and take the stairs or lift down. On the lower level the collection opens with a large-format hall, then the monographic rooms, with the temporary exhibition space, shop and café also below ground. You come back up and leave by the **new north-façade exit and footbridge into the Tuileries** (since 2025). One secondary guide notes that "the stairs down are easy to read as the way out". The audio should say clearly that the collection is down there and that the exit is not.

## 3. Is "lilies → collection → back up to lilies" practical?
- **Yes, physically.** The stairs and lift come back up by the hall, next to Room 1, so the return costs about 2 minutes of walking. Timed tickets cover a single visit, and nothing in the sources stops a visitor going back into the lily rooms.
- **But** it fights the new one-way flow: the exit, since 2025, is a separate north-façade exit, and the stairs "read as the way out". The lily rooms are busiest from late morning, so the second visit will often be the more crowded one. Most travellers reviewing the Orangerie spend 60–90 min in total.
- **Recommendation:** keep the order. Make the return a short **optional coda** (one "second look" track plus one sitting track), clearly labelled, with an "if you're short of time, the exit is this way" line at the end of the collection. Drop the V4 claim that skipping it "skips most of what we built".

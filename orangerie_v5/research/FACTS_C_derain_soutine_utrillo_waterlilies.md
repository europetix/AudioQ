# PART 1 — THE WATER LILIES (LES NYMPHÉAS)

## 1.1 The cycle at a glance
- **8 compositions on 22 panels, all 2 m high, 91 m long in total**, hung in **two oval rooms** that "form the infinity symbol" (the museum's wording). [ORG-ARCH] OFF; "eight compositions in twenty-two panels" also in the museum's Monet–Clemenceau text, via search snippet [ORG-MC] OFF. Some secondary sources say "93 m" or "nearly 100 m". **Use 91 m.** It also matches the sum of the official widths below (600+1275+850+1275+850+1275+1275+1700 cm = 9,100 cm).
- All eight compositions are the same height but different widths, so they fit the curved walls. [WL-HIST] OFF
- The museum's own figure for the height is 2 m. One English page gives "6.5 ft / 1.97 m" [WL-HIST snippet], a rounding difference.
- **Dates:** museum captions give **1914–1926** (e.g. Soleil couchant, Les Deux Saules); GAC/NGV-style captions give "c. 1915–1926" or "c. 1918–26". **Use "painted between 1914 and 1926"** (OFF). The cycle was "designed from 1915 until his death in 1926" [GAC] OFF.
- **Inventory:** "Les Nuages" carries **INV 20100** (search snippet from a museum-data page; SEC1). I found no other Nymphéas inventory numbers. **Do not voice.**
- **Room content:** "The first room brings together four compositions showing the reflections of the sky and the vegetation in the water, from morning to evening, whereas the second room contains a group of paintings with contrasts created by the branches of weeping willows around the water's edge." [WL-HIST] OFF
- **Orientation:** "Their east–west orientation places them symbolically within the course of the sun and the great historical Parisian axis (the royal way) that runs from the Arc de Triomphe to the Louvre." [WL-HIST] OFF. The museum's teaching material says scenes of sunrise sit to the **east** and twilight scenes to the **west** [ORG-DOSSIER] OFF (snippet only).
- **Visitor experience as Monet planned it:** he set "the forms, volumes, positioning, rhythm and the spaces between the various panels, as well as the unguided experience of the visitor through several entrances to the room. A vestibule provides access to the two rooms and marks the transition from the outside world." Light enters through the ceiling. [WL-HIST] OFF
- **Frontispiece quote:** "the illusion of an endless whole, of a wave without horizon and without shore" [WL-HIST] OFF. The words were Monet's, as reported by Roger Marx in the *Gazette des Beaux-Arts*, June 1909. See [G1] on the 1909 attribution of this and of the "peaceful meditation" phrase.

**Note on the panel count:** "22 panels" is official. The counts above (1+3+2+?+2+3+3+4) reach 22 only if **Matin has 4 panels**. The RMN caption for Matin reads "panel 3 of an ensemble of 4 adjacent panels" (OFF), while GAC-type captions say "three panels". The teachers' pack says "nineteen panels of 4 m × 2 assembled by 2, 3 or 4" (snippet). That fits Matin having 2 standard panels plus 2 narrower ones, with Soleil couchant as one non-standard panel. **Writers: say "twenty-two panels" for the whole cycle and don't give a panel count for Matin.**

**Room 1 vs Room 2:** Room 1 = Setting Sun, Clouds, Green Reflections, Morning. Room 2 = Reflections of Trees, Morning with Willows, Clear Morning with Willows, The Two Willows. [WL-HIST] OFF + G1 search. One travel blog puts Setting Sun in Room 2. **Wrong; ignore.**

**What the visitor sees (OFF-grade, general):** reflections of sky and plants in the water, "from morning to evening" in Room 1. In Room 2, contrasts made by the branches of weeping willows at the water's edge [WL-HIST]. Room 1 moves "from clouds at the water's surface to reflections where the water lilies emerge … with quick and dynamic brushstrokes" [ORG-DOSSIER snippet] OFF.

**Hooks (sourced):**
- Monet wanted to "sign them on Victory Day": the gift started as two panels offered the day after the Armistice and grew into a whole building [ORG-MC] OFF.
- He never saw the rooms finished. He died on 5 December 1926; they opened on 17 May 1927 [ORG-ARCH] OFF.
- For about 40 years (1960s to 2000) the lilies lived under a false ceiling. The 2000–2006 works gave them back the sky [ORG-HIST] OFF.
- The rooms run east–west, in step with the sun and on the Louvre–Arc de Triomphe axis [WL-HIST] OFF.

# PART 2 — ANDRÉ DERAIN (1880–1954)

## Holdings and relationship with Paul Guillaume
- **Count: contested; no official figure found.** Search summaries give 31 (source unclear; the old V4 script also says 31), 29 or 27. The often-repeated breakdown "Derain 28 / Soutine 22 / Utrillo 10" in a 146-painting collection is **NONE** (not found in any official snippet). **Writers: say "more than twenty-five paintings" or "one of the largest groups in the collection", or nothing.**
- The museum describes its Derains as "a corpus of landscapes, still lifes, nudes and portraits, representative of Paul Guillaume's taste for the classicizing side of the artist." (Snippet, attributed to the museum's Derain material.) OFF
- **How they met:** Derain met the young dealer Paul Guillaume through the poet **Max Jacob**, on the advice of **Guillaume Apollinaire**. [Portrait de Paul Guillaume page] OFF
- **1923:** Derain signs an exclusive agreement with Paul Guillaume. Earlier he had worked with Vollard and Kahnweiler. Met Museum, Leonard A. Lauder Research Center, Modern Art Index: https://www.metmuseum.org/research-centers/leonard-a-lauder-research-center/research-resources/modern-art-index-project/derain · SEC1 (highly reputable)
- Guillaume **commissioned** *Arlequin et Pierrot*. Derain painted **Domenica twice** (1925, and 1928–29). [artwork pages] OFF

### Derain — Arlequin et Pierrot (Harlequin and Pierrot)
- c. 1924 · oil on canvas · **175 × 175 cm** (SEC1: NGV "Renoir to Picasso" catalogue page https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/orangerie/derain.html) · inventory: a search summary gave "RF 1960-41" (**unverified, do not use**) · Jean Walter–Paul Guillaume collection
- On display 2026: **unknown**
- What you see: two Commedia dell'arte figures. Harlequin in his colourful chequered costume and bicorne hat; Pierrot in a white smock with a ruff and a black skullcap. Each has one knee raised and plays a guitar, against a neutral background, "in a never-ending dance like marionettes or puppets." Their gazes don't meet; their faces are serious; "the painting is suffused with a certain melancholy." [page] OFF
- Story: commissioned by Paul Guillaume. "The figure of Pierrot was finally recognised as a portrait of Paul Guillaume." [page] OFF. The model for both figures was the Spanish painter **Salvado**, who worked for Derain and for Picasso [Arlequin à la guitare page] OFF. Note the tension: Salvado posed, but Pierrot is read as Guillaume's portrait. Present both as the museum does.
- The NGV catalogue notes the musicians play **stringless instruments** in a barren landscape. SEC1. Check this against the painting before voicing it.
- Hook: the dealer who commissioned it ended up inside it, in white, as Pierrot. OFF
- Sources: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/es/obras/arlequin-et-pierrot-196175 (OFF); https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/arlequin-la-guitare-196176 (OFF); NGV (SEC1)

### Derain — Portrait de Paul Guillaume (Portrait of Paul Guillaume)
- Dated **1919** (it appeared with that date in Kahnweiler's 1920 book on Derain) · oil on canvas · dimensions: **NONE** · provenance: Paul Guillaume from 1919 → Mme Jean Walter → **acquired 1959** for the Louvre with help from the Société des Amis du Louvre → Orangerie. OFF
- On display: unknown
- What you see: three-quarter view, very elegant, gaze turned away, "impassive and detached", painted in cool tones. The bust stands out against a blue background of horizontal brushstrokes. His suit and bow tie are blue, like his eyes; **blue was his favourite colour**. He holds a nearly burnt-out cigarette in his left hand ("the only relaxed aspect of the painting") and leans on an open book (an art book or catalogue?). The likeness is not strong: the features are misshapen. OFF
- Exhibited at the Musée national d'art moderne, Paris, 1954–55, and in Japan (OFF snippet)
- Hook: the cigarette is "the only relaxed aspect". The dealer as a cool, blue, detached figure. OFF

### Derain — Portrait de Madame Paul Guillaume au grand chapeau (Madame Paul Guillaume with a Large Hat)
- Autumn or winter **1928–29** · oil on canvas · **92 × 73 cm** · Paul Guillaume collection → Mme Jean Walter → **bought 1959** (Louvre) → Orangerie. OFF
- On display: unknown

# PART 3 — CHAÏM SOUTINE (1893–1943)

## Holdings and relationship with Paul Guillaume / Domenica
- **Count:** 22 paintings is the figure repeated in secondary sources and in the old script; **no official count found** (SEC1/WEAK). **Official and safe:** "Paul Guillaume's early taste for the artist has resulted in the Orangerie housing **the largest collection of Soutine's paintings in Europe**." [https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/articles/chaim-soutine-105466] OFF
- Paul Guillaume was **one of the first** to take an interest in Soutine's painting. **Through him, the American collector Albert C. Barnes discovered Soutine**, which brought the painter international recognition. [same page] OFF
- Barnes saw Soutine's work in Paris in **1922** (a pastry-cook painting caught his eye) and bought **more than 50** works in a few weeks, through Guillaume. SEC1 (search summary; the Barnes Foundation print shop lists "The Pastry Chef (Baker Boy), c. 1919": https://prints.barnesfoundation.org/detail/489202/). Safe wording: "Barnes bought dozens of Soutines in 1922–23, with Guillaume as go-between."
- Soutine's first dealer was **Léopold Zborowski**, who sent him to **Céret** (1919–22) with room and board in exchange for his paintings [Les Maisons, Paysage avec personnage pages] OFF.
- **Domenica / Jean Walter:** several Soutines (e.g. *Le Petit Pâtissier*, bought "in the early 1930s") entered the collection late. One snippet gives *Le Garçon d'étage*'s provenance as "Henri Bing; Paul Guillaume … acquired 1977". **1977 is the year Domenica died and the usufruct ended. Unverified link; don't voice.**

### Soutine — Le Petit Pâtissier (The Little Pastry Cook)

- Hooks: a dealer buying a second pastry cook to replace the first one he let go (to Barnes; the Barnes link is SEC1). A kitchen boy posed like a king of France (Fouquet), per the museum. OFF

### Soutine — Le Garçon d'étage (The Room-Service Waiter / Bellhop)

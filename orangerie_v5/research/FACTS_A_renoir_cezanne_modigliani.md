## Collection-level facts (all OFF)

- The Jean Walter–Paul Guillaume collection: **148 works** from the 1860s to the 1930s. Impressionist-period holdings:
  **25 Renoir, 15 Cézanne, 1 Gauguin, 1 Monet, 1 Sisley**. Twentieth century: **5 Modigliani, 1 Van Dongen** (plus 12 Picasso,
  10 Matisse, 9 Rousseau, ~30–31 Derain, 10 Utrillo, 22 Soutine, 6–7 Laurencin; the official pages differ slightly on
  Derain/Laurencin).
  Sources: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/collection/new-presentation-collection ;
  https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/collection/permanent-collections-walter-guillaume-collection (OFF)
  - Note: one non-official search summary claimed "24 works by Renoir", and a tourism site said "16 Cézanne". **Use the
    official 25 / 15.**
- The state acquired the collection **in two stages: 47 paintings in 1959 and 99 paintings in 1963**, subject to usufruct
  (Domenica Walter kept the use of it). The Société des Amis du Louvre ran a public funding pledge so that the RMN could buy the
  "most important works", raising 135 million francs of the time. Domenica Walter asked for the collection to be named after
  her two husbands. Malraux and Domenica Walter opened a first temporary display on **31 January 1966**. The collection
  became state property when she died in **1977**. It has been on permanent display only **since 1984**. (OFF:
  https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/article/history-jean-walter-paul-guillaume-collection ;
  https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/collection/les-arts-a-parishistory-of-the-collection)
  - Inventory logic: RF 1960-xx = first tranche (1959 purchase, recorded 1960); RF 1963-xx = second tranche. This is
    consistent with all RF numbers found below, but it is my inference.
- Domenica Walter (1898–1977) was the widow of Paul Guillaume (1891–1934) and then of the architect Jean Walter (1883–1957).
  She fulfilled Guillaume's wish to create "the first French museum of modern art". (OFF, same pages)
- Current display (official "current presentation" page): the collection is on the lower level, in galleries dug out of the
  basement. The visit opens with large works by the modern "primitives" (Picasso, Rousseau, Derain, Modigliani, Matisse), in
  line with Apollinaire's vision. There are **monographic galleries**, plus two newer galleries: one of highlights and one of
  contemporary counterpoints. There is also a deposit of African and Oceanian sculpture from the former Paul Guillaume
  collection (musée du quai Branly – Jacques Chirac). (OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/collection/new-presentation-collection)
  - **No room numbers were found.**

# PIERRE-AUGUSTE RENOIR (1841–1919): 25 works (OFF)

### Renoir and Paul Guillaume / Domenica Walter

### Renoir — Jeunes filles au piano (Young Girls at the Piano)
- c.1892 · oil on canvas · **116 × 81 cm** · **RF 1960-16** (OFF: Orangerie artwork page; Joconde 00000089507; Smartify
  caption gives the same data)
- Provenance (OFF): left by Renoir on deposit with Durand-Ruel from 1 Sep 1914 to 27 Jul 1917 → **in Renoir's studio at his
  death** → bought by **Paul Guillaume in 1928** → Mme Jean Walter → state, 1959 tranche. Exhibited in the Paul Guillaume
  collection show at **Bernheim-Jeune, Paris, 1929**.
- Display 2026: it was in the 2024–26 touring show (listed on the exhibition pages). Its 2026 status is unconfirmed.
- Story (OFF: Orsay notice https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/jeunes-filles-au-piano-1164):
  - Around 1892, Renoir's friends were annoyed that the French state had never bought any of his works.
  - **Stéphane Mallarmé and Roger Marx** (a young official of the Beaux-Arts administration) pushed for Impressionist works to
    enter national museums.
  - After an informal state commission, one version was bought for the **Musée du Luxembourg in 1892**. That version is now at
    Orsay (116 × 90 cm).
  - There are three other finished versions (one in the **Metropolitan Museum**, two private), **"a sketch in oils at the Musée
    de l'Orangerie"**, and a pastel of the same size (private).
- **Hook:** the Orangerie picture is the version Renoir **kept for himself**: still in his studio when he died, then bought by
  Guillaume. The "official" version went to the state. (OFF; framing is a writer's inference from the provenance)

### Renoir — Yvonne et Christine Lerolle au piano (Yvonne and Christine Lerolle at the Piano)
- c.1897 · oil on canvas · **73 × 92 cm** · **RF 1960-19** (Joconde 00000089515, OFF). Guillaume's acquisition date: not found.
- Visitor sees (OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/yvonne-et-christine-lerolle-au-piano-196512):
  - Yvonne at the piano on the left. Christine on the right turning the pages of the score.
  - On the wall of the family salon are **two Degas paintings from their father's collection**: *Avant la course* and
    *Danseuses sous un arbre*.
- Story (OFF):
  - The girls were the two eldest daughters of the painter and collector **Henry Lerolle** (1848–1929). They were then about
    18 and 20.
  - Music mattered in the Lerolle family: they held musical soirées and regularly received **Claude Debussy**.
- Hook: a painting with paintings inside it. Renoir slips his friend Degas onto the wall. (OFF facts)
- Related (OFF, Orsay): Orsay holds a **Degas photograph / self-portrait with Yvonne and Christine Lerolle**
  (https://musee-orsay.fr/en/node/39841) and an anonymous photo of the girls at the piano with their brother and mother. This is
  good corroboration for a script.

### Renoir — Claude Renoir en clown (Claude Renoir as a Clown)
- 1909 · oil on canvas · **RF 1960-17** (Joconde, OFF)
- Dimensions **unverified**: one school PDF gives 120 × 70 cm.
- Story (OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/claude-renoir-en-clown-196520):
  - **Claude ("Coco"), about eight**, hated putting on the **red clown costume** and the **stockings that prickled and
    tickled** him. The portrait needed several sittings.
  - He finally agreed to wear the stockings for a short time **in return for a box of oil paints and a train set**.
  - Renoir wanted a ceremonial portrait of his son, to **measure himself against the old masters, Velázquez in particular**
    (OFF, Spanish version of the artwork page as summarised).
- Hook: the bribe (paints plus a train set) is the best child-sitter anecdote in the collection. (OFF)
- Provenance: Paul Guillaume → Mme Walter → state 1959 (RF 1960). The Amis du Louvre support is mentioned. **Ignore** a
  summary's "state acquired in 1977"; see the Contested list.

### Renoir — Gabrielle et Jean (Gabrielle and Jean)
- 1895–1896 · oil on canvas · **65 × 54 cm** · **RF 1960-18** (OFF: Joconde / Orangerie page)
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - Gabrielle holds little Jean on her knee and keeps him busy with **toy farm animals**.
  - The figurines are loosely brushed and melt into the tabletop. "We can barely recognise the cow in Gabrielle's hand."
- Story (OFF): one of an extensive series of 1895–96 bringing together Renoir's **second son Jean** (the future film director:
  general knowledge, SEC) and **Gabrielle** (Gabrielle Renard), the family's faithful servant. It is "a veritable album of his
  son's early childhood". Gabrielle could always hold the young model's attention.
- Hook: the nanny as Renoir's assistant in the studio. She keeps the toddler still so the painter can work. (OFF)
- Related: Washington NGA *Child with Toys – Gabrielle and the Artist's Son, Jean* belongs to the same series (SEC: nga.gov).

### Renoir — Femme nue dans un paysage (Nude in a Landscape)
- **1883** · oil on canvas · **65 × 54 cm** · **RF 1963-13** (OFF)
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - A traditional bather in a timeless waterside setting.
  - The nude woman has brown hair pinned up. She sits on a white cloth and dries her leg with it.
  - The contours and line show a "more confident style".
- Story (OFF):
  - After his **1881 trip to Italy**, Renoir reached a turning point in 1883.
  - This picture announces the line-based manner that led to **Les Grandes Baigneuses (1884–87, Philadelphia Museum of Art)**.
  - "Some historians believe" the model resembles **Suzanne Valadon**, whom Renoir often painted then. The museum hedges this,
    so keep the hedge.
- Display: it was in the touring show. 2026 status unconfirmed.

### Renoir — Femme nue couchée (Gabrielle) (Reclining Nude, Gabrielle)
- **1906** · oil on canvas · **67 × 160 cm** · **RF 1960-22** (Joconde, OFF)
- Story (OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femme-nue-couchee-gabrielle-196519):
  - The model is Gabrielle, hired by the Renoirs to look after their children, who "quickly became one of the painter's
    favourite models".
  - The picture belongs to the European tradition of reclining nudes indoors: **Titian, Rubens, Goya, Ingres, Manet**. In his
    last decade, **Titian and Rubens** came to the fore in Renoir's idea of the nude.
  - It is one of the **few indoor nudes** in his work. Between 1903 and 1907 he painted three large horizontal reclining
    figures on cushions.
- Hook: the same Gabrielle who minded baby Jean (above) becomes, ten years later, Renoir's Venus. (OFF facts)

### Renoir — Baigneuse aux cheveux longs (Bather with Long Hair)
- c.1895 · oil on canvas · **82 × 65 cm** · RF not found (OFF)
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - A nude bather comes out of the water, drawing a white drape across her chest.
  - Her loose golden hair echoes the movement of the vegetation behind her.
- Story (OFF):
  - Typical of Renoir's **"pearly period"**, named for its luminous skin tones.
  - An almost identical 1895 *Bather* is at the **Barnes Foundation**, Philadelphia.

### Renoir — Baigneuse assise s'essuyant une jambe (Seated Bather Drying Her Leg)
- **1914** · oil on canvas · **51 × 41 cm** (OFF)
- Story (OFF): **the latest Renoir nude in the Orangerie**. Paul Guillaume bought it **in 1924**, five years after the
  painter's death.
- Hook: compare it with *Femme nue dans un paysage* (1883). It is the same gesture of drying a leg, painted 31 years apart.
  This works if both are hung.

### Renoir — Paysage de neige (Snowy Landscape)
- c.1875 · oil on canvas · **51 × 66 cm** (OFF)
- Story (OFF):
  - Probably painted in early 1875 after the **heavy snow of winter 1874–75**.
  - Renoir experiments with **coloured shadows**, showing that snow reflects the colours of the things around it rather than
    being simply white.
- Hook: Renoir is said to have disliked winter. This is **NOT verified; do not use**.

### Renoir — Femme à la lettre (Woman with a Letter)
- 1890–1895 · oil on canvas · **65 × 54 cm** (OFF)
- Visitor sees (OFF): a young woman seated in three-quarter view, her head resting on her right hand, pondering a letter in her
  left hand.
- Story (OFF): the letter is a pretext. Renoir paints her in the manner of **Fragonard's 18th-century "fantasy figures"**.

### Renoir — Pommes et poires (Apples and Pears)
- c.1895 · oil on canvas · **32 × 41 cm** (OFF)
- Story (OFF): the Renoir still life showing **the greatest influence of Cézanne**, in its subject and its "highly intricate
  composition".
- Hook: a bridge to the Cézanne room. Renoir and Cézanne were friends; they painted together at L'Estaque in the 1880s
  (SEC, general art history; it is not on the official page found).

### Renoir — Bouquet de tulipes (Bouquet of Tulips)
- c.1905 · oil on canvas · **44 × 37 cm** (OFF)
- Visitor sees (OFF): a shimmering bouquet in reds, greens and yellows; a green vase of tulips and other flowers; a background
  of red and beige strokes.
- Story (OFF): probably painted at **Cagnes** soon after his final move to the south. It stayed in his studio until his death.
  Guillaume bought it in **1929**.

### Renoir — Portrait de deux fillettes (Two Little Girls)
- 1890–1892 (OFF). Two girls sharing a pastime, one of Renoir's favourite subjects in the 1890s.
- Dimensions not found.

### Renoir — Gabrielle au jardin (Gabrielle in the Garden)

### Renoir — Femme accoudée (Woman Leaning on Her Elbow)

### Renoir — Blonde à la rose (Blonde with a Rose)
- Bought by Guillaume in **1929**. It had stayed in Renoir's studio and then passed to his son **Claude Renoir**. (OFF)

### Renoir — Bouquet dans une loge (Bouquet in a Theatre Box)

# PAUL CÉZANNE (1839–1906): 15 works (OFF)

### Cézanne and Paul Guillaume / Domenica Walter (OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/articles/paul-cezanne-105454)
- In the **1920s Paul Guillaume** bought several major Cézannes: *Madame Cézanne au jardin*, *Portrait de Madame Cézanne* and
  *Portrait du fils de l'artiste*.
- His widow **Domenica, "who liked Cézanne's paintings"**, bought more after his death, notably landscapes: *Le Rocher
  rouge* and *Paysage au toit rouge / Le Pin à l'Estaque*.
- **14 May 1952**: Domenica Walter bought ***Pommes et biscuits*** at the **Gabriel Cognacq sale, Galerie Charpentier** (lot 28)
  for "a vast sum" / "an unprecedented sum". "This sensational purchase attracted public attention both to her and to her
  magnificent collection." (OFF) The exact price was **not found**.

### Cézanne — Pommes et biscuits (Apples and Biscuits)
- c.1880 · oil on canvas · **45 × 55 cm** · **RF 1960-11** (RF and dimensions from a wikidata/search summary; date OFF).
  Treat RF and dimensions as needing a check.
- Visitor sees (OFF): "a very balanced composition with just a plate and a few apples arranged on a chest". His experiments in
  stylising form and **expressing volume through colour** are fully developed here.
- Status (OFF): "one of Paul Cézanne's most stunning masterpieces and one of the symbols of his great mastery of still life".
- Hook: the 1952 auction. A widow's record-breaking bid made the collection famous. (OFF)

### Cézanne — Madame Cézanne au jardin (Madame Cézanne in the Garden)
- c.1880 (artwork page) · oil on canvas · **80 × 63 cm**. The Cézanne article groups it under "1885–1895": **date conflict, use
  "around 1880" as on the artwork page.**
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - A full-length portrait outdoors, which is unusual: Cézanne preferred close framings and bare interiors.
  - Hortense's face is expressionless.
  - The canvas seems unfinished: the lower part is barely painted and a large area of white priming shows.
- Story (OFF):
  - **Hortense Fiquet** was a young model who lived with Cézanne and bore him a son in **1872**. He married her only in
    **1886**.
  - He painted **twenty-five portraits of her** (per the Orangerie page). Philadelphia Museum of Art says "at least forty-four
    paintings", which probably counts differently. **Use the Orangerie's 25 and attribute it.**
  - With people close to him Cézanne put formal research first, giving the model "monumentality and permanence".
- Provenance: Paul Guillaume (1920s) → Domenica Walter. (OFF)
- Display: it was in the touring show. It may be lent to Grand Palais "Cézanne et nous" (unverified).

### Cézanne — Portrait de Madame Cézanne (Portrait of Madame Cézanne)
- c.1885–1895 (OFF). Dimensions and RF not found.
- Visitor sees (OFF): Hortense seated on a simple armchair, hands in her lap, in a "very hieratic pose". Her face is
  "completely expressionless and rather unflattering". She wears a simple black dress against an indeterminate blue-green
  scumbled background. The painting is unfinished.
- Provenance: Paul Guillaume, 1920s (OFF).
- **Possibly on loan to the Grand Palais, Sep 2026–Jan 2027** (search summary only; unconfirmed).

### Cézanne — Portrait du fils de l'artiste (Portrait of the Artist's Son)
- c.1880 (Orangerie). Another source dates it 1881–82; that and the 34 × 37.5 cm size come from a non-Orangerie summary and are
  **unverified**.
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - Young Paul seems to sit on the arm of an armchair; only the chair back is painted, to the right, against a plain
    blue-green ground.
  - The close framing crops both model and chair, which is "reminiscent of photography".
  - The forms are simplified with defined outlines that "recall the work of Gauguin". The composition is an interplay of curves:
    armchair, shoulders, neck, face, hair.
- Story (OFF): "the most ambitious and the most accomplished" of Cézanne's portraits of his young son.
- Provenance: Paul Guillaume, 1920s (OFF).

### Cézanne — Le Rocher rouge (The Red Rock)
- c.1895 / 1895–1900 · oil on canvas · about **92 × 68 cm** (Google Arts & Culture lists it as w680 × h920 mm) ·
  **RF 1960-14** (Gianadda press list via search summary: SEC)
- Visitor sees (OFF): a patch of trees in **cross-hatched brushwork** standing out against **orange rock**, topped by blue sky.
- Story (OFF): the deserted **Bibémus quarries** near Aix, a motif Cézanne returned to many times between 1895 and 1904. "One of
  the masterpieces of Cézanne's maturity."
- Provenance: bought by Domenica (OFF, Cézanne article).

### Cézanne — Dans le parc de Château Noir (In the Park of Château Noir)
- 1898–1900 · oil on canvas · **92 × 73 cm** · **RF 1960-15** (Gianadda list via search: SEC; dimensions via search summary).
- No official description was retrieved. **Gap.**

### Cézanne — Paysage au toit rouge ou Le Pin à l'Estaque (Landscape with Red Roof, or The Pine at L'Estaque)
- 1875–76 · oil on canvas · **73 × 60 cm** (OFF)
- Story (OFF):
  - Painted during a stay at **L'Estaque in 1876**. It shows **Pissarro's influence**: the vertical format and the foreground
    tree trunk and low wall.
  - The leaning trunk and branches lead the eye to the trees in the centre.
- Provenance: bought by Domenica (OFF).

### Cézanne — Arbres et maisons (Trees and Houses)
- c.1885 · oil on canvas · **54 × 73 cm** (OFF)
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - A hamlet among two patches of leafless trees, near **Le Jas de Bouffan**, the Cézanne family house near Aix.
  - The dark tree trunks stand against very pale, schematic houses.
  - Traditional perspective dissolves as the planes blur together.
- Story (OFF): one of three versions. The others are in the **Metropolitan Museum** and the **Nasjonalmuseet, Oslo**.

### Cézanne — Fleurs et fruits + Fleurs dans un vase bleu (Flowers and Fruit / Flowers in a Blue Vase)
- Dates and dimensions not retrieved.
- Story (OFF, both artwork pages): **one unfinished painting cut in two**.
  - A dealer probably cut it into at least two parts between 1904 and 1914.
  - **Paul Guillaume bought *Fleurs et fruits* from Ambroise Vollard in 1931.**
  - Years after his death, **Domenica bought *Fleurs dans un vase bleu* without knowing it was the other half.**
  - The curator **Michel Hoog** pieced the story together in **1992**.
  - Cézanne may have used **paper flowers** as a model, as he sometimes did.
- **Hook (strong, OFF):** husband and wife unknowingly reunited the two halves of one canvas.

### Cézanne — La Barque et les baigneurs (The Boat and the Bathers)
- c.1890 · oil on canvas · **30 × 125 cm** · **RF 1973-55; RF 1960-12; RF 1960-13** (three numbers for three pieces) (OFF)
- Story (OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/la-barque-et-les-baigneurs-196030):
  - It was commissioned as a **decoration for the Paris apartment of the collector Victor Chocquet**, meant to hang above a door
    with its pendant *La Vasque au paon* (private collection).
  - According to Georges Rivière, both panels were still unfinished when Chocquet died in **1891**.
  - The painting was later **cut into three**. **Domenica gave two of the pieces to the state.** The national museums bought the
    **missing central part in 1973** (from Mme Yvonne Hébert-Blondez), and the work was reassembled.
  - The Orangerie held a "Focus collection: La Barque de Cézanne" display (https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/node/218581).
- **Hook (strong, OFF):** a frieze cut into three and put back together in 1973.

### Cézanne — Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe (Luncheon on the Grass)
- 1876–1877 · oil on canvas · **21 × 27 cm** (a tiny picture) (OFF)
- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - Young people picnic in nature near a village whose **bell tower** shows.
  - The figures sit either side of a vertical axis set by a **standing woman in the centre, her silhouette extended by the
    church spire**.
- Story (OFF):
  - It may recall Cézanne's admiration for **Poussin's Bacchanals**, which he copied at the Louvre, or happy childhood memories
    around Aix.
  - The title is uncertain and may have been added later. If it is Cézanne's own, it may be a **humorous nod to Manet's
    *Déjeuner sur l'herbe***.

### Cézanne — Vase paillé, sucrier et pommes (Straw-covered Vase, Sugar Bowl and Apples)
- 1890–1894 · oil on canvas · **36 × 46 cm** (OFF)
- Story (OFF):
  - "One of the finest examples of Cézanne's inventiveness": objects are seen from improbable viewpoints, and he chooses a
    coherent composition over a realistic one.
  - Some elements are hard to identify, such as a vertical line right of the vase and an oblique one ("a knife handle?").
- Provenance: Paul Guillaume, **1927** → Domenica Walter (OFF).

### Cézanne — Fruits, serviette et boîte à lait (Fruit, Napkin and Milk Jug)
- c.1880 · **60 × 73 cm** (OFF). One search summary called it "pastel"; that is **almost certainly an error**, so check the
  medium on the page.
- Story (OFF):
  - The recurring props (chest, apples, knife, wallpaper) appear here.
  - The unusual viewing angle "was a true innovation and even shocking in Cézanne's time".
  - It is more austere than his other still lifes: fainter greens and geometric lines, stressed by the **wallpaper pattern**.

### Cézanne — Nature morte, poire et pommes vertes (Still Life, Pear and Green Apples): attribution doubtful
- 1873–1875 · oil on canvas · **22 × 32 cm** · **RF 1963-10** (OFF / Gianadda)
- Visitor sees (OFF): four fruits (a pear and three green apples, "or perhaps a peach and two plums") on a table against a bare
  wall.
- Story (OFF):
  - The museum itself says there is **doubt about the attribution**.
  - **John Rewald rejected it** in his 1996 catalogue raisonné. It **could be by Paul Gachet** (1873–1962), son of Dr Gachet.
  - It stayed in Gachet's home until **Domenica Walter bought it in 1951, in exchange for two Soutine paintings**, through an
    intermediary (Methey or Mathey).
- Hook: a candid "is it even a Cézanne?" stop. The museum is open about the doubt. (OFF)

### Modigliani — Paul Guillaume, Novo Pilota
- **1915** · **oil on cardboard mounted on cradled plywood** · **105 × 75 cm** (SEC: NGV Melbourne caption; Orangerie artwork
  page https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/paul-guillaume-novo-pilota-196295). RF not found.
- Visitor sees:
  - Guillaume, **aged 23**, is an elegant, self-assured young man looking straight at us. His figure stands out against a **red
    background**, and his dark suit and hat contrast with his face and white shirt. (OFF)
  - Inscriptions (SEC: NGV Melbourne, Orangerie touring catalogue text):
    - "PAUL GUILLAUME" at upper left
    - a **Star of David with "Stella Maris"** at upper right
    - **"Novo Pilota"** at lower left
    - signature at lower right with a **swastika**, used in its earlier Sanskrit sense of good omen. **Handle with care in
      audio, or omit.**
- Story (OFF):
  - Painted at the home of Modigliani's companion, the British poet **Beatrice Hastings** (1879–1943), in Montmartre.
  - "Novo Pilota" means **"new pilot / new helmsman"**. Modigliani hails the young dealer as the guide of modern art, "like a
    sailor or one of the wise men guided by a star".
- **Hook:** the painter literally writes his verdict on the canvas: "new pilot". (OFF)
- Display: this is the museum's emblematic Modigliani. The modern "primitives" (Modigliani included) open the collection
  route, but **room placement was not confirmed for 2026**.

### Modigliani — Le Jeune Apprenti (The Young Apprentice)

- Visitor sees (OFF):
  - A seated young man, pensive, his head resting on one hand. Chair and table merge with his body.
  - A soft palette of greys and browns, broken only by the **white shirt collar and the intense blue of the eyes**.
  - The thin, smooth young face contrasts with his **coarse hands**.
  - Curved contours and flattened forms show **Gauguin's influence**.

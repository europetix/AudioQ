# 1. HENRI MATISSE

## Holdings
- **10 paintings by Matisse** in the Walter-Guillaume collection. [OFF: musee-orangerie.fr Walter-Guillaume collection page https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/node/197501 ; Joconde notice of Les Trois Sœurs also states "10 Matisses"]
- 9 of the 10 identified by title (below). **The 10th was not identified** (gap).

## Matisse, Paul Guillaume and Domenica
- Domenica Walter-Guillaume "only kept paintings essentially produced in the artist's Nice period (1917–1929)". [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/articles/henri-matisse-105458]
- In Nice Matisse painted many odalisques in the 1920s, drawing on Ingres and Chassériau. In 1927 critics compared them to a "wallpaper dealer's display". [OFF: same page]
- Paul Guillaume was "the first art dealer to devote an exhibition confronting" Matisse and Picasso, in his gallery in **1918**. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artist/pablo-picasso]
- Paul Guillaume "contributed a great deal to the constitution of Doctor Barnes' collection" (Barnes Foundation, Philadelphia). [OFF: Les Trois Sœurs page]

### Matisse — Les Trois Sœurs (The Three Sisters)
- 1917 · oil on canvas · 92 × 73 cm · inventory: a search summary of Joconde gave "RF 1963-63" — **unconfirmed, verify** · provenance: Auguste Pellerin collection (Pellerin sale, **7 May 1926**) → Paul Guillaume → acquired **1963** from Mme Jean Walter with the support of the Société des Amis du Louvre. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/les-trois-soeurs-196463 ; Joconde https://pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/00000089475]
- **Which version?** The Orangerie owns this single 92 × 73 canvas. **Three other paintings of the same three sisters** ("each time in different clothing and poses") are at the **Barnes Foundation**, Philadelphia. [OFF: Orangerie page]
- Display 2026: unknown.
- What you see: three young brunettes seated before a bistre (brown) background. Two look out at the viewer; the third is absorbed in her reading. Matisse balances "seemingly incompatible elements": the sisters' different attitudes, discordant colours, and several levels of perspective side by side. [OFF]
- Context: possible sources are Manet, Japanese prints, and *Les Dames de Gand* (Louvre, then attributed to Jacques-Louis David). [OFF]
- Story: the Barnes sister paintings "very likely" passed through Guillaume's gallery on their way to the US. The Orangerie canvas "was one of the few works that Paul Guillaume bought at a public sale, certainly in memory of the paintings ceded to Doctor Barnes." [OFF]
- **Hook:** a dealer who sold the other three sisters to America, then bought the last one at auction for himself. [OFF]

### Matisse — Le Boudoir (The Boudoir)
- 1921, Nice · oil on canvas · 73 × 60 cm · inventory not found · provenance not found. [OFF for title and page: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/le-boudoir-196469 ; date and dimensions from fr.wikipedia and Wikidata = WEAK]
- What you see: an interior with two women. Whites, greys and light blues, warmed by pink tiles, a bouquet of flowers on the dresser at right, and yellows in a skirt, an armchair and the walls. The very light palette and technique "recall watercolour". [WEAK: fr.wikipedia "Le Boudoir". Likely paraphrases the museum page but **not confirmed**]
- Sitters: "likely" Marguerite Matisse (the artist's daughter) and Henriette Darricarrère. [WEAK, do not use as fact]
- Setting: a room in Matisse's hotel in Nice, the Hôtel de la Méditerranée. [WEAK. The hotel itself is OFF via Femmes au canapé, below]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Femmes au canapé ou Le Divan (Women on a Sofa, or The Divan)
- 1921 · oil on canvas · dimensions, inventory and provenance not found. [OFF page: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femmes-au-canape-ou-le-divan-196470]
- Context: Matisse often stayed at the **Hôtel de la Méditerranée** in Nice from 1916, "captivated by the light of the Midi". There he developed the theme of an oppressive interior opening onto the liberating outside world through a door or window. Here he paints his hotel room. [OFF]
- What you see: two perspectives in one picture. One looks down at the floor, where the tiling shows surface but not depth. The other looks far out through the window. A triangular composition, tempered by a **large red curtain** at right, leads the eye to a point in the sky beyond the window. There is no reflection, so nothing distracts the gaze. The two women at right are "more or less sketched". The divan was repainted to get the best angle. Reds and greens of varying intensity, warm and cold, "reflect these contradictory emotions". [OFF]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Femme au violon (Woman with a Violin)
- 1921–1922 · oil on canvas · 55 × 46 cm (framed 78 × 69.5 × 8 cm) · inventory and provenance not found. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/es/obras/femme-au-violon-196467]
- What you see: a young seated woman with a violin on her knees and a bow in her right hand, set against the **blue circle pattern of a Moorish screen**. The open violin case lies on the table where her elbow rests. A typical Nice interior of c. 1921–23. [OFF]
- Related: a very similar, slightly smaller version with the same title is at the Baltimore Museum of Art. [OFF]
- Paul Guillaume "owned several paintings on the music theme". [OFF]
- Loan history: lent to the **Pinacoteca di Brera, Milan**, for "Quattro chiacchiere fra Matisse e Bonnard" (12 Sept 2023 – 14 Jan 2024), shown beside Bonnard's portrait of Marthe. [SEC2: pinacotecabrera.org ; finestresullarte.info ; cultura.gov.it]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Femme à la mandoline (Woman with a Mandolin)
- c. 1921–22 (exact official date not captured) · oil on canvas · dimensions not found · inventory: a search summary gave "RF 1963-69" (Joconde 00000089479) — **unconfirmed, verify** · provenance: bought from Matisse by Bernheim-Jeune on **15 January 1923** → Paul Guillaume → Mme Jean Walter → state, 1963. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femme-la-mandoline-196471]
- Context: "The window is a recurrent theme in Matisse's work, enabling him to open up the space… or introduce a painting within a painting." Musical instruments recur in the Nice paintings, "when music played an important role in the painter's daily life". [OFF]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Odalisque bleue ou l'Esclave blanche (Blue Odalisque, or The White Slave)
- Date, dimensions and inventory not captured · provenance: bought from Matisse by Bernheim-Jeune on **22 March 1922**, sold **26 Sept 1922**, then Paul Guillaume collection from **1929**. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/it/opere/odalisque-bleue-ou-lesclave-blanche-196468]
- What you see: the décor is reduced to a **Moorish screen**, and the model is enclosed in a restricted space. The raised arm emphasises the curves of the chest. Painted in blue tones, so fluidly that it "brings watercolours to mind". [OFF]
- Context: for Matisse the odalisque theme was a pretext "to paint the nude". He wanted to integrate the model's body and shadow into an ornamental décor that affirms the flatness of the picture. [OFF]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Nu drapé étendu (Reclining Draped Nude)
- 1923–1924 · oil on canvas · dimensions and inventory not captured · provenance: Paul Guillaume from **1929** → Mme Jean Walter → acquired 1963 with the Société des Amis du Louvre. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/es/obras/nu-drape-etendu-196472 ; Joconde 00000089482]
- What you see: unlike the other odalisques, the décor is greatly simplified to a red curtain, a neutral white sheet over the sofa, and one or two cushions. Contours are strongly outlined in black. White bloomers add volume. Pink-highlighted folds of the model's right side echo the folds of the cloth at her hips. She is in a pose Matisse loved: legs drawn in, arms raised above the head. [OFF]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Odalisque à la culotte rouge (Odalisque with Red Trousers)
- c. 1924–1925 · oil on canvas · 50 × 61 cm · inventory: a search summary gave "RF 1963-66" — **unconfirmed** · provenance: Paul Guillaume from **1929** → Domenica Walter → acquired 1963 with the Société des Amis du Louvre. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/odalisque-la-culotte-rouge-196473 ; date and dimensions SEC1 from a 2023 press article]
- **Do not confuse it with** the *Odalisque à la culotte rouge* of 1921 at the **Centre Pompidou**, a different painting with the same title. [OFF: centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/cpb4nq exists. The difference in date is from general knowledge; verify before using]
- Exhibitions: Japan tour 1998–99 (Tokyo, Nagoya, Hiroshima, Niigata, Kyoto). [OFF]
- Display 2026: unknown.

### Matisse — Odalisque à la culotte grise (Odalisque with Grey Trousers)
- 1926–1927 · oil on canvas · 54 × 65 cm (artchive, WEAK) · inventory: a search summary gave "RF 1963-67" — **unconfirmed**. [OFF page: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/odalisque-la-culotte-grise-196476 ; museum shop reproduction titled "Odalisque with Grey Trousers, 1926–1927" = OFF for the date]
- What you see: geometry and strong colour dominate. The odalisque, in sober flesh, green and grey, seems to melt into the décor. Big fabric motifs that Matisse stretched on a folding frame, and a red-dominated palette, "annihilate all feeling of space". [Search summary. Probably official wording, but grade SEC1 and verify]
- Model Henriette Darricarrère (Matisse's main model 1920–27): **not confirmed for this canvas**. [WEAK]
- Display 2026: unknown.

# 2. PABLO PICASSO

## Holdings
- **12 works by Picasso** in the Walter-Guillaume collection. [OFF: Walter-Guillaume collection page]
- All 12 identified (page IDs 196488–196499), plus 1 archival letter (not counted, not exhibited).

## Picasso and Paul Guillaume / Domenica
- Guillaume "was not Picasso's official dealer", but "their shared interest in primitive art" brought them together. He staged the first Matisse–Picasso confrontation in his gallery in 1918. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artist/pablo-picasso]
- The Orangerie's Picassos "bear witness to his various periods, from the early 1900s to the 1920s": Blue (L'Étreinte), Rose (Adolescents, Femme au peigne), proto-Cubist (Nu sur fond rouge, "the first of a series… leading up to *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*", MoMA), the classical 1920s, and late Cubism (Grande nature morte, Femme au tambourin). [OFF, same page]
- Domenica kept Femme au peigne, "unlike the many other Picassos that she sold in the years following her husband's death". [OFF: Femme au peigne page] Grande nature morte is "the only Cubist work that was preserved by Domenica". [OFF: Grande nature morte page]
- Archive: a handwritten letter from Picasso to Paul Guillaume, undated, 18 × 13 cm, **not currently exhibited**. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/correspondance-manuscrite-pablo-picasso-paul-guillaume-196487]

### Picasso — L'Étreinte (The Embrace)
- **1903** · **pastel** · 98 × 57 cm · first inventoried as **RF 1960-34** (Paintings Dept), later moved to the Louvre's **Cabinet des dessins**. **On long-term loan (dépôt) to the Orangerie.** Provenance: Ambroise Vollard → Paul Guillaume (acquired **1930**). [OFF: Louvre collections https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl020039499 ; https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/letreinte-196488]
- Display 2026: **unknown**. As a pastel it is light-sensitive. The Louvre notice says it can be seen "by appointment in the reference room" for prints and drawings, which suggests it is not permanently on the wall. **Do not promise it.**
- What you see: a nude man and woman embracing in a bedroom, in blues and pinks. **The woman is pregnant.** Cold Blue Period colours. [OFF]
- Context: one of a series of drawings of a standing nude couple that culminated in **La Vie** (1903, Cleveland Museum of Art). Made in Barcelona, in the studio Picasso had shared with Carlos Casagemas, whose suicide in Paris in 1901 deeply affected him. [OFF/SEC: Orangerie artist page and Apollo Magazine]
- Quote attributed to Picasso, as reported in the search summary: "What I want is that my picture should evoke nothing but emotion." [SEC1 — **verify the exact source before using**]

### Picasso — Les Adolescents (The Adolescents)
- **1906** · oil on canvas · **157 × 117 cm** · inventory not found · provenance: Ambroise Vollard → Paul Guillaume (bought **1930**) → Domenica Walter → acquired by the state 1959/1963 with the Société des Amis du Louvre (sources disagree on the year). [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/les-adolescents-196489]
- What you see: two nudes, outlined and modelled on a pink and red monochrome ground, with a sculptural, classical feel. The young man crosses his arms above his head. The young woman, "or androgynous adolescent", balances a **pitcher on her head**. **Below the legs you can see wavy lines: the trace of another, horizontal composition the artist erased.** [OFF]
- Context: the pink-ochre monochrome appeared after Picasso's stay at **Gósol** (Catalan Pyrenees) with Fernande Olivier. The village earth had an unusual ochre colour. This "pink classicism" seems to anticipate his 1920s "return to order". [OFF]
- **Hook:** look for the ghost of an abandoned picture under their feet. [OFF]

### Picasso — Femme au peigne (Woman with a Comb)
- **1906** · large **gouache on paper** · dimensions and inventory not found · provenance: Paul Guillaume, bought from Picasso in **1929** → kept by Domenica. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femme-au-peigne-196491]
- What you see: a standing nude combing her long black hair. Unbalanced body, foreshortening, a disproportionate head, an emerging stylised face. Triangles for chest and pubis herald Cubism. [OFF]
- In Picasso's other 1906 versions (e.g. a bronze in the Baltimore Museum of Art) the woman crouches. **Here she stands**, which makes this sheet unusual. [OFF]
- Display: unknown (works on paper rotate).

### Picasso — Nu sur fond rouge (Nude against a Red Background)
- **Late 1906** · oil on canvas · **81 × 54 cm** · inventory not found · provenance: Vollard → Paul Guillaume (**1929**) → Domenica Walter → acquired **1963** by the Louvre with the Société des Amis du Louvre. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/nu-sur-fond-rouge-196490]
- What you see: a long-haired nude against a flat red ground, head tilted, holding a lock of hair. Geometric simplification of the body. **Eyes as two black, expressionless slits**, giving a mask-like face. [OFF]
- Context: painted a few months before *Les Demoiselles d'Avignon*. It "contains the seeds" of that revolution. [OFF]
- Model: Fernande Olivier. [SEC1 only (Museu Picasso Barcelona Fernande exhibition, via a press summary) — verify]

### Picasso — Composition : paysans (Composition: Peasants)
- **1906** · **gouache on paper** · **70 × 50 cm** (framed 88 × 67) · inventory not found. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/composition-paysans-196492]
- What you see: a father with his hand on his daughter's shoulder "in a gesture of protection and tenderness". A rapidly sketched **garland of flowers** above. Earth tones from Gósol in the father's trousers. White faces with blue shadows recall the Blue Period. [OFF]
- Context: a study for the large oil **Paysans** at the **Barnes Foundation**. [OFF]

### Picasso — Grande nature morte (Large Still Life)
- **1917** per the museum (other sources say 1917–18) · oil on canvas · **87 × 116 cm** (framed 109 × 138.5 × 9.5) · inventory not found. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/grande-nature-morte-196493]
- What you see: a table with a compote dish, bottle and glass, seen from several viewpoints at once. The tabletop is a large beige block "floating almost vertically", so the objects balance precariously. [OFF]
- Story: "the only Cubist work that was preserved by Domenica". [OFF]

### Picasso — Grand nu à la draperie (Large Nude with Drapery)
- **1921–1923** · oil on canvas · **160 × 95 cm** · inventory not found. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/grand-nu-la-draperie-196494] (A Flickr photo caption says 1920–21: WEAK.)
- What you see: a seated woman covering her right shoulder with a drape held in a **disproportionately large hand**, head inclined, **eyes closed**. Greys and pinks with white touches on the drapery. [OFF]
- Influences: Antiquity, Raphael's *Fornarina*, Ingres, Renoir. [OFF]

### Picasso — Femme au chapeau blanc (Woman in a White Hat)
- **1921** · oil on canvas · **118 × 91 cm** · inventory not found. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femme-au-chapeau-blanc-196495]
- What you see: a seated woman leaning against the chair back, one hand delicately on her knee, lost in thought. Blue, red and white softened by grey and brown, like "a large pastel with muted colours". [OFF]
- The face "could be that of his first wife Olga" (Khokhlova, 1891–1955). The monumentality recalls the bathers of the same years. [OFF, hedged by the museum; keep the hedge]
- Note: the brief's "Femme au chapeau" = this work. No other "Femme au chapeau" by Picasso was found at the Orangerie.

### Picasso — Grande baigneuse (Large Bather)
- **1921** (the museum's exact dating was not captured; some sources say 1921–22) · oil on canvas · dimensions not captured · provenance: Paul Guillaume **after 1930** → Domenica → acquired 1963 from Mme Jean Walter with the Société des Amis du Louvre. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/grande-baigneuse-196496]
- What you see: a **monumental seated female nude** with massive, sculptural proportions and drapery. Antique canons, exaggerated. Distorted bodies and solid, heavy faces. [OFF]
- Context: the "return to order". Picasso looked to Greek and Roman antiquity, Ingres and Renoir. Derain (also in the collection) took part in the same movement. [OFF]
- Story: "meticulously conserved by his widow Domenica, since it corresponded perfectly with her tastes". [OFF]
- **Hook:** pair it with the Renoir nudes and Derain in the same collection. [OFF basis]

### Picasso — Femmes à la fontaine (Women at the Fountain) — TWO studies
- **1921** · oil on canvas · one study **50 × 52 cm** (framed 71.5 × 73.5). The second study's dimensions were not captured. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femmes-la-fontaine-196497 and …-196498]
- Context: Picasso stayed at **Fontainebleau** with his family, **May–October 1921**. These are studies for the large **Trois femmes à la fontaine** (MoMA, New York). With this series he "inaugurates his neo-classical period". Three women in antique drapery draw water at a fountain. [OFF]
- Difference between the two studies: the left figure carries the jug **on her head** in the larger study, but **holds it by the handle with one finger** in the smaller one (the pose Picasso finally kept). [OFF]

### Picasso — Femme au tambourin (Woman with a Tambourine)
- **1925** · oil on canvas · **97 × 130 cm** · inventory not found · provenance: Paul Guillaume, acquired **1927** → Mme Jean Walter. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/femme-au-tambourin-196499]
- What you see: a reclining woman on cushions, propped on her elbow, holding a tambourine. "Almost jarring" colours: **mauve, yellow, garnet red, turquoise**. Superimposed viewpoints. [OFF]
- Context: after his Neo-Classical period Picasso returned for a while to decorative Cubism. The languid woman "is reminiscent of the odalisques that Henri Matisse was producing at this time". [OFF]
- **Hook:** a direct visual bridge to the Matisse odalisques in the same collection. [OFF]
- Note: the official Grande nature morte page calls that canvas "the only Cubist work" Domenica kept, while the artist page classes Femme au tambourin as "late cubism". Writers should not say "Domenica sold all the Cubist Picassos".

# 3. HENRI ROUSSEAU ("le Douanier")

## Holdings
- **9 works** from the Walter-Guillaume collection. [OFF: Walter-Guillaume collection page]
- **Plus 2 acquired in late 2024**: *Portrait d'homme* and *Portrait de femme*, **1887**, small oils on wood showing a couple, in medallion format (evoking early photography / daguerreotypes). Bought with support from the Société des Amis des musées d'Orsay et de l'Orangerie (SAMO) and Mathias Ary Jan. **Total now 11**, "the most important collection… after the Barnes Foundation". [SEC1+OFF: The Art Newspaper France, 26 Nov 2024, https://artnewspaper.fr/2024/11/26/le-musee-de-lorangerie-acquiert-deux-portraits-du-douanier-rousseau ; listed on https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/collection/acquisitions ; artprice repeats "nine… plus two small portraits acquired recently"]
- Identified 8 of the 9 historical works (below). **The 9th was not identified** (the museum's page 196426 was not resolved).

## Rousseau and Paul Guillaume
- Guillaume discovered Rousseau's canvases through **Guillaume Apollinaire**. On his advice he acquired masterpieces including *La Carriole du père Junier* and *La Noce*. [SEC1: artprice.com news 12743. The same claim is made in museum exhibition material but was not captured directly]
- Guillaume acted as an intermediary when **Albert Barnes** bought **eighteen** Rousseaus. [SEC1: artprice — verify]

## 2026 exhibition (important for "on display")
- **"Henri Rousseau. L'ambition de la peinture / A Painter's Ambition"**, Musée de l'Orangerie, **25 March – 20 July 2026**, co-organised with the **Barnes Foundation**. About 50 works, including **La Bohémienne endormie (The Sleeping Gypsy, MoMA)**. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/whats-on/exhibitions/henri-rousseau-ambition-painting ; SEC2: offi.fr, parisjetaime.com, euronews 26 Mar 2026, Burlington Magazine]
- La Noce was shown in the exhibition. [SEC: search summary]
- **By Oct 2026 the exhibition has closed.** Where the collection Rousseaus hang now is **unknown**. Do not mention the exhibition as current.

### Rousseau — La Noce (The Wedding Party)
- **c. 1905** · oil on canvas · dimensions and inventory not captured · provenance: Paul Guillaume (on Apollinaire's advice, SEC1) → acquired **1959** from Mme Jean Walter with the support of the Société des Amis du Louvre → entered the Musée de l'Orangerie in **1977**. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/es/obras/la-noce-196428 ; Joconde https://www.pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/00000089535 ; Google Arts & Culture]
- What you see: "one of the most ambitious compositions ever undertaken by Rousseau". A large group portrait with **each face individualised**, in front of trees whose species are meticulously differentiated. The trees and the **yellow-ochre background** form a kind of **mandorla** around the group. The critic **Adolphe Basler** compared this to idealised figures in medieval frescoes and to ex-votos by the "Primitive" masters. **A dog** occupies the foreground. The trees recede sharply as trunks and leaves shrink. [OFF]
- **Hook:** the bride seems to float. Do not say this unless confirmed; see Contested.

### Rousseau — La Carriole du père Junier (Old Junier's Cart)
- **1908** · oil on canvas · dimensions and inventory not captured. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/es/obras/la-carriole-du-pere-junier-196429 ; museum shop "La Carriole du Père Junier, 1908"]
- Story: a homage to neighbourhood friendship. **Claude Junier** and his wife ran a **grocery** near Rousseau's home. They likely commissioned the portrait in 1908. **Junier and Rousseau himself** sit on the front seat. Behind are **Madame Junier**, her niece **Léa Junier**, and her nephew's daughter. Junier was also a horse trainer, very proud of his **white mare, Rosa**. [OFF]
- What you see: the mare stands "curiously on the tips of her hooves", which shows off the shadows and makes her look "suspended… like a dancer on points". Except for Junier, the passengers face us in strict frontality, "like Byzantine icons". **Three dogs**, which is rare in Rousseau's work. [OFF]
- **Hook:** find the painter himself in the cart. [OFF]

### Rousseau — L'Enfant à la poupée (Child with a Doll)
- **c. 1892** (museum date not captured; Wikipedia says c. 1892 — WEAK) · oil on canvas · 67 × 52 cm (WEAK) · inventory not found. [OFF page: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/lenfant-la-poupee-196422]
- What you see: a frontal portrait of a child in a **red dress** holding a **doll** and a **white flower**. [WEAK; consistent with the image, but no official wording captured]

### Rousseau — Les Pêcheurs à la ligne (The Anglers)
- **1908–1909** · oil on canvas · **RF 1963-31** (search summary from Joconde; verify) · Walter collection. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/les-pecheurs-la-ligne-196430]
- What you see and context: Rousseau "liked to depict ordinary people at leisure". **An aeroplane flying overhead** and **a factory chimney** in the background add a modern touch. [OFF]
- **Hook:** an aeroplane in a 1908–09 painting, at the dawn of aviation. [OFF for the plane; any link to a specific flight is NOT sourced]

### Rousseau — La Fabrique de chaises à Alfortville (The Chair Factory at Alfortville)
- **1897** · oil on canvas · **RF 1963-32** (search summary; verify) · dimensions not captured. [OFF: https://www.musee-orangerie.fr/en/artworks/la-fabrique-de-chaises-alfortville-196425]
- What you see: a landscape portrait of a chair factory, chosen "because it was so unusual". The building is **oversized** compared with the landscape and especially the figures. [OFF]

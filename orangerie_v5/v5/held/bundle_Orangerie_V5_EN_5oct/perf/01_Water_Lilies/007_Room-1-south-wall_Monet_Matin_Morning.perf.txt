[lightly] Here's a small riddle. Of the eight compositions in these two rooms, three are called Morning. Only one is an evening.

You've already met the evening, Setting Sun. And this is the first of the mornings. Matin, Morning, as long as Clouds across the room. It hangs on the south wall, the Seine side, the side where the old orangery was all glass. Where the orange trees once took the winter light, Monet's morning now hangs.

In the official order of this room, Morning comes fourth. So the order you've just walked runs from evening round to morning. [pause] Which suits a man who was still painting mornings in his eighties.

Not that Monet wanted you to follow anyone's order. He planned these rooms to be entered by more than one way, and walked without a guide. Any starting point is the right one. So treat this as a beginning, too.

[warmly] Give this one time. Stand far enough back to take in the whole length, if the crowd lets you. Then let your eye travel slowly from one end to the other, and watch the light in the water change as you go. Now look across the room for Setting Sun, and back again. Evening, then morning, in the same breath. It's a whole day, held in one room.

[curious] Through the passage, the water changes company. The second room adds the branches of weeping willows at the water's edge, and the paintings there are built on the contrasts those branches make. Two more mornings are waiting, and the longest composition of all. So is the hardest part of Monet's story.

Walk on through the passage into the second oval room, and stop near the middle.

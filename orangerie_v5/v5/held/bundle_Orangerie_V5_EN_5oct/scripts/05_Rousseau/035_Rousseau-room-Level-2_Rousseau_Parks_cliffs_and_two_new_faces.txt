For decades, this museum's Rousseaus numbered nine, the ones that came with Paul Guillaume and Domenica's collection. Since the end of 2024, there are eleven.

The newcomers are a pair. Two small portraits on wood, of a man and a woman, a couple, painted in 1887. They came to the museum with the support of its society of friends, and of Mathias Ary Jan. Notice how small they are, and step in close. Two quiet faces, a couple kept together.

The other two pictures come with no legend at all. That makes them the perfect place to do your own looking.

Promeneurs dans un parc, Walkers in a Park, is a small canvas, about half a metre wide, painted sometime in the first decade of the twentieth century. People walking in a park. That's the promise of the title. Take a moment with it, and see what it tells you on its own.

Then La Falaise, The Cliff. Which is not a bad way to meet Rousseau. No anecdote, no famous owner's story. Just a painter, a subject, and you.

Now head for Henri Matisse's room, and look for his painting of three sisters, seated against a plain brown background. Listen to it there.

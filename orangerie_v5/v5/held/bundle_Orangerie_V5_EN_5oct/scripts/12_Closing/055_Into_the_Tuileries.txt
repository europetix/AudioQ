Remember the winter coat for orange trees? Look back at it now. Glass on the side that faces the Seine, very nearly a blank wall on the garden side. It was built to keep trees warm through the winter, not to be admired.

Turn and look along the length of the building. You heard at the start about two panels, promised for Victory Day. This is how far that promise grew. The museum calls the gift a monument to peace.

Now turn the other way. Around you is the Tuileries garden. It goes back to Catherine de' Medici in the sixteenth century, and the great garden designer Le Nôtre reshaped it in the seventeenth.

Inside, you spent time with Monet's water and Monet's sky. Out here, you have the real ones. Walk slowly if you can, and give the garden the same patience you gave Monet's walls. Watch a cloud cross the sun, or the wind turn the leaves, and see how the colours shift. After the Water Lilies, you've had some practice. The light out here may never look quite ordinary again.

Thank you for spending this time with me, with Paul Guillaume's painters, and with Monet. You've met a dealer who ended up inside a painting he ordered, the widow who reshaped his collection, and an old painter who gave his water lilies away. Whatever stays with you from the faces downstairs, I hope you keep a little of that light.

Enjoy the garden, and take your time out here.

These paintings have been through a war of their own. In August 1944, during the Liberation of Paris, a shell struck this building, and some of the panels were damaged. A series of photographs recorded the harm. Afterwards the walls were repaired, and so was the paint.

[pause] That was not the end of the worry. From the late 1940s, the people looking after the Water Lilies began to fret about the air in these rooms, and about the light. They still do. Condition reports and careful treatments carry on to this day, and during building works in 2025 the paintings were cleaned. [wry] Ninety-one metres of canvas, and every centimetre of it a patient.

So what you're looking at has been watched over for nearly a century. Reflets verts, Green Reflections, eight and a half metres wide. If Clouds was the airy end of this room, the title tells you where this one sits. Down in the green, among the reflections of the plants, where the water lilies rise.

[curious] Here's a question to take with you along the canvas. Where does a lily stop, and its reflection begin? Pick one flower and stay with it. Follow its edges. The strokes here are quick and full of movement, and the line between the thing and its mirror image is never quite where you expect.

Then widen your gaze to the water around it, the reflections of the plants that give the painting its name.

[quietly] That's the green of the title. Not a colour so much as a place to sink into.

To finish this room, go to Morning, the long composition on the Seine side.

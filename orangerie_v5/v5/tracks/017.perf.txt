[curious] Most portraits tell you who someone is. This one tells you what he's going to be, and it says so in writing, right on the picture.

The young man is Paul Guillaume, he's twenty-three, and he has been a dealer for only about a year. In nineteen fifteen and the year after, Modigliani painted him four times.

This one was painted at the home of Modigliani's companion, the British poet Beatrice Hastings. And it isn't on canvas at all, but on cardboard, mounted on plywood.

Now look at the man. He's elegant and sure of himself, and he looks straight back at you. The dark suit and hat stand out against a red background. Between them, his face and his white shirt catch all the light. There's nothing humble about this young dealer, and Modigliani doesn't pretend there is.

[pause] Then look for two words painted on the board itself. [long pause] Novo Pilota. The new pilot, or the new helmsman.

The museum reads it this way. Modigliani is hailing Guillaume as the one who will steer modern art, like a sailor at the helm, or one of the wise men following a star. Coming from a painter to the man who sold his work, that's quite a compliment. It's also a bet. Modigliani was betting that this young man would find the way. And Guillaume, paying for that Montmartre studio, was betting on him.

[warmly] Both bets, in their way, paid off. You're standing in the proof.

Guillaume was painted many more times. Look for the other portraits of him, and of his wife Domenica, by Derain and by Van Dongen, and the story continues in front of them.

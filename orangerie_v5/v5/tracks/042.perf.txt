[warmly] This one came to Paul Guillaume straight from Picasso, in nineteen twenty-nine. And Domenica kept it.

It's a large sheet in gouache, from nineteen oh six. A nude woman stands combing her long black hair. Look at how unsteady she is, the body leaning off balance and the head too big for it. The features of the face are just becoming stylised. [pause] And find the triangles that stand for her chest and her lap. Those flat little shapes are a first whisper of Cubism.

[curious] And here's the surprising part. Picasso made other versions of this woman that same year, including a bronze now in Baltimore. In those she crouches, but here she stands. That makes this sheet the odd one out.

Now look for a second gouache from that same year. A father rests his hand on his daughter's shoulder, a protective, tender gesture. Above them hangs a quickly sketched garland of flowers. The earthy colour of his trousers comes from Gósol, the village in the Pyrenees where Picasso had been staying. Yet the white faces with their blue shadows look back to his Blue Period. It's a study for a much larger painting, Peasants, now at the Barnes Foundation in Philadelphia.

[quietly] Then jump fifteen years. A woman in a white hat sits leaning against the back of her chair, one hand resting lightly on her knee, lost in thought. Blue, red and white are softened with grey and brown, so the whole canvas looks like a large pastel in muted colours. Her face could be that of Olga, Picasso's first wife. Could be, says the museum, and it's right to be careful.

Her heavy, monumental calm points straight ahead, to the giants of those same years.

Find the huge seated bather, a nude built like a statue. Listen to her story there.

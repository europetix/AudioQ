[quietly] Nothing on these walls has changed since you first walked in. But you have.

You've met a pastry cook and a hotel waiter, a dealer dressed as Pierrot, a girl who grew up in her uncle's paintings. And here's something worth knowing as you look at the water again. Monet painted these panels between 1914 and 1926. So while Soutine was painting his pastry cook, and Derain his Harlequin and Pierrot, an old man at Giverny was still at work on these. [pause] The same years. A completely different idea of what a painting is for.

[long pause] Remember that upper floor? The collection you've just seen once hung on it, right above these rooms. Now the two live side by side, the lilies in the light and the collection below.

So the light you're sitting in is a kind of homecoming. It comes in through the ceiling, and these rooms run east to west, with the course of the sun. Depending on the sky outside, the water can look quite different from an hour ago.

[reverent] Take a few minutes. There's nothing more to find here, only more to see.

When you're ready, follow the exit signs out to the garden side of the building. The last stop is waiting on the footbridge.

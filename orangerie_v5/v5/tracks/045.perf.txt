[warmly] Most of the faces in this collection have names. Dealers, wives, nieces, the painter's own children. This young man has none. He's simply the apprentice, and Modigliani gives him as much care as he ever gave a patron.

Modigliani had come to Paris from Livorno, in Italy. In nineteen fourteen the poet Max Jacob introduced him to a very young dealer, Paul Guillaume, who rented him a studio in Montmartre. Over the next two years Modigliani painted Guillaume four times. You may already have met him, the elegant young man of Novo Pilota.

[curious] This picture comes from later, and from much farther south. Near the end of his life, Modigliani travelled to the Midi, on a trip paid for by the dealer Léopold Zborowski. There he painted young farmers, apprentices and workers. Ordinary people, posed with great seriousness.

Look at how this one sits. Head resting on one hand, lost in thought, a pose the museum traces back to Cézanne. The chair and the table seem to melt into his body. The curving outlines and flattened forms show what Modigliani had learned from Gauguin.

Now the colour. Almost everything is grey and brown. Except two things. The white of his shirt collar. [pause] And the eyes, an intense, startling blue. Then look down at the hands. The face is thin and smooth, but the hands are coarse. A boy's face, and a working man's hands.

[quietly] Modigliani didn't have long left. He died in January nineteen twenty, of tubercular meningitis, at thirty-five. Two days later his companion, Jeanne Hébuterne, died too. She was twenty-one, and eight months pregnant with their second child. Ten years on, their families laid them together in one grave, in Père-Lachaise.

[long pause] The apprentice never knew any of that. He just sits, and thinks, and looks back at you with those blue eyes.

Zborowski was also Soutine's first dealer. Find Soutine's little pastry cook in white. Play its track there.

[lightly] Renoir is famous for faces and flesh. But some of his most revealing pictures here are small, and have no people in them at all.

Start with the apples and pears. It's a little canvas, painted around eighteen ninety-five. The museum singles it out as the Renoir still life most influenced by Cézanne, both in its subject and in its highly intricate composition. Here, you can watch Renoir trying things the Cézanne way, building the arrangement with care.

Then the tulips. The bouquet shimmers in reds, greens and yellows, in a green vase, against a background of red and beige strokes. He probably painted it at Cagnes, soon after his final move to the south. It stayed in his studio until his death, and Paul Guillaume bought it ten years later.

[curious] And finally, the snow. This is the earliest picture of the three, probably painted in early eighteen seventy-five, after a heavy winter snowfall. Snow is supposed to be white. Renoir looked harder. He was experimenting with coloured shadows, showing how snow reflects the colours of everything around it. Stand back a little and look into the shadows for that colour.

[pause] So three small pictures, across thirty years, and three different painters in one man.

Now to the painter behind that little still life. Find the room of Cézanne's paintings, and play its track there.

[amused] By the standards of this room, this is the small one. Six metres wide, Le Soleil couchant, Setting Sun, is the shortest of the eight compositions. It's also the only one painted on a single panel. Every other composition is made of several canvases, joined edge to edge.

Why so short? Because of the stretch of wall where it had to go. Monet didn't leave that to the builders. He settled where each painting would hang, the rhythm from one to the next, even the spaces between them. Nothing in these rooms is where it is by accident.

[curious] Then there's the sun itself. These rooms run east to west, in step with the path of the sun. The scenes of sunrise sit toward the east, and the twilight toward the west. [pause] So a painted day follows the real one across the building. And here, in the smallest frame of all, the day ends.

Now the looking. Start from a few steps back, and let the whole panel settle in front of you. The title tells you the hour, and then it's up to your eyes. Where does the light sit in the water? Which colour does your eye go to first?

[quietly] Then walk up close. In this room the brushwork is quick and full of energy, and up close the reflected sky turns back into strokes of paint. Step back again, slowly, and watch for the moment it becomes water.

That's the whole magic trick of these rooms, and you've just done it yourself.

Next, find Clouds. It's one of the two long compositions in this room. The other, on the Seine side, is Morning, and we'll save that for last. Listen to the Clouds track in front of it.

[amused] By the standards of this room, this is the small one. Two metres high and six metres wide, Le Soleil couchant, Setting Sun, is the shortest of the eight compositions. It's also the only one painted on a single panel. Every other composition is made of several canvases, joined edge to edge.

Why so short? Because of the stretch of wall where it had to go. All eight compositions share one height, but each was given its own width, so it would fit its own stretch of curved wall. Monet didn't leave that to the builders. He settled where each painting would hang, the rhythm from one to the next, even the spaces between them. Nothing in these rooms is where it is by accident.

Then there's the sun itself. These rooms run east to west. And the museum's teaching notes put the scenes of sunrise toward the east, and the twilight toward the west. [pause] So a painted day follows the real one across the building.

[quietly] Monet worked on this cycle from 1914 until his death, from his seventies to the age of eighty-six. It's tempting to read a farewell into a sunset painted that late in life. That may be reading too much into it. He painted mornings here too.

Now the looking. Start from a few steps back, and let the whole panel settle in front of you. The title tells you the hour, and then it's up to your eyes. Walk up close. In this room the brushwork is quick and full of energy, and up close the reflected sky turns back into paint. Step back again, and it becomes water.

Next, find Clouds. It's one of the two longest compositions in this room. The other long one, on the Seine side of the room, is Morning, and we'll save that for last. Play the track for Clouds in front of it.

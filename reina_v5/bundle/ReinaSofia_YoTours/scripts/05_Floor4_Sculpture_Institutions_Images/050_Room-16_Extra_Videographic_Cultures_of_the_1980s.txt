In 1987, the Reina Sofía Art Centre was still new, and one of its early shows was about a medium barely twenty years old. It was called The Sublime Image. It was the centre's first exhibition of video art.

The curator, Manuel Palacio, traced Spanish video art from 1970 to that year, with more than thirty works. Those pieces became the seed of the museum's whole audiovisual collection. Some of those artists are on the screens around you, Eugènia Balcells and Miralda among them, and so is Salvador Dalí.

One film here records a famous day. In 1992, Tino Calabuig took his camera into the street to film Guernica's journey from the Casón del Buen Retiro to this museum. The painting travelled in a crate built so the canvas could not roll up, and a lift was built just to get it inside.

On the wall, find Chillida's ink drawing, all black curves closing around an empty space. It is the original design for the art centre's first logo. That symbol stood for the place until it became the National Museum.

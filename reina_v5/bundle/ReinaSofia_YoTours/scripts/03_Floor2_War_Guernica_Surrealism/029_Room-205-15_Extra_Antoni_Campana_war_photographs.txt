In 2018, a box came back to light. People call it the red box. Inside were photographs by the photographer Antoni Campañà, from the years of the Civil War.

This war had more press coverage than any before it. New, compact cameras like the Leica made a new way of photographing possible, and it was put to the test here, in Spain. These rooms tell that story.

In these pictures, Campañà turned his camera on something unusual: the artists working behind the lines. He photographed the work of the Union of Professional Draughtsmen of Catalonia, as they covered railway carriages and cars with anti-fascist slogans and images.

Read the walls of those trains. The land is yours, work it. We will crush them. Discipline. One carriage carries the Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse. The trains themselves became moving posters.

You've seen the posters on the walls of Room 205.05. Here you can see the same war of images rolling down the tracks.

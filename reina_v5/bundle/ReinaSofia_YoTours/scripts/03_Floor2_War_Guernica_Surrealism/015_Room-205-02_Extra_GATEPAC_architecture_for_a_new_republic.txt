"Everything but decoration." That was the demand a young architect, José Manuel Aizpurúa, put in print in 1930. He wanted buildings with hygiene, soundness, comfort, rationalism and economy. Nothing else.

That October, a group of young architects met at the Grand Hotel in Zaragoza and founded GATEPAC, the Group of Spanish Architects and Technicians for the Progress of Contemporary Architecture. They split into three groups, North, East and Centre, and launched a magazine called AC.

Then the Second Republic was proclaimed, and suddenly they had a client. The country urgently needed mass housing, schools, libraries, museums and hospitals. In Room 205.01, you may have passed their plan for a tuberculosis hospital with four hundred beds.

The East group, in Barcelona, thought bigger still. With Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret, they drew up the Plan Macià for the growth of the city. And they imagined a second city, just for rest and holidays, with folding cabins spread across the Llobregat estuary.

The modern eye was selling things too. Look for the photomontages and adverts by Pere Català Pic and Josep Sala, for pencils, jewellery and Ford cars, and for Man Ray's portrait of Picasso.

Walk on through Room 205.03 and Room 205.03 bis, with women photographers and posters. Room 205.04 holds John Heartfield's photomontages, another short Extra.

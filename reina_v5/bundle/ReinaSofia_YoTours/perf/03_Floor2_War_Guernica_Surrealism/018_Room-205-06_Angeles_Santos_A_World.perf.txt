[curious] In the autumn of 1929, the writer Ramón Gómez de la Serna walked into the Autumn Salon in Madrid, in the Retiro park, through the fallen leaves and the mud. He came out talking about a revelation. [pause] The revelation, he wrote, was a seventeen-year-old girl.

Her name was Ángeles Santos, and this was her painting. She made it far from Paris, in Valladolid. She had never seen the new art being made abroad with her own eyes. What she had was her own surroundings, a pile of avant-garde magazines, and one book. It was by the German critic Franz Roh, and its pictures introduced her to Joan Miró and to the new German realists.

Roh had given a name to a whole mood of the 1920s. He called it magical realism. Very careful painting, very still scenes, and something strange creeping into everyday life.

Now look at what she did with it. The world is a square block, hanging in a dark sky. Its surfaces are crowded with tiny houses and tiny people going about their lives. It's an ordinary town, and it's floating in space. The room text puts it well. It's her inner vision, and it comes close to the ideas of the Surrealists.

[warmly] And remember the size. Almost three metres by three. A teenager decided that the canvas had to be as big as the idea.

Gómez de la Serna cast her as a kind of Saint Teresa of painting, listening to doves and stars that seemed to guide her brush. Her other painting here, Tertulia, The Gathering, comes from the same astonishing year.

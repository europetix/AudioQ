[curious] Picasso painted this woman in Madrid, sent her to a national exhibition, and then left town. The painting was forgotten for decades.

In 1901 he was not yet twenty. He had already been to Paris once, and now he spent the first months of the year in Madrid. He met the writers of the Generation of '98. He became artistic director of a small magazine called Arte Joven, Young Art, and drew its illustrations.

That spring he painted a series the museum calls courtesan portraits. This is one of them. And you can watch him borrowing from everywhere. From the Modernisme he had known in Barcelona. From Van Gogh and Toulouse-Lautrec, whom he had seen in Paris. And from the old Spanish masters in the Prado.

[pause] Look at the way she fills the frame, upright and composed. For that, Picasso took the composition of a royal portrait, Velázquez's Queen Mariana of Austria. Then look at the brushwork, and think of El Greco. A queen's pose and an old master's hand, given to a woman of Madrid's night life.

[wry] He sent the painting to the General Exhibition of Fine Arts in Madrid that same year. It attracted very little notice. And before the show had even closed, Picasso was gone, back to Paris to prepare his first solo exhibition there. Nobody seems to have missed the picture.

It surfaced again decades later, when it entered what was then Spain's national museum of modern art. In time, it came here.

[warmly] So take a last look at her. A canvas its young painter walked away from, at the very start of the career that would change modern art.

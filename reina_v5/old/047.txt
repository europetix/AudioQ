 Yves Klein, blue
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A canvas, or a sponge relief, soaked in a single, almost unreal ultramarine blue so deep and even it seems to
    hum.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Yves Klein, the French provocateur of Nouveau Réalisme, and his signature International Klein Blue — a
    pigment so saturated he patented the formula. He believed this one blue could open onto pure space, the
    infinite, the void, and he used it on everything: flat monochromes, sponges, even, notoriously, bodies pressed
    onto canvas.
    LISTEN FOR
    Stand close enough that the blue fills your vision and you'll feel what he was after — not a picture of
    anything, but an immersion, colour as a door out of the material world. Klein was half mystic, half showman,
    and dead at thirty-four, but this blue outlived him as one of the few colours in art history that belongs to a
    single name. After Fontana's cut and Klein's void, the most bodily painter of all waits right this way.

TRACK 042 · FLOOR 1                                W-FOCUS                              178 WORDS · ~1 MIN

 Pablo Palazuelo, geometry
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A canvas or a slim metal sculpture of interlocking lines and angular shapes — restrained, precise, in deep
    muted colour, with the calm of a diagram and the feel of a piece of music.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Pablo Palazuelo, the solitary master of Spanish geometric abstraction. The museum holds him from the 1950s
    — works with titles like Solitudes — through to later pieces like Sylvarum of the 1980s. Trained partly as an
    architect in England, he came to believe that geometry was a living, generative force — that a line, followed
    honestly, would grow and branch according to its own hidden law, the way a crystal or a plant does.
    LISTEN FOR
    So don't read these as cold design. Palazuelo thought of them as organisms — forms unfolding, almost
    growing, from a single seed of line. He was deeply read in alchemy, music and Eastern thought, and the work
    has that meditative charge: it asks you to slow down and follow the lines rather than take in the shape at a
    glance. After the calm, the wider world arrives — just beyond.

TRACK 039 · FLOOR 1                                W-FOCUS                               183 WORDS · ~1 MIN

 Jorge Oteiza, Homage to Malevich
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A small, austere construction — a cube or box of metal, opened up and hollowed out, so that the empty
    interior is clearly the subject and the metal is just its edges.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Jorge Oteiza, Chillida's great rival, and a piece like his Homage to Malevich of the late 1950s. Oteiza spent
    years on what he called his "experimental purpose": a systematic emptying-out of sculpture, removing mass
    until only structured void remained. Then, having decided he'd solved sculpture, he largely stopped — and
    turned to poetry, theory and furious public argument instead.
    LISTEN FOR
    The homage in the title is to the Russian Kazimir Malevich, who'd tried to reach zero in painting with his
    black square; Oteiza wanted to reach zero in three dimensions. So look at the holes, not the metal. He
    believed an empty, opened form could be spiritually active — a kind of shelter for the spirit, he said. It's
    cerebral, stubborn, and oddly moving once you stop waiting for it to be a "thing" and start seeing the nothing
    it frames. A few more abstractionists, then the world beyond Spain, just ahead.

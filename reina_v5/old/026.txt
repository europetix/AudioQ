 Julio González, Montserrat
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the reconstructed Pavilion near Guernica — a figure in sheet iron: a peasant woman standing upright, a
    child on one arm, her face lifted, a sickle in her hand.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    La Montserrat, the work Julio González made for the Spanish Republic's Pavilion in 1937 — and a complete
    change of register from his airy abstract iron. Here he turns realist on purpose: a hard, plain, defiant peasant
    woman, named for the mountain that is the enduring symbol of his native Catalonia. She is the Republic itself
    — ordinary, rooted, refusing to bow.
    LISTEN FOR
    Set her beside Guernica. Picasso gave the war its scream; González gave it a figure to stand firm against the
    scream — endurance rather than agony. The original lives now in a museum in Amsterdam, so what stands in
    the Pavilion reconstruction here is the museum's reference to it, holding the place she occupied in 1937. That
    a master of pure abstraction reached, in the emergency, for a plain human figure tells you everything about
    what that year did to artists. Calder's strange mercury fountain and Miró's lost mural complete the Pavilion —
    round this corner.

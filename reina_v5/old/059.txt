 Cristina Iglesias, built nature
    WHERE YOU ARE
    An installation rather than an object — perhaps a hanging canopy or a screen of woven, vegetal-looking
    material, or a chamber you can step toward, often casting intricate latticed shadows.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Cristina Iglesias, born in San Sebastián in 1956 and among the most admired sculptors Spain has produced
    since the war. She makes spaces rather than statues — suspended ceilings, walls of cast foliage, passageways
    and wells — in materials like resin, bronze, woven esparto and water, that blur the line between architecture
    and nature, the built and the grown.
    LISTEN FOR
    Her work plays a subtle trick on you. What reads as dense vegetation turns out to be carefully cast and
    constructed; what looks like a natural grotto is engineered to the millimetre. She's fascinated by thresholds —
    the canopy you stand under, the screen you can't quite see through, the water you can't reach — so the
    experience is always about your own body, waiting at an edge. It's the inheritance of Chillida and Oteiza's
    obsession with space, carried into a new generation. Juan Muñoz, who filled space with unsettling figures, is
    round this corner.

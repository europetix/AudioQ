 The Second Floor: Spain enters the modern age
 This is the floor most people come for, and it holds the heart of the collection. Everything up here was
 made roughly between 1900 and the end of the Second World War — the decades when Spanish artists
 tore up the rulebook and then watched their country tear itself apart. The rooms run in a loop, and
 they're hung by theme rather than by name, so you'll meet Picasso, Miró and Dalí more than once as the
 story moves.

 It opens quietly, with a young Picasso still finding his feet. Then it gathers speed: Cubism, the language
 he and Juan Gris built out of broken planes; the Surrealists and their dreams; and then, at the centre of
 the floor, the room everything has been leading toward — the Spanish Pavilion of 1937 and Guernica.
 After that come the rooms of the Civil War itself.

 A word on the light. It's kept low up here, and the rooms are cool — a lot of this work is on paper, and
 paper is fragile. Bring a layer if you feel the chill. Take your time; this is not a floor to rush. Just along
 here, in the first rooms, is where it begins.

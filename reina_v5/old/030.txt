 Josep Renau, photomontage
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Among the war material — bold, graphic posters and photomontages, sharper and more modern than
    anything painted, made to be read across a street.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Josep Renau, the Valencian artist who became the Republic's master of propaganda design. He took the
    avant-garde technique of photomontage — cutting and recombining photographs — and turned it into a
    weapon, producing posters on hunger, fascism, the defence of Madrid and the education of the people, in
    punchy reds and blacks with slicing diagonals.
    LISTEN FOR
    Renau was, in fact, the Republican official who commissioned Guernica from Picasso for the Pavilion — so
    he sits at the very centre of this museum's story. His montages show the other face of wartime art: not the
    anguished masterpiece, but the cheap, fast, mass-produced image meant to move a population to act. He paid
    for his loyalty with exile, ending up in Mexico and then East Germany. Look at how modern these still feel
    — the design language of the whole twentieth century is already here, pressed into the service of a desperate
    cause. Now we'll leave the war behind and make our way down to Floor 1, just here.

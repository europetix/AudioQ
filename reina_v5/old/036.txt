 Rafael Canogar, The Accident
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A stark canvas — a sprawled human figure seen from above, painted in heavy blacks, greys and dirty whites,
    half-abstract, half-horribly recognisable.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Rafael Canogar, the youngest of the El Paso founders, and a piece like El accidente of 1963. Canogar began
    as a pure abstract gesturalist, then did something the others mostly didn't — he let the figure back in, painting
    bodies that look like news photographs of beatings, accidents and the repressed: social reality forced up
    through the abstraction.
    LISTEN FOR
    That move matters. As the 1960s wore on, gesture alone started to feel evasive to some of these painters —
    too easy to admire as decoration. Canogar dragged the human body back onto the canvas to make the
    violence unmistakable, a bridge between El Paso's abstraction and the political figuration that came next. The
    flattened, fallen figure is deliberately hard to place: victim, protester, ordinary citizen, you. The Basque
    sculptors took the same charged energy into iron and stone, a few steps on.

 Lucio Fontana, Spatial Concept
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A monochrome canvas — often a single flat colour — with clean slashes or punctures cut straight through it,
    opening the surface onto the shadow behind.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Lucio Fontana, the Italian-Argentine founder of what he called Spazialismo — Spatialism — and his
    Concetto spaziale, "Spatial Concept." The radical move sounds simple and was anything but: he stopped
    painting on the canvas and started cutting through it, slicing it with a blade or piercing it with holes, so the
    work reached into real space behind the flat surface.
    LISTEN FOR
    It looks like vandalism and is actually the opposite — a calm, deliberate philosophical act. For centuries a
    canvas was a window pretending to be deep; Fontana ended the pretence by making the depth literal, letting
    the dark behind the cloth become part of the work. Resist the urge to peer through the cut; just register the
    shock of it — the violence of the gesture against the serenity of the empty colour. It changed what a painting
    could be. The Briton Francis Bacon is just here.

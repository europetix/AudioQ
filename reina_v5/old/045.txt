 The wider world
 Spain didn't make this art in a vacuum, and the museum threads the major international currents of the
 post-war decades through these floors so you can see the conversation.

 Look for the Italian Lucio Fontana, who took a knife to the canvas itself; for the Frenchman Yves Klein
 and his single, saturated, patented blue; for the raw figures of the Briton Francis Bacon; and, scattered
 through the collection, for Arte Povera from Italy, Neo-Concrete art from Brazil, and the experimental
 edges of Latin American art that the Reina Sofía has worked hard to bring into the story. It's a
 deliberately broad, deliberately international picture — the museum's argument that Spanish modernism
 belongs inside a global one, not in a national box.

 Two of these outsiders are worth stopping for in particular: an Italian who attacked the canvas, and a
 Briton who attacked the body. Fontana first, over here.

 Back out into Madrid
 That's the century, more or less — from the hope of the avant-garde, through the scream of Guernica,
 into the long silence of the dictatorship and out into everything that came after. It's worth saying what
 makes this museum unusual. The Prado, up the road, is a treasure-house: the spoils of kings, gathered
 for pleasure. This place is something else. It's a museum that takes its own country's worst wound and
 keeps it on the wall, unhealed, on purpose — so that nobody gets to look away. That's why Guernica
 doesn't sit behind glass. You were meant to stand close enough to feel small in front of it.

 You've walked the whole collection now — the avant-garde and the war down on Floor 2, the long grey
 aftermath and the Transition on Floor 1, and up here, the open present. Whatever's left to discover is in
 the Nouvel building across the way: the big temporary exhibitions, and the rooftop terraces, where
 there's open-air sculpture and one of the finest views in Madrid. It changes constantly; that's rather the
 point.

 When you step back outside, you're in the corner of Madrid they call the art triangle — the Prado and
 the Thyssen are a short walk north, and Atocha station is right there if you're moving on. But give
 yourself a minute first. Sit in the Sabatini garden, or in the courtyard under Nouvel's red roof. A
 museum like this asks a lot of you. It's allowed to. Thank you for walking it with me.

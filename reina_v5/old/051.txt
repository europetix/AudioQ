 Antonio López García, realism
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A painting of something utterly ordinary — a kitchen sink, a half-eaten meal, a bare Madrid street at first
    light — rendered with a patience and precision that stop you in your tracks.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Antonio López García, Spain's greatest living realist. He paints the commonplace — his family, his
    bathroom, the view down the Gran Vía at dawn — with a slowness that is almost legendary; some canvases
    occupy him for ten or twenty years, returning to the same spot at the same hour, year after year, to catch the
    exact light.
    LISTEN FOR
    This is realism as devotion, not nostalgia. While his contemporaries tore paintings apart, López did the
    hardest thing of all — he looked, and kept looking, at the world as it is. There's a strange melancholy in it: the
    light is real, the moment is true, and you can feel how much time is buried in the paint. He became famous far
    beyond art circles thanks to a film about him trying, and failing, to finish painting a quince tree before its fruit
    ripened and fell. Equipo Crónica turned figuration the other way — toward irony — a few steps on.

TRACK 053 · FLOOR 1                                 W-FOCUS                               160 WORDS · ~1 MIN

 Eduardo Arroyo, political pop
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A flat, bold, almost poster-like canvas, sharp-edged and biting, often skewering a recognisable figure of
    power or a cliché of Spanish identity.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Eduardo Arroyo, the sharpest satirist of Spanish painting, who spent much of the Franco years in exile in
    Paris because his work was too pointed to show at home. He used the clean, flat language of Pop and
    narrative figuration as a scalpel — mocking the dictatorship, the bullfight, the tourist-poster version of Spain,
    and the pieties of the art world itself.
    LISTEN FOR
    Arroyo is the wit to set against the wounds elsewhere on this floor — he fights with irony rather than anguish.
    The targets are specific and the jokes are sharp, but underneath is real anger at a country forced into a
    costume. He shares the political bite of Equipo Crónica nearby; they were all weaponising the cool surfaces
    of Pop against a regime that couldn't take a joke. The art of the Transition is close by.

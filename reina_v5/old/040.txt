TRACK 040 · FLOOR 1                                  W-FOCUS                               165 WORDS · ~1 MIN

 Martín Chirino, iron spiral
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A piece of black forged iron coiled into a spiral or a curl, like a gust of wind or a wave caught and frozen in
    metal.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Martín Chirino, from the Canary Islands, an El Paso member and, with Chillida and Oteiza, one of the
    pre-eminent Spanish iron sculptors. His lifelong motif is the spiral — the "wind," the espiral del viento —
    drawn from the swirling Atlantic winds of his islands and from the ancient spiral carvings of their first
    inhabitants, the Guanches.
    LISTEN FOR
    Where Chillida grips space and Oteiza empties it, Chirino sets it spinning. He forged the iron himself at the
    anvil, so the metal keeps the memory of the hammer, and the spiral form feels alive — uncoiling, breathing,
    never quite still. It ties the cool intellect of modern sculpture back to something very old and very local: the
    wind over a volcanic island, marked in rock thousands of years ago. The cooler, more lyrical strain of
    abstraction is a little further on.

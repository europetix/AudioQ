TRACK 035 · FLOOR 1                                 W-FOCUS                               189 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Manolo Millares, burlap
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Not really a painting and not quite a sculpture — a piece of coarse sackcloth, torn, knotted, stitched and
    stretched, stained black and white, with holes that open onto darkness behind.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Manolo Millares, another founder of El Paso, made his art out of arpillera — burlap, the cheap sacking used
    for potatoes and sand. He'd rip it, knot it, sew it back together with rough twine, and let it sag and bulge off
    the frame, then work it in black and white. He came from the Canary Islands, and the bound, bandaged forms
    carry an echo of the mummies in the islands' museums that fascinated him as a boy.
    LISTEN FOR
    Think of these as wounds, or bodies, or shrouds — Millares wanted exactly that ambiguity. After a war and
    under a dictatorship, he made art that looks like something damaged and clumsily patched, beautiful and
    disturbing at once. The holes matter as much as the cloth: he's letting the darkness show through. It's the most
    physical art on this floor — protest you could almost touch. The Basque sculptors took the same questions
    into iron and stone, ahead of you.

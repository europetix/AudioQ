 Ángeles Santos, A World
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A big, strange, unforgettable canvas — a cube of a planet hanging in dark space, with tiny figures, houses and
    trees clinging to its edges and angels drifting around it. Room 205.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Un Mundo — A World — painted in 1929 by Ángeles Santos, who was eighteen years old when she made it.
    Read that again. A teenager, barely trained, produced among the most original Spanish paintings of the entire
    century: the whole world reimagined as a tilting cube you could fall off the side of, half cosmic vision, half
    adolescent dread. She showed it at the Autumn Salon in Madrid and it caused a sensation; for a brief moment
    she was the most talked-about young artist in Spain, befriended by Lorca, compared to the boy-genius poet
    Rimbaud.
    LISTEN FOR
    And then, almost as quickly, the story darkened. The pressure of sudden fame, family disapproval, and a
    breakdown pushed her out of that visionary mode; she lived a long life — she died in 2013, past a hundred —
    but never painted anything this astonishing again. So you're looking at a kind of comet: one teenager's
    enormous, lonely vision of the universe, made and then never repeated. The museum has rightly given it pride
    of place; for years she was a footnote, and she should never have been. Stand with it a moment longer than
    you planned to. Then, right along here, another woman the textbooks tried to forget.

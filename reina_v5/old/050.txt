 The return of the image
 Not everyone went abstract. Alongside the matter and the gesture, a counter-current ran through these
 years: artists who insisted on the recognisable world, often as their own private form of resistance.

 The towering figure is Antonio López García, Spain's great realist, who paints Madrid — a sink, a
 quince tree, a street at dawn — so slowly and so exactly that a single canvas can take him years; the
 patience itself feels like a moral position. Look too for Equipo Crónica, a duo who raided the Prado's
 own masterpieces — Velázquez, Goya — and spliced them with comic-strip flatness and pop colour to
 make sharp, sly comments on Spain under Franco. And for the cooler, stranger figuration of Luis
 Gordillo. Between them and the abstractionists next door you're seeing two ways of surviving the same
 silence: one that dissolved the world into matter, and one that described it so precisely you couldn't
 pretend not to see it. Antonio López first, ahead of you.

TRACK 038 · FLOOR 1                                  W-FOCUS                               191 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Eduardo Chillida, forged iron
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A piece of heavy dark iron or weathered steel — bars and blades that bend, hook and reach into the air around
    them, sitting with enormous grave weight.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Eduardo Chillida, the Basque master, who started as a footballer, trained as an architect, and then gave his life
    to the question of space. The museum holds his work across the decades — from an early carved torso of
    1948 to the great CORTEN-steel tables of the 1980s, one a homage to Goethe, who died begging for "more
    light." He forged iron at the anvil like a blacksmith, and the forms always seem to be doing something to the
    air: gripping it, framing it, sheltering it.
    LISTEN FOR
    Don't just look at the metal — look at the space it holds. That's the whole point of Chillida: the emptiness
    inside the iron is as much the sculpture as the iron itself. He spoke about the wind and the sea and light as his
    real materials. Walk around it slowly and watch the gaps open and close as you move. His rival in the next
    breath, Oteiza, took that idea to its extreme — close by.

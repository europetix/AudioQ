 Joan Miró, The Reaper (lost)
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Pavilion display — but look for photographs and documentation rather than a painting, because the
    work itself no longer exists.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    El Segador — The Reaper, also called Catalan Peasant in Revolt — was the giant mural Joan Miró painted
    directly onto the Pavilion's staircase wall in 1937. It showed a Catalan peasant, sickle raised, in a blaze of
    Miró's fierce late-thirties colour: the same defiant rural figure as González's Montserrat, but howling rather
    than standing still.
    LISTEN FOR
    And then it vanished. The Pavilion was a temporary building; when the exhibition closed, The Reaper —
    painted on panels that were never properly conserved — was lost, and has never been found. So one of Miró's
    most political, most important works survives only in black-and-white photographs, like a ghost of the
    Pavilion. The museum shows that documentation here, which is its own sober lesson: the Republic's great
    display was dismantled, scattered and partly destroyed, exactly as the Republic itself was. Guernica is the one
    wall that made it home. The rooms of the war itself are next, a short way on.

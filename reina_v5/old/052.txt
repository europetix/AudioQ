TRACK 052 · FLOOR 1                                W-FOCUS                             179 WORDS · ~1 MIN

 Equipo Crónica, pop history
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A bright, flat, almost cartoon-like canvas in which figures you half-recognise from the Prado — Velázquez's
    princess, Goya's firing squad — collide with comic-strip colour and modern intrusions.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Equipo Crónica — "Chronicle Team" — was a partnership of two Valencian painters, Rafael Solbes and
    Manuel Valdés, working together from the mid-1960s. They took the language of Pop Art and aimed it
    squarely at Spanish history and the Franco regime, raiding the masterpieces of the Prado and recasting them
    in flat, poster-bright style, full of sly anachronism and political bite.
    LISTEN FOR
    The trick is the double-take. You recognise Las Meninas or the Third of May, and then you notice what's been
    changed, added, or made absurd — and the joke turns sharp. Under a censorship that watched everything,
    humour and quotation were weapons: you could say the unsayable if you said it through Velázquez. It's the
    wittiest art on this floor, and the angriest underneath the wit. From here the collection moves to the moment
    everything changed — the death of the dictator, and the Transition — right along here.

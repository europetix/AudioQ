 After the war: a country in grey
 Make your way to Floor 1 — and brace for a change of mood. If the second floor was about a country
 dreaming, this floor is about a country surviving. The work here mostly comes from the long decades
 after the Civil War, under Franco's dictatorship, when the Republic had lost, the avant-garde was
 scattered into exile, and a heavy silence settled over Spanish culture.

 One honest warning before we start. This part of the collection is in the middle of a years-long re-hang
 that runs to 2028, so the rooms up here shift more than the ones downstairs. I'll guide you by theme
 rather than by door number; trust what's on the wall in front of you over any number you see.

 What you're looking for falls into two big responses to that grey time. One is abstraction — artists who
 couldn't say anything openly, so they said everything through raw matter and gesture, in work that slips
 past the censor because it names nothing. The other, later, is the slow return of recognisable images, and
 finally the art of the Transition, when Franco died and Spain edged back toward democracy. We'll end
 on the floor with two of the most charged objects in the building. This way.

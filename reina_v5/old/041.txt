 Cool heads: geometry and the Cuenca group
 Not all the abstraction of these years was a scream. Alongside the matter and the fury ran a cooler, more
 lyrical line — artists who reached for order, geometry and silence rather than raw emotion.

 The standout is Pablo Palazuelo, a near-mystic of geometry, who built quiet, crystalline structures of
 line and muted colour that feel closer to music or mathematics than to the wounded surfaces next door.
 And look for the artists of the so-called Cuenca group — Fernando Zóbel, Gustavo Torner, Gerardo
 Rueda — who in the 1960s founded a little museum of abstract art in the hanging houses of the old town
 of Cuenca, and made delicate, contemplative work in dialogue with one another.

 Together they're a reminder that "post-war Spanish abstraction" wasn't one thing. Some artists howled;
 others withdrew into a pure, ordered calm — two ways of stepping outside a reality you couldn't
 change. Palazuelo's geometry is through here.

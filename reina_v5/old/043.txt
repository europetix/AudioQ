TRACK 043 · FLOOR 1                                W-FOCUS                              166 WORDS · ~1 MIN

 Fernando Zóbel, lyrical abstraction
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A pale, contemplative canvas — fields of soft grey and white veiled in mist, with a few fine, precise dark
    lines drawn across them like calligraphy or the trace of a thought.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Fernando Zóbel, a Spanish-Filipino painter of rare refinement and the founding spirit of that little museum of
    abstract art in the hanging houses of Cuenca. He'd lay down delicate washes of grey, then inject single
    hair-fine lines with a syringe, so the gesture floats over the haze with extraordinary control.
    LISTEN FOR
    If the El Paso painters were a shout, Zóbel is a held breath. His abstraction is all distance, silence and
    exactness — closer to Chinese landscape painting or to music than to Western expressionism. He was a great
    collector and teacher as much as a painter, and gathering his Cuenca circle into one museum was itself a small
    act of resistance: making space for contemplation in a noisy, narrow time. His countryman Lucio Muñoz
    worked the opposite way, in rough matter, round this corner.

 Pablo Gargallo, metal mask
    WHERE YOU ARE
    A sculpture in sheet metal or bronze — often a head or mask — that turns solidity inside out, building a face
    as much from the hollows and gaps as from the metal itself.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Pablo Gargallo, an Aragonese sculptor and a contemporary of González, working the same revolutionary
    seam: metal as line and void rather than mass. He's famous for masks and heads in hammered copper, lead
    and iron, in which a curl of metal stands in for a lock of hair and an empty socket does the work of an eye.
    LISTEN FOR
    The trick that delights everyone is the way absence becomes presence — a hole reads as an eye, a gap as a
    cheekbone, so your mind completes the face out of nothing. Gargallo kept one foot in classical elegance even
    as he embraced industrial materials, which gives his work a strange grace the rougher moderns lack. Between
    him and González, you're watching sculpture step out of stone and into the machine age. The Surrealist rooms
    are next, a short way on.

[lightly] In 1919, a Spanish ballet conquered London. Manuel de Falla's The Three-Cornered Hat, staged by the Ballets Russes, was a triumph. Suddenly everyone wanted Spanish dance to be modern.

Two women led the way. Antonia Mercé, known as La Argentina, wanted a refined dance that could speak to the whole world, and she founded her own company, the Ballets Espagnols. Encarnación López, La Argentinita, went the other way. She collected the folk songs and dances of every corner of Spain.

In 1933 La Argentinita launched a large new company in Madrid. At its debut she premiered this ballet, The Pilgrimage of Cuckolds. The music was by Gustavo Pittaluga, and the libretto was based on a ballad by Federico García Lorca.

[pause] The sets and costumes were by the sculptor Alberto Sánchez. He imagined the stage as a dry, dreamlike Castilian landscape, full of skeletal, winding forms. Look for the costume of a Castilian countryman, made for the ballet. And look at Carlos Sáenz de Tejada's little models nearby: a dancer, a guitarist, a man clapping the rhythm, a bullfighter.

Those forms echo the work Alberto was doing with his friend Benjamín Palencia, in what came to be called the School of Vallecas. You'll meet them in Room 206.01.

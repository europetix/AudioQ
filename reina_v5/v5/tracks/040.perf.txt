[quietly] In 1983, heroin killed more than a hundred people in Spain. By the early 1990s it was one of the leading causes of death among young people in the big cities.

This room is about that generation, so often romanticised and so often blamed, and yet, the room insists, one of the most creative of the Transition. The new photographers and comic artists of La Movida showed drugs from street level, in the first person. Ceesepe's comic Vicios modernos, Modern Vices, is here.

So is Alberto García-Alix's photograph Willy's Absence. Willy was his brother, who died of an overdose. He isn't in the picture. There's only his shirt on the ground, and a pencil sketch of him. A portrait, and a gravestone.

[pause] And then there is Arrebato, Rapture, the film Iván Zulueta made in 1979, perhaps his most ambitious. It's a self-portrait, and a portrait of underground Madrid and its experiments with drugs. In it, the room text says, cinema itself becomes a vampire, a metaphor for heroin, for making art and for giving your life to it.

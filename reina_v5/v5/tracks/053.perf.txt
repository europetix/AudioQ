[wry] Landscape is one of painting's oldest genres. This little room sets out to take it apart.

The curators put it simply. Photography is so close to the way the eye sees that it makes the perfect tool for exposing the clichés of a view. And one of the great ideas that postmodern thinking dismantled was the neat split between nature and culture.

Now find Perejaume's Serra de Busa, from 1989. A serra is a mountain range, and the title names one in Catalonia. But what hangs here is a strip of wood and canvas, three and a half metres long and only a few centimetres high. [pause] A whole range of mountains, reduced to the line it makes against the sky. Or is it just a picture frame, waiting for its picture?

Nearby, the duo Bleda y Rosa work with historical memory. Their series is called Battlefields. Each title gives a place and a date, here Las Navas de Tolosa, in the summer of 1212. Look at the picture, then ask yourself what the landscape still remembers.

[quietly] None of these views is innocent. Each one has been framed by someone.

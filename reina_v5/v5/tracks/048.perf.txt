[wry] Most paintings get a wall. This one has been thrown under a chair.

Ángela de la Cruz moved to London in the late eighties and early nineties, just as the Young British Artists were making their name with daring new materials. Later she was a finalist for the Turner Prize.

Her series called Nothing is about the impossibility of painting. She took canvases painted in a single colour, crumpled them up and cast them aside. Here, in a work from 1999, a large crumpled canvas has been tossed under a plain chair.

[pause] She had a phrase for these works. "Paintings waiting." And then: "Unable for the moment to be anything else, they wait."

It's one of her first truly sculptural pieces. It also takes you back to the studio, where a painting is never quite finished, only on its way to becoming something.

The rest of Room 14 is about art made with other people. Bestué and Vives turn their own home into a stage for actions. Álvaro Perdices works with schoolchildren.

[warmly] Take one more look at the canvas. It has been waiting since 1999. It's in no hurry.

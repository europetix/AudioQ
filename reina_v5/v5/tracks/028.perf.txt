[warmly] In 1935, André Breton, the leader of the Surrealists, travelled a long way from Paris. He went to Tenerife, in the Canary Islands.

He was there because of a magazine. Gaceta de Arte was published in Santa Cruz de Tenerife from 1932 until the Civil War. Its editor, Eduardo Westerdahl, had met the avant-garde on his travels abroad, and he brought it home. The magazine printed Rationalism and Surrealism side by side, and it kept in touch with the modern architects of GATEPAC, whom you met at the start of these rooms.

Surrealism was there from the first issues, partly through the reports a young painter from the island sent back from Paris. His name was Óscar Domínguez. Thanks to these links, Breton came to support the Second International Exhibition of Surrealism in Tenerife. He gave two lectures, and he arranged a screening of L'âge d'or, the controversial film by Buñuel and Dalí that was later banned.

[pause] Now find Domínguez's small painting, Butterflies Lost in the Mountain. He painted it in Paris, and it's full of homesickness for Tenerife. In the case are his father's butterflies, carefully preserved. Two women in clothes that recall the dress of the Guanches, the islands' ancient people, stand high on a mountain. It may be Teide. They gaze at the case almost in worship.

[curious] There's another reading. Some say Domínguez gave the picture to Westerdahl, to mark a day of flying kites shaped like black paper birds. It was staged by a group of island poets, actors and artists called Pajaritas de Papel, Little Paper Birds.

Either way, it's a small picture of a very long distance, from a Paris studio back to a mountain at home.

[wry] Most people who cut up photographs try to hide the joins. John Heartfield wanted you to see them.

He was a Berlin artist, part of the Dada circle that formed as the First World War ended, alongside George Grosz, Raoul Hausmann and Hannah Höch. Germany was in turmoil. They cut photographs apart, combined the pieces with bold type, and turned the result into political protest. Heartfield became one of the great pioneers of this photomontage.

He put it on the covers of books for the Malik publishing house, then on the pages of magazines like AIZ. His side was the workers' struggle. His enemy was fascism. [pause] And he never pretended that his images were real. The tears and the scissor cuts stay visible, so you always know someone has taken reality apart to make a point.

This portfolio, 33 Photomontages, was published six years after his death, to carry his best work around the world. Look for the Communist Party election poster from 1928 built around an outstretched hand. The Hand Has 5 Fingers, it says. With 5 you seize the enemy.

His work had a powerful influence in Spain, on the photomontages Josep Renau made from the 1930s onward. You'll meet Renau in a moment.

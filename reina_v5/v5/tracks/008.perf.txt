[curious] José Ortiz Echagüe wanted his photographs to give you what he called "the strange feeling of travelling to a different time". Look around this small room and see if it works on you.

He began photographing everyday life in Spain in 1903, and from 1916 he did it systematically, travelling from region to region. He wanted to record ways of life he believed were about to disappear. His aims were documentary. His methods were not.

[pause] Find Village Sermon, one of his earliest pictures, taken in 1903 in the parish church of Viguera, in La Rioja. Look at the blacks. They are deep and velvety, and the softer tones break into tiny dots. Ortiz Echagüe printed everything himself, with his own version of the carbon process. He called it Carbondir. The results look closer to an engraving than a photograph.

[amused] Not everyone approved. A review in 1923 criticised how heavily he worked his prints by hand.

His portraits of Spanish types and costumes became books, first in Berlin and then in Madrid. The busts beside them are by the sculptor Julio Antonio, who travelled Spain with the same idea, portraying working people with a classical calm.

[wry] He called himself an entrepreneur, or contractor, in poetic affairs. His name was Ernesto Giménez Caballero, and everyone knew him as Gecé.

His great creation was a magazine, La Gaceta Literaria. For the young writers and artists of the generation of 1927, it was the guide to everything modern. Gecé gathered contributors of every kind around it. The painter Gabriel García Maroto designed its heading. Maruja Mallo, whose fair you've just seen, was among its contributors.

He loved modern architecture too. He opened a shop and bookshop, La Galería, designed by the architect Fernando García Mercadal, where he sold tubular-metal furniture. In 1928 he founded a film club with Luis Buñuel, and he went on to direct two short avant-garde films himself.

[pause] Then the story turns. Gecé moved toward fascism. He quarrelled with many of the people who had made his magazine, and found himself isolated. In the end he wrote the last issues entirely on his own, calling himself a literary Robinson. The final issue came out in May 1932.

Around you is the modern world his pages celebrated: the clean lines of a restaurant on Monte Ulía by Aizpurúa and Labayen, and Cassandre's railway poster, Étoile du Nord.

# DE notes, tracks 001–028 (Katja, Germany)

All 28 JSON files are in reina_i18n/de/001.json to 028.json. `assemble.py --check … de` gives OK for every track from 001 to 028. Word counts are between 1% and 8% under the English, and sentences average 9 to 13 words. No semicolons, no parentheses and no dashes appear in the speech.

## Conventions used
- Room titles appear in German quotation marks „…“, translated naturally. Examples: „Heilige Boheme. Vom Elendsviertel zur Gran Vía“, „Sprechende Wände“, „Nach dem Kubismus“, „GATEPAC, die Dringlichkeit der Einrichtungen“, „Gecé: Der Kanalinspektor“, „Die spanische Nacht“, „Alberto: Die Wallfahrt der Gehörnten“, „Die universelle Zeit der Kultur. Gaceta de Arte“, „Kriegsfotografie“. The room numbers are written exactly as in the English, for example "205.03 bis".
- Established German titles: Mein Katalonien (Orwell), Der Dreispitz (Falla), Tod Mariens (Mantegna), Das Triadische Ballett / Der Abstrakte (Schlemmer), Der Surrealismus und die Malerei (Breton), Johannes vom Kreuz for San Juan de la Cruz (002). Works with no established German title were translated naturally: Frau in Blau, Haus mit Palme, Frauenkopf (Fernande), Das offene Fenster, Eine Welt, Fallender Mann, Frau mit Fächer, Verlorene Schmetterlinge im Gebirge, Kubistische Komposition, Porträt II, and others. Titles the English leaves in their original form also stay in that form in German: Equal-Parallel: Guernica-Bengasi, Inversion, Toki egin, Totalisateur, Atocha, Dubonnet, Aidez l'Espagne, Tertulia, Mono y mona.
- Spoken text uses regnal numbers in words: Karl der Dritte (001) and Alfons der Dreizehnte (004). "Portrait II" is spoken as "Porträt Zwei", while the title field keeps "Porträt II". Spoken dates are in words, for example "am sechsundzwanzigsten April" and "elften Juni 1938". Every year stays in digits.
- 009: "the late 1800s" is a century, not a year, so it became "Ende des neunzehnten Jahrhunderts". The checker does not require digits here. Change it to digits if the reviewer prefers.

## Small adaptations (facts unchanged)
- 012: the English "one critic" is Jan Avgikos, a woman according to the sources. German has "so ein Kritiker", which is the generic masculine.
- 027: the English "For him" became "Für Santos Torroella", because "ihn" could be misheard as referring to Dalí.
- 019: "Two young painters" (Mallo and Dalí) became "Zwei junge Künstler".

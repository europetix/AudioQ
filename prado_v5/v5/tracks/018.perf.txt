[curious] Hidden in this painting is one of the newest gadgets of its day. It's a telescope, modelled on the very first one ever patented, in 1608. Jan Brueghel painted it here only nine years later.

This is Sight, one of five panels on the five senses, and its companions hang here with it. Since Aristotle, sight had been counted the most important sense, so it gets the grandest stage. Two Flemish masters shared the work. Brueghel, nicknamed Velvet Brueghel, built the rooms and the treasures in tiny, glittering strokes. Rubens added the figures. The museum calls it one of their most successful collaborations.

At the centre sits Venus. Cupid holds up a painting for her, and it's a Christian subject: Christ healing a blind man. Sight of the eyes, and sight of the soul. Nearby, a copy of Raphael's Saint Cecilia hangs among ancient Roman busts.

[pause] Now go hunting. Find the telescope. Then the magnifying glasses, and even a pair of pince-nez. Look for compasses, and instruments for reading the stars. Look up at the paintings stacked frame to frame towards the ceiling.

[warmly] Brueghel was a protégé of the Archdukes Albert and Isabella, his great patrons. He slipped their portraits into the picture, and a view of their palace at Mariemont. The set later passed from prince to prince as a gift, until a Spanish duke gave it to the king, most likely Philip the Fourth.

[lightly] One last thing to find. The painting is signed and dated on a little sheet of paper, close to Venus's seat. It's a picture about the pleasure of looking, and it rewards anyone who keeps looking.

Now go down one floor to Floor 1, by the stairs or the lift near these rooms. Follow the signs to Room 1, the round room by the Goya entrance.

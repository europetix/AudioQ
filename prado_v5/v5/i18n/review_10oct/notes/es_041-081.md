## Quotations (rule 11)
- 064: «una mujer vestida de maja». This wording is from the 1814 inventory and is verified verbatim on …/coleccion/obra-de-arte/la-maja-vestida/a3121efc-6924-454c-8a9f-e4320f26d3d0 (it also appears on the La maja desnuda page). It is the only Spanish quotation in quotation marks.
- 064: the engraver (González de Sepúlveda) said the nude was "without drawing and dull in the colouring". The original on the Maja desnuda page reads "sin divujo ni gracia en el colorido", with that old spelling. It is given as reported speech without quotation marks: "Escribió que no tenía dibujo ni gracia en el colorido."
- 056: the inventory entry "a Fable of Arachne by Velázquez" is reported speech without quotation marks. The Spanish page gives no verbatim inventory wording.
- 049: Palomino ("most illustrious of all his works") is reported speech, as in the English.
- 065: the Grimaldi letter is reported speech. "El rostro … es severo pero sin altanería" follows the museum's Spanish wording, verified on the Carlos III page, but is not in quotation marks.
- 068: Goya's letter to a friend is reported speech, as in the English.
- 077: "in the museum's own words" is rendered as unquoted reported speech close to the museum's Spanish ("uno de los grandes manifiestos políticos de toda la [historia de la] pintura española en defensa de la libertad del hombre").
- 055: the Manet remark is reported speech, as in the English. 061: "Thanks is what Rubens is saying to life" becomes the unquoted "Gracias es lo que Rubens le dice a la vida".
- 077: "Sail from Gibraltar…, came the promise" stays as free indirect speech without quotation marks, as in the English.

## Choices and flags
- 041: "El Greco himself" becomes "el Greco". 042: the "American scholar" is rendered as "una investigadora estadounidense" (Delphine Fitz Derby, per @sources).
- 043: Spinola is written "Spínola" and Justin of Nassau "Justino de Nassau", the usual Spanish forms.
- 058/059/070: Isabella Farnese is written "Isabel de Farnesio".
- 060: Peter Paul Rubens is written "Pedro Pablo Rubens". 081: "Habsburgs" is rendered as "los Austrias".
- 067: the "Not jokes" pun on "cartoons" was dropped because it does not exist in Spanish. The definition of a cartoon is kept.
- 070: the museum's Spanish author line is "Escuela de Pasiteles", while the English says "Pupil of Pasiteles". I followed the English ("Discípulo de Pasiteles"). Worth harmonising.
- 075: "After it fell, he went to France" is rendered neutrally as "acabó en Francia". The English does not say he was taken prisoner, so I did not add that.
- 076: "mid-thirties" becomes "unos treinta y cinco años". 078: "late twenties" becomes "casi treinta años". 071: "in his seventies" becomes "con setenta y tantos años".

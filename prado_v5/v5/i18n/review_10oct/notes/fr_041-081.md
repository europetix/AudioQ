# FR notes — Prado tracks 041–081 (Vivienne, vous)

Self-check: `assemble.py --check … fr` prints OK for all 41 tracks (041–081). Every track is shorter than the English, by about 1 to 6 %.
I wrote these from the 10 Oct 2026 English and did not reuse `stale_old_numbering/`.

## Titles and conventions
- I used the established French titles where they exist: Les Ménines, La Reddition de Breda (Las Lanzas, Les Lances), Le Triomphe de Bacchus (« Les Buveurs »), La Forge de Vulcain, Les Fileuses, Le Lavement des pieds, Moïse sauvé des eaux, Le Jardin d'amour, Le Jugement de Pâris, La Maja nue / La Maja vêtue, Le Deux Mai 1808 / Le Trois Mai 1808, Les Peintures noires, Le Sabbat des sorcières, La Laitière de Bordeaux.
- Other titles are natural translations: Nature morte aux récipients, L'Ombrelle, Le Marchand de vaisselle (the vendor is a man in the invoice), L'Aire de battage ou L'Été, Garçons sur la plage, Par les yeux des rois.
- French artist names: le Greco, Titien, le Tintoret, Véronèse, Annibal Carrache (the English has Annibale Carracci), Guido Reni, Antoine van Dyck, Pierre Paul Rubens.
- Royal names: Élisabeth Farnèse, Marie-Louise, Marie-Anne, Marie-Amélie, Isabelle la Catholique, Jeanne.
- Regnal numbers are written as words in speech (Philippe quatre / trois / deux, Charles quatre / trois / premier, Charles Quint, Ferdinand sept). The @title of 062 and 065 keeps the display form "Charles IV" / "Charles III". Change these to words if titles are ever read aloud.
- Centuries are spelled out (dix-huitième / dix-neuvième siècle). Track 070 uses "vers l'an 10 avant Jésus-Christ", with no abbreviation.
- Room letters are spaced (salle 9 A, 15 A, 16 B, 61 B, 62 B, 63 B, 60 A), and floors are written "niveau 0 / 1 / 2". Every conditional is kept, including the rehang caveats in 062, 063 and 064, and the "if you have time" and "if you're skipping the Extras" lines.

## Quotations
Rule 11 (originally French quotes) applies to one passage only, in 055 (Manet).
- 041: the banner "Gloria in excelsis Deo" is kept in Latin, without quotation marks.
- 042: the signature "Jusepe de Ribera, español" is kept as it is inscribed.
- 056: « une Fable d'Arachné de Velázquez ». This is from the 1664 inventory, originally Spanish. I translated it faithfully from the English quote and introduced it the same way.
- 064: « sans dessin et terne dans le coloris » (the engraver, 1800) and « une femme habillée en maja » (the later record). Both were originally Spanish. I translated them faithfully from the English and introduced them the same way.
- 055: Manet's remark on Pablo de Valladolid was originally French, from his letter of 1865. I kept it as reported speech without quotation marks ("Manet y a vu peut-être le morceau de peinture le plus étonnant jamais réalisé"), so it does not pass as his own wording.
- 061: the museum line "Thanks is what Rubens is saying to life" is reported without quotation marks, as in the English.
- The following stay as reported speech, as in the English: Palomino in 047 and 049, the museum lines in 043, 058, 062, 066, 072, 074 and 077, Grimaldi in 065, and Goya's letter in 068.
- I kept "the museum says / calls / suggests" only where the English has it.

## Translation choices worth checking
- 042: for "an American scholar" I used « une chercheuse américaine », because French needs a gender. The source names Delphine Fitz Derby.
- 045: "Los Borrachos, the drunks" became « Los Borrachos, les ivrognes ». The title uses the glossary form « Les Buveurs ».
- 067: I dropped the English pun "Not jokes" on cartoon. It does not exist in French, because "carton" has no joke sense, and no fact is lost.
- 069: "most of a century" became « presque un siècle ».
- 076: "in his mid-thirties" became « vers trente-cinq ans ».
- 078: "in his late twenties" became « il approchait de la trentaine ».

## Possible issues in the English (translated as written)
- 060: "in 1628, Rubens came back to Madrid". His earlier Spanish visit, in 1603, was to the court at Valladolid, not Madrid. "Came back" is only loosely right. Please verify.
- 041: "Around you hang four canvases from an earlier altarpiece" lists three in that paragraph. The fourth, Pentecost, comes in the next paragraph. This is consistent, but a listener may count only three.
- 064: the cabinet's two "paintings then given to Titian" are rendered as « alors attribués à Titien ». This is correct, but please check against the museum page if needed.

## Official Spanish titles used
None. German needs no Spanish titles, so I fetched no museodelprado.es pages. Titles are given in natural German. Where an established German title exists I used it: Der Garten der Lüste, Der Heuwagen, Die Kreuzabnahme, Der Triumph des Todes, Die Andrier, Der Parnass, Der Gesichtssinn, Der Tastsinn. Las Meninas is not in this range.

## Conventions
- Rooms are spaced for the voice ("Saal 55 A", "Saal 79 B") in the titles, @where and speech. Floors are "Ebene 0 / 1 / 2". "Shown at its doorway" is rendered as "am Eingang angeschrieben" and "Look for 55A at the doorway" as "Achten Sie am Eingang auf die Nummer 55 A". "Extra" is rendered as "ein kurzer Extra-Titel".
- Regnal numbers are written in words everywhere, including the titles and @where: Philipp der Zweite / Philipps des Zweiten, Karl der Fünfte, Karl der Erste von England, Ferdinand der Siebte, Heinrich der Achte, Franz der Erste.
- Centuries are written in words ("im achtzehnten Jahrhundert") and decades as "1490er-Jahre", "1560er-Jahre", "1940er-Jahre".
- Leoni's "Fury" is rendered as "der Furor", the established German term for this group.

## Things in the English that looked odd (translated as written)
- 003 says "Isabella Farnese" and 034 says "Isabel Farnese" for the same queen. The German uses "Isabella Farnese" in both, for consistency. Usual German usage is "Elisabeth Farnese"; I did not use it, to stay close to the English.
- 011: "On the museum's map it's called the Dormition" is an odd phrase (map? catalogue? label?). It is translated literally as "Auf dem Plan des Museums".
- 007 @where: "gold Golden Fleece" is redundant. The German says "mit dem goldenen Vlies".
- 028: "in his seventies" around 1562 is rendered as "mit über siebzig Jahren". It depends on Titian's uncertain birth year but is consistent with the museum.

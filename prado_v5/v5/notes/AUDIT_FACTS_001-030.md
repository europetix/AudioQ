# Fact audit: tracks 001–030 (independent check, 10 Oct 2026)

**Method.** I read each track's spoken body (and the @where/@what wayfinding lines) and checked every load-bearing claim against the official museodelprado.es page(s) listed in its @sources. I fetched the pages one at a time (about 45 fetches) and cross-checked rooms against research/*_result.tsv and the official 2026 plan (research/map/plano_ing_2026.pdf). No track was edited.

**Verdicts.** OK = the official page supports it. FRAMED = told honestly as tradition or opinion. WEAK = plausible general knowledge that is not on the page. WRONG = the page contradicts it. UNVERIFIED = I could not check it.

This file lists only the WEAK, WRONG and UNVERIFIED items. A track marked "No issues" had every load-bearing claim OK or FRAMED.

All rooms in tracks 001–030 match the official room data: 055A, 056A, 055B, 056B, 049, 058, 058A, 051A, 076, 079B, 082, 083, 001, 041, 042, 044, 002, 003, 005, 007A, 008B, 009B, 009 and 009A. The plan also confirms Leoni's *Philip II* in Room 27 and the map title "The Dormition of the Virgin" for the Mantegna in Room 56B. The research TSV gives the other Senses in Room 083.

## 001 · Welcome
No issues. The history page confirms the 1785 Villanueva design for the Natural History Cabinet by order of Charles III, his grandson Ferdinand VII, the November 1819 opening, and the Moneo extension by the Jerónimos.

## 002 · Patinir, Charon Crossing the Styx
- **WRONG.** "Devils, angels and animals shrank."
  - Evidence: the [Patinir page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/wd/c51349b6-049e-476c-a388-5ae6d301e8c1) says the changes were made "by reducing the number of demons and damned souls, and by scaling them down in size". It does not mention angels or animals.
  - Fix: "Devils and damned souls were thinned out and made smaller."
- **WEAK.** "A giant demon on the left bank was painted over."
  - Evidence: the same page says "The gigantic demon visible from the bank is eliminated". It does not say which bank.
  - Fix: "A giant demon by the riverbank was painted out."
- **WEAK.** "Around 1500, artists in Flanders began letting the landscape take over the picture. Joachim Patinir was a pioneer of that idea."
  - Evidence: the page only says that "once again the landscape … predominates". The pioneer claim is standard art history.
  - Fix: keep, or say "Patinir was one of the first to make landscape the star."

## 003 · Bruegel, The Triumph of Death
- **WEAK.** "The painting passed through Italian ducal collections before a Spanish queen, Isabella Farnese, bought it in the eighteenth century."
  - Evidence: the [provenance](https://www.museodelprado.es/coleccion/obra-de-arte/el-triunfo-de-la-muerte/d3d82b0b-9bf2-4082-ab04-66ed53196ccc) also lists Anna Carafa, princess of Stigliano, and the Spanish II Duke of Medina de las Torres, "Nápoles, 1644-1655 y Madrid, 1655-1668", before Isabel de Farnesio acquired it in 1746–1759.
  - Fix: "…passed through Italian and Spanish noble collections before…"

Everything else is OK, including the music details, the lovers "ajena", the reddish-brown tone and the fact that some people fight while others give in.

## 004 · Bosch, The Garden of Earthly Delights
- **WRONG (@where, visual).** "the enormous open triptych almost four metres wide"
  - Evidence: the [Garden page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/the-garden-of-earthly-delights-triptych/02388242-6d6a-4e9e-a992-e1311eab3609) gives wings of 76.5 cm and a centre panel of 172.5 cm. Open, it is about 3.25 m wide.
  - Fix: "more than three metres wide".
- **UNVERIFIED.** "A bird-headed creature … swallows the greedy whole."
  - Evidence: the page confirms "a theriomorphic creature with a bird's head … seated on a type of child's lavatory stool". I did not check what it devours or who.
  - Fix: "swallows sinners whole" (safe visually).

## 005 · Bosch, Haywain, Table of the Seven Deadly Sins, Stone of Madness
No issues. The 1570 Guevara version is a copy. The Table was owned before 1560 and kept in Philip II's private apartment, and the page says it is signed by Bosch. The Stone of Madness flower is a waterlily that "generally" stands for money.

## 006 · Dürer, Self-portrait, and Adam and Eve
- **WEAK (@where, visual).** "leaning on a window ledge"
  - Evidence: the [Self-portrait page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/selfportrait/8417d190-eb9d-4c52-9c89-dcdcd0109b5b) describes "the arm leaning on the foreground ledge", with the window "in the back wall".
  - Fix: "resting his arm on a ledge, a window behind him".
- **WEAK (clarity).** "Its inscription says the work was made after the Virgin gave birth."
  - Evidence: the [Eve page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/eve/930c0fdf-fcfc-47df-b216-e375f5719084) quotes the inscription "faciebat post virginis partum - 1507". It is a dating formula meaning 1507 years after the Virgin gave birth, and the museum reads it as an allusion to Mary as the new Eve. As written, the line may puzzle listeners.
  - Fix: "Its inscription dates the work to 1507 years after the Virgin gave birth: a reminder, the museum says, of Mary as the new Eve."

## 007 · Fra Angelico, The Annunciation
- **WEAK.** "painted a few years later for his own convent at Fiesole"

  - Evidence: the page does not mention this. It is correct visually.
  - Fix: keep.

## 009 · Botticelli, Nastagio degli Onesti
- **WEAK.** "on the third panel the Medici arms sit between the arms of the two families"

As far as I know the Medici shield is in the centre, so the line is probably right.

## 010 · Raphael, The Cardinal and The Pearl
- **WRONG (header @what/@where).** The @what gives "The Holy Family, or 'The Pearl' … 116 × 147.4 cm", and the @where says "The Pearl is the wider painting nearby".
  - Evidence: the [Pearl page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/the-holy-family-or-the-pearl/f3d745b8-f800-437a-a664-9f3d065ce127) gives "Width 116 cm; Height 147.4 cm". It is an upright picture.
  - Fix: @what "147.4 × 116 cm"; @where "the larger, upright painting nearby".
- **WEAK.** "When Philip the Fourth first saw it, the story goes…"
  - Evidence: the page says tradition holds he said it "when he saw it". It does not say "first".
  - Fix: drop "first".
- **WEAK.** "Raphael painted him around 1510, in Rome."
  - Evidence: the [Cardinal page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/the-cardinal/4c01eae6-feed-4135-88d9-6736140212fb) does not name a place. Rome is true in general knowledge, since Raphael lived there from 1508.
  - Fix: keep.

## 015 · Rembrandt, Judith at the Banquet of Holofernes
- **WRONG.** "Rembrandt signed this canvas in 1634, when he was just twenty-eight."
  - Evidence: the [Judith page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/judith-at-the-banquet-of-holofernes/08f69e61-f7c5-43f9-a76d-b24fbdc63681) records the inscription "Rembrandt / f : 1634" but says: "As for the signature, the unsteadiness of the stroke and, above all, the yellow colour make it dubious." It accepts the date as "consonant with the style". Rembrandt was born on 15 July 1606, so he was 27 or 28 in 1634.
  - Fix: "Rembrandt painted this canvas in 1634, when he was about twenty-eight." Also drop "signed" from the @what line.
- **WRONG (header @what).** "154.7 × 143 cm"
  - Evidence: the page gives "Width: 154.7 cm; Height: 143 cm".
  - Fix: "143 × 154.7 cm".
- **WEAK (dated source).** "This is the only fully accepted Rembrandt in any Spanish public collection."
  - Evidence: the [source](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/whats-on/new/dutch-painters-at-the-prado/bb52efff-f275-4a4f-8fe5-91b2b0de00c6) is a news item dated 3 Dec 2009.
  - Fix: "The museum has described it as the only fully accepted Rembrandt in a Spanish public collection."
- **WEAK.** "These top-floor rooms hold the finer, smaller pictures from Flanders and Holland."
  - Evidence: not on either page.
  - Fix: keep, or "These top-floor rooms hold Flemish and Dutch painting."

## 017 · Clara Peeters
No issues. The exhibition page confirms: the first Prado exhibition devoted to a woman painter, four works in the Prado, reflections in at least eight works, and that it is not known whether she painted after 1621.

## 018 · Brueghel and Rubens, The Sense of Sight
No issues. The page confirms the Lippershey telescope of 1608, the signature on a paper near the seat, and the 1636 chain from Neuburg to the Cardinal-Infante to Medina de las Torres to Philip IV.

## 019 · Leoni, Charles V and the Fury
- **WEAK (unsupported hook).** "In 1549, the emperor Charles the Fifth ordered a bronze of himself, with one unusual request. He wanted to be able to take his armour off." The @listen line repeats it: "the emperor who asked for armour he could take off".
  - Evidence: the [Leoni page](https://www.museodelprado.es/en/the-collection/art-work/emperor-carlos-v-and-the-fury-the-nude-figure/dc052be4-d3f4-4d00-9af8-edcbfb338e60) says only that it was "commissioned by Charles V … in 1549 from … Leone Leoni", that Leoni "had it cast in Milan in separate pieces", and that the screws make it "easy to attach or remove them". Nothing says the emperor asked for this.
  - Fix: "In 1549, the emperor Charles the Fifth ordered a bronze of himself. What he got has a secret: the armour comes off." Change @listen to match.
- **WEAK.** "His son Pompeo later finished the work in Spain."
  - Evidence: this is supported only by a bibliography title on the page (Arciniega 2013, "…su acabado en España por Pompeo Leoni"). Pompeo is a co-author on the record.
  - Fix: keep.

## 020 · Titian, The Andrians, and Self-portrait

TRACK 070 · 60–63                                   W-FOCUS                               229 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Vicente López, Portrait of Goya
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Find the portrait of an old man in a dark coat, seated and half-turned, holding the tools of a painter — a
    vigorous, white-haired figure looking out with sharp, undimmed eyes.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    An elderly man sits turned toward us, his face strongly lit against a plain dark ground. His hair is white and
    unruly, his expression alert and faintly amused, his eyes fierce. He wears a dark coat; one hand holds a brush.
    The handling is smooth and polished, almost official — the opposite of its sitter's own wild late style.
    LISTEN FOR
    Look at the eyes. This is one great painter studying another — and the old man's gaze gives nothing away.

 Before we go on into the nineteenth century proper, stop and meet the man whose shadow falls over this
 entire museum. This is Francisco de Goya, painted in 1826 by Vicente López, the leading court
 portraitist of the day — and it is the face to carry back through every Goya room you have walked.
 Goya is around eighty here, deaf for decades, near the end of a life that ran from sunny tapestry designs
 to the horrors of war and the Black Paintings. He had returned briefly to Madrid from his self-imposed
 exile in France, and López caught him in a single sitting. Look how alive he is — the bristling white
 hair, the faintly mocking set of the mouth, the eyes that have plainly seen everything and forgiven little.
 There is no flattery here, but there is enormous respect.

 The sly irony lives in the paint itself. López works in the smooth, correct, official manner of the
 academy — exactly the polished finish Goya had spent his last decades tearing apart with palette knife
 and thumb. So we meet the wild old revolutionary of the Black Paintings rendered in the same style he
 had left behind, gazing out as though he is in on the joke. Hold his eye a moment. Then the nineteenth
 century begins in earnest — a queen dictating her will, this way now.

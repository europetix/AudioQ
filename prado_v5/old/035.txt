 Rubens and the Flemish Baroque
 The Spanish kings collected one northern painter as avidly as they collected Titian: Peter Paul Rubens.
 He was Flemish, from Antwerp, and he was the most successful, most energetic painter in all of Europe
 — a diplomat and scholar as well as an artist, who ran a vast workshop and produced grand, surging
 canvases by the hundred.

 Rubens twice visited the Spanish court, where he met and befriended the young Velázquez, and the king
 bought his work in enormous quantity. What he brought was the full force of the Baroque: movement,
 abundance, glowing flesh, swirling drama, colour learned directly from his hero Titian. Where Spanish
 painting tends toward gravity and shadow, Rubens floods the room with energy and light and generous,
 healthy bodies.

 He is the great counter-current on this floor — the north answering Venice, exuberance answering
 restraint. The Prado's Rubens holdings are among the world's richest, from tender mythologies to
 towering altarpieces. Begin with his most beautiful and personal late work, a hymn to the female body
 and to love itself, right this way.

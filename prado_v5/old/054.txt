 Raphael, The Holy Family ("La Perla")
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Move to the tender group of the Virgin, the Christ child, the infant John the Baptist, and figures behind,
    arranged in a soft pyramid before a landscape.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The Virgin Mary sits and steadies the Christ child, who reaches toward the little Saint John the Baptist
    offered by Saint Elizabeth. Saint Joseph leans on a staff in the shadow behind. The figures form a gentle
    pyramid; a soft, luminous landscape with ruins opens behind them.
    LISTEN FOR
    Watch the two babies reaching for each other — Raphael builds the whole tender scene around that single
    childish gesture.

 They called this painting "La Perla" — "the Pearl" — and the nickname came from a king. When it
 entered the Spanish royal collection, Philip the Fourth is said to have exclaimed that it was "the pearl of
 my paintings," and the name stuck. It is one of Raphael's most beloved Holy Families. The scene is
 intimate and warm: the Virgin Mary seated with the Christ child on her knee, the infant John the Baptist
 reaching toward him, presented by his mother Saint Elizabeth, while Saint Joseph leans silently in the
 shadows behind. Raphael arranges them in his characteristic gentle pyramid — stable, harmonious,
 deeply satisfying to the eye — and softens everything with a tender play of light and a hazy,
 atmospheric landscape opening behind.

 The heart of it is the contact between the two children: the little Saint John offering, the Christ child
 reaching, the mothers watching over the exchange. It is the High Renaissance ideal of grace and human
 tenderness, sacred feeling made warm and natural. By this date Raphael ran a busy workshop, and his
 pupils had a hand in the execution — but the design, the sweetness, the perfect balance are
 unmistakably his. Now, perhaps the single greatest treasure on this floor — Van der Weyden's Descent
 from the Cross, just here.

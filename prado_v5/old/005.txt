 El Greco, The Adoration of the Shepherds
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Move to the tall night scene of shepherds kneeling around the newborn Christ — the whole canvas lit,
    impossibly, by the glowing body of the baby itself.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A nocturnal Nativity. The Christ child lies on white cloth, radiating light that floods upward over the Virgin,
    the shepherds, and a swirl of angels above. The figures are stretched tall as candle-flames, their robes acid
    yellow, green and red. There is no daylight here — only the holy light of the child.
    LISTEN FOR
    Compare this to the Trinity nearby. Forty years on, the Italian has burned away, and the pure El Greco
    remains.

 If the Trinity showed you El Greco arriving, this shows you El Greco at the very end — and it is one of
 the most moving things in the museum, because of why he made it. This was the altarpiece for his own
 tomb. An old man now, he painted it for the chapel where he expected to be buried, and you can feel
 that knowledge in every inch of it.

 Everything has changed in forty years. The space no longer pretends to be a stable; it has dissolved into
 a dark, flickering nowhere. The figures have stretched to almost twice life's proportions, their limbs like
 flames, swaying as though the whole canvas were underwater or on fire. And the light — there is no
 sun, no lantern. The only source is the body of the newborn Christ himself, a small blaze of white that
 throws its glow up onto the astonished faces leaning in. El Greco makes the theology literal: the light of
 the world, lighting the world.

 This is painting as pure spiritual sensation — the quality that had him dismissed as a madman for three
 centuries. The twentieth century saw it differently; Cézanne, Picasso and the Expressionists all looked
 back and recognised an ancestor. The kneeling shepherd in front, by the way, is often read as a
 self-portrait — the old painter placing himself at the foot of the manger, the last figure he would set into
 his own resting place.

 You have met the threshold of Spanish painting. Walk on toward the Velázquez galleries — where
 everything El Greco made possible comes to its summit.

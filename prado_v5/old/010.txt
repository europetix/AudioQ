TRACK 010 · 10–12                                   W-FOCUS                              266 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Velázquez, The Court Jesters (Pablo de Valladolid,
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Look for the smaller portraits of the palace jesters and dwarfs — a man in black standing alone in empty
    space, and seated figures low on the ground, gazing steadily out at you.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A gallery of the king's "men of pleasure." Pablo de Valladolid, an actor in black, stands declaiming in a void
    with no floor and no wall — only his own soft shadow. Francisco Lezcano, the boy from Vallecas, slumps
    against a rock with cards in his hands. Don Sebastián de Morra sits square on the ground, legs straight out,
    fists clenched, staring directly at us.
    LISTEN FOR
    Hold the gaze of Don Sebastián de Morra. There is no mockery in how Velázquez paints him — only a
    person, looking back.

 Every great court of the age kept them: dwarfs, jesters, "fools," human curiosities maintained for the
 amusement of the powerful. Velázquez painted them over and over, and what he made of that cruel
 subject is one of the small miracles of the Prado. He painted them as people.

 Start with Pablo de Valladolid — a comic actor, not a dwarf, caught mid-speech with one arm flung
 wide. There is no setting at all: no floor, no corner, nothing but a warm grey nowhere and the shadow he
 throws across it. Velázquez conjures a whole believable space out of pure tone, around a single
 gesturing man. The story goes that Manet stood before this picture, said the background was simply air,
 and went home to rebuild his own painting because of it.

 Then the dwarfs, and here the mood deepens. The boy from Vallecas, Francisco Lezcano, sits against a
 rock in the mountains, a loose deck of cards in his hands, his gaze drifting. Don Sebastián de Morra sits
 bolt upright on the floor, short legs thrust toward us, fists tight, and he looks straight out with an
 intelligence and a steadiness that stop you where you stand. Velázquez gives him no costume of
 ridicule, no joke at his expense — only the unwavering dignity of a man returning your stare. Whatever
 the court believed these people were for, the painter plainly saw something else, and made certain we
 would see it too.

 Hold that gaze a moment longer. Then we leave the court behind for a Greek myth hidden inside a
 Madrid workshop, over this way.

 Titian, Danaë and the Shower of Gold
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Stand before the painting of a reclining nude woman on a bed, gazing up as a cloud above her dissolves into a
    shower of golden coins.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A young woman reclines nude on white sheets, her body turned toward us, her face lifted. Above her, a dark
    cloud breaks open into a glittering rain of gold. To the right, an old servant woman tries to catch the falling
    coins in a cloth. Rich drapery frames the bed.
    LISTEN FOR
    Watch the gold falling from the cloud — that is the god Zeus himself, arriving as pure light and treasure.

 This is one of the most sensuous paintings in the museum, and a piece of mythology made
 unforgettable. The princess Danaë was locked away by her father, who had been warned that her son
 would one day kill him. But the god Zeus desired her, and reached her by transforming himself into a
 shower of gold that fell through the roof of her prison — and from that union the hero Perseus was born.
 Titian paints the instant of the god's arrival: the dark cloud above the bed bursting into a cascade of
 golden light and coins. Danaë reclines beneath it, her body soft and luminous, her face turned up in a
 mixture of wonder and surrender. Titian had painted the female nude many times — these mythological
 pictures he called poesie, "poems" — but here the handling of flesh and light reaches a rare tenderness
 and freedom.

 The cunning touch is the old servant on the right, frantically trying to catch the divine gold in her apron
 — base human greed set against the princess's rapture, comedy beside transcendence. Titian made
 several versions for royal patrons; Philip the Second prized his. But Titian also painted the Habsburgs
 their nightmares — turn to two of the darkest, most overpowering canvases in the building, in the room
 beyond.

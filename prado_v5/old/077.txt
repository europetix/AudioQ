 Goya, The Threshing Ground (Summer)
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Step up to the wide, sunny scene of peasants resting and playing in a harvest field, a great cart piled with
    sheaves and a warm sky stretching above.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A broad summer landscape at harvest time. Peasants gather around a laden hay-cart — some working, many
    resting, eating, joking, a few sprawled asleep in the sun. Children play; a dog dozes. The light is golden, the
    sky vast and warm, the whole scene relaxed and full of rural good humour.
    LISTEN FOR
    Look how many figures are simply resting and laughing — this is Goya celebrating ordinary country life,
    with real affection.

 Here is the young, sunny Goya at his most expansive. This large cartoon, representing Summer, was
 designed for a tapestry to hang in a royal dining room, and it is a warm, generous picture of country life
 at harvest time.

 The scene unfolds across a wide, golden landscape: it is the threshing season, and peasants have
 gathered around a great cart piled high with sheaves of grain. But Goya is less interested in the work
 than in the rest. Most of his figures are taking their ease in the summer heat — eating, drinking, joking,
 a few stretched fast asleep in the sun, children playing nearby. It is a relaxed, affectionate vision of rural
 Spain, full of light and good nature.

 What is striking, knowing the later Goya, is the sheer warmth and humanity here. He gives these
 ordinary working people real individuality and dignity, and clearly takes pleasure in their company. The
 composition is ambitious and full of air; the colour is fresh and bright. This is Goya the talented young
 court designer, decades before despair touched him. Now, one of the most charming small cartoons he
 ever made — The Parasol: it hangs in Room 90, just along from here, in the newly rehung cartoon
 rooms. Walk on and find it waiting.

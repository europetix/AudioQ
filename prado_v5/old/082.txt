 Among the hundreds of male names in this collection, here is another remarkable woman. Clara Peeters
 was a Flemish painter of the early seventeenth century and one of the pioneers of still-life painting — a
 genre in which women, often barred from grander subjects, could excel and even lead.

 This refined picture shows her mastery. On a simple ledge she arranges a hooded hunting hawk, a pile
 of dead game birds from the hunt, pieces of precious Chinese porcelain, and a careful display of exotic
 seashells — objects that speak of wealth, of trade, of a wide world of luxury goods flowing into the Low
 Countries. Each one is painted with crisp, almost microscopic precision: the soft barring of the hawk's
 feathers, the cool glaze of the porcelain, the iridescent spirals of the shells.

 Still life was, in part, a meditation on the pleasures and vanities of worldly things, and Peeters handles it
 with both sensuous delight and restrained control. That she signed and built a successful career at all, as
 a woman in that era, makes her a significant figure — and the Prado is proud to hold several of her
 works. Now, for the very last treasure of the tour — and it is literally a treasure, waiting at the end of the
 route.

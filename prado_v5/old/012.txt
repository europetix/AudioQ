 Velázquez, The Triumph of Bacchus (Los
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Find the painting of a pale, half-naked young god seated outdoors among a circle of grinning, sunburnt men,
    one kneeling to be crowned with vine leaves.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Out in the open air, a soft-skinned young Bacchus, naked to the waist and crowned with vine, sits on a wine
    barrel and leans to crown a kneeling soldier with ivy. Around him a ring of ruddy, weather-beaten men raise
    bowls of wine, laughing out at us. One lifts a brimming glass.
    LISTEN FOR
    Notice the two worlds — the pale, smooth god and the weathered, real faces beside him. Velázquez paints
    both as completely true.

 This is one of Velázquez's earliest pictures for the king, painted in his twenties, and you can feel the
 young man showing the court exactly what a painter from Seville could do. The subject is mythological
 — Bacchus, god of wine, holding court — but Velázquez tells it like a scene he stumbled on in a tavern
 field.

 There are really two paintings fused here. On the left, the god and his attendant are pale, smooth,
 idealised, lifted from Italian art. Turn to the men on the right and the temperature changes completely:
 real Spanish faces, sunburnt and gap-toothed and a little drunk, grinning straight out at you as if inviting
 you to pull up a stool. Velázquez gives the working men every bit as much dignity and presence as the
 deity — more, even. The god is a borrowed convention; the drinkers are observed life. That is the whole
 revolution of Velázquez in one canvas. His Seville years had been spent ennobling water-sellers and
 kitchen scenes, and here he drags that same democratic eye right into the heart of classical myth. The
 Spanish nickname tells you which half won the public's heart: not "The Triumph of Bacchus" but Los
 Borrachos — "The Drinkers."

 Look at the light, too — warm, raking, catching the rims of the bowls and the sweat on a brow. It is
 earthier and more solid than the silvery air he reaches later; this is Velázquez before Italy loosened his
 hand. Keep that in mind, because the next painting was made in Italy, and you will see the change at
 once. The Forge of Vulcan is right here.

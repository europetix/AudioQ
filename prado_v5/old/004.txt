TRACK 004 · 8–10                                    W-FOCUS                               254 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 El Greco, The Holy Trinity
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Turn to the tall vertical canvas of an aged God the Father holding the dead Christ across his knees, a white
    dove above, angels crowding in on every side.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    An aged, bearded God the Father in a deep blue-green robe supports the pale, lifeless body of Christ, just
    taken from the cross. A golden triangle of light and the white dove of the Holy Spirit hover overhead. A ring
    of grieving, brightly-robed angels presses close, filling the canvas edge to edge.
    LISTEN FOR
    This is El Greco's first great Spanish work — watch how the bodies stretch and the colours blaze. Italy is still
    in him here.

 When El Greco reached Toledo around 1577 he needed to prove himself fast, and this was part of the
 audition: one of a group of paintings for the church of Santo Domingo el Antiguo, his very first Spanish
 commission. Stand close and you can see a painter at a hinge in his life — one foot still in Italy, one
 stepping into the strangeness that will become entirely his own.

 The subject is unusual and tender. We expect the Trinity enthroned in glory; El Greco gives us grief
 instead. God the Father, heavy with age and sorrow, cradles the broken body of his son exactly as a
 mother would — the Trinity crossed with a Pietà, the Father mourning the way Mary mourns. Above,
 the dove completes the three persons; below, the dead weight of Christ pulls everything earthward.
 Look where Italy still lives in him. The muscular, sculptural Christ owes a clear debt to Michelangelo,
 whom he had studied in Rome; the saturated colour — that astonishing blue, the rose and gold of the
 angels — comes straight from his Venetian years under Titian. But look, too, at what is already his
 alone: the bodies beginning to elongate, the figures packed into a space too small to hold them, the
 emotional temperature turned all the way up.

 This is the painter El Greco was when Spain first met him — already extraordinary, not yet impossible.
 Now step to his last word on these same themes, painted nearly forty years later, right this way.

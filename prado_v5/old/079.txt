 The Dutch and Flemish cabinet
 This upper floor also serves as a kind of jewel-box — a series of smaller rooms holding choice Dutch
 and Flemish pictures, the more intimate northern works in the royal collection. After the grand
 altarpieces and the vast Goyas, these rooms reward close, unhurried looking.

 The Spanish kings were, of course, rulers of the Low Countries for much of this period, so northern art
 flowed naturally into their collection. Here you will find still lifes of astonishing delicacy, allegories
 crammed with detail, and — a real rarity — the single painting by Rembrandt that the Prado possesses.
 The Dutch Golden Age is not the Prado's strongest suit; the great Protestant republic of Holland was a
 political and religious rival, and its art entered the Spanish royal collection only sparingly. That very
 scarcity makes these pieces feel special.

 Look closely and slowly here. These are paintings about precision, observation, and the pleasures of the
 eye — fur and feather, glass and pearl, paint and light. Begin with the one and only Rembrandt, on the
 facing wall.

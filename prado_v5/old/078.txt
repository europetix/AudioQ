 Goya, The Parasol
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Walk on into Room 90, just along from here — the small, bright cartoon of a young woman seated on a bank,
    a little dog in her lap, while a young man behind shades her with a green parasol.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A pretty young woman in fashionable dress sits on a grassy bank, a small dog curled in her lap, looking out at
    us with a faint smile. Behind her, a young man leans in to hold a green parasol over her head, shading her
    from the sun. The colours are fresh and the mood flirtatious and light.
    LISTEN FOR
    Notice the green parasol casting soft shade on her face — a small, clever touch of light that shows off Goya's
    eye.

 This little painting is one of the most beloved of all Goya's tapestry cartoons, and a perfect distillation of
 his bright early style. It is pure Rococo charm — light, pretty, flirtatious, and beautifully observed. A
 fashionable young woman sits on a bank in the open air, a tiny dog dozing in her lap, gazing out with a
 soft, knowing smile. Behind her, a young man — her admirer, or perhaps a servant — leans in to hold a
 green parasol over her, shading her face from the bright sun. The whole thing is a small scene of leisure
 and gentle courtship, the kind of pleasant, decorative subject the court wanted for its walls. But look at
 the touch of real artistry: the green parasol casts a cool, tinted shadow across the young woman's face, a
 subtle, sophisticated observation of coloured light that lifts the picture above mere prettiness. The
 composition is gracefully balanced, the colours clear and fresh. It is Goya enjoying himself —
 charming, accomplished, and entirely happy. Hold this image of the young, sunlit Goya in mind; it is the
 brightest note in his whole story. Now we leave Goya for a cabinet of northern treasures, beginning with
 the Prado's only Rembrandt — a short walk across this floor, to Room 76.

 Sorolla, Boys on the Beach
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Move to the painting of three naked boys lying at the water's edge on a sunlit beach, seen from above, the wet
    sand shimmering around them.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Three young boys lie at the edge of the sea on a bright Mediterranean beach, their wet, sun-browned bodies
    stretched on the shallow water and glistening sand. Seen from a high angle, they are bathed in brilliant
    sunlight; the water shimmers with reflected violets, golds, and blues. Pure light and warmth fill the canvas.
    LISTEN FOR
    Look at the water on their skin — Sorolla makes sunlight itself the subject. You can almost feel the
    Mediterranean heat.

 We end the Spanish nineteenth century in blazing sunshine. Joaquín Sorolla was the great painter of
 Mediterranean light — sometimes called the Spanish master of luminismo, the painting of pure sunlight
 — and this beloved canvas shows him at his most joyful.

 The subject could hardly be simpler: three boys lying at the edge of the sea on a beach in his native
 Valencia, sunbathing in the shallows. But Sorolla turns it into a hymn to light, water, and youthful
 well-being. Seen from above, the boys' wet, tanned bodies glisten; the shallow water around them
 shimmers with astonishing colour — violets and golds and blues caught in the reflections. The
 brushwork is broad, confident, and brilliant, the whole canvas seemingly drenched in warm
 Mediterranean sun.

 There is no story, no message — just the pure pleasure of light on skin and water, captured by a painter
 who loved the sea and the sunshine of his homeland above all things. After Goya's darkness and the
 century's grand tragedies, Sorolla feels like stepping out into the open air and warmth. He brings the
 Prado's story of Spanish painting to a bright, modern close. One last stop on this floor — an ancient
 treasure in marble, the San Ildefonso Group, deeper into the gallery.

 The Italian Renaissance arrives
 Now to Italy, and one of the great turning points in the history of art. In these rooms the Middle Ages
 give way to the Renaissance — that great rebirth, beginning in fifteenth-century Florence and Venice,
 when painters rediscovered the natural world, the human body, perspective, and the art of antiquity.
 Watch the change happen across these walls. The gold backgrounds begin to recede, replaced by real
 space, real light, real landscapes seen through windows. Figures gain weight and grace; faces fill with
 individual life. The flat, eternal world of the medieval altarpiece opens up into something that breathes.
 The Spanish kings, for all their love of Titian and the Venetians, also gathered key works of the earlier
 Italian Renaissance, and the Prado's holdings here are small but choice — a handful of genuine
 masterpieces by some of the founding names. You will meet a devout Dominican friar, a master of icy
 precision, and the great storyteller Botticelli. Begin with the most beloved of them, the painter-monk
 Fra Angelico, a few steps on.

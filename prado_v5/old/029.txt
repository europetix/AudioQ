 Titian, The Bacchanal of the Andrians
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Move to the crowded outdoor scene of men and women drinking, sprawling and dancing on a grassy slope,
    with a sleeping nude in the lower right corner.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    On the island of Andros, a tangle of figures revels by a stream that runs with wine. People drink from glass
    jugs, sing from a sheet of music, dance and recline on the grass. In the lower right a beautiful nude woman
    sleeps, abandoned. Trees and a luminous sky rise behind.
    LISTEN FOR
    Find the music score lying in the grass — it's a real song about wine. Then the sleeping nude: total, unguarded
    relaxation.

 If Charles V showed Titian's gravity, this shows his other face entirely: a riot of pagan pleasure that has
 intoxicated painters for five hundred years. It depicts a legend of the island of Andros, where a stream
 ran not with water but with wine, and the islanders spend their days in blissful drunken celebration.
 Titian fills the canvas with warmth and movement — bodies twisting, drinking, dancing, sprawling in
 the grass under a glowing sky. There is even a real piece of music painted into the foreground, a French
 song whose words translate roughly as "he who drinks and does not drink again knows not what
 drinking is." And in the lower corner sleeps a magnificent nude, completely relaxed, abandoned to wine
 and sun — one of the most influential figures in all of art, copied by Rubens, by Poussin, echoed for
 centuries.

 This was painted for a private "camerino," a study of pleasure-pictures for an Italian duke, and it is
 Titian celebrating the body, colour, and joy without apology. The Spanish kings kept it close. Velázquez
 studied it on his trips to Italy and clearly remembered it. Now, Titian at his most daring and erotic — a
 goddess and a shower of gold, right in front of you.

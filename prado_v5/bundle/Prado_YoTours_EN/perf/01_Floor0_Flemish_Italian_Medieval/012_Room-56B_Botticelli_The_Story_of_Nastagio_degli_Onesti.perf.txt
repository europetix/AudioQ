[curious] These paintings were a wedding present. A naked woman, hunted through a forest by a knight and his dogs. Every Friday. For years.

The story comes from Boccaccio's Decameron. Nastagio, a young man from Ravenna, loves a daughter of the noble Traversari family. She will have nothing to do with him. Heartbroken, he leaves the city. In 1483, Sandro Botticelli and his workshop told the story in four panels. Three are here, and the fourth is in a private collection.

[pause] Start with the panel on the left. In a pine forest by the sea, Nastagio sees a woman running, savaged by mastiffs and chased by a horseman. The rider explains. He loved her, she scorned him, and he died for it. When she died too, she was condemned to this. Every Friday he hunts her down, for as many years as the months she ignored him.

In the second panel, the knight tears out her heart and throws it to the hounds. Look into the background. The chase is starting all over again.

[wry] Now the third. Nastagio has a plan. He invites the Traversari family to a banquet in that same pine wood. Just as dessert is served, the ghostly hunt appears, exactly as he predicted. The guests are horrified. And the young lady decides, on reflection, that she would rather marry Nastagio than end up like that. On the right, her maid comes to him with a less proper offer. He turns it down. He wants an honourable marriage.

These panels were commissioned by Antonio Pucci for the wedding of his son to Lucrezia Bini. Lorenzo de' Medici helped arrange the match, and on the third panel the Medici arms hang beside those of the two families. The fourth panel, the one that got away, shows the wedding feast.

[lightly] A lovely gift. With a very clear message for the bride.

[amused] If the Garden of Earthly Delights is a riddle, the Haywain is a sermon. Bosch builds it on an old Flemish proverb. The world is a haywain, and each man takes what he can.

Look at the centre panel. A huge cart of hay rolls forward, and people of every class, clergy included, claw at it and fight over it. Behind the cart ride the powerful: a pope, an emperor, a king with French lilies, a duke. On top, a couple enjoy themselves to music, while a guardian angel looks up to Christ in despair. And who is pulling the cart? Devils, steering everyone towards hell on the right wing.

[pause] Hay is worth nothing, and the whole world chases it anyway.

Philip the Second bought a version of this painting in 1570, believing it was an original. Technical analysis later showed it was a copy of this one.

[conspiratorial] Now find the wide panel with the great eye. Philip owned it before 1560 and kept it in his private apartment. At the centre of the eye, in the pupil, Christ rises from his tomb. Beneath him are the Latin words: beware, beware, the Lord is watching. Around the iris run the seven deadly sins, acted out by ordinary people. A woman admires herself in a mirror held by a devil. A family overeats at the table. Two dogs fight over a bone while a couple envy a rich man's falcon. In the four corners wait death, judgement, hell and heaven. Imagine living with that eye on you every day.

[lightly] Before you leave, find the small panel with gold lettering. A quack in a funnel hat, a sign of deceit, cuts into a man's head to remove the stone of madness. The patient's name is written below: Lubbert Das. And what comes out isn't a stone at all. It's a flower, probably standing for the money the quack is about to take.

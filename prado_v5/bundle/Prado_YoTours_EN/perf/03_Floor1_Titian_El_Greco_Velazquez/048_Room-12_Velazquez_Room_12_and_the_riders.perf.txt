[curious] One palace hall once held five of these riders. Around 1635, the king's new Hall of Realms was hung with his family on horseback, and Velázquez was put in charge.

He painted the king and the little prince himself. Philip the Fourth rides in calm profile on a rearing horse, the hills outside Madrid behind him. In the corner lies a sheet of paper, blank, just like the one in Breda. His queen, Elisabeth of France, rides a white horse and looks straight at you. His parents are here too, Philip the Third and Margaret of Austria. On her chest hangs a famous pearl, La Peregrina. In these three, Velázquez's own hand shows most clearly in the horses.

[warmly] Prince Baltasar Carlos, about six, charges toward you on a round-bellied pony. The picture hung above a door, so the pony is foreshortened for eyes looking up. Behind him, late snow still lies on the Guadarrama peaks.

The Count-Duke of Olivares, the king's all-powerful favourite, wasn't part of that hall. He had his own portrait painted, for himself. [wry] He twists in the saddle and stares you down. The king never needed to.

Then the king off duty, in hunting dress with his dog and gun. Look at his cap. It was added later, and the changes still show through the paint. And finally a small bust of Philip in middle age, in black and grey. He disliked watching himself grow old, yet Velázquez made this one of his greatest portraits.

Stay in Room 12. The biggest crowd here is gathered around Las Meninas.

This port never existed. Claude Lorrain built it in his imagination, as the ancient harbour of Ostia, near Rome. Then he quietly furnished it with real buildings. Among the palaces is the Villa Medici in Rome, and far out on the horizon stands the lighthouse of Genoa.

Claude was working in Rome. Around 1639 he painted this as one of four big canvases for a new royal palace in Madrid, the Buen Retiro. All four belong to the Prado. Each set a sacred story inside a landscape.

The story here is Paula's. She was a Roman widow who gave up her worldly life for prayer and penitence, and sailed east to join Saint Jerome. So where is she? Look among the crowd in the foreground. A whole party has come down to the quay to see her off. She is there among them, small and easy to miss. If you're not sure, the painter left a label. Somewhere on the canvas, an inscription spells it out: the embarkation of Saint Paula of Rome, for the Holy Land.

But be honest: your eye didn't go to Paula first, it went to the sun. Claude puts it low over the sea, right at the far end of the harbour, and lets its golden light pour towards you between the buildings. The museum says the light partially blinds the viewer. That's exactly the effect. You squint a little, the way you would on a real quayside.

Notice how the buildings and ships frame that light like the wings of a stage. Everything leads your eye out to sea, along the route Paula is about to take.

A saint's farewell, turned into a blaze of light.

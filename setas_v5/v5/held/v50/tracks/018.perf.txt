[curious] Within weeks of this square opening, it was full of protesters, and some of them camped here. In a way, that was the building passing its first test.

This is the Plaza Mayor, the raised square. It sits on the roof of the market, under the parasols, open to the sky and used for events. It's one of the four layers of the design. Its architect, Jürgen Mayer H., hoped the Plaza de la Encarnación would become, in his words, "the new contemporary urban centre".

[pause] In the spring of 2011, Seville's indignados, the protest movement that swept Spain that year, made this square their meeting place. An academic study of those demonstrations argues that the architecture changed how people could use a public space. A year later, the square was a finalist for the European Prize for Urban Public Space.

[warmly] Mayer himself called it "an urban, democratic, open cathedral that is held together by the people and the life in the center of the city." Look around you. The people were always the point.

Below you, the market carries on. And when Aurora is running, the timber above you lights up after sunset.

To leave, follow the signs down to the street, by the stairs or the escalators. From there you can visit the market, too.

[quietly] You started below the street, beside the remains of Roman and Moorish Seville. You climbed to the highest point of the parasols, and you've finished here in the middle, where the city meets.

[warmly] Thank you for walking the Setas de Sevilla with us. We hope the view stays with you.

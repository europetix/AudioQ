The "kissing birds" mosaic, the symbol of the Antiquarium, "reconstructed on the wall of the museum" (ACOM), "at the centre of a large mosaic" (ACOM), "pieced together … displayed on the wall" (ACOM-B). KRAMER describes, in the Hospitium de los Triclinia, "a perfectly executed geometric composition with two birds on a crater in a small square panel as the central motif", with two borders creating an illusory water movement and smaller zig-zag panels forming a wave effect. That the emblem and Kramer's panel are the same mosaic is an inference (audit §2 #1): spoken as "may well be", checked by the label. Why such scenes: KRAMER, mosaics alluding to the plants and animals of nature were common in Roman houses, especially in areas for passage; their meaning "always eludes us". No species, no century, no "kissing" as fact.

[curious] Of all the gods and monsters found under this square, the museum chose two small birds as its emblem.

Somewhere along the way, look for a mosaic hung upright on a wall: two birds facing each other over a wine bowl. It began as part of a floor. The pieces were put back together and hung on the wall, so you see it standing up instead of underfoot.

[amused] Some people call them the kissing birds. Kissing, or just sharing a drink? You decide.

The scholar who reviewed these mosaics describes a panel very like it. Two birds on a crater, the big bowl Romans mixed their wine in. It sits as a small square at the centre of a geometric design. Around it, two borders are laid to look like moving water, and smaller zigzag panels add to the ripple. [pause] It may well be this one. Either way, look past the birds to the borders, and see if the water seems to move.

Why birds on a floor at all? Romans liked mosaics that brought nature indoors, plants and animals, especially in the parts of a house people walked through. What one image meant to the family who lived with it, the scholar admits, always eludes us.

[warmly] Out of all of it, the museum picked the gentlest image to stand for the whole.

Walk on, and look for the label for the casa almohade, the Almohad house. Play the next stop in the Antiquarium there: The Almohad house. If you passed it earlier, play that stop now, before you leave the Antiquarium.

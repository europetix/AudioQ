[curious] Summer in Seville can be fierce. Yet the operator says that even on the hottest days, this square stays noticeably cooler.

This is the Plaza Mayor, the raised square. It covers almost four thousand square metres, and it stands about five metres above the street, on the roof of the market. The operator calls it the largest shaded pedestrian square in Seville. It was designed for big crowds: concerts, sport, fairs and the performing arts.

[pause] Now look up. That's where the shade comes from: the underside of the parasols. It isn't a solid roof. It's a grid of timber panels crossing at right angles, about a metre and a half apart. That's how this square can be shady and still open to the sky.

Follow the timber down, and you come to the trunks that hold it all up. Two of them are concrete, not wood, and the lifts run up inside them.

[wry] One of the first big crowds here didn't come for a concert. In the spring of 2011, only weeks after this square opened, Seville's indignados made it their meeting place. They belonged to the protest movement that swept Spain that year, and some of them camped right here, under the timber.

An academic study of those demonstrations argues that the architecture changed how people could use a public space. Built for fairs and concerts, the square held a protest camp in its very first season.

[warmly] So a square designed for crowds found one almost at once. Just not the kind anyone had put on the calendar.

Stay on the Plaza Mayor, and find a quiet spot away from the way down. The market is right under your feet, and the next stop is its story: The market that waited 37 years.

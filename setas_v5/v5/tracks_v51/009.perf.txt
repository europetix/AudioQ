[warmly] In 2004 Seville put a question to architects worldwide. What should it do with a square that had been a car park, then a dig, for some thirty years? Sixty-five entries came in.

The brief asked for a public space for the city, and for a design that would conserve the Roman remains and put them on show. [pause] So the ruins were no surprise. They were in the brief from the start.

The competition ran in two rounds, and ten projects reached the second. That June, the winner was made public: Jürgen Mayer H., from Berlin, still in his thirties. His project, Metropol Parasol, was a huge shade over the square, standing on a handful of trunks, with a walkway across the top.

[amused] He didn't set out to build mushrooms. But Sevillanos called it las Setas anyway. The nickname stuck, and even the architect came round to it.

So where did the shapes come from? Mayer has said he took cues from the cathedral's vaulting and from the great shade trees of a neighbouring square. The city names them as the giant fig trees of the Plaza del Cristo de Burgos. The operator calls them centennial, and the city cares for them as singular trees. Trees and vaults, then, not mushrooms.

[pause] As you step out, the timber is all around you. Engineered with the firm Arup, it's laminated veneer lumber made in Finland, a type called Kerto-Q: thin sheets of wood glued into big panels. Some 3,400 pieces were milled in Bavaria and trucked to Seville. And every beam wears a waterproof skin of polyurethane a few millimetres thick.

[curious] But it isn't all wood. The lift you just rode runs inside a concrete trunk.

Long billed as the largest wooden structure in the world, it is certainly one of the largest. Its engineers say "one of the largest timber structures ever built".

[warmly] A competition, a nickname nobody planned, and thousands of pieces of Finnish wood. That's what's all around you now.

Walk on along the walkway until the Giralda, the cathedral's tall tower, is in clear view above the rooftops. The next stop is The Cathedral and the Giralda.

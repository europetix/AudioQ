[warmly] Somewhere under this square lies a Roman house with a mosaic of Medusa. Close to thirty metres above it, people stroll across a giant timber canopy. This guide connects the two.

Officially it's Metropol Parasol. Sevillanos took one look and called it las Setas, the mushrooms. It was designed by the Berlin architect Jürgen Mayer H., and it opened on 27 March 2011.

[pause] It's one building in four layers. Roman and Moorish remains lie under the street. The market is at street level. Above it is a raised public square, the Plaza Mayor. On top, a walkway winds across the parasols. You'll start at the bottom, go to the very top, and finish in the middle.

First comes level minus one, with the taquilla, the box office, and the Antiquarium, the museum of the remains. [curious] Even before you go in, you may glimpse some of the ruins through the glass. Then lifts inside the concrete trunks take you up to the walkways and the Mirador 360º, the viewing platform on top. The way down passes an exhibition and ends on the Plaza Mayor.

The full tour, with the Antiquarium, takes about an hour and ten minutes. The essential version takes about forty minutes, and the highlights about twenty-five, with the walking and the lifts, but not the Feeling Sevilla film. Whichever version you have, play the stops in order. The shorter ones skip some, so the track numbers on your phone may jump. Every stop tells you where to go next.

[lightly] The Antiquarium is a separate museum, with its own entrance and its own admission, and only the full tour goes there. If you're on the full tour and it's closed, go to the lifts beside its entrance and play A plaza with many lives.

Some walkway ramps are steep, and the Mirador is reached only by steps. From the north entrance, the way down leads to the same underground level.

Now go down to level minus one, following the signs for the taquilla. From Calle Imagen, it's the ramp beside the escalators. The next stop, Found under a car park, plays at the door of the Antiquarium.

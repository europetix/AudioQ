[curious] The timber around you is wired for light. After sunset, a system built into the parasols can turn the Setas into a slow show of light and sound. It's called Aurora.

It was added in 2021, for the building's tenth anniversary, and the architects call it a tribute to the light of Seville. The concept and the software came from a design studio called Skandal Technologien.

What makes it unusual is that it listens. The operator says it responds to how many people are up here and how they move, to the weather and the wind, and to the time of year. Special programmes mark days like Semana Santa, Holy Week, and the Feria.

[lightly] It's restrained, too. According to one trade report, usually under thirty percent of the lights are on at any moment. The aim is a sense of movement, with less light spilling into the night sky.

If you're here by day, picture it. If you're here after dark and Aurora is running, you're standing inside it.

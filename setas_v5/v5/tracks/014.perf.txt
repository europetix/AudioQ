[curious] When the Setas opened, in March 2011, some Sevillanos came out to protest. They argued that its cost had swallowed money their own neighbourhoods needed.

For years it was the most argued-about building in Seville. People objected to the money, and to the look of it in the middle of the old town. Its critics said the Setas suited the old town "like two pistols on a Christ", a Spanish way of saying wildly out of place.

[wry] The British critic Rowan Moore was dry about it: "Oh my God, it's an icon. How very last decade." The mayor who opened it, Alfredo Sánchez Monteseirín, saw something else entirely: "a modern urban cathedral".

[pause] Then there was the delay, and the bill. Meant to open in 2007, it took four more years, years that ran straight into Spain's financial crisis. The city told Spain's Ombudsman that the cost had roughly doubled. The budget was about 51 million euros. The final bill came to about 102 million. Changes to the timber structure, the archaeology and disputes with the builder all played a part.

And yet. It won a Red Dot "Best of the Best" design award. It was a finalist for the European Union's Mies van der Rohe Award for architecture. And by one count, over a million people went up to the walkway in its first year.

[quietly] You're standing on it now, so you get a vote too. Icon, folly, or a bit of *both*?

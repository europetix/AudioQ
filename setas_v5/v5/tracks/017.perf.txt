[curious] On a Sunday in the spring of 2011, Seville's mayor opened a building the city had been arguing about for seven years.

The exhibition around you, the operator says, shows how the Plaza de la Encarnación changed, up to today.

Building began in the mid-2000s, on a square that had already been a convent, a market and a car park. On the twenty-seventh of March 2011, the mayor opened it, and the museum of Roman remains below opened the same evening. The rest followed in stages through that spring.

[pause] José de la Peña, project director for the engineers, Arup, saw the arguments coming. Buildings like this, he said, "always create a great impact, and are always debated". He pointed to the Eiffel Tower, which Paris had once argued about too. Seville, he said, could be proud of a new symbol of modernity and technology.

Sacyr, the company that built it, helped finance it, and runs it under a long concession from the city. Ten years after the opening, it renewed the building, adding the Aurora lights and the Feeling Sevilla room.

In 2026, the Setas turns fifteen.

[wry] Building site, quarrel, symbol. The Eiffel Tower took that road too.

At the end of the way down, you come out on the Plaza Mayor, the big raised square under the parasols. Find a spot out of the way, where you can see the timber overhead, and play the next stop there: Under the timber.

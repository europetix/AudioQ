[curious] Seville was an important Roman city, yet it has kept surprisingly few Roman mosaics. The best group it has is down here.

The Romans called the city Hispalis. Under this square stood a quarter of its houses, and they were never finished in one go. The archaeologists traced phase after phase of building, over five or six centuries, starting in the first century.

One scholarly review of the mosaics puts it plainly. In a city where few have ever been found, these are unmatched for their quality and their variety.

The house to find here is the Casa de la Columna, the House of the Column. Its mosaic shows Medusa, the snake-haired Gorgon of Greek myth. [pause] In the old stories, a single look from her turned people to stone, until the hero Perseus cut off her head. And here she is, under a Seville street.

The other houses have names too. There is the Casa Romana, the Roman House, and the Casa de las Basas, the House of the Column Bases. There is the Casa del Sigma, and the Casa de la Noria, the House of the Water Wheel. They are modern names, not Roman ones.

Read the labels as you go. Some pieces here are originals, some are replicas, and some mosaics may appear only in photographs taken during the dig. The labels tell you which is which.

[warmly] For a city that kept so little of its Roman floors, this is where Hispalis still shows its face.

Walk on and look for the label for the Casa de Baco, the House of Bacchus. The next stop in the Antiquarium is Bacchus, the Nymph and the Ocean.

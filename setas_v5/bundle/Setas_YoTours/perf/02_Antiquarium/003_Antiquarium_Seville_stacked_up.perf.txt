[curious] The first proper look under this square was paid for by the market traders.

In the autumn of 1991, the traders' cooperative of the Encarnación market commissioned a test dig on the site of their old market hall. What the archaeologists found was Seville stacked like a layer cake. [pause] Near the top, the remains of the convent that once stood here. About three metres down, Moorish houses. Then Roman Seville, seven to nine metres below the street.

Getting down there was the hard part. As soon as they dug deep, they hit groundwater. They had to shrink the dig to a pit about three metres across and shore up its sides, just to reach the Roman levels.

[wry] It was a tiny, waterlogged window. But it showed that everything was there, one age on top of another.

Years of bigger excavations followed. And when the Setas came to be designed, the building had to make room for all of this. Its architect, Jürgen Mayer H., has described it as "a structure that sits on the Roman ruins".

Wherever you stand in here, you are already below the street, inside those layers. Somewhere above your head are the market, the raised square and the parasols. [quietly] The whole history of the city, in one cut through the ground.

Follow the walkway on into the Roman houses, and look for the label for the Casa de la Columna, the House of the Column. The next stop in the Antiquarium is The House of the Column and its Medusa.

[warmly] The architect, Jürgen Mayer H., said that standing up here is like being on a cloud above the city. You've reached the highest point of the Setas.

How high is that? Sources disagree by a few metres, so let's say close to thirty metres, about the height of a nine-storey building. Below you, six parasols spread out over a hundred and fifty metres, and the walkway snakes across their tops. This platform crowns it.

Three directions are worth fixing, even if you found some of them on the walkway. Find the Giralda, the cathedral's tall bell tower, with the great bulk of the cathedral beside it. That way is south, less than a kilometre off.

Now put the Giralda on your left. You're facing west, toward the river, the Guadalquivir, and the rooftops of Triana, the old neighbourhood across the water. Beyond them rises the Aljarafe, the higher ground on that side of the river.

Last, put the Giralda at your back. You're looking north now, toward the Macarena district, about a kilometre away.

[lightly] The rest of the skyline is yours to explore, at your own pace. Take a slow turn around the rail before you go.

Then think of what's stacked beneath your feet. Below the street lie the remains of Roman and Moorish Seville. At street level, the market. Above it, the Plaza Mayor, the raised square. And above that, the timber, rising all the way to this platform.

[pause] [quietly] From below the street to the highest point, one building holds two thousand years of the same square. For a moment, you're on that cloud.

When you're ready, go back down the steps and walk on along the walkway toward the way down. Play the next stop as you go: Love it or hate it.

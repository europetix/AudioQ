Somewhere under this square lies a Roman house with a mosaic of Medusa. Close to thirty metres above it, people are strolling across a giant timber canopy. This guide takes you from one to the other.

Officially it's Metropol Parasol. Sevillanos took one look and called it las Setas, the mushrooms. It was designed by the Berlin architect Jürgen Mayer H., and it opened on 27 March 2011.

It's one building in four layers. Roman and Moorish remains lie under the street. The market is at street level. Above it is a raised public square, the Plaza Mayor. On top, a walkway winds across the parasols. You'll start at the bottom, go to the very top, and finish in the middle.

First comes level minus one, with the taquilla, the box office, and the Antiquarium, the museum of the remains. Then the lifts inside the concrete trunks take you up to the walkways and the Mirador 360º, the viewing platform at the highest point. The way down passes an exhibition and ends on the Plaza Mayor.

The full tour, with the Antiquarium, takes just under an hour. The essential version takes about thirty-five minutes and the highlights about twenty-five, with the walking and the lifts, but not the Feeling Sevilla film. Whichever is on your phone, play it in order: the numbers may jump, and every stop tells you where to go next.

The Antiquarium is a separate museum, with its own entrance and its own admission, and only the full tour goes there. If it's closed when you're here, go to the lifts beside its entrance and play A plaza with many lives.

Lifts take you up, but some walkway ramps are steep, and the Mirador at the very top is reached only by steps. If you came in from the north side, you'll reach the same underground level.

Now go down to level minus one, following the signs for the taquilla. From Calle Imagen, it's the ramp beside the escalators. The next stop, Found under a car park, plays at the door of the Antiquarium, beside the taquilla.

The screen in this room is 21 metres long. And the room itself sits about 21 metres above the ground.

This is the Sala Feeling, the film room, home to Feeling Sevilla. The operator, Sacyr, gives it more than two hundred square metres. One trade report says its stands hold ninety-six people. The curved LED screen, three metres high, runs that whole 21 metres. Add sound from every side, images on the floor, moving air and even scents. It's a room built to work on all your senses. The film lasts about a quarter of an hour.

It arrived in 2021, ten years after the opening, together with Aurora, the light show. Sacyr calls it part of a complete renewal of the building.

But a floor up here was always part of the plan. The architects' design put a level inside the parasols, about 21 metres up. And the timber makers record that the platform built at this height is steel and concrete, not wood. Not everything in the Setas is what it seems.

After the film, or if you skip it, follow the way down, past the exhibition. Play the next stop as you go: From building site to city symbol.

On the way up, you heard about the old fig trees that the city links to the parasols. You can stand under them today.

They grow in the Plaza del Cristo de Burgos. Look up into the branches, and see whether the parasols look familiar.

They need looking after now, and the city has been giving them specialist care to keep them stable and safe.

Roman Seville also survives above ground nearby, in the Alameda de Hércules, the old promenade. There stand two Roman columns, from the age of the emperors Trajan and Hadrian, and possibly from a temple. They were set up in the 1570s, on the orders of the Count of Barajas. On top are two statues: Hercules, the city's mythical founder, and Julius Caesar.

Under the Setas, Roman Seville was dug out of the ground. At the Alameda, the city stood a piece of it back up, more than four centuries ago.

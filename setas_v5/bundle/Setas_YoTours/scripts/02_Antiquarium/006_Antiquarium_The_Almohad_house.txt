Down here, a thousand years can be just a few metres of earth.

This is the Almohad house. It comes from the Almohad era, the 1100s and 1200s, when Seville was a capital of Muslim Spain. The excavators found a Moorish house here, and traces of the Almohad neighbourhood around it.

When the first test dig went down, the archaeologists met this Moorish level about three metres below the street. The Roman floors lay about twice as deep. So this house stood high above the Roman city, on ground that the centuries had slowly built up over it. If you've just come from the Roman houses, you've walked through a thousand years in a few minutes.

Those were great decades for Seville. In the same age, the Almohad caliph Abu Ya'qub Yusuf built the city's Great Mosque, and in 1184 work began on its minaret, the tower we now call the Giralda. You'll see it from the top.

Later still, a convent rose over this ground. Its story, and the story of the square above you, comes by the lifts.

That's the end of the Antiquarium. Follow the exit signs, then go to the lifts beside its entrance, the Mirador access. The next stop plays by the lifts, before you go up: A plaza with many lives.

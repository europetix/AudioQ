Down here, a thousand years can be just a few metres of earth.

At the label for the casa almohade, you reach Moorish Seville. The house comes from the Almohad era, the 1100s and 1200s, when Seville was a capital of Muslim Spain. The excavators found a Moorish house on this site, and traces of the Almohad neighbourhood around it.

When the first test dig went down, the archaeologists met this Moorish level about three metres below the street. The Roman floors lay about twice as deep. So the house stood high above the Roman city, on ground that the centuries had slowly built up over it. If you've just come from the Roman houses, you've walked through a thousand years in a few minutes.

Those were great decades for Seville. The Almohad caliph Abu Ya'qub Yusuf built the city's Great Mosque. In 1184, work began on its minaret. Today we call it the Giralda. Down by the river, the Torre del Oro, the Tower of Gold, is Almohad work too. An Almohad governor had it built around 1220.

Those towers stood in the open air, and they stand there still. You'll see the Giralda from the top. The house had a different fate. Later, a convent rose over this ground, and later still a market. The house survived because it was buried.

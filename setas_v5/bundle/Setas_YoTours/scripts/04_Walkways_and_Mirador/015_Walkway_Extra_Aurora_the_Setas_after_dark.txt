The timber around you is wired for light. After sunset, a system built into the parasols can turn the Setas into a slow show of light and sound. It's called Aurora.

Aurora was added in 2021, for the building's tenth anniversary, as a tribute to the light of Seville. It came from the architects' own office, with a studio called Skandal Technologien making the concept and the software.

What makes it unusual is that it listens. The operator says it responds to how many people are up here and how they move. It also follows the weather, the wind and the season.

It has a repertoire, too. One trade report counts fourteen light patterns, and twelve of them are for special dates. The architects mention days like Semana Santa, Holy Week, and the Feria, Seville's spring fair.

It's restrained. The same report says usually under thirty percent of the lights are on at once. That gives a sense of movement, with less light spilling into the sky.

If you're here by day, picture it. After dark, when Aurora is running, you're standing inside it.

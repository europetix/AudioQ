The architect, Jürgen Mayer H., said that standing up here is like being on a cloud above the city. You've reached the highest point of the Setas.

How high is that? Sources disagree by a few metres, so let's say close to 30 metres, about the height of a nine-storey building. The whole structure is about 150 metres long and 70 wide, spread over six parasols. A walkway about 250 metres long snakes across the top, and this platform crowns it.

Two landmarks are worth fixing first. Find the Giralda, the cathedral's tall bell tower, with the great bulk of the cathedral beside it. That way is south, less than a kilometre off. Now put the Giralda on your left. You're facing west, toward the river, the Guadalquivir, and beyond it the Aljarafe, the high ground west of the city.

The rest of the skyline is yours to explore, at your own pace. Take a slow turn around the rail before you go.

Then think of what's stacked beneath your feet. Below the street lie the remains of Roman and Moorish Seville. At street level, the market. Above it, the Plaza Mayor, the raised square. And above that, the timber, rising all the way to this platform.

From below the street to the highest point, one building holds two thousand years of the same square. For a moment, you're on that cloud.

When you're ready, go back down the steps and walk on along the walkway toward the way down. Play the next stop as you go: Love it or hate it.

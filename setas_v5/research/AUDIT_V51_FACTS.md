Web re-checks: 27 fetches/searches, WebSearch and WebFetch only, one page at a time per site, in sequence. Official and academic pages came first, and Wikipedia was not used. URLs are given with each finding and in the list at the end.

**Totals: 0 MUST · 11 SHOULD · 12 NICE.** I found no FALSE or UNVERIFIED row from the old-guide audit (§3–§4), and no PLAN.md NOT item, that has come back. ## Findings

### 004 Medusa, the guardian

**004.1 SHOULD, dated number stated as present.** "Only about a dozen are on show."
- Problem: Kramer (2018) reports López Monteagudo's 2015 count: "there were 13 mosaics, in situ on the floor, or exposed as vertical panels", and "eight pieces were waiting to be arranged". So the number on show today may be higher. The audit's own note says it was "the count at publication". O4 has not counted them.
- Source checked: https://acikerisim.uludag.edu.tr/bitstreams/5d489fdf-1c78-4a17-b073-8b9e36726fc6/download
- Replace with: "When the specialist study came out, only about a dozen were on show." (Or keep the present tense only if O4 counts about a dozen.)

**004.2 SHOULD, attribution (also affects 005 and 006).**
- Quoted wording in the tracks:
  - 004: "One scholarly review puts it plainly…" / "The scholar who studied these mosaics calls this one a warning and protective Medusa." / "The same scholar notes that she looks unhappy."
  - 005: "One scholar of these floors says…"
  - 006: "The scholar who reviewed these mosaics describes a panel very like it."
- Problem: there is one source here, Maja Kramer's *review* of Guadalupe López Monteagudo's book *Los mosaicos de la Plaza de la Encarnación* (ICAS 2015). The English words ("warning and protective", "unhappy facial expression") are Kramer's summary. The study is López Monteagudo's; Kramer says the author links the expression to Apollodorus. 004 makes it sound like two different people ("review", then "the scholar who studied"). 006 says Kramer "reviewed these mosaics", but she reviewed the book.
- Replace with one consistent attribution, set up once in 004: "The specialist study of these mosaics, by Guadalupe López Monteagudo, calls her a warning and protective Medusa…" and later "The same study notes that she looks unhappy." In 005 and 006 use "The study of these floors…". A second academic review confirms the book covers the Medusa, the Triumph of Bacchus, the asarotos oikos and "la crátera en la que se posan dos aves" (San Nicolás Pedraz, *Espacio, Tiempo y Forma* II 30, 2017): https://revistas.uned.es/index.php/ETFII/article/download/19847/17101/40711

### 005 Bacchus and the dinner party

**005.1 SHOULD, overstated source.** "One scholar of these floors says it was the favourite Bacchus scene in Roman southern Spain for a long time."
- Problem: Kramer has two separate statements. The first: "A version of Bacchus triumph with the presence of Ariadne is the main motif in the triclinium in Casa de Baco." Then, in the next paragraph: "the version with Bacchus triumph with Ariadne **in the carriage** was the most common motive in Baetica for a long time." So the review does not say the Casa de Baco floor *is* the carriage version. And "most common" is not "favourite".
- Source checked: Kramer PDF (above), re-fetched for context.
- Replace with: "It was a popular theme. One version of it, with Ariadne riding in Bacchus's chariot, was for a long time the most common scene of its kind in Roman southern Spain."

**005.2 SHOULD, two sources fused.** "When you find the Bacchus floor, look at its corners for the drinking vessels."
- Problem: the corner vessels come from ACOM ("the elaborate drinking vessel at the corners of the mosaic floor of Casa de Baco"). The triumph is Kramer's "main motif in the triclinium". Nothing says they are the same floor, or that the triumph floor is on show (O4). "The Bacchus floor" leads the listener to look for the triumph.
- Source checked: https://andalucia.com/cities/seville/museum-antiquarium.htm
- Replace with: "When you find the mosaic floor of the Casa de Baco, look at its corners for the drinking vessels."

**005.3 SHOULD, implies the mosaic is on show.** "[quietly] The guests are long gone. Their dinner party is still on the floor."
- Problem: this payoff tells the listener the unswept-floor mosaic of the Hospitium de los Delfines is lying on the floor here. Whether it is on show at all, and whether in situ or as a panel, is an O4 question (notes, 005 O4 CRITICAL). PLAN_V5_1 also says "never a position".
- Replace with: "The guests are long gone. Their dinner party was set down in stone."

**005.4 NICE, position-sounding word.** "Here, the main mosaic of that dining room showed the triumph…"
- Replace with: "In this house, the main mosaic of the dining room showed…"

**005.5 NICE, date too narrow.** "in late Roman times it was often written like a C"
- Problem: the lunate (C-shaped) sigma was common from the Hellenistic period through Roman times. The couch is already called a *sigma* in Martial (1st century AD). General knowledge, TRUE\*.
- Replace with: "in Roman times it was often written like a C".

**005.6 NICE, slight overstatement and close to a NOT item.** "The sea runs all through these floors. The scholar lists dolphins, fish and water birds."
- Problem: Kramer says "abundant numbers of mosaics with marine fauna such as dolphins, fish, and water birds", which describes the mosaics found, not necessarily those on show. PLAN.md 005 NOT says "'dolphins on the floor', unless seen".
- Replace with: "Among the mosaics found here, the study counts many sea creatures: dolphins, fish and water birds."

Checked and OK in 005: the asarotos oikos is in the Hospitium de los Delfines (Kramer: "a motif otherwise only represented a few times in Hispania"; San Nicolás: "único de momento en la musivaria hispano-romana"), so "rare" is safe. "Unswept floor" is the standard meaning of *asarotos oikos* (Sosos of Pergamon, whom Kramer cites). The Casa del Sigma and stibadium line is label-dependent and hedged ("If the label speaks of a stibadium"). It still rests on one visitor blog (TTS); see Unverified.

### 006 Two birds and a wine bowl (HOLD)

**006.1 SHOULD, inference stated as fact before it is hedged.**
- Quoted wording:
  - 005 cue: "look for a mosaic hung upright on a wall, with two birds and a wine bowl"
  - 006: "look for a mosaic hung upright on a wall: two birds facing each other over a wine bowl"
  - title "Two birds and a wine bowl"
  - later: "It may well be this one."
- Problem: the only sources for the *emblem* are ACOM ("the kissing birds, can be seen at the centre of a large mosaic which has been reconstructed on the wall of the museum") and ACOM-B. Neither mentions a bowl. The bowl comes from Kramer's Hospitium de los Triclinia panel ("two birds on a crater in a small square panel"). The audit (§2 #1) says the identity of the two "is my inference; confirm by the label". The track hedges in paragraph 4 but has already asserted the bowl in paragraph 2, in the cue from 005 and in the title. This is probably right: the two descriptions match, a "central motif" of a larger mosaic. But it is not sourced.
- Sources checked: https://andalucia.com/cities/seville/museum-antiquarium.htm ; Kramer PDF ; https://revistas.uned.es/index.php/ETFII/article/download/19847/17101/40711 (confirms the crater-and-birds mosaic is in the corpus, with no building and no display position).
- Fix: hold until the O4 label or photo. If it is not confirmed, make paragraph 2 "look for a mosaic hung upright on a wall, with two birds at its centre", the 005 cue "…a mosaic hung upright on a wall, with two birds at its centre", and the title "Antiquarium — The two birds". Keep the bowl inside the Kramer paragraph only.

**006.2 NICE, unstated in the cited source.** "It began as part of a floor."
- Problem: ACOM does not say so. It is a reasonable inference: Kramer's panel is the central motif of a "mat", Kramer says some mosaics are "exposed as vertical panels", and San Nicolás describes the corpus as floor pavements.
- Replace with (if O4 can't confirm from the label): "Like the others, it was made for a floor."

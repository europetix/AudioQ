# Setas de Sevilla (Metropol Parasol) + Antiquarium — Verified Fact Base

Prepared 30 Oct 2026 for the Yo Tours English audio guide. Scope: facts and stories only. The visit route, what is open, prices and hours are covered by the other researcher. Prices and hours are left out on purpose.

## Key

- **Status:** OK = safe to state. HEDGE = true but attribute it or soften it (sources conflict or it comes from one party). LEGEND = popular story, not established. UNVERIFIED = could not confirm; leave out of scripts. WRONG = popular claim that is false or out of date.
- **Grade:** OFF = official or primary (operator, City of Seville, architect, engineer, maker, ombudsman, Guinness, MoMA, academic excavation reports). SEC = major press, trade press or peer-reviewed or academic secondary source. SNIP = second-hand (a quote relayed by another outlet, Wikipedia-derived, a blog). Wikipedia was used only to find leads. Nothing below rests on it alone.

### Good stories
- **A square made by Napoleon's brother.** The plaza exists because the French regime cleared a convent in 1810. The market hall, the car park, the excavation pit and now the Setas have filled that same gap ever since. (FG2009)
- **"Temporary" for 37 years.** The fish and vegetable traders moved to provisional stalls in 1973 for three years. They got a permanent home only in December 2010. (S-HIST, MERCASA)
- **A car park that became a time machine.** The underground parking was dropped because the city literally hit Roman Hispalis. (VS-ANT, AAA91)

| "It is perhaps the most complex timber structure ever built" | QUOTE | METSA OFF (Mika Kallio, Finnforest) | Attribute. |

### Good story
**The glue that had to be baked.** Joining thousands of beams with glued steel rods in a city that tops 40 °C meant scientists at Germany's Fraunhofer wood institute put test parts in climate chambers. They found the glue could reach almost 60 °C inside the joints, which was too close to its limit. The fix was to heat every glued part again so the resin cured harder. (FRAUN, METSA)

### Good stories
- **Garum under the market.** Two thousand years before the fishmongers upstairs, Romans here were salting fish in tanks. Archaeologists found the tanks still packed with fish remains. (GA2010)
- **Seville stacked up.** From street level down: convent, medieval floor, Almohad house about 3 m, Roman city 7–9 m. The groundwater was so high that the 1991 diggers had to shore up a tiny pit to reach Roman levels. (AAA91)
- **A building on stilts over a dig.** The trunks were placed so the structure stands over the ruins. Mayer called it "a structure that sits on the Roman ruins." (INHAB, DEZ26)

## 11. Popular claims that are wrong or need care

1. **"The largest wooden structure in the world."** This is the operator's marketing line. The engineers say "one of the largest". Since March 2025 Guinness lists Osaka's Expo Grand Ring. Always attribute or soften it.
2. **"It's made entirely of wood."** The trunks are reinforced concrete (with lifts), the joints are glued steel rods, and the walkways include steel.
3. **"The architect designed mushrooms."** The cited inspirations are the cathedral's vaults and the shade trees of a nearby square. *Setas* is the locals' nickname.
4. **"The Roman ruins were found while building the Setas."** They were first found in a 1991 dig and during the 1990s car-park plan. The big excavations came in 1998–2010.
5. **"There was an underground car park here."** It was planned, never built. There was a surface car park on the empty lot.
6. **"The market dates from the 1840s."** It was designed and largely built 1832–1837, with later works into the 1840s.
7. **"The convent was destroyed in 19th-century anticlerical confiscations (desamortización)."** It was demolished under Napoleonic rule, by a 1810 decree.
8. **Exact height or cost figures stated as fact.** Height varies by source from 26 to 30 m. Use the cost only as "roughly doubled to about €100 million".
9. **"The market is underground."** It is at street level. The Antiquarium is underground.
10. **"16 million bolts" and "€143 per resident".** Unverified. Do not use.

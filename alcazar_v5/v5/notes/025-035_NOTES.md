4. **For the 012 writer / lead (not used by me).** Pleguezuelo, "Niculoso Francisco Pisano y el Real Alcázar de Sevilla" (Apuntes del Alcázar, core.ac.uk/download/51386392.pdf): Pisano also made a retablo for the **king's oratory "en la planta baja … en la sala hoy llamada del techo de Carlos V"**, now lost except for fragments. That supports 012's "designed as a chapel".
5. **Annunciation.** The same article says the altar's **frontal** shows the Annunciation; the central scene is the Visitation. 034 names only the Visitation (PLAN bans "the Annunciation" as the subject). It is not an error to fix elsewhere unless someone calls the whole altar an Annunciation.

"Most accounts date it to the 1540s" rests on a Univ. of Seville thesis abstract (Castro Fuertes 2016, riarte.es) that cites works of 1543 from Gestoso, plus the Bridgeman and Album captions (search snippets). The "bride already gone" turn is hedged "most probably".

**033 Apeadero (CLOSE, 293w).** Read: Apuntes 16 (Resta 1607; three spaces divided by engaged columns carrying round arches; organised like a church rather than a civil building); Tabales AAA 2016 (17th-century main access; today's exit; Recinto I wall 11th century, possibly Almoravid); leaflet (walls around the Patio de Banderas are Islamic); Divisare (2009–13 dig; Roman building 60–30 BC, about 5 m down); prepara-la-visita (the Puerta del Apeadero opens on the Patio de Banderas). "Apeadero" = place to dismount: dictionary sense. Callbacks: Pedro in the Cathedral (BAD, via audit) and the Contratación papers in the Archivo de Indias (UAH/AGI). The recap names the slave trade plainly. Not said: where the Patio de Banderas lies, a Giralda view, re-entry, logistics.

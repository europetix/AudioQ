[warmly] Charles the Fifth rode into Seville on 10 March 1526. That same night, in this room, he married a woman he had never met.

She was Isabel of Portugal, daughter of King Manuel of Portugal and María of Aragon. She was also his first cousin. She was twenty-two, he was twenty-six. Because they were so closely related, the Church had to grant a dispensation. In fact the first one was not enough, and a wedding by proxy had to be repeated in January.

Isabel reached Seville a week before him, on 3 March. The city welcomed her with triumphal arches celebrating the virtues a prince should have. Charles came south from Toledo.

[conspiratorial] He arrived in the evening, and nobody waited. That night the papal legate, Cardinal Salviati, married them by words of present consent. The chronicler Sandoval places it in "the great hall that they call the half orange". [pause] This hall. After midnight, now 11 March, the Archbishop of Toledo said Mass in the palace and gave the couple the nuptial blessing. Their godparents were the Duke of Calabria and the Countess of Haro.

The celebrations did not last long. News arrived that Charles's sister Isabel, Queen of Denmark, had died, and the festivities were cut short. The couple stayed on in Seville for a while, then travelled on to Granada.

[quietly] Look up. That night the dome was already nearly a century old. The marriage had been arranged for reasons of state, and they had thirteen years together. Isabel died in 1539, after her last childbirth. Charles refused to marry again. He remained a widower for the rest of his life.

In 2026, Seville marked the five hundredth anniversary of this wedding.

Number 16 on your Alcázar map, the Salón del Techo de Felipe II, has a short Extra on the Peacock Arch and two wooden ceilings. The next main stop is number 18 on your Alcázar map, the Patio de las Muñecas, the smallest courtyard on your visit.

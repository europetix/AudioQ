[lightly] Gardens follow fashion, just like clothes. And this one is from the start of the twentieth century.

Most of what you have seen so far is older. The Italianate gardens near the palaces, of the Damas, the Danza, Troya, the Galera and the Flores, belong to the Renaissance. But the Alcázar's gardens kept growing. In the early twentieth century, two new ones were added on either flank. The Alcázar's leaflet calls them a sample of the styles that dominated at that time.

This is the first of them, the Jardín Inglés, the English garden. The second, number 50 on your Alcázar map, the Jardín Marqués de la Vega Inclán, is in the French style.

[curious] That name matters. The Marquis de la Vega-Inclán bought a Gothic gateway from the town of Marchena. He bought it on behalf of King Alfonso the Thirteenth, to place it in these new gardens. You will meet it in a few minutes.

[pause] On the way there is a maze garden. Remember the floor of the Pabellón de Carlos V, with the outline of the old labyrinth. The maze is an old idea in these gardens.

Go through number 47 on your Alcázar map, the Jardín del Laberinto, and on past numbers 48 to 50. The next main stop is number 51, the Puerta de Marchena: the Gothic gateway with wild men holding shields.

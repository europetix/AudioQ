[curious] The god in the middle of this pool is a twin. He looks exactly like the bronze set up here in 1576. But he is not that bronze.

The pool itself is older than the statue. It goes back to the Islamic palace. In the sixteenth century, under Charles the Fifth and his son Felipe the Second, it became the centrepiece of a new Renaissance garden. Its water came from the old Carmona aqueduct. In 1576 the statue was added. It was designed by the sculptor Diego de Pesquera and cast by Bartolomé Morel, a founder who worked for the Seville mint. Some years earlier, Morel had cast the Giraldillo, the bronze weathervane on top of the Giralda.

[pause] Then, in November 2022, the restorers drained the pool. As they cleaned the old bronze, they found something under the grime. Traces of gold. Mercury, the child figures and the finials had once been gilded.

So the figure you see now is a faithful replica, and it was made in a very modern way. The original was scanned in three dimensions. Every piece was printed in plastic. Those prints went to a foundry and were cast in bronze by the old lost-wax method. Then the new bronze was gilded by electroplating. [lightly] That is why this Mercury shines.

Look at him now. He stands on a sphere, with his winged helmet and his caduceus, the messenger's staff. The pool was refilled in 2024 and shown restored in 2025.

[amused] Game of Thrones fans may know this view. Scenes with Prince Doran of Dorne were filmed at the painted gallery in front of this pool.

The painted gallery is the next stop: number 40 on your Alcázar map, the Galería del Grutesco, the long painted wall along one side of this pool.

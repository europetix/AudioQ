[curious] Every fountain in this part of the garden once depended on water that came from far outside the city walls.

You are in number 38, the Jardín de la Danza, the Garden of the Dance. It belongs to a chain of small Italianate gardens below the palace. The Alcázar's map names them: the gardens of las Damas, la Danza, Troya, Galera and Flores, laid out in order under the shelter of the old Almohad wall.

Their water arrived along the old Carmona aqueduct. It was carried through channels and fountains, and the big raised pool at the top of the steps served as a reservoir for watering the gardens. That is how they stayed green through the hottest months.

This garden has changed more than once. The Alcázar's own exhibition text dates it, as we have it, to the eighteenth century, and says it had a small stage for dances and performances. Hence, perhaps, the name.

[lightly] Now find the two columns on the upper level, by the steps. In 2023, during Storm Bernard, big branches came down on them. They were restored in 2024, and one of them stands on a new pedestal.

Felipe the Third's opening into the Baths is in this garden too, the way you came out earlier. The next stop is just above.

Up the steps at the head of the Danza to number 39 on the Alcázar map, the Estanque de Mercurio: the big raised pool with a bronze god in the middle.

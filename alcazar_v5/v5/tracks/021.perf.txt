[warmly] When Charles the Fifth married Isabel of Portugal in 1526, the celebrating may have moved in here. The Alcázar's own teaching notes call this the Sala de las Fiestas, the hall of celebrations. They say the wedding banquet was held in this room.

This is number 33, the Sala de las Bóvedas, the Hall of the Vaults, also known as the Salón Gótico. Look up first, at the Gothic vaults of Alfonso the Wise's palace. Then look at the walls. They are tiled to a height of about two metres.

The tiles are sixteenth-century Mannerist work, from the last phase of the Renaissance. They are crowded with figures and plants, and many stand out against a bright yellow ground. [curious] Choose one panel. Find a figure, then follow the leaves and flowers around it.

Two more tapestries hang here, with scenes of the sea. They are much younger than they look. They were made for the Ibero-American Exposition held in Seville in 1929.

[lightly] Through the long glass-panelled gallery, the garden is already in view. That is where you are going next.

Out into the gardens and down into number 38 on your Alcázar map, the Jardín de la Danza. Number 34, the Baños de Doña María de Padilla, opens off it under the palace: a doorway into a long pool under Gothic vaults.

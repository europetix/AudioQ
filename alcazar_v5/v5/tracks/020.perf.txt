[reverent] The Virgin on this altar is a copy. The original is one of the oldest paintings in Seville.

She is the Virgen de la Antigua, and the original is in her own chapel in Seville Cathedral. The Alcázar describes it as painted on a wall of the old mosque, after the mosque became a church in the thirteenth century. The copy here is attributed to the painter Diego de Castillejo.

Look up. This small chapel keeps its medieval rib vaults, from Alfonso the Wise's Gothic palace. The tiles around the lower walls came later. They are a Renaissance addition of the sixteenth century.

The Alcázar's description of the chapel also lists two more paintings. One is by Domingo Martínez, from 1742. It shows the Virgen de los Reyes, the Virgin of the Kings, patroness of Seville, between Saint Hermenegild and Saint Ferdinand, the king who took the city in 1248. The other shows the Immaculate Virgin with a donor, and Christ dressed as a priest.

[curious] See which of them hang here today. Then come back to the altar, and to that copy of a Virgin first painted on a mosque wall.

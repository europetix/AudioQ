[warmly] This little pavilion was built to honour a wedding. The groom never saw it.

In 1526 Charles the Fifth married Isabel of Portugal in the Salón de los Embajadores. They stayed in Seville only until May. Charles never came back. Yet this pavilion was put up in honour of that marriage. Its builder was Juan Hernández, and most accounts date it to the 1540s.

It did not start from nothing. Its origin lies in a qubba, a small domed pavilion of the Islamic tradition. The building you see mixes the old Mudéjar style with the new Renaissance one.

[quietly] There is a sad turn here. Isabel died in 1539, after the birth of her last child. Charles never remarried. So when this pavilion rose in honour of their marriage, the bride was most probably already gone.

Now look at it. Inside it is a single square room, with a fountain in the middle, a magnificent carved wooden ceiling, and a window on each side to let the breeze through. The tiles are from the sixteenth century, from the workshop of the Polido brothers. And look down. The floor traces the outline of the old labyrinth.

After a full restoration, the pavilion is open again, so you can see the tiles and that floor up close.

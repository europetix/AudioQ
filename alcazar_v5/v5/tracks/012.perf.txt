[lightly] This room was a royal chapel before it was named after an emperor. And you are still inside King Pedro's palace, not in the Gothic palace.

It was the king's oratory, his private chapel, on the ground floor of the palace. In 1504 the ceramic artist Niculoso Pisano made a tiled altarpiece for it. Only a few scattered tiles that may come from it survive. His altar for the queen, made the same year, survives upstairs.

The historian Alfonso Pleguezuelo identifies this room as the place where, after midnight, the wedding Mass of Charles the Fifth and Isabel of Portugal was said. The vows themselves, the chronicler tells us, were exchanged in the great domed hall, number 15, the Salón de los Embajadores.

[amused] Now look up. The ceiling was put in during the early 1540s: a Renaissance coffered ceiling, with busts of human figures looking down from it. That is why the room carries the emperor's name. Yet Charles had left Seville long before, and he never came back to see it.

Next door is number 14 on the Alcázar map, the Sala de los Infantes, rooms said to have been used by the king's daughters.

Back into number 10, the Patio de las Doncellas, and on to number 15, the Salón de los Embajadores, at the far end of the courtyard: look up for the gilded wooden dome.

[lightly] This room was a royal chapel before it was named after an emperor. And you are still inside King Pedro's palace, not in the Gothic palace, whatever some old guides say.

It was the king's oratory, his private chapel, on the ground floor of the palace. In 1504 the ceramic artist Niculoso Pisano made a tiled altarpiece for it. Only a few scattered tiles that may come from it survive. The historian Alfonso Pleguezuelo also identifies this room as the setting of the Mass for the marriage of Charles the Fifth and Isabel of Portugal.

[amused] Now look up. The ceiling was put in during the early 1540s: a Renaissance coffered ceiling, with busts of human figures looking down from it. That is why the room carries the emperor's name. Yet Charles had left Seville long before, and he never came back to see it.

Next door is number 14 on the Alcázar map, the Sala de los Infantes, rooms once used by the king's daughters.

Back into number 10, the Patio de las Doncellas, and on to number 15, the Salón de los Embajadores: look up for the gilded wooden dome.

[warmly] This state dining room belongs to a house that is still lived in, by a king, on his visits.

The Cuarto Real Alto is the upper floor of a medieval palace, but much of what it shows is far younger. In the nineteenth century, the Bourbon monarchs added rooms to this top floor and refurbished the old ones. They brought in tapestries, clocks, furniture and paintings, and crystal lamps from La Granja, the royal glassworks.

Three rooms belong together here. Number 22, the Antecomedor, is the anteroom. Number 23 is the Comedor de Gala, the state dining room. And number 24 is the Sala de Fumar, the smoking room, a very nineteenth-century kind of room.

[pause] The upper floor is not just a display. It is the official residence of the head of state in Andalucía, with bedrooms, a sitting room, an office, an oratory and original royal furniture. The King sleeps here on his visits and holds official events.

[lightly] So the rooms you walk through are not frozen in the past. A medieval palace, a Bourbon makeover, and a modern monarchy, all on one floor.

That is the end of the Cuarto Real Alto stops. Back downstairs, pick up the main tour where you left it, usually at number 18, the Patio de las Muñecas.

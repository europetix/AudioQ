# Audit of the facts in the old guide (V4): Real Alcázar de Sevilla

Audit date: 30 Oct 2026. Independent fact-check of `old/001.txt`–`020.txt` and the front matter in `source/old.txt` (lines 1–190).
Scope: history, dates, attributions, quotations and numbers. Route, closures and object locations go to the other researcher. Items here that touch them are marked **→ROUTE**.

The V4 "On sources" note says every claim was checked against primary sources. **That is not true.** The V4 text has at least 45 factual errors. Several of them contradict the Patronato's own pages.

### The worst errors (fix first)

1. **The Puerta del León is not 12th-century Almohad.** Tabales's archaeological report (Junta de Andalucía, AAA 2016) dates the gate and its wall to Pedro I's reign, around the 1350s (C-14 centred on 1368). The oldest wall it found (Recinto I) is 11th-century. The guide also says repeatedly that walls or masonry from 913 are visible (in the Patio de Banderas and in the "ninth-century walls"). That is unsupported, and "ninth century" is simply wrong because 913 is in the 10th century. (T01, T03, T19, T20)
2. **The María de Padilla stories are wrong.** She died in **1361**, before Pedro's palace was finished (inscription dated 1364, completed 1366), so she cannot have "held court" in the Patio de las Muñecas or seen its stucco "every morning". The claimed "secret marriage in 1361" is also wrong: Pedro declared in 1362 that he had married her **before** his 1353 marriage to Blanche. (T13)
3. **The Tudor claim is false.** Henry VII descends from John of Gaunt through Katherine Swynford, not through Constanza of Castile. Constanza's verified line runs to **Isabel the Catholic** (her great-granddaughter) and **Catherine of Aragon** (her great-great-granddaughter), who was Henry VIII's first wife, not his mother. (T13)
4. **The "Almohad cisterns" are wrong.** The Patronato's own page says the Baños de María de Padilla are the **lower level of the Patio del Crucero**: a sunken summer garden whose vaults were made Gothic under **Alfonso X**, then remodelled under Felipe III. The guide's "late twelve hundreds Almohads" and "same brick as the Giralda" are wrong. (T14, T16)
5. **The Court of the Lions timing is wrong.** The Alhambra Patronato dates the Palace of the Lions to 1362–1391, during Muhammad V's second reign. Craftsmen cannot have "just finished" it before 1364. The Comares facade (1370) is also later than Pedro's facade. (T06, T09)
6. **The Pedro I chronology is wrong.** The guide says he commissioned the palace in 1364 "after returning to power", but he lost the throne in 1366 and regained it in 1367. It says he sheltered Muhammad V in Seville in 1359, but Muhammad went into exile in Fez (SNIP). It says his body was moved "at his daughter Constanza's request" to Seville. In fact it went from Montiel to Puebla de Alcocer, then to Santo Domingo el Real in Madrid in 1446, and reached Seville Cathedral only after that convent was demolished in the 19th century (OFF, Diputación de Badajoz). (T09)
7. **The 1526 wedding account has many errors.** Charles came from **Toledo**, not Madrid. Isabel was **22**, not 23. Her mother was **María** of Aragon, not Isabel of Aragon. The nuptial Mass (velación) was said **after midnight in a chamber of the Alcázar** by the Archbishop of Toledo, not "next morning at the cathedral". Felipe II was born on 21 May 1527, about **14 months later**, so he was not "conceived within weeks" or "born nine months later". The mourning for Charles's sister **followed** the wedding (the news interrupted the festivities). The Pedro Mexía quote and the "emperor's memoir" are untraceable. (T11, T12, T19)
8. **The Game of Thrones scenes are wrong.** According to the Andalucía Film Commission, the Baths were where **Ellaria is arrested**, not where the Sand Snakes plot. The Charles V pavilion area was the **fight** with Jaime and Bronn, not the Myrcella–Trystane kiss. Myrcella and Trystane walk in the Jardín de las Damas. Doran appears by the Grutesco and the Mercury pond. Use of the Patio de las Doncellas is unverified. The Dan Weiss quote is untraceable. (T14, T17, T18)
9. **Garden dates are wrong.** The Mercury statue dates from **1576**. **Pesquera designed it and Morel cast it**; the guide reverses them and gives 1573. The Galería del Grutesco is by **Vermondo Resta, 1613–1621**, not 1593–1612, with paintings by Diego de Esquivel finished in 1629. It sits on the pond's **east** side. Charles V never used the 1540s pavilion: he did not return to Seville after 1526. (T16, T17)
10. **The Patio de las Doncellas columns are 16th-century Renaissance**, not Roman spolia. The reused Caliphal and Roman columns are in the **Patio de las Muñecas** (Patronato). The Muñecas upper floors are **19th-century**, not from Felipe II's time. Archnet (Almagro and Ramón-Laca) says the Doncellas garden "was buried after the assassination of Peter I and it seems that it was never used". The guide's "as Pedro's court saw it" is therefore contested. The restoration was done by the Patronato, not "the cathedral chapter". (T10, T13)
11. **Other errors:** Alfonso X is Pedro's **great-great-grandfather**, not his great-grandfather. The 1427 dome is not a "Renaissance dome". The Niculoso Pisano altar (1504) shows the **Visitation**, not the Annunciation. The claim that Charles V "spent much of his Spanish residency" in the Cuarto Real Alto is false: he was in Seville from about 10 March to May 1526.
12. **Banned content:** prices, hours, free-hour advice, the "cap of 750", bag storage and arrival-time instructions, and "admission is free" at the Archivo. The Espacio Santa Clara exhibition (11 Mar–30 May 2026) is **over**, and the guide also gave the wrong start date ("late March").

## 3. Verified good stories (safe to build on)

1. **Pedro I is "sultan" in Arabic.** Arabic dados throughout the palace read "'izz li-mawlana al-sultan don Bidru ayyadahu Allah": "Glory to our lord the sultan Don Pedro, may God help him". In the former chapel the word *malik* (king) replaces *sultan*. On the facade, the Gothic Castilian text dated era 1402 (1364) frames the Nasrid motto "wa-la ghaliba illa Allah". (Marquer, *Anales de Historia del Arte* 23, 2013. OFF)
2. **The doors of the Salón de Embajadores carry their own date.** An Arabic inscription on the doors of the Salón de la Media Naranja dates the work to Spanish era 1404, which is 1366. (Marquer 2013. OFF)
3. **The 1427 dome** by Diego Ruiz, the **1599 king portraits** by Diego de Esquivel, and the late 16th-century **iron balconies** under Felipe II. (Ayuntamiento de Sevilla / Real Alcázar on Google Arts & Culture. OFF)
4. **The Lion Gate is Pedro's, and the patio was a theatre.** The Puerta del León belongs to Pedro I's 1350s works and was long called the Puerta de la Montería. The Patio del León held a theatre (the Corral de la Montería) from the 1620s until a fire in 1691. It then became stables and housing, and was cleared in 1936–37 by Juan Talavera to create today's visitor entrance. (Tabales, AAA 2016, Junta de Andalucía. OFF)
5. **The buried garden of the Doncellas.** On 22 Aug 2002 Tabales's team found the walks, pool and beds of Pedro's sunken patio under a 16th-century level. The courtyard had been paved and the pool was gone by 1584. Almagro and Ramón-Laca think the garden was buried soon after Pedro's murder and possibly never used. (Cabeza Méndez, Patronato director, SEC; Archnet. OFF)
6. **Pedro lived in the Patio del Yeso** (recorded in 1358) before his new palace existed. The Patio del Yeso is the remnant of an Almohad residence of about 1190; it was rediscovered in 1855 and restored in 1918–20 and 1969–71. (Archnet. OFF)
7. **The Baths are a sunken garden, not a cistern.** They are the lower level of the Patio del Crucero, an Almohad-origin summer garden whose vaults were made Gothic under Alfonso X and reworked under Felipe III. (Patronato, alcazarsevilla.org/?p=10943. OFF)
8. **Recycled columns in the Muñecas.** The Patio de las Muñecas has reused Caliphal and Roman columns and capitals, and its two upper floors are 19th-century. (Patronato, ?p=10868. OFF)
9. **Casa de la Contratación.** Real Provisión of the Catholic Monarchs, Alcalá de Henares, 20 Jan 1503. The damp Atarazanas were rejected and the Casa was put in the Cuarto de los Almirantes, where it stayed until 1717. (AGI Patronato 251 via UAH. OFF)
10. **The Virgin of the Navigators as imperial self-portrait.** Painted 1531–36 for the chapel of the Casa's audience hall. The traditional identifications (Columbus as a magus-like figure in gold, Charles V, a Pinzón, Cortés, Vespucci or Matienzo) are all disputed. Indigenous men and women stand in the background under the cloak. The side saints are Sebastian, James, Elmo and John on Patmos. (Phillips, *Renaissance Quarterly* 58, 2005. OFF)
11. **The 1526 wedding.** Isabel entered Seville on 3 March. Charles arrived from Toledo on 10 March. Cardinal Salviati married them by words of present consent that night in the great domed hall. After midnight the Archbishop of Toledo said the nuptial Mass, with the Duke of Calabria and the Countess of Haro as godparents. News of the death of Charles's sister, Queen Isabel of Denmark, then cut the festivities short. (García Parody & Caballero 2018, SEC; Patrimonio Nacional for 11 March. OFF) The city's arches celebrated seven virtues: Prudence, Fortitude, Clemency, Peace, Justice, the Theological Virtues and Glory (SEC). Isabel died in 1539 and Charles never remarried (Patrimonio. OFF).
12. **The Mercury statue.** Designed by Diego de Pesquera and cast by Bartolomé Morel, the Giraldillo's founder, it was added in 1576. The pond is of Islamic origin and was fed by the Carmona aqueduct. A bronze replica was cast in 2024 during a restoration, and the pond reopened in 2025. (Alcázar EXPO-MERCURIO PDF. OFF)
13. **The Grutesco.** Vermondo Resta turned the old Islamic wall into the Grutesco between 1613 and 1621, and Diego de Esquivel spent seven years painting it, finishing in 1629. (EXPO PDF. OFF)
14. **The Cenador de Carlos V** has its origin in a Muslim qubba. It is by Juan Hernández, was built in honour of the 1526 marriage, has tiles from the Polido workshop, and its floor traces an old labyrinth. (Patronato, ?p=11014. OFF)
15. **Game of Thrones (season 5, filmed October 2014).** The Hall of Ambassadors is Doran's hall. Doran appears at the Grutesco by the Mercury pool. Myrcella and Trystane walk in the Jardín de las Damas. The fight scene is by the Charles V pavilion. Ellaria is arrested in the Baths. (Andalucía Film Commission. OFF)
16. **Pedro's posthumous journey.** He was killed at Montiel in 1369 and buried at Puebla de Alcocer. In 1446 he was moved to Santo Domingo el Real in Madrid. After that convent was demolished his remains went to the Museo Arqueológico, and finally to Seville Cathedral. (Diputación de Badajoz. OFF)
17. **A Castilian queen descended from Pedro.** Pedro and María de Padilla's daughter Constanza married John of Gaunt. Through their daughter Catherine of Lancaster, Pedro is the great-great-grandfather of **Isabel the Catholic**, and Constanza is the great-great-grandmother of **Catherine of Aragon**. 18. **The Court of the Lions came after.** The Alhambra's Palace of the Lions dates from 1362–1391, so Pedro's palace is a contemporary, and partly an earlier sibling. (Alhambra Patronato. OFF)

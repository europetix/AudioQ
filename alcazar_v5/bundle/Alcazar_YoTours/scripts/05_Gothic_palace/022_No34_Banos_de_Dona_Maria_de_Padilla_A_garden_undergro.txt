This is not a bath, and it is not a cistern. It is a garden that went underground.

You are standing in the lower level of the Patio del Crucero, the courtyard of the Gothic palace above your head. Much of that courtyard goes back to the Islamic palace. Down here was a sunken garden, dug into the earth for the summer. The Alcázar describes it as having just the right temperature and humidity to fight the heat.

Then the kings of Castile reshaped it. Under Alfonso the Tenth, in the thirteenth century, its vaults were rebuilt as Gothic vaults, at the same time as his palace was going up overhead. Centuries later, under Felipe the Third, it was remodelled again. It gained a Mannerist fountain, and an opening into the Jardín de la Danza, the garden you came in from. At one end of the pool, look for what is left of that fountain.

So why is it called after María de Padilla? She was King Pedro's companion, the mother of his children, and she died in 1361. There is no evidence she ever bathed here. The Alcázar's own notes for school groups tell the romantic tale, that the king built these baths for love of her. Then they add, for the teachers, a short warning. No historical basis.

Television has its own story for this place. In Game of Thrones, this is where the guards arrest Ellaria Sand.

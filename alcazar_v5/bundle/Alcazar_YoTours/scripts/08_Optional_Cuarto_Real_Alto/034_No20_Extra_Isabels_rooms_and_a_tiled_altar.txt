Upstairs, this palace is still a royal home. The Cuarto Real Alto, the upper royal rooms, is the King's official residence in Seville.

This short Extra is for the foot of the stair, number 19, before you go up or after you come down. The upper rooms sit on top of King Pedro's palace. Tradition says this floor was built, or rebuilt, for Isabel the Catholic. The first rooms carry her name: number 20 on the Alcázar map, the Dormitorio de Isabel la Católica, and number 21, her chapel.

That chapel is tiny, little more than a niche under an arch. Its back wall is a painted tile altar, and the artist signed it: Niculoso Francisco, Italian, made me. He is known as Niculoso Pisano. In his day, scholars point out, "Pisano" simply meant Italian.

Look for the scene in the centre: the Visitation, Mary greeting her cousin Elizabeth. Around it grows the Tree of Jesse, the family tree of Christ. Look too for the emblems of Isabel and Fernando, the yoke and the arrows, and the motto Tanto Monta.

The altar is dated 1504. That was the last year of Isabel's life.

The next Cuarto Real Alto stop is number 23, the Comedor de Gala, the state dining room, with number 22, the Antecomedor, as its anteroom.

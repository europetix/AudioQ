A Christian king of Castile signed this palace in two scripts. One of them, in Arabic, uses the very words of the Muslim rulers of Granada.

The first inscription is in Castilian, in Gothic letters. It calls him "the very high, very noble, very powerful and very conquering Don Pedro, by the grace of God king of Castile and León", and it says that he ordered this work. It is dated in the old Spanish era, the year 1402. In our calendar, that is 1364.

Framed by that Castilian text is a second inscription, in angular Kufic letters on blue and white tiles. It reads wa-la ghaliba illa Allah: "there is no victor but God". It was the motto of the Nasrid sultans of Granada.

Why would Pedro borrow it? The historian Julie Marquer reads it as propaganda. The Castilian court took the Nasrid motto and made it work for a Castilian king. Pedro had craftsmen from Seville and Toledo, and probably from Granada too.

It is tempting to think he simply copied the Alhambra. But the Alhambra's Palace of the Lions was built from 1362 until near the end of that century. Pedro's palace was begun a few years before it, and finished within two years after this inscription. These were not master and copy. They were neighbours, growing up at the same time.

Now look up at the bands of lettering. The Gothic Castilian and the Kufic tiles belong to one message, the king's name and the motto read together.

Two scripts, one king. And a motto that, however you read it, puts God above him.

Number 6, the Cuarto del Almirante, opens off this courtyard: the range along its right-hand side as you face the palace front, behind a row of columns, where the Casa de la Contratación worked. Look for the sign Casa de la Contratación.

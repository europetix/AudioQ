This little pavilion was built to honour a wedding. The groom never saw it.

In 1526 Charles the Fifth married Isabel of Portugal in the Salón de los Embajadores. They stayed in Seville about two months. Charles never came back. Yet this pavilion was put up in honour of that marriage. Its builder was Juan Hernández, and it is usually dated to the 1540s.

It did not start from nothing. Its origin lies in a qubba, a small domed pavilion of the Islamic tradition. The building you see mixes the old Mudéjar style with the new Renaissance one.

There is a sad turn here. Isabel died in 1539, after the birth of her last child. Charles never remarried. So when this pavilion rose in honour of their marriage, the bride was most probably already gone.

Inside, there is a single square room, with a fountain in the middle, a magnificent carved wooden ceiling, and a window on each side to let the breeze through. The tiles are from the sixteenth century, from the workshop of the Polido brothers. And its floor traces the outline of the old labyrinth.

If the pavilion is still behind screens, look at its tiled outer walls. The room, the ceiling and that floor are waiting inside.

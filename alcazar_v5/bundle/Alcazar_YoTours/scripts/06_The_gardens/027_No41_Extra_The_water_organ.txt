This fountain hides a musical instrument. Inside it is an organ that is played by water.

The Alcázar calls it the only seventeenth-century organ fountain preserved in Spain, and one of just four left in the world. Fountains like this were a fashion of the villas of Renaissance and Baroque Italy. The original machinery used water to move air, and the moving air made the sound.

In 2006, two specialists brought it back to working order. One was Leonardo Lombardi, an Italian professor of archaeology, who restored its hydraulic chamber. The other was Rodney Briscoe, a British craftsman. He was said to be the only craftsman able to restore the machinery of an organ fountain of this kind.

The restored organ was made to run on its own, on the air pressure that the water produces. Whether it is playing as you pass is up to the water. If you hear it, you know what it is.

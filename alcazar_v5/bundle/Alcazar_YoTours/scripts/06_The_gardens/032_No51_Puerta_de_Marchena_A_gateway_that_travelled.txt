This gateway is a long way from home. It was made to be the front door of a palace in another town.

It comes from Marchena, a town east of Seville, where it was the entrance to the palace of the Dukes of Arcos. It was built in 1492, in the reign of Isabel the Catholic. Its design may be linked to the architect Juan Guas.

Then the gateway changed its coat of arms. In 1540, after a change in the family line, the heraldry had to follow. So the old arms were altered, and the carved figures you see were added. The sculptor Esteban Jamete made them, and they can be dated to 1544.

Look closely at those figures. They are wild men, a favourite figure of late medieval art, holding up the shields of the new owners. Around them the stone mixes three languages: Gothic decoration, later Renaissance elements and touches of Mudéjar work.

From the eighteenth century the gateway belonged to the Dukes of Osuna. Then the Marquis de la Vega-Inclán bought it, on behalf of King Alfonso the Thirteenth, to stand in the Alcázar's new gardens. So a door built for a duke's palace now opens onto a king's garden. The French-style garden of number 50, the Jardín Marqués de la Vega Inclán, carries the name of the man who brought it here.

From here, follow the Salida signs past the beds of number 52 on the Alcázar map, the Jardín de la Alcubilla, just beyond this gateway. They lead to number 53, the Apeadero: the columned hall that opens onto the Patio de Banderas.

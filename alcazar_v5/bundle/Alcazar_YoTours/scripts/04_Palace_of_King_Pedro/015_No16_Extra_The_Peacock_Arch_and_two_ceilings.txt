In this palace, rooms are often named after what is over your head. Number 13, the Salón del Techo de Carlos Quinto, is named for a ceiling. The Hall of Ambassadors was long named for its dome, the half orange. And so is this room.

This is number 16, the Salón del Techo de Felipe Segundo, the Hall of Felipe the Second's Ceiling. Its name comes from its wooden ceiling, curved like half a barrel. Look up and follow the curve from one wall to the other.

An arch here leads back into the Hall of Ambassadors. It is known as the Peacock Arch, after the birds worked into its decoration. See if you can find them.

Number 17 on the Alcázar map, the Sala de los Reyes Católicos, is named after the Catholic Monarchs, Fernando and Isabel. Its wooden ceiling is worked with ribbons and heraldic emblems.

In this palace, look up and the ceiling usually tells you whose room it was.

On to number 18, the Patio de las Muñecas: the small courtyard with galleries rising above its arches.

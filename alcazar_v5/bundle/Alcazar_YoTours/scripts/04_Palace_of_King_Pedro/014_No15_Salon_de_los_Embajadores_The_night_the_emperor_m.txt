Charles the Fifth rode into Seville on 10 March 1526. That same night, in this room, he married in person a woman he had never met.

She was Isabel of Portugal, daughter of King Manuel of Portugal and María of Aragon. She was also his first cousin. She was twenty-two, he was twenty-six. Because they were so closely related, the Church had to grant a dispensation.

Isabel reached Seville a week before him, on 3 March. The city welcomed her with triumphal arches celebrating the virtues a prince should have. Charles came south from Toledo.

He arrived that day, and nobody waited. That night the papal legate, Cardinal Salviati, married them by words of present consent. The chronicler Sandoval places it in "the great hall that they call the half orange". This hall. After midnight, now 11 March, an altar was set up in a chamber of the palace, and the Archbishop of Toledo said Mass there and gave the couple the nuptial blessing. One historian of the Alcázar places that Mass in the king's old oratory, number 13, the Salón del Techo de Carlos Quinto. Their godparents were the Duke of Calabria and the Countess of Haro.

The celebrations did not last long. News arrived that Charles's sister Isabel, Queen of Denmark, had died, and the festivities were cut short. The couple stayed on in Seville for a while, then travelled on to Granada.

Look up. That night the dome was already nearly a century old. The marriage had been arranged for reasons of state, and it lasted thirteen years. Isabel died in 1539, after her last childbirth. Charles refused to marry again.

In 2026, Seville marked the five hundredth anniversary of this wedding.

Number 16 on the Alcázar map, the Salón del Techo de Felipe Segundo, has a short Extra on the Peacock Arch and two wooden ceilings. The next main stop is number 18 on the Alcázar map, the Patio de las Muñecas, the smallest courtyard on your visit.

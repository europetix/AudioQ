 Stay in the gardens for this track. Wander if you like, or find a bench. Anywhere with a view of palm
 trees and a fountain will do. The whole conversation works wherever you stop.


A meaningful fraction of the people walking through these gardens right now did not come for the
Almohads.

They came because of a television show. Game of Thrones. Specifically, the Dorne storyline of
season five — the part with the Sand Snakes, with Prince Doran in his wheelchair, with Jaime
Lannister and Bronn arriving by sea to rescue Princess Myrcella from her arranged marriage to
Trystane Martell. The kingdom of Dorne, the southernmost realm of fictional Westeros, was
filmed almost entirely here. In this palace. In these gardens. In the year twenty fourteen.

I'm not going to apologize for this. The show is one of the most-watched television productions in
history, the Alcázar is one of the most beautiful palaces on earth, and the moment HBO's
location scouts walked through here in twenty thirteen they recognized exactly what they were
looking at. The Mudéjar architecture of southern Spain is, in the geographic imagination of the
show, the architectural equivalent of the Dornish desert — hot, southern, vaguely Mediterranean,
half-Moorish, half-Christian. Dan Weiss, the show's co-creator, has said publicly that the Alcázar
was as if designed for them.

Four specific places inside the Alcázar were used. Let me name them.

The Salón de los Embajadores — the room you stood in with me an hour ago, looking up at the
ten-fold knot lacería dome — was used as Prince Doran Martell's throne room. The scene where
Jaime Lannister, brought before the wheelchair-bound prince after his failed rescue attempt,
negotiates the return of Princess Myrcella to King's Landing. The opening camera shot of that
scene, framed from the floor looking up past Jaime toward the gilded dome above, used the
actual dome — Diego Ruiz's fourteen twenty-seven masterpiece — as its backdrop.

The Patio de las Doncellas was used as the central courtyard of the Martell palace — the
so-called Water Gardens of Sunspear. Several of the scenes of Trystane and Myrcella walking
together were filmed in the galleries around the long reflecting pool.

The Baños de Doña María de Padilla — the underground vaults where we just were — were
used as the meeting place of Ellaria Sand and the Sand Snakes, where they conspire to abduct
Myrcella in revenge for Oberyn Martell's death at the Mountain's hands.

And the gardens — most especially the area around the Estanque de Mercurio — were used as
the open palace gardens of Sunspear, where Prince Doran sits in his wheelchair and
contemplates the politics of the south. The Pabellón de Carlos Quinto, as I mentioned, was used
for the kiss scene between Myrcella and Trystane.

In the show's plot, these locations belong to the Martell family — a fictional dynasty descended
from Rhoynar refugees who arrived in Westeros from across the sea, blending Eastern and
Western traditions. House Martell's words are "Unbowed, Unbent, Unbroken." Their sigil is a red
sun pierced by a golden spear. They are, in George R. R. Martin's geography of his fantasy
world, the most Mediterranean-inflected house in Westeros. The Alcázar was chosen because
Mediterranean-inflected, ornate, and openly fusion of Christian and Muslim architectural
traditions is exactly what the Alcázar is.

There is nothing dishonest about this layer of the Alcázar's modern fame. The show brought
hundreds of thousands of new visitors to Sevilla. It brought the gardens, in particular, to
audiences who would never otherwise have heard of Almohad water engineering or Mudéjar tile
decoration. Many of those visitors then discovered the actual history of the place — Pedro the
First, Muhammad the Fifth, Charles and Isabel, the buried fourteenth-century garden — and
went away knowing more than they came to know.

The actors are gone. The cameras are long packed up. The gilded dome is back to doing what it
has done for almost six hundred years, which is being a dome. But if you came for Dorne —
which, if you are one of the meaningful fraction, you did — please take in the rest of what's
around you too. The fictional kingdom that drew you here is gone in seven years. The real palace
it was filmed in has been here for eleven hundred. There is no contradiction. They both belong to
you now.

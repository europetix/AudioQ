[quietly] On the 6th of August, 1936, three weeks into the Spanish Civil War, the president of FC Barcelona drove out of Madrid and up into the mountains. He wanted to visit Republican troops at the front. Near the Alto del León pass, without realising it, his car crossed the front line into ground held by the Nationalists. He was identified, and shot without trial.

His name was Josep Sunyol. He was born in Barcelona in 1898, and in 1935 the members made him president by acclamation, without needing a vote. He was a politician too, a leader of Esquerra Republicana de Catalunya, the Republican Left of Catalonia, and a member of the Spanish parliament. And he owned a weekly paper, La Rambla. Its motto was sport and citizenship.

Three men died with him: a journalist, a militia lieutenant and their driver. [pause] In Barcelona, nobody knew for about a week.

He was shot by Franco's troops, and his remains have never been found. Searches were made in 2010 and again in 2022, with the club's support. Both found nothing.

The war went on, and the club kept a place for him. From late 1937, its board named him the absent president, and it went on calling him that until the start of 1939.

In 1996 a memorial plaque was put up near the place where he died, in a park in Guadarrama.

In August 2026, ninety years on, the club laid flowers there.

[pause] He was president for about a year, and the club has no grave to visit. What he left behind is the motto his paper carried. Sport and citizenship.

[amused] This one needs nothing to look at, so find somewhere to lean or sit. It's about how Barça's fans got their nickname, and it involves backsides.

In its first ten years the club played on four different grounds. One of them stood where the Hospital de Sant Pau stands today. Then, in March 1909, Barça opened its first real ground, with a stand, on Carrer de la Indústria. Today the street is called Carrer de París.

[lightly] The ground had a wall around it, and here is the club's own explanation. People walking past saw a long row of spectators sitting along the top of the wall, backsides to the street. Cul is Catalan for backside. So the people on the wall became culers.

It began as a jibe, and the club admits it was a rude one. Over the years the fans grew attached to it, and now they wear it with pride.

[pause] Not everyone buys the story. A writer who goes by the name Roger Vinton searched the archives for that famous wall of backsides. He found one photograph.

Wherever it came from, look at the people around you in the club's shirts. Culers, every one of them.

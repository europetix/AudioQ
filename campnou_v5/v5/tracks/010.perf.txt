In 2011, at Wembley, the man who lifted the European Cup for Barça was a defender just back from a serious illness. His name was Eric Abidal.

[warmly] Barça had beaten Sir Alex Ferguson's Manchester United three goals to one. For the team, it was a third European Cup in six seasons. For Abidal, it was the end of a long way back.

Four years later, the final was in Berlin, at the Olympiastadion, on the sixth of June, 2015. The opponents were Juventus. Ivan Rakitić scored in the fourth minute. Álvaro Morata equalised early in the second half. Then the Uruguayan, Luis Suárez, put Barça back in front, and Neymar added a third at the very end. Three–one. Catalan News counted it as the club's fifth European Cup, and its fourth in ten years.

The coach was Luis Enrique, in his first season in charge, and the cup completed the club's second treble. [amused] The club still likes to recall that a black cat turned up at his very first game. So much for bad luck.

The captain who lifted the cup in Berlin was Xavi. It was his last match for the club. By then he had scored in seventeen seasons in a row, a club record he shares with Charly Rexach and, later, Messi. [pause] His last game, and his last trophy in these colours.

Now look at these two cups, from 2011 and 2015, the last of the five so far.

[warmly] Four European Cups in ten years.

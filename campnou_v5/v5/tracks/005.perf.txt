[amused] The very first Barça shirt was split down the middle. Half blue, half claret, with the sleeves the other way round. Blaugrana, blue and claret.

[pause] And nobody wrote down why. The board's minutes from 1899 record the colours, Ara, the Catalan paper, reports, without a single word of explanation.

The club's history pages call an English school's rugby shirt the most likely theory: the kit of Merchant Taylors' School, where the Witty brothers, two of the club's first members, had studied.

[conspiratorial] Another story says the founder, Joan Gamper, brought the colours from his old Swiss club, Basel. The club itself leans to the English school.

[lightly] Even the first badge wasn't the club's own. It was the city's coat of arms, with a crown and a little bat perched on top.

When you get inside, look for the blue and claret on the oldest shirts. For now, go in at the museum's door, through the ticket check, and stop in the entrance hall, out of the way of people coming in: The entrance hall · From under the stand to an ice rink.

Nobody owns Barça. [pause] Its socis, the members, do.

As one Barcelona university summed it up, Barça is one of only four professional clubs in Spain still owned by their members. The others are Real Madrid, Athletic Club and Osasuna. And the members elect the president themselves, by direct vote.

By the club's latest count, there are more than a hundred and fifty thousand members. Around the world there are also about twelve hundred and seventy official supporters' clubs, on all five continents.

[warmly] And the members' votes run right through the story of this stadium. The land it stands on was bought after the first referendum in the club's history. In May 1978, with Franco gone, they went to the polls to choose a new president. In December 2021 they voted again, to let the club borrow for these works. The club says no referendum of its own had ever drawn more voters.

When Spotify's name went on the shirt and on the stadium, the members' delegates ratified the deal, by six hundred and twenty-five votes to forty-nine.

In March 2026, more than a hundred and fourteen thousand members could vote for president. Nearly fifty thousand did, and Joan Laporta was re-elected. That September, the delegates' assembly approved more financing to finish the job.

The model in front of you is the stadium they voted to pay for. The ground, the name on the shirt and the president: here, the members decide.

[curious] In the year 2000, a boy of thirteen from Rosario, in Argentina, had a problem. He had a hormone deficiency that was holding back his growth, and he needed treatment. None of the clubs that looked at him would guarantee to pay for it.

That September he came to Barcelona for a trial. Charly Rexach, the club's technical secretary, went to watch. [amused] A walk round the pitch that should have taken three or four minutes took him fifteen, because he couldn't stop watching the smallest boy out there. As Rexach tells it to ESPN, he turned to the two coaches beside him and said: "Sign him. Don't even think about it."

The signing took weeks of talks.

[pause] On the fourteenth of December, Rexach met the boy's agent, Horacio Gaggioli, and Josep Maria Minguella, who had helped bring him over, at a tennis club in the city, the story goes. He asked a waiter for a paper napkin, and wrote on it. In a few lines, the technical secretary of FC Barcelona undertook, under his sole responsibility and despite some contrary opinions, to sign the player Lionel Messi. The two men were his witnesses.

It was a moral promise, not a contract. But it held. At thirteen, Messi crossed the Atlantic and joined the club's under-fourteens.

The napkin itself was sold at auction in 2024. You won't find it in this museum. What you will find is what came after it.

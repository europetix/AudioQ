The first time the Camp Nou filled up was on its opening day, the twenty-fourth of September 1957. It was La Mercè, the city's feast day.

The morning was solemn. The archbishop said Mass before a large statue of the Virgin of Montserrat, and there was a performance of Handel's Hallelujah. Only about half the seats were taken.

By the afternoon the place was packed. The big Catalan clubs paraded round the pitch, with Espanyol, the city's other club, at the back. There were sardanas, the Catalan circle dance. And about eleven thousand white doves were let go into the sky.

The ceremony closed with the Spanish flag raised and the national anthem played, the whole stadium standing. This was Franco's Spain, and that was part of the day too.

Then came the football. Barça played a team billed as a Warsaw eleven, and won four goals to two. More than ninety thousand people watched, though the works weren't finished. The first goal in the new stadium came after eleven minutes, from Eulogio Martínez.

His goal that night was the first of thousands in that bowl.

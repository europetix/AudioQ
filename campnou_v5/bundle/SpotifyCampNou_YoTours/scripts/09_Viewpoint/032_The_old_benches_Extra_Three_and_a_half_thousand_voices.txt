In November 1974, the club turned seventy-five. On the twenty-seventh, to mark it, a new anthem was sung in this stadium for the very first time: the Cant del Barça, the Song of Barça.

The words were by two writers, Jaume Picas and Josep Maria Espinàs, and the music by Manuel Valls. Some three and a half thousand singers from seventy-eight choirs gathered on the pitch to sing it. Oriol Martorell conducted.

They didn't sing only that. The same evening, as the club remembers it, the stadium heard songs that had been banned under Franco. One was the Cant de la Senyera, sung there for the first time ever.

It was a year before Franco died. In those same years, the club's board put Catalan back on the stadium's announcements.

The song itself, in Catalan, says that it doesn't matter where you come from. Look at the bowl in front of you, and imagine it full, singing it.

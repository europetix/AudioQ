These benches came out of the old stadium, and the club put them here for visitors. So, if there's room, you're sitting where its legendary players sat.

But the stadium in front of you was never only the club's. In 1982 it staged the opening ceremony and the first game of the World Cup. That November, the stadium held something else entirely.

Pope John Paul the Second said Mass here, before a hundred and twenty thousand people. It was the first time a Pope had said Mass in Catalonia. He spoke some words in Catalan, to a stadium that was, the club's history pages say, exceptionally silent.

In August 1992 came the Olympics, and Spain met Poland here in the football final. It was three–two, and Kiko scored the winner in the last minute. Two Barça players were in Spain's team, Pep Guardiola and Albert Ferrer, less than three months after winning the European Cup at Wembley. It was Spain's first Olympic gold in football.

And one night here belonged to two other clubs altogether. In May 1999, at the Champions League final, Bayern Munich led Manchester United from the sixth minute almost to the end. Then, in the dying seconds, two substitutes, Teddy Sheringham and Ole Gunnar Solskjær, scored twice.

More recently, Barça's women filled it twice in a month, with world-record crowds.

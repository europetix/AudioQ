La Masia, the club's academy, is named after a farmhouse, and the farmhouse is older than you might think. It was built in 1702. It came with the land the club bought for its new stadium, and before any young player slept there, it had another job. It was the workshop where the model of the stadium was made, and a work area for its architects and builders.

Later it became the club's headquarters. Then, in 1979, it became a home: a residence for the young players the club was bringing up.

Of about sixty boys, twelve lived in the farmhouse. The other forty-eight lived under the Gol Nord, the stand behind one goal, inside the stadium itself. The club's history says the boys in the farmhouse could see the stadium through their bedroom windows.

It wasn't easy. Andrés Iniesta arrived at twelve, and he later said that leaving his family "was one of the worst days I have experienced."

The club names Xavi, Iniesta and Messi among the champions who came through. In the 2010 World Cup final, seven of Spain's starters were Barça players.

In 2011 the residence moved to a new building at the training ground.

If the farmhouse is in the photographs here, find it: a farmer's house that ended up as a football school. Most of the boys slept under a stand, a few steps from the pitch they wanted to play on.

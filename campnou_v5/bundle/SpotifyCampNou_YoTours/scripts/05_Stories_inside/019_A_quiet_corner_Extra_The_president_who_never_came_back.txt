Three weeks into the Spanish Civil War, on the sixth of August 1936, the president of FC Barcelona drove out of Madrid and up into the mountains. He wanted to visit Republican troops at the front. Near the Alto del León pass, without realising it, his car crossed the front line into ground held by the Nationalists. He was identified, and shot without trial.

His name was Josep Sunyol. He was born in Barcelona in 1898, and in 1935 the members made him president by acclamation, without needing a vote. He was a politician too, a leader of Esquerra Republicana de Catalunya, the Republican Left of Catalonia, and a member of the Spanish parliament. And he owned a weekly paper, La Rambla. Its motto was sport and citizenship.

Three men died with him: a journalist, a militia lieutenant and their driver. In Barcelona, nobody knew for about a week.

He was shot by Franco's troops, and his remains have never been found. Searches in 2010 and 2022, with the club's support, found nothing.

The war went on, and the club kept a place for him. For more than a year, its board called him the absent president.

In 1996 a memorial plaque was put up near the place where he died, in a park in Guadarrama.

In August 2026, ninety years on, the club laid flowers there.

He was president for about a year, and there is no grave you can visit. What he left behind is the motto his paper carried. Sport and citizenship.

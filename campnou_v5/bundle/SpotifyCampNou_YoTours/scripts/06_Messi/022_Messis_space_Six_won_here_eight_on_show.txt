Six of these golden balls he won as a Barça player: in 2009, 2010, 2011 and 2012, then in 2015 and 2019. He won eight in all. The last two came after he had left. He came from Rosario as a boy and played here until 2021.

The Ballon d'Or, the golden ball, is football's best-known prize for a single player. This museum shows all eight of his. The eighth, from 2023, arrived as his own gift in 2024, to stand beside the others.

Count them, if you can see the whole row. Beside them are his Golden Boots. He won the European Golden Shoe six times, as well.

Then there are the numbers. Six hundred and seventy-two goals for this club: more than Pelé scored for Santos, and the most anyone has scored for one club. In 2012 alone he scored ninety-one, beating the record for a calendar year that Gerd Müller had set in 1972. And he won thirty-five major trophies here.

The club's page for this space calls him "the best player in our history".

He is also part of a longer line. By the club's count, Barça players have won twelve men's Ballons d'Or. The first went to Luis Suárez, who in 1960 became the first Spanish-born winner. The last six were Messi's. And twelve, the club notes, is exactly the same number as Real Madrid.

Six won in these colours. Eight on the shelf.

Before 1992, Barça had reached two European Cup finals, and lost them both. In Bern in 1961, against Benfica, they lost three–two and hit the woodwork four times. The goalposts, the club adds, were square in those days. In 1986, against Steaua Bucharest, they lost on penalties.

The team that finally did it belonged to Johan Cruyff, back at the club as coach, who had built the side people called the Dream Team. On the twentieth of May 1992 it walked out at Wembley, in London, to face Sampdoria of Italy. They were no strangers. Three years earlier, Cruyff's Barça had beaten them in another European final, played, of all places, in Bern.

One detail survives from the dressing room. Moments before the players went out, the club's history says, Cruyff gave them his last instructions, in Spanish: "Salid y disfrutad." Go out and enjoy it. It sounds simple. At Wembley it was the hardest thing to do.

Ninety minutes went by without a goal, and most of extra time too. Pep Guardiola was in midfield that night. Then, with penalties not far off, Barça won a free kick. Ronald Koeman hit it past the goalkeeper, Pagliuca. Soon after, the captain, Alexanko, came on for Guardiola, and at the end it was Alexanko who lifted the cup. The club had its first European Cup, in its ninety-third year.

If the cups are labelled, find the one from 1992. That's Wembley, and every one beside it came after it.

Two finals lost. The third, at Wembley, changed everything.

On Saturday the twenty-second of November, 2025, Barça came home, after nine hundred and nine days away from its own stadium.

The old stadium played its last match on the twenty-eighth of May, 2023, against Mallorca. Within days the works were under way inside, and the first thing to go was the pitch itself. For two whole seasons, the team played its home games at Montjuïc, the Olympic stadium on the hill.

Then came the night back. More than forty-five thousand people were let in, almost every place the licence then allowed. Barça beat Athletic Club four–nil. Robert Lewandowski scored the first goal in the new stadium. Lamine Yamal set up the second, with the outside of his foot, for Ferran Torres, who went on to score twice.

Nine of the players who took part were Catalan. According to the club's report of the night, you have to go back to 1937 to find a home game with as many.

Now look at the stadium in front of you. It has been playing ever since, every home game, with its top still unfinished.

In March 2026 the Gol Nord, the stand behind one goal, opened again.

Nine hundred and nine days away, and they came home to a building that isn't finished yet.

You can listen to this one in the queue, or standing anywhere by the door. It starts with a small ad, a few lines long, with two spelling mistakes in it.

On the twenty-second of October 1899, in the Miscellaneous section of a Barcelona sports weekly, readers found this: "Our friend and colleague Mr Kans Kamper, of the foot-vall section." The ad went on to say that this former Swiss champion wanted to organise some games in Barcelona. Anyone fond of the sport should come to the paper's offices on Tuesday or Friday evenings, between nine and eleven.

The paper got the game wrong, and the man too. He was Hans Gamper, from Winterthur in Switzerland, living in Barcelona since the late 1890s. He had placed the ad with a fellow Swiss, Walter Wild. In Barcelona he always used the Catalan form of his name: Joan.

The ad worked. On the twenty-ninth of November 1899, twelve men met in the Solé gymnasium, just off the Rambla. They came from Switzerland, England, Germany, Catalonia and elsewhere in Spain. Together they founded a football club, and gave it the name of the city: Barcelona.

Then the twelve needed a president. It wasn't the man who placed the ad. The club's history of its presidents says the others unanimously chose Walter Wild, the first president the club ever had.

And the founder? A Catalan encyclopedia gives the reason: he didn't want to be president. He wanted to play.

So it was a club named for its city, by men from somewhere else, like a good many of the visitors around you today.

So here is the club you've just walked through.

It began with a small advert in a sports paper that couldn't even spell its founder's name. It was shut down for months after its crowd whistled an anthem, and it lost the man who founded it. But it held on to his words that the club must never die. A Dutchman, Johan Cruyff, taught it a way of playing. It put its young players to bed under one of its own stands. It made a boy from Argentina a promise on a paper napkin. And its women's team filled the stadium with the biggest crowds women's football had ever seen.

Now think about where it has lived. Four grounds in its first ten years. Then a proper ground on Carrer de la Indústria, in 1909. Then Les Corts, in 1922, built in three months. Then, in 1957, the new ground that everyone came to call the Camp Nou.

While that stadium was being rebuilt, the team spent two seasons on the hill at Montjuïc, and the museum moved into the club's old ice rink. Now the team is back home, in a stadium still being finished. One more spell away is planned, for when the roof goes on.

As you heard at the start, it has packed up and moved house, again and again.

A club that keeps leaving home, and keeps coming back.

Thank you for spending this time with the club, and with me. Enjoy the rest of your day in Barcelona.

On the seventeenth of January 1968, a new president took office and gave a speech in Catalan. He was Narcís de Carreras, and in the club's English version he began: "Barcelona is more than a football club."

The short form, Més que un club, more than a club, became the motto. [pause] But the idea was older than the man. In April 1933, a club bulletin had already said that FC Barcelona meant "much more than a mere football club".

In between came the Franco years. In 1941, the club's name was given a Spanish form: Club de Fútbol Barcelona. The letters on the crest changed from FCB to CFB. And two of the four red and yellow bars of Catalonia were taken off. The bars came back for the club's fiftieth birthday. The original letters only came back in late 1974. For a generation, even the crest was edited.

In the summer of 2026, the club's institutional vice-president, Elena Fort, put it this way: "Barça is more than a club because it represents a country." That is her reading.

[warmly] Now look at the crest on any shirt you can see, even one a visitor is wearing. At the top, Saint George's red cross and the red and yellow bars. Across the middle, the letters FCB. Below, the club's colours and a ball. Those are the bars they took off, and put back.

[warmly] In 1988, Johan Cruyff came back, this time as coach. He had been a star here as a player in the 1970s. The club's history says that return put into motion "the best era in the history of the club".

His side, the one people called the Dream Team, won four Spanish leagues in a row in the early nineties. It won the Cup Winners' Cup. And in 1992, at Wembley, it brought the club its first European Cup.

But the trophies are only half of it. He trusted the club's youth teams, and he wanted one shared way of playing, from the youngest side to the first team. The club now goes this far: "Cruyff gave birth to the idea that today defines Barça."

It didn't end gently. He left in 1996, after disagreements with the president, Josep Lluís Núñez.

[pause] [quietly] He died in Barcelona in March 2016, of lung cancer.

[warmly] A few days later, before a Clásico at home against Real Madrid, the stands held up a mosaic: Gràcies Johan, thank you, Johan. And when the clock reached the fourteenth minute, for what the club calls his famous number, the whole stadium broke into applause.

His name lives on. In 2019 the club opened the Estadi Johan Cruyff, the stadium where its second team, its women and its under-nineteens play. A statue of him stood at the main stand's entrance from 2019. If the Dream Team's photographs or shirts are here, look at them, then think of that ground: the young players' stadium carries his name.

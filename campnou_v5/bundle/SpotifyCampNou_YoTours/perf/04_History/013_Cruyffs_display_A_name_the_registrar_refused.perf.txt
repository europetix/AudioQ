[curious] Jordi Cruyff has told it himself: "My birth date was decided because of a Real Madrid versus Barcelona game." It starts with his father.

Johan Cruyff signed from Ajax in August 1973. Even then he had to wait. The Dutch federation refused to approve the transfer, saying it had to go through in July, so for weeks he could only play friendlies. When he finally played his first league match, at the end of October, Barça were second from bottom. He scored twice against Granada. It was the first of twenty-six games without defeat, and that season they won the league for the first time in fourteen years.

That winter his wife was due to have their third child, by a planned caesarean. Barça were due in Madrid in the middle of February. So the baby came first, on the ninth of February 1974, eight days before the game.

[pause] In Madrid, Barça won five–nil, and Cruyff scored the second. Juan Manuel Asensi, who scored twice, kept one picture from that night: "The image I remember most is the Bernabéu fans applauding us as we went into the dressing room."

Then came the paperwork. As Jordi tells it, the registrar refused the name. It had to be the Spanish version, Jorge. Jordi is the Catalan form of George, Catalonia's patron saint. His father answered that the boy had a Dutch passport, and that his name was Jordi. Spain's law only caught up in January 1977, when Catalan names could be registered.

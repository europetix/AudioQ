Barça signed Diego Maradona, then the most expensive footballer in the world, nine days before the World Cup opened in the stadium in front of you. That was June 1982. His Argentina, the world champions, lost the opening game one–nil to Belgium.

His time at Barça was short and never dull. He scored in his first official game, though Barça lost it. That December he fell ill with hepatitis. He came back, and in 1983 Barça won two finals against Real Madrid: the Spanish cup, and the league cup over two legs.

One leg of that league cup final was played at the Bernabéu. Maradona dribbled past the goalkeeper, Agustín, and then watched a defender, Juan José, crash into the post trying to stop him, before he scored. [pause] And the club's history records what happened next: the Real Madrid fans stood up to applaud his goal.

The rest went badly. A tackle by Athletic's Andoni Goikoetxea left him with a serious ankle injury. He came back again, but the 1984 cup final against Athletic ended in a fight on the pitch. He was banned for three months, and that summer he left for Napoli, with thirty-eight goals in fifty-eight games behind him.

The Bernabéu would do it again, in November 2005, when Barça won three–nil there. An eighteen-year-old called Messi, playing his first Clásico, set up the first goal. Ronaldinho scored the other two, and at the final whistle the Madrid crowd gave him a standing ovation.

[warmly] Two players in Barça's shirt, twenty-two years apart, and two Madrid crowds on their feet.

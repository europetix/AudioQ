[wry] This is the story of a stadium whose real name nobody used. You won't need to look at anything until the end.

For more than forty years, its official name was the Estadi del FC Barcelona, the FC Barcelona stadium. But nobody called it that. Everyone said camp nou, which simply means the new ground.

New, compared with Les Corts. That ground had opened in 1922, against the Scottish club St Mirren, and it went up in just three months. It held twenty-two thousand at first, then tens of thousands, and still not enough.

The site for something bigger changed twice before a stone was laid. It was settled by a president elected in 1953, Francesc Miró-Sans. His campaign slogan, as the club's history pages give it in English, was: "We need, we want and we will have a new grounds."

[pause] He got them. In March 1954, about sixty thousand people came to watch a first stone being laid. The architects were Francesc Mitjans and Josep Soteras, with Lorenzo García-Barbón. [wry] And Mitjans, the club points out, was the president's cousin.

The bill was less funny. The stadium cost more than four times the estimate, and the club says the debt was widely blamed for the lean years that followed.

Then, at the turn of the century, the members voted once more. Just over sixty-eight per cent chose to make the nickname official.

Now look at the stadium in front of you, and say its name.

The nickname won.

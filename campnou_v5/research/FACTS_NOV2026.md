## 0. Method and keys

- **Tools.** WebSearch and WebFetch only. One page at a time per site, no bursts. No curl, no browser, no CAPTCHA. All reads were done on 14 Nov 2026.
- **WebFetch caveat.** WebFetch returns a model summary, and verbatim excerpts are capped at about 125 characters. So every "verbatim" quote is **as relayed**. **Re-check the exact wording against the page before quoting on air.** The original language is marked: (EN), (CA), (ES).
- **Grades.**
  - **OFF**: official. FC Barcelona (fcbarcelona.com / .es / .cat and its regional mirrors, barcaacademy.fcbarcelona.com, espaibarca.fcbarcelona.com, the club's annual reports and statutes), the Ajuntament de Barcelona, the Generalitat. Also, for their own facts only: UEFA, FIFA, the Holy See, Limak (contractor) and Nikken Sekkei (architect). **OFF pages contain slips and the club's own superlatives**; both are flagged.
  - **SEC**: a named secondary source read directly: scholarly works, reputable press (La Vanguardia, El Periódico, ARA, El País, 3Cat/Esport3, betevé, Catalan News, El Nacional, Crònica Global, The Athletic, BBC, Guardian, Reuters/AFP/AP reprints) and named historians.
  - **SNIP**: a search-result snippet or title only.
  - **WIKI**: lead only, never sole support.
- **Verdicts.**
  - **OK**: safe to state as written.
  - **HEDGE**: true in substance, or sources conflict; use the safe wording.
  - **LEGEND**: tradition or tour lore; script only as "the story goes…".
  - **UNVERIFIED**: do not script.
  - **ERROR**: a claim found in circulation, or on an official page, that is wrong or contradicted.
  - **DATED**: a 2025–2026 fact that may change. Re-check before release.
- **Club superlatives** ("the most visited museum", "the best player in our history", "largest immersive room in Europe in a sports museum") are graded OFF for *what the club says*, and scripted **attributed** ("the club calls it…").
- **Politics.** Franco-era, Catalan-identity and rivalry material is attributed and even-handed: what happened, who says what, and where historians disagree.
- **Sources** are listed in §24 as [O…] (FC Barcelona), [P…] (other official bodies) and [S…] (secondary).
- **Blocked or failed this session** (none retried): listed in §24.
- **Tally (§1–17, 276 numbered rows; primary verdict):** OK 222 · HEDGE 28 · DATED 9 (plus 30 OK rows also flagged DATED) · UNVERIFIED 11 · LEGEND 3 · ERROR 3. §18 adds 43 popular errors and official-page slips.

## 20. Story bank (strongest, all from rows above)

1. **The ad with two spelling mistakes** (1.3–1.7): "Kans Kamper" of the "foot-vall" section; twelve men in a gym off the Rambla; a club founded by foreigners and named for the city.
2. **The colours nobody wrote down** (3.1–3.5): half blue, half claret; an English school's rugby shirt, says the club; Basel, says the legend; the minutes are silent.
3. **Backsides on a wall** (2.3–2.7): the Carrer de la Indústria and the word *culer*, the club's own story, and the man who doubts it.
4. **The whistle that closed the club** (4.1–4.8): June 1925, British sailors, God Save the King applauded; six months' closure; the founder forced out; his death in 1930 (quiet tone).
5. **The president who never came back** (4.9–4.16): Sunyol's wrong turn across the front, August 1936; the "absent president"; no grave.
6. **Eleven–one** (4.19–4.24): what is certain, what the players said, what Madrid says, and why both cities still argue. Even-handed.
7. **The refugee signed at half past eight** (5.1–5.4, 5.22–5.23): Kubala, Hungaria, Samitier; seven goals in a match and a newspaper that said he played badly.
8. **The stadium the nickname named** (5.8–5.21): a site that moved twice, a president's cousin as architect, four times the budget, 11,000 doves, and forty years as "Estadi del FC Barcelona" while everyone said Camp Nou.
9. **The farmhouse that drew the stadium** (9.1–9.7): 1702 Can Planes, first the architects' model workshop, then the boys' home; most of them actually slept under the Gol Nord, looking at the pitch.
10. **Words older than the man** (3.18–3.22): "more than a club", 1968 and 1933; Catalan back on the PA; an anthem sung by three and a half thousand voices in 1974, a year before Franco died.
11. **Go out and have fun** (7.1–7.19): Cruyff the player (0–5 in Madrid), Cruyff the coach (Dream Team), Wembley 1992 after two lost finals; the motto on his statue.
12. **Nine days before** (6.2–6.4, 8.3–8.6): Maradona signs, then the World Cup opens here and his Argentina lose; hepatitis, an injury, a brawl, Napoli.
13. **The night the Bernabéu stood up** (8.10): Ronaldinho, 19 Nov 2005, and an 18-year-old's assist.
14. **A napkin, a tennis club and a promise** (10.1–10.6): 14 Dec 2000; 52 words; a moral, not legal, contract; sold at auction; 672 goals later.
15. **Six out of six** (11.3–11.6): Guardiola's 2009, Iniesta in the 93rd minute, Rome, Abu Dhabi and a goal scored with the chest.
16. **The shirt that paid to be worn** (14.7): 107 years without a logo; then Barça paid UNICEF.
17. **From a charity match without colours to 91,648** (13.1–13.4, 6.13): the women's team's half-century; Gothenburg, Oslo; Alexia and Aitana's Ballons d'Or.
18. **The members own it** (14.1–14.5): no owner, 114,504 voters, referendums on the stadium and the shirt.
19. **The ice rink museum** (16.1–16.24): from 950 m² under the Tribuna in 1984 to a temporary home in the old ice rink, a 78-metre wall of screen, and a new museum waiting inside the stadium.
20. **A building in the middle of becoming itself** (15.1–15.18, 17.4): the 1957 bowl kept, the top rebuilt, 3,000 repairs instead of 800, a roof to come, and one more exile to Montjuïc.

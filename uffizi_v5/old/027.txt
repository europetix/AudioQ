 Luca Signorelli
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The room of Luca Signorelli, master of the painted human body in motion.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Powerful late-fifteenth-century painting by Luca Signorelli of Cortona — muscular, sculptural nudes and
 dramatic religious scenes, including a circular Holy Family that fed directly into Michelangelo's most famous
 panel.
 LISTEN FOR
 Why Michelangelo owed this painter a real debt, and the work to connect to what's coming.


 Stand here and look at bodies, because that is what Signorelli is about — and it's the reason a far greater
 name owed him a real and lasting debt.

 Luca Signorelli, from the hill town of Cortona, was the great painter of the muscular human figure in the
 generation just before Michelangelo. Where Botticelli gives you flowing line and Perugino gives you
 sweetness, Signorelli gives you anatomy: tense, sculptural, twisting nudes, figures caught at the violent
 peak of movement, the body understood as a structure of bone and straining muscle beneath the skin.
 His masterpiece is the tremendous fresco of the Last Judgment in the cathedral at Orvieto, a whole wall
 of writhing, falling, resurrecting nude bodies — one of the boldest displays of the human form in all of
 Italian art.

 Here, look for his circular Holy Family, a tondo in which the figures are knotted into a compact,
 twisting, sculptural group. Keep it firmly in your eye, because in just a few rooms you'll reach
 Michelangelo's Doni Tondo — also a round Holy Family of muscular, twisting figures — and
 Michelangelo studied Signorelli closely, learning from exactly this. The line runs straight from this
 room to that one: Signorelli is, in a real sense, the bridge between the early Renaissance's first
 fascination with the nude and Michelangelo's total, world-changing command of it. Look at how these
 figures are built and how they move, then continue on. Whenever you're ready, the next track picks up
 in the room that follows.

 Simone Martini and the Sienese
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The room of Sienese fourteenth-century painting — smaller, jewel-bright gold-ground panels, with one tall,
 dazzling triptych as the centrepiece.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Sienese panels from the early to mid-1300s: works by Simone Martini and by the brothers Pietro and
 Ambrogio Lorenzetti. Bright, saturated colour, flowing line, deeply tooled gold. The room's showpiece is a
 tall gold Annunciation.
 LISTEN FOR
 What Siena chose that Florence didn't, who the Lorenzetti were, and which panel to give your time to.


 If Florence chose weight, Siena chose grace — and this room is Siena's case for the other path, made by
 some of the greatest painters of the century.

 These artists worked in the same decades as Giotto, but they kept faith with everything he was leaving
 behind: the gold, the flowing line, the dazzling colour, the love of pattern and surface. Nothing here is
 trying to look heavy or solid. It's trying to look exquisite, and it succeeds completely. Alongside Simone
 Martini you'll find work by the brothers Pietro and Ambrogio Lorenzetti — the same Lorenzetti who
 painted Siena's famous town-hall frescoes of good and bad government, and who were both, most
 likely, carried off by the Black Death of thirteen forty-eight that devastated the city. Their panels here
 show a Siena pushing its own elegant tradition toward more tender, more observed human feeling.

 But the painting to stand in front of is the tall gold Annunciation that dominates the room — one of the
 most beautiful things in the entire gallery, and the high point of Sienese Gothic art. Go and stand in front
 of it, and play the next track.

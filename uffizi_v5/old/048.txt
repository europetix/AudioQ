 The Pollaiuolo Brothers — the Body in Motion
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The room of the Pollaiuolo brothers, pioneers of anatomy and movement, near the Lippi room.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Small but ferociously energetic works by Antonio and Piero del Pollaiuolo — goldsmith-trained Florentines
 obsessed with the muscular human body in violent action, including their tiny panels of the labours of
 Hercules and a famous profile portrait of a young woman.
 LISTEN FOR
 Why these small panels mattered so much to the history of art, and what to look for.


 Don't let the modest size of these panels fool you — the Pollaiuolo brothers helped change how the
 human body was painted forever, and this room is where you meet them.

 Antonio and Piero del Pollaiuolo were trained first as goldsmiths, and they brought a metalworker's fine
 precision and a scientist's restless curiosity to the human figure. Antonio in particular was, by tradition,
 among the first artists ever to dissect human bodies in order to understand the muscles working beneath
 the skin — and it shows in everything he touched. Look at their small panels of the labours of Hercules,
 where the hero strains and twists against giants and monsters: the bodies are taut, sinewy, caught at the
 violent peak of effort, every muscle standing out under tension, the figures lunging into real,
 three-dimensional movement. Nobody before had painted the human body in motion with such
 anatomical conviction, and the lesson fed straight into the great figure-painters who followed —
 Michelangelo above all.

 Look, too — if it has not travelled to an exhibition — for the exquisite profile portrait of a young
 woman shown among these first-floor rooms: cool, precise, her elaborate braided hair and brocade
 costume rendered with a goldsmith's loving care, one of the loveliest portraits of the entire early
 Renaissance. When you've looked your fill, you've reached the end of these first-floor early rooms.
 From here the route moves on to the great sixteenth-century galleries. There's more next door; play the
 next track once you're inside.

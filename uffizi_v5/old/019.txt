 The Tribune — the First Museum
 WHERE YOU ARE
 At the doorways of the octagonal Tribune. You look in from the threshold; the room itself can't be entered.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 A small octagonal chamber blazing with colour: a dome encrusted with mother-of-pearl, walls hung in
 crimson velvet, an inlaid coloured-marble floor, and around the walls the most prized ancient sculptures and
 portraits of the Medici, gathered under a lantern open to the sky.
 LISTEN FOR
 Why this one small room is the ancestor of every museum you have ever walked into.


 Stop at the doorway and look in, and take your time, because you're looking at the place where the
 whole idea of a museum begins.

 The Tribune was built in the early fifteen-eighties by the architect Bernardo Buontalenti for Grand Duke
 Francesco the First, to gather the most precious things the Medici owned into one perfect room — and it
 was designed as a little model of the universe. Look up: the dome is encrusted with thousands of pieces
 of mother-of-pearl, standing for water. The walls are hung with crimson velvet, for fire. The lantern at
 the very top, open to the air, is air itself. And the floor is a great inlaid flower of coloured marbles —
 that's earth. The four elements, in one octagon, with the treasures of the dynasty at the centre.

 Among those treasures stood the Medici Venus, the celebrated ancient marble that drew travellers from
 all over Europe; for two centuries this little room was the climax of the Grand Tour, the place people
 crossed a continent to stand in. You can't walk in today — the inlaid floor can't bear the weight of
 modern crowds — so you take it in from the doors, which is plenty; the whole jewel is visible at once.
 As you look, hold onto the idea: a private treasure-room, deliberately arranged to be shown and
 admired, and in time opened to the public. That idea, born right here, is the reason you can walk through
 everything else in this building today. Beyond the Tribune the gallery turns to the Renaissance outside
 Italy. Walk on, and play the next track.

 A quick word on where you are, because it can be confusing. As the Uffizi reorganises its rooms, a
 group of major early-Renaissance Florentine paintings has been rehoused down here on the first floor —
 works by Filippo Lippi, the Pollaiuolo brothers, and Botticelli — so the story you walked upstairs picks
 up again, briefly, in these rooms.

 The star is Filippo Lippi, one of the leading Florentine painters of the generation after Masaccio, and the
 teacher of Botticelli — you can see his influence in everything from upstairs. A Carmelite friar by
 training, Lippi led a famously scandalous life, and his greatest painting hangs here: the Madonna and
 Child with Two Angels, often called the Lippina. After all the solemn gold Madonnas, it comes as a
 breath of warm, human air. There's a track for it. Go and stand in front of it, and play the next track.

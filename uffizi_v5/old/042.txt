 Bernini — the Room of Sculpture
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The final room of the collection, holding its single most spectacular object: an early marble by Gian Lorenzo
 Bernini.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 A virtuoso marble by the young Bernini — the Martyrdom of Saint Lawrence, the saint writhing on the
 burning gridiron — the only sculpture by the great Baroque master to be seen in Florence.
 LISTEN FOR
 Why this one marble is extraordinary, and the track to play in front of it.


 Save your attention for this last room, because it holds something genuinely rare: a marble by Gian
 Lorenzo Bernini, the supreme sculptor of the Baroque, the man who would later fill Rome with
 fountains and ecstatic saints. This is the only sculpture by him you can see anywhere in Florence.

 It shows the Martyrdom of Saint Lawrence, roasted alive on a gridiron — and Bernini carved it as a
 teenager, barely older than a schoolboy, as a dazzling demonstration of what he could do.

 One note, in the spirit of honesty: this single marble is much in demand for exhibitions, and every so
 often it travels — so on a rare day you may find its place empty. If that happens, the room's Contini
 paintings still repay the few steps, and you can listen to the next track anyway, because the Bernini
 always comes home. If it's here in front of you, go and stand before the writhing marble figure, and play
 the next track.

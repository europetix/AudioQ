 Filippino Lippi & Piero di Cosimo
 WHERE YOU ARE
 A room pairing two distinctive late-fifteenth-century Florentines: the nervous, elegant Filippino Lippi and the
 eccentric Piero di Cosimo.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Two strong personalities side by side: Filippino Lippi — son of Filippo, pupil of Botticelli — with his
 restless, sharp-lined religious paintings, including the Adoration of the Magi he painted to finish a
 commission Leonardo had abandoned; and Piero di Cosimo, the eccentric of his generation, with strange,
 vivid mythologies and luminous devotional works.
 LISTEN FOR
 How to tell these two apart, the painting with a Leonardo connection, and what to enjoy in each.


 Two very different temperaments share this room, and it's a pleasure to play them off against each other.

 Filippino Lippi carried serious bloodlines: he was the son of the friar Filippo Lippi, whose tender
 Madonna hangs downstairs, and the star pupil of Botticelli. But his own style is more nervous and
 electric than either — sharp, flickering line, crowded and agitated compositions, faces full of unease.
 Look for his Adoration of the Magi, and note the curious history: it was painted in fourteen ninety-six
 for the monks of San Donato a Scopeto, to replace the very Adoration that Leonardo had begun for them
 years earlier and abandoned, unfinished, when he left for Milan — that ghostly brown panel you'll meet
 two rooms along. So Filippino was, in a sense, finishing Leonardo's homework.

 Beside him, Piero di Cosimo is the great oddball of Florentine painting — a man Vasari describes as
 living half-wild, eating only hard-boiled eggs cooked in batches, terrified of thunder and refusing to
 have his rooms tidied. His work is unforgettable: strange, dreamlike mythologies full of odd hybrid
 creatures and primitive landscapes, like his Perseus freeing Andromeda, and, on the other hand,
 devotional paintings of glowing, jewel-like clarity. Where Filippino is anxious and linear, Piero is
 uncanny and inventive. Enjoy the contrast, then move on. Take your time, then play the next track in the
 room ahead.

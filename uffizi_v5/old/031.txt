 Raphael and Michelangelo
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The room holding Raphael and Michelangelo together — two of the supreme names of the High Renaissance,
 face to face.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Two titans in one room: Michelangelo's Doni Tondo, the only finished panel painting by his hand in

 LISTEN FOR
 What you've reached, the rivalry in the air, and the order to take the two masters in.


 You've reached the summit. This room sets two of the three supreme artists of the High Renaissance
 side by side — Raphael and Michelangelo — and the contrast between them is one of the great
 pleasures of the whole gallery.

 On one side, Raphael: harmony, sweetness, effortless grace, the lessons of Perugino and Leonardo fused
 into something serenely, almost impossibly perfect. On the other, Michelangelo: a sculptor first and
 always, who painted bodies as though he were carving them, full of muscular tension and difficulty, in
 colours that almost vibrate off the panel. They were rivals as much as contemporaries — Michelangelo,
 the older and pricklier man, openly disdained the young charmer Raphael — and Florence loved to
 weigh one against the other. Now you can do it yourself, in a single room.

 There's a track for each of the two headline works. Look out, too, for Raphael's paired portraits of
 Agnolo and Maddalena Doni — the very couple whose marriage Michelangelo's round painting was
 made to celebrate, so the two masters are literally working for the same patrons here. Start with
 Michelangelo's Doni Tondo, the round painting of the Holy Family in its elaborate carved frame. Go
 and stand in front of it, and play the next track.

 LISTEN FOR
 Why one painting carried up from the north jolted the way Florence painted.


 This room is really about one painting, and about a jolt that Florentine art took from the north — proof
 that the Renaissance wasn't only an Italian story.

 The enormous triptych here is by Hugo van der Goes, painted in Bruges in the fourteen-seventies for
 Tommaso Portinari, the Medici bank's agent in Flanders. When it was shipped to Florence and installed
 in fourteen eighty-three, it stopped local painters in their tracks — because it was done in oil, a
 technique the Flemish had mastered and the Italians were only beginning to try, and it carried a depth of
 colour, a softness of light and a patience of detail that egg tempera simply could not match.

 It's worth knowing the contrast before you look: everything around it on this floor is Italian tempera,
 warm and linear; this is northern oil, cool and exact. Go and stand close in front of it, and play the next
 track.

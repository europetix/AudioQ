 Veronese
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The room of Paolo Veronese, the great Venetian master of light, splendour and silvery colour.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Sumptuous, festive painting by Veronese — religious and mythological scenes staged like grand Venetian
 banquets, full of silver and rose colour, shimmering fabrics, architecture and crowds, the painter of Venice at
 its most magnificent.
 LISTEN FOR
 Why Veronese is the painter of Venetian splendour, his run-in with the Inquisition, and what to enjoy.


 Paolo Veronese is the painter of pure Venetian splendour, and this room is a feast — quite literally,
 because he loved to stage even the most sacred subjects as glittering, well-dressed banquets.

 Where Tintoretto gives you drama and engulfing shadow, Veronese gives you light, silver, rose and
 gold, and sheer worldly magnificence: grand classical architecture, shimmering silks and velvets,
 handsome aristocratic crowds, faithful dogs and musicians, everything bathed in a cool, pearly, even
 daylight. He painted the good life of the richest city in Europe and calmly lifted it to the level of the
 divine. That love of luxury once landed him in real trouble: hauled before the Inquisition to explain why
 he had filled a painting of the Last Supper with dogs, dwarfs, drunkards and German soldiers, he was
 ordered to remove them — and instead simply renamed the enormous picture Feast in the House of Levi
 and carried serenely on, an early artist quietly insisting on his own freedom.

 Look here for that unmistakable shimmer of fabric and light, the soaring painted architecture, the festive
 crowd, and the cool silvery key of his colour, quite unlike anyone else's. Enjoy the magnificence — this
 is the high noon of Venetian painting, the last great blaze before the century turns — then move on
 toward the last of the Venetian spaces. Whenever you're ready, the next track picks up in the room that
 follows.

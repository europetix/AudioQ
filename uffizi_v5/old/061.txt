 Titian — the Dedicated Room
 WHERE YOU ARE
 In the room given entirely to Titian, the supreme master of the Venetian Renaissance.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 A room devoted to Titian (Tiziano Vecellio): the Venus of Urbino, his earlier Flora, and other works, hung
 together — with Sebastiano del Piombo's Fornarina nearby — behind protective glass, the colour and
 sensuality of Venice at its absolute peak.
 LISTEN FOR
 Who Titian was, why he matters so enormously, and the one painting here to give your full attention.


 Stand still a moment as you come in, because you're in the presence of the greatest painter Venice ever
 produced, and one of the greatest in the history of the world. Titian — Tiziano Vecellio — dominated
 European painting for most of the sixteenth century. He painted emperors, kings and popes; Charles the
 Fifth, the most powerful man in Europe, is said to have stooped to pick up a brush Titian dropped. And
 he lived into great old age, his style growing freer, looser and more astonishing right to the very end,
 until he was almost inventing modern painting centuries early.

 The museum has gathered several of his works in this dedicated room. There's the earlier Flora, a
 golden-haired beauty offering flowers, all warm flesh and Venetian glow, and nearby hangs Sebastiano
 del Piombo's so-called Fornarina, three celebrated 'ladies' kept together behind protective glass. But the
 painting everyone comes for — and the one with its own track — is the Venus of Urbino, reclining
 along the far wall, one of the most famous and most influential nudes ever painted. Go and stand in front
 of the Venus of Urbino, and play the next track.

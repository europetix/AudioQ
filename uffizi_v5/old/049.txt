 The Sixteenth Century — Lombardy
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The first room of the first-floor galleries, opening the sixteenth-century rooms with painting from Lombardy
 in the north of Italy.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Northern Italian painting of the early 1500s from Lombardy — the region around Milan, marked by soft
 modelling, atmospheric light and the long shadow of Leonardo, who spent his most productive years in
 Milan.
 LISTEN FOR
 Where you are now, why the gallery changes character here, and what Lombardy brought to the new century.


 You've come down a floor and forward a century, and the whole character of the museum changes here.
 The second-floor rooms walked you from the birth of the Renaissance to its towering summit; these
 first-floor galleries — rehung and reopened only recently, in a major modernisation the museum calls
 the New Uffizi — carry the story on into the sixteenth century and the seventeenth. Notice that the
 colour on the walls now changes with the schools of painting, so let the rooms themselves guide you as
 you go.

 This first room is Lombardy, the region around Milan in the far north of Italy. Its painters worked in the
 long wake of Leonardo da Vinci, who spent his most productive years in Milan and left behind a whole
 generation of artists chasing his discoveries — that soft, smoky modelling he called sfumato, the subtle
 fall of light, the melting of edges into shadow. Look for that softness here: contours that dissolve rather
 than cut, faces that emerge gently out of darkness, a quiet, atmospheric naturalism quite different from
 the bright, linear Florentine painting you've spent the morning with upstairs.

 It's a deliberately measured, low-key way to begin the century, before things turn strange and violent in
 the rooms just ahead. Take your time, then play the next track in the room ahead.

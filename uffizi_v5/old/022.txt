 The Southern Corridor — Over the Arno
 WHERE YOU ARE
 The short southern corridor that joins the two long wings, with windows looking straight down the Arno.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 The short connecting corridor at the river end of the U, lined like the others with ancient statues, but famous
 for its windows: a head-on view down the Arno, with the Ponte Vecchio just below and the hills of the
 Oltrarno beyond.
 LISTEN FOR
 Why this corridor is worth stopping in, what you can see from it, and where you go next.


 You've reached the bottom of the U — the short corridor that crosses the river end of the building and
 links its two long wings. Don't hurry through it, because it holds the best view in the whole gallery, and
 a glimpse of the Medici at their most secretive.

 Like the other corridors it's lined with ranks of ancient marble, but go to the tall windows. From here
 you look straight down the Arno: the Ponte Vecchio almost directly below, still crowded with its
 goldsmiths' shops as it has been for centuries, and beyond it the river bending away between the bridges,
 with the green hills of the Oltrarno — the 'other side' of the Arno — rising softly on the far bank. Look
 carefully along the top of the bridge and the buildings beside it and you can trace the Vasari Corridor,
 the private elevated passage the Medici built so they could walk, unseen, from these offices all the way
 to the Palazzo Pitti on the far side, never touching the public street.

 It's the same view travellers have stopped at this exact spot to admire for hundreds of years, and a good
 moment to rest your eyes and your feet before the gallery's grand finale. When you're ready, turn and
 head back up the western side of the building, where the High Renaissance begins and the greatest
 names of all are waiting. Play the next track as you start up the Western Corridor.

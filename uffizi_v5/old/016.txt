 Botticelli — Primavera
 WHERE YOU ARE
 In front of the Primavera — slightly larger than the Venus and painted on wood, a dark grove crowded with
 nine figures.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Nine figures in a shadowed orange grove strewn with flowers: Venus presiding in the centre under a
 blindfolded Cupid; the Three Graces dancing at the left; Mercury at the far edge; and at the right, the wind
 seizing a nymph who transforms, flowers spilling from her mouth, into Flora, goddess of spring. Tempera on
 panel, around 1480.
 LISTEN FOR
 How to read this puzzle from right to left, why scholars still argue, and the detail underfoot you mustn't miss.


 If the Birth of Venus is simple to feel, the Primavera, painted around fourteen eighty, is a puzzle to read
 — and people have argued about exactly what it means for five hundred years.

 Read this one right to left, against the way you normally scan. At the far right, the slate-blue wind-god
 Zephyr lunges out of the dark trees and seizes the fleeing nymph Chloris; look closely and you'll see
 flowers already spilling from her mouth as she transforms, mid-step, into the figure right beside her —
 Flora, the goddess of spring, who strides forward in a flower-printed gown, scattering blossoms from a
 fold she holds like an apron. In the centre stands Venus, calm, presiding, one hand raised, with a
 blindfolded Cupid aiming his flaming arrow above her head. To the left, the Three Graces dance in a
 slow ring in transparent gowns — follow their linked hands and interlaced fingers, a piece of pure visual
 music — and at the very edge Mercury, in red, turns his back on the whole scene and reaches up with
 his staff to brush away a few small clouds.

 It's usually read as an allegory of spring, or of love ripening from raw desire on the right into something
 refined and civilised on the left — possibly painted for a Medici wedding, possibly a Neoplatonic
 diagram of love rising toward the divine. The meaning stays open, and that openness is part of its hold.
 What isn't in doubt is the craft: look down at the grass beneath their feet, scattered with something close
 to two hundred identifiable species of plant, each painted with real botanical care, many of them
 genuine Tuscan spring flowers. Stand at the centre of the room, between this and the Birth of Venus,
 and you're in the single most concentrated spot of beauty in the building. The next rooms, ten to twelve,
 are closed for renovation, so carry on to the next open room, where one huge Flemish painting is
 waiting. Play the next track there.

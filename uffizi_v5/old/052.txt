 Andrea del Sarto and Pontormo
 WHERE YOU ARE
 A room linking the serene Andrea del Sarto to his brilliant, troubled pupil Pontormo — the hinge between
 High Renaissance and Mannerism.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Painting by Andrea del Sarto, the 'faultless' Florentine master, set beside early work by his pupil Jacopo
 Pontormo — the moment a perfectly balanced art begins to twist into the nervous, original strangeness of
 Mannerism.
 LISTEN FOR
 How to watch one style turn into the next, master beside pupil, in a single room.


 This room stages a hand-off between two generations, and if you look from one wall to the next you can
 almost watch the temperature of Florentine painting change before your eyes.

 You met Andrea del Sarto upstairs — the painter Vasari called faultless, all warm glowing colour, soft
 smoky shadow and serene, unstrained balance, the very summit of calm Florentine skill. Here he is set
 beside his most gifted pupil, Jacopo Pontormo, and the contrast between them is the whole story of
 where painting went next. Pontormo took his master's flawless technique and bent it into something
 restless and deeply original: acidic, unexpected colours — sharp pinks, sulphurous yellows, cold greens
 — figures with a nervous, elongated grace, and compositions that feel emotionally charged and slightly
 off balance, as if the floor had tilted beneath the saints. He was, by every account, a solitary, anxious
 man; he even kept a strange, fretful diary recording his ailments and the eggs he ate, and that inwardness
 seems to seep straight into the unsettled intensity of his painting.

 Look from the calm, secure Sarto to the troubled, searching Pontormo and you are watching the High
 Renaissance turn into Mannerism in real time — in the work of a teacher and the pupil who loved him,
 learned everything from him, and then broke his serene rules wide open. Onward to the next room, and
 play the next track there.

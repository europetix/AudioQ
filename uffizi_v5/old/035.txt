 LISTEN FOR
 Why Vasari called him 'the faultless painter,' the work to find, and the human story behind the skill.


 Andrea del Sarto is the great Florentine painter of the generation right after Raphael and Michelangelo
 — and the one Vasari famously called the painter 'without errors,' the faultless painter.

 Stand in front of his work and you'll see what that meant: a warm, glowing palette, soft smoky shadow
 that wraps the figures in atmosphere, and a technical command so complete it never seems to strain.
 Look for the Madonna of the Harpies, his masterpiece here — the Virgin standing serenely on a tall
 pedestal carved with the little harpy-like figures that give the painting its name, two saints at her sides,
 the whole group monumental, balanced and beautifully coloured. Notice how the shadows seem to
 breathe; that soft, smoke-like modelling is his signature, and it points the way toward the strange,
 restless painters who came straight out of his workshop.

 There's a human story under all that skill, too. Andrea spent a spell at the French court of King Francis
 the First, then was sent home with royal money to buy art for the king — and, by Vasari's telling, spent
 it on a house for himself and his wife Lucrezia instead, and never went back. Lucrezia, beautiful and by
 reputation difficult, was his constant model; you may be looking at her face in these very Madonnas.
 Vasari, who admired him, also wondered aloud whether all that effortless perfection lacked the last
 spark of fire of the very greatest. Judge for yourself, then move on. Carry on through; the next track will
 meet you in the next room.

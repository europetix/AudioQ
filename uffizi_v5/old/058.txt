 The Vasari Corridor Entrance
 WHERE YOU ARE
 At the entrance to the Vasari Corridor — a doorway leading to the famous elevated passage over the city. The
 corridor itself requires a separate reserved ticket and isn't part of this route.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 The entrance to the Vasari Corridor: the kilometre-long private passage Giorgio Vasari built in 1565 to link
 the Uffizi, across the Ponte Vecchio, to the Pitti Palace — so the Medici could move between government
 and home unseen.
 LISTEN FOR
 What lies through this doorway, the speed it was built, and why you're not going in.


 Pause at this doorway, because through it runs one of the most romantic pieces of architecture in all of
 Florence — even though you won't be walking it today.

 This is the entrance to the Vasari Corridor, the long, private, elevated passage that Giorgio Vasari built
 in fifteen sixty-five — and built astonishingly fast, in just a few months, on the orders of Duke Cosimo
 the First, to be ready for a grand Medici wedding. It runs for nearly a kilometre along the tops of the
 buildings, out over the shops of the Ponte Vecchio, all the way to the Palazzo Pitti across the river, so
 that the Medici could pass between their offices here — the Uffizi were literally the 'offices' of the
 Tuscan state — and their home in the Pitti without ever setting foot in the public street, or mixing with
 their own subjects. There's a story that the duke had the butchers' shops on the bridge replaced with
 goldsmiths, so the family wouldn't have to smell the meat as they passed above; the jewellers are still
 there today. For centuries the corridor was hung with the gallery's famous collection of artists'
 self-portraits, the very ones you may have walked among earlier.

 The corridor has reopened after a long restoration, but it needs its own reserved ticket and a separate
 visit, so this route stays in the main gallery. Glance through, picture the grand dukes hurrying along it
 high above the Arno, and walk on. Head through whenever you like, and the next track will be ready
 next door.

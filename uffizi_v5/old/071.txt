 Painting by Candlelight
 WHERE YOU ARE
 A darkened room of nocturnal scenes lit by candle and lamp — the painters who carried Caravaggio's drama
 across Europe.
 WHAT YOU SEE
 Seventeenth-century 'night pieces': scenes lit by a single hidden candle or lamp, by the international followers
 of Caravaggio — among them the Dutchman Gerrit van Honthorst, known in Italy as Gherardo delle Notti,
 'Gerard of the Nights.'
 LISTEN FOR
 How one painter's idea became a European craze, and what to look for in the dark.


 This deliberately dimmed room is a tribute to influence — to how completely Caravaggio's drama
 spread across the whole of Europe after his death, carried by a generation of painters who turned his
 shadows into a speciality all their own.

 These are night pieces: scenes lit by a single candle, lamp or torch, the flame often half-hidden behind a
 hand or an object, so that the warm light seems to bloom out of the painting itself and model the faces
 gathered around it in glowing gold and deep, velvety shadow. The master of the genre here is the
 Dutchman Gerrit van Honthorst, who worked for years in Italy and so delighted in these effects that the
 Italians affectionately renamed him Gherardo delle Notti — Gerard of the Nights. Look for the trick of
 the concealed light source, for fingers that glow translucent red where the flame shines through them,
 for the intimate hush of figures leaning together around one small fire. It's Caravaggio's harsh tenebrism
 turned tender, warm and domestic.

 These painters, the Caravaggisti, spread that style from Rome to Utrecht to France, making the dramatic
 dark the international language of the early Baroque. Enjoy the warm gloom — let your eyes adjust to it
 — then move toward the very last room of the tour. The next track is in the room that follows — start it
 when you arrive.

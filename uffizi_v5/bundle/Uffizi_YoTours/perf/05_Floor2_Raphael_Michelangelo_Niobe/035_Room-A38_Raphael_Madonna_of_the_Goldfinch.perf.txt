[warmly] This was a wedding present. Vasari tells us Raphael painted it for Lorenzo Nasi, who married in Florence in February 1506.

Raphael was in his early twenties, newly arrived in the city and taking in everything. Look at the shape of the group. The Virgin sits at the top, and the two children settle at her knees, so the three make a calm, steady pyramid. The museum traces that shape to a lost drawing by Leonardo of the Virgin with Saint Anne, shown in a Florentine church, and to a marble Madonna by Michelangelo. And the softness, the way the faces melt into shadow and the hills dissolve into haze, is Leonardo's sfumato.

[pause] Now the bird. The little Saint John holds out a goldfinch, and the Christ Child reaches to touch it. It looks like a game. But the goldfinch was linked to the Passion, so this fragile bird foreshadows Christ's sacrifice. Even the small book in Mary's hand points the same way.

[quietly] Then disaster. In 1547 a landslide destroyed the Nasi family house, and this panel broke into seventeen pieces. It was probably given to the painter Michele di Ridolfo del Ghirlandaio to put back together. After that, it vanishes from the record for a century, until it turns up in the collection of a Medici cardinal.

It was restored again early in this century. Someone, long ago, gathered up the pieces of a wedding present and refused to let it go.

[warmly] A painting about a fragile future, which very nearly had none.

Back in the corridor, turn left. The next doorway, Room A39, is the long Niobe Room: a hall of marble figures fleeing.

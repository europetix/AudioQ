[curious] Two old men, a young wife alone in her garden, and a threat. Lorenzo Lotto painted the story small, and he let the people in it speak.

This is Susanna and the Elders, signed and dated 1517. In the Bible story, two elders surprise Susanna as she bathes. Give in, they say, or we will accuse you of adultery. Look for the words on the painted scrolls. On hers, in Latin, Susanna answers that she would rather die than sin. In the end the young Daniel questions the men separately and catches them out.

[pause] You've crossed into Venice here. Florence built its pictures on drawing, on line and design, what it called disegno. Venice built them on colore, on colour and light laid down in oil.

In the same room, Titian painted the churchman Ludovico Beccadelli in Venice. He had once tutored a son of Duke Cosimo the First. The letter in his hand records when and where the portrait was made.

In Room D21, the narrow room beyond, hangs El Greco's Saint John the Evangelist and Saint Francis, painted in 1600. Born on Crete, where he trained, he worked in Venice and Rome, then settled in Toledo. [lightly] Find the golden cup in John's hand. A small winged dragon is climbing out of it. The legend says he was dared to drink poison, and the poison left the cup as a dragon. His long, strained figures were rediscovered only in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Walk on through Room D21 and through the small antechamber, D22, into Room D23. Look for Titian's Venus of Urbino, the reclining nude with a little dog asleep at her feet.

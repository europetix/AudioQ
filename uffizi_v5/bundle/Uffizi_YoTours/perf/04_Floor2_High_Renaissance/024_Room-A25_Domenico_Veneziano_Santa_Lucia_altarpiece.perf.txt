[wry] Vasari tells a dark story about the man who painted this. He says a jealous rival, Andrea del Castagno, murdered him. It's a gripping tale. It's also impossible. Castagno died in 1457, and Domenico Veneziano outlived him by four years.

Domenico came from Venice and worked in Florence. Around 1445 he painted this altarpiece for the high altar of the little church of Santa Lucia de' Magnoli. And instead of gold, he gave it daylight.

Look at how the light comes in from the right. It falls across the marble loggia and lays soft, exact shadows on the floor. Above the arches, branches of orange trees push into a clear sky. Four saints stand in that light. Saint Francis and John the Baptist are on the left. On the right are Saint Zenobius, a bishop of Florence, and Saint Lucy, holding a dish with her own eyes on it.

[curious] Then drop your gaze to the step under the Virgin's throne. Domenico signed it there, and added a prayer: "Mother of God, have mercy on me". The small scenes that once stood beneath the altarpiece are now scattered between Washington, Cambridge and Berlin.

A younger painter worked as his assistant in the 1430s, and learned what this kind of light could do. His name was Piero della Francesca.

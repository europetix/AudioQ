The man who paid for this painting is in it, pointing at himself. A few years later, he was convicted of fraud.

He was Gaspare del Lama, a businessman and an agent for the bankers' guild. In 1469 he founded a chapel in Santa Maria Novella dedicated to the Magi, and this painting stood over its altar. Find him in the group on the right: the white-haired man in light blue.

Then the kings. Vasari says the old king kneeling at the Child's feet looks like Cosimo the Elder de' Medici. Kneeling in red in the centre is probably his son Piero. The young man with black hair on the right, dressed in black and red, is probably Lorenzo the Magnificent, then in his twenties. The museum calls the whole picture a true family portrait of Medici power.

And look to the far right, for the young blond man who looks straight out at you. He may be Botticelli himself. Painters often placed themselves just like that.

Walk on, still in the Botticelli rooms, to the Primavera, the wide dark grove of nine figures.

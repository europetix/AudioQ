Two round Madonnas frame the Venus. In the Madonna of the Magnificat, Mary is writing.

She writes the opening words of her own hymn, Magnificat anima mea Dominum, "my soul magnifies the Lord", and her son guides her. His hand rests on a pomegranate, whose red seeds recall the blood he will shed. Two angels hold a crown over her head. They have no wings, and they're dressed like fashionable young Florentines. A round painting this big, more than a metre across, may have hung in a court of the Florentine Republic. His Madonna of the Pomegranate hangs on the Venus's other side.

Then find the Calumny of Apelles, a small, crowded panel. Painted around 1495, in the Florence of Savonarola, it closes Botticelli's story here. He recreated a lost picture by the ancient Greek painter Apelles, known only from a written description.

Read it from the right. A king with donkey's ears sits in judgment, advised by Ignorance and Suspicion. Calumny, holding a torch, drags her victim by the hair, while Fraud and Deceit dress her hair. Penitence, in black, turns towards Truth. Truth is naked, and no one else in the picture looks at her.

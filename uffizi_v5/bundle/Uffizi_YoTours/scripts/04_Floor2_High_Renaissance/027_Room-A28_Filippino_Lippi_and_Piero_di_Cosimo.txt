Filippino Lippi grew up with painting all around him. His father was the friar painter Filippo Lippi, and his teacher was Botticelli.

In 1496 Filippino finished this Adoration of the Magi for the Augustinians of San Donato a Scopeto. They had a reason for asking him. Years before, they had ordered the same subject from Leonardo da Vinci, who started it and never finished. Leonardo's abandoned panel is in Room A35, a few rooms on along this floor.

Look at the kneeling man on the left, in a yellow robe lined with fur, holding an astrolabe. He is thought to be Pierfrancesco de' Medici. His sons may be here too, perhaps the crowned king and the young fair-haired man with the vase. One of those sons, Lorenzo, is the Medici whose heirs kept the Primavera in their house.

Now the long, low panel by Piero di Cosimo. It tells the story of Perseus freeing Andromeda from a sea monster, and Perseus appears three times. See if you can find all three, and notice the unlikely musical instruments on the shore. It was probably made for a wedding chamber.

Vasari describes Piero as an eccentric. He boiled his eggs in batches, he was terrified of thunder, and he never let anyone tidy his rooms.

Room A31 has a short Extra on Signorelli: go on through Room A29 and turn into Room A31. The next main stop is Room A35, Leonardo. If you skip the Extra, go back through A27, A26 and A25 to the corridor, turn left, pass the doorway of Room A34 and go into Room A35.

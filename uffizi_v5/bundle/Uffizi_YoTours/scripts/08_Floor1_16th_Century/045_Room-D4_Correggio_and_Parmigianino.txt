Parmigianino isn't really a name. It's an address. It means "the little one from Parma".

You've walked through Room D1, sixteenth-century painting from Lombardy, past D2, Dosso Dossi and his circle. This room belongs to Parma, and to two great painters. Antonio Allegri took his name from his home town, Correggio. He did his greatest work in Parma, painting domes that seem to open straight into the sky. Francesco Mazzola, the younger man, was born in Parma, and the city became his name.

Until the tenth of January 2027, Correggio's Adoration of the Christ Child, which normally hangs in this room, is on loan to Dresden.

Now look at the tallest panel here, Parmigianino's Madonna with the Long Neck. It was never finished.

You might think nobody would want an unfinished altarpiece. Over a century and a half later, a Medici prince did. Grand Prince Ferdinando bought it for his collection, and in his letters he praised it as "drawn as if by Raphael, finished with the soul but without pain". Strange, cool and incomplete, it has been loved for exactly that.

Stand back for a moment before you go close. Notice how the figures crowd one side, while the other side is almost empty.

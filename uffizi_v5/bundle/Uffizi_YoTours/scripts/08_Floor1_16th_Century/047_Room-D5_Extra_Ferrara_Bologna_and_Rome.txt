When Raphael died in Rome in 1520, his style didn't die with him. In these four small rooms you can watch it travel.

Start in D5, with the painters of Ferrara. In Garofalo's Annunciation, the angel holds a stem with three lilies in bloom. Mary turns, caught at prayer. Her eyes look frightened, but her body is already saying yes.

D6 is Bologna, and D7 is Rome, in the shadow of Michelangelo. Here are two of Raphael's own pupils. Giulio Romano painted his Virgin and Child in Rome just after Raphael's death. Look at the baby. He's laughing, with a little bunch of flowers in his fist. In the same room, Perin del Vaga's Child stands on a ledge and blesses you. Find the jewelled pin in the Virgin's hair.

In D8, Francesco Salviati's Charity knots a young mother and three children into a single body. The grouping may remind you of the Doni Tondo upstairs.

From Room D8, go through to the landing of the Buontalenti staircase and into the short passage, D9, leading up toward the corridor. Room D12 opens off it: the small red-winged angel with a lute. Don't take the staircase or enter the closed Rooms D10 and D11, and if the passage is shut, ask a warden for Room D12.

To get his portrait right, the Duke of Urbino sent his armour to Venice months in advance, so that Titian could copy every plate.

Francesco Maria della Rovere was a soldier, commander of the armies of Venice. Titian painted him around 1537 in that polished parade armour, against crimson velvet, a baton gripped in his fist. On the shelf behind him are a helmet crowned with a dragon and the batons of the states he had served: the papacy, Florence and Venice. Find the oak branch too. Rovere means oak, and it recalls his uncle, the warrior pope Julius the Second.

His wife, Eleonora Gonzaga, was painted as his partner: he the man of arms, she the faithful bride. The little dog beside her stands for fidelity.

Here's the link. Their son Guidobaldo is the man who bought Titian's Venus of Urbino in 1538. You'll meet her a few rooms on, in Room D23.

And look for one more duke in armour, painted by Federico Barocci in 1572: their grandson, Francesco Maria the Second. Barocci borrowed his proud pose from Titian's portrait of the grandfather.

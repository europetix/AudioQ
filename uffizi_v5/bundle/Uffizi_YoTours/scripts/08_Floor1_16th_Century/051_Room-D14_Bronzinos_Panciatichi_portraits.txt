Around her neck, Lucrezia Panciatichi wears a message in French, worked into her gold chain: "sans fin amour dure". Love lasts without end.

Agnolo Bronzino, court painter to Duke Cosimo the First, painted her and her husband Bartolomeo in the early 1540s, as a pair. Bartolomeo, from a merchant family, was born in France and spent his boyhood at the court of the French king. He became one of Cosimo's trusted diplomats.

Until the seventh of February 2027, Bronzino's Pygmalion and Galatea, from this room, is on loan to an exhibition in Rome.

Look at the two of them. Lucrezia sits very still in a deep red gown, lit so strongly that her skin looks like alabaster. A prayer book rests on her lap. Bartolomeo leans at a balustrade in black, his red beard combed into two points. Down at his feet sits a small black puppy.

The message on the chain is usually read as a declaration of love to her husband. Some scholars read it another way, as God's love for humankind, and the prayer book supports them. It matters, because the couple had a secret. In Lyon they had drawn close to the new movements of religious reform. Back in Florence, they were tried for heresy.

Bronzino also painted a Holy Family for Bartolomeo, in this room. Look for the Panciatichi coat of arms on the banner at the left.

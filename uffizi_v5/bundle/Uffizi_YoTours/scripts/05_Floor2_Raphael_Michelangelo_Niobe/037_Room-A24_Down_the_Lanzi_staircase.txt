Before 1769, visitors came into the gallery through a small door in this corridor. When the Grand Duke opened it to the public that year, the route was turned round and a new grand entrance was built, the one still in use.

Take a last look back along the Western Corridor, its ancient statues in facing pairs under painted ceilings. This is the end of the second floor.

Before you go down: the panoramic terrace, with the museum's café, is at this end of the floor. The route doesn't come back up here, so if you'd like the view, see it now, then come back to these stairs. If reaching it means going through an exit, skip it.

Now the most important direction in this guide. Take the Lanzi staircase down one floor only, to the first floor, not out to the street. There's also a lift by the stairs. The stairs carry on down to a street exit. Don't follow them. Once you're out in the street, you can't get back to the cloakroom or the audioguide desk, which are on the other side of the building.

Downstairs, a great deal more is waiting: a private collection given to the state, a long run of artists' self-portraits, and the sixteenth century, with Bronzino and Titian.

Go down one floor only and leave the stairs on the first floor, by the Info Point. At the foot, the hall of the Contini Bonacossi collection, Room B1, leads away from the stairs. Room B2 is at its near end, on the side away from the Info Point: look for a big gold altarpiece of the Madonna of the Snow.

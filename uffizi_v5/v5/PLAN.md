### Conventions
- **@room** = the map code, written "Room A4". Corridors: "Room A2" (Eastern), "Room A23" (Southern), "Room A24" (Western). The Tribune is "Room A16". The Verone is "Room D28". The opening track is "Outside". The closing track is "Magliabechi staircase". Where a track covers several rooms, @room is the room where the listener stands to press play (named in the table).
- **Botticelli rooms (015–019):** @room uses the artwork pages' codes (A10, A11, A12), but the map legend says "A9 Botticelli" and "A10–A12 Work in progress". This must be confirmed on site (ONSITE O7). **Spoken cues never use A9, A10, A11 or A12.** They describe position instead: "straight after Room A8, the Botticelli rooms", "the Primavera", "the room of painted Virtues".
- **@title** = "<Room code> · <title>", e.g. "A4 · Giotto — Ognissanti Maestà". Extras: "<Room code> · Extra · <title>", e.g. "A6 · Extra · Florence in the fourteenth century". ANCHOR and CLOSE take a plain title ("Welcome to the Uffizi", "The way out").
- **Room codes in speech:** say them the way the signs show them, "Room A four", "Room D twenty-three". Use the room title once when it helps ("Room D15, the Room of the Dynasties"). The museum is replacing all its signage (16 Jun 2026 news), so the title helps if a door label has changed.
- **Corridor rule (from the map):** on both floors, the painting rooms open off the side of the corridor **away from the courtyard windows**. Cues may use that rule. Left/right is given only where the map makes it unambiguous for someone walking the route's direction (the Western Corridor: walking from the river end toward Piazza della Signoria, the rooms are on your **left**). Every left/right is an ONSITE check.
- **Cue pattern:** the last paragraph of every track except CLOSE gives the destination room code and one thing to look for. A track before an Extra announces it like this: "Room X has a short Extra on…; the next main stop is…". Then a listener who skips the Extra still knows where to go. Never say "next track" (lint).
- **Dated items** are written as a separate sentence that can be cut ("DATED" in the table, and the DATED list in section 6).
- Writing rules as in V5_AUDIO_STANDARD.md. Vasari anecdotes are voiced as "Vasari tells…", never as fact. No prices, hours, tickets, queue tips or director names. No commercial advice (café, shop). "The museum says" at most once per track.

### The route as drawn on the map (doors as drawn; every item is checked on site, see ONSITE)
**Second floor (p-3, hi-3).**
1. Go up the Granducal staircase to A1, the Lorenese Vestibule. Then go out into the Eastern Corridor A2.
2. A3 is the first room, at the very start of the corridor, beside the vestibule. It opens only onto the corridor.
3. A4 opens onto the corridor. It is the hub of the next rooms:
   - A5 opens off A4, through the doorway near the corridor in their shared wall.
   - A6 opens off A5, at the back, away from the corridor. The high-resolution map draws an A6–A7 gap at the back of their shared wall (erratum, audit 24 Oct 2026: this line used to say none is drawn), and a gap toward the vestibule: ONSITE O4.
   - A7 and A8 each open off the far side of A4, away from the corridor. No A7–A8 doorway is drawn.
4. The Botticelli rooms:
   - From A8 the only doorway drawn into the Botticelli rooms leads into **A9**. A9 is shaded "not open" and listed as work in progress.
   - From A9, doorways are drawn into A10, and from A10 into A11–A12.
   - A11–A12 also opens into **A13**. A10, A11 and A12 have **no doorway onto the corridor**.
   - So the entry from A8 is either through A9 used as a passage, or in reverse from A13. This is the most important on-site check (O7).
5. A13 leads to A14 (Maps) and to A15 (Mathematics). A15 also opens onto the corridor. Then comes the Tribune A16.
6. A17–A21 are shaded closed. At the end of the corridor is A22, the Cabinet of Miniatures. Then the corridor turns into A23, the Southern Corridor, and A24, the Western Corridor.
7. **The A25–A35 suite (on your left in A24):**
   - A25 has its own doorway onto the corridor. It is the first doorway after the corner.
   - Then A25 → A26 → A27 → A28 → A29. A30 is a dead-end side room off A29.
   - Then A29 → A31 → A32 → **A33 (work in progress)** → A34 → back to the corridor. A34 also has its own corridor doorway.
   - No doorway is drawn between A25 and A34. A35 has its own corridor doorway, just beyond A34's.
   - If A33 is shut, the suite is a dead end at A32, and visitors walk back through A31, A29, A28, A27, A26 and A25.
8. Walking on along A24:
   - A36 is shaded closed.
   - Next comes the short passage to the Buontalenti staircase. A37, the Cabinet of Marbles, opens beside the staircase.
   - Then come A38 and A39, which have corridor doorways. A40–A42 are closed. Then the west end of the corridor.
   - At the west end, the panoramic terrace (café icons) is on the left and the **Lanzi staircase** is ahead.
   - The map warns that once you are out (the ground-floor street exit below), you cannot return to the cloakroom or the audioguide desk.

**First floor (p-2).**
1. The Lanzi staircase lands at the west end, beside C1 and the Info Point.
2. B1, the Sala degli Stemmi, is a hall running away from the stair foot. B2, B4 and B6 open on one side of it, B3, B5 and B7 on the other, and B8 is at its far end. The map draws this as a dotted out-and-back.
3. From the stair foot, the line goes up C1 → C2 → C3. It then turns along C4 → C5 → **C6, C9, C10, C11, C12** (work in progress) → D1. C7 and C8 hang off C6 and are closed.
4. The D-block is a long corridor on the courtyard side, with D1, D6, D13, D14, D15 and D26 along it.
   - D1 leads into D2 and D4. D3 is closed. D5 opens off D6, and D7 off D6 or D13. D8 opens down into the lobby at the top of the Buontalenti staircase.
   - **D9** is the short passage from that lobby up through D13 to the corridor. The Classic line uses it. The notice lists it as work in progress.
   - **D12 opens off the D9 passage**, on the river side. D10 and D11 are closed.
5. D15 has two doorways in its back wall: one into D16 (which leads to D17 and D18) and one into the narrow D19.
6. D19 opens into D20 near its corridor end and into the D21 strip at its far end. D21 leads into the D22 antechamber, which leads into D23. Then D23 → D24 → D25 → D26 → the D27 Veronese Corridor → the Verone (D28).
7. At the top of the Verone, turn along the corridor to E4 (nearest the Verone) → E5 → E6 → E7. From E7 the route leads to the Magliabechi staircase and down to the ground floor. On the ground floor are the cloakroom and the audioguide desk, and the way out is the "back way out of the building" on the Palazzo Vecchio side.

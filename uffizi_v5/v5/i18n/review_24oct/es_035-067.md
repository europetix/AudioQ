## Names and conventions
- Regnal names use the Spanish form with the number in words: Cosme Primero, Segundo and Tercero. Also Fernando Primero, Francisco Primero (Medici, and the king of France), Julio Segundo, Urbano Octavo and Francesco Maria Segundo. Other Medici: Pedro Leopoldo, Leopoldo de Médici, el gran príncipe Fernando, don Antonio de Médici, Francisco (Cosimo's son, 055) and Juana de Austria.
- Artists use the established ES forms: Rafael, Miguel Ángel, Tiziano, el Veronés (Paolo Veronés), Pedro Pablo Rubens, Anton van Dyck. All other names are as the museum gives them.
- Generic "the corridor" on the first floor → "el corredor". "Passage" → "el pasillo". "Warden" → "un vigilante". "Info Point" is kept as on the signs.
- 037 keeps the Lanzi warning exactly ("un solo piso, hasta la primera planta, y no hasta la calle"). It also keeps the conditional about reaching the terrace.
- No Botticelli room codes were added anywhere.

## Quotations
None of the quotations in 035–067 was originally Spanish. Each one is a faithful translation of the English, introduced the same way:
- 040 «pequeño Bramante»: gloss of the nickname.
- 044 «Michelangelo de las Bambocciate»: the inscription on the back, originally Italian. It is translated from the English and keeps the Italian word *bambocciate*, which the text explains.
- 045 The grand prince Ferdinando's letter (originally Italian) is translated from the English: «dibujado como por Rafael, acabado con el alma pero sin pena».
- 046 The Latin inscription (FATO PRAEVENTUS…) is translated from the museum's English rendering, introduced as "En español dice:". The hymn line «Tu cuello es como una columna» (Latin *Collum tuum ut columna*) is also translated from the English.
- 051 «sans fin amour dure» is kept in French, as in the English, with the translation after it.
- 057 «la mujer desnuda»: Guidobaldo's letter (originally Italian), translated from the English.
- 063 Book of Judith: «El Señor lo ha abatido por mano de una mujer», translated from the English, not from a Spanish Bible. Artemisia's trial words (originally Italian): «He dicho la verdad y siempre la diré, porque es verdad», translated from the English.
- 067 "in the museum's own words" stays as reported speech with no quotation marks, as in the English.

[warmly] In these rooms, for once, the artists are the ones looking at you.

Cardinal Leopoldo de' Medici, in the seventeenth century, was known as the Prince of Collectors. He collected, among much else, painters' faces painted by their own hands. The collection never stopped growing, and today it holds more than two thousand self-portraits. A selection is on view in these rooms.

The cardinal himself is here, in marble. His nephew, Grand Duke Cosimo the Third, ordered it in his honour. Look at the lace on the cassock, the curls, the way the body twists in the chair.

[wry] From 1973 to 2016, part of the collection hung inside the Vasari Corridor, where few visitors could see them. Since 2023 they have been here on the main route. When these rooms opened, the walls were painted pink, a nod to the cardinal's robes.

[pause] Now find a small panel of a young man in a dark cap and plain dark clothes. That's Raphael, at about twenty-three, the young man who painted the Goldfinch Madonna upstairs. It's the same face he later gave himself in his School of Athens. The plain clothes are a statement: this is a man at work.

Room C2 has a short Extra on Bill Viola's video self-portrait, and Room C3 one on painters at work. The next main stop is Room D4, Correggio and Parmigianino. Walk on through C2 and C3, along C4 and C5, then follow the museum's route straight on through the rooms being rehung, C6 to C12. That brings you to Room D1, and through it to Room D4. If a barrier stops you, ask a warden for the way to Room D1.

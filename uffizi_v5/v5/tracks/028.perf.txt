[conspiratorial] Hold on to this painting. In a few rooms, you'll see what it taught Michelangelo.

Luca Signorelli came from Cortona, and he painted bodies like a sculptor. Around 1487 he painted this round Holy Family, which Vasari says was made for the audience hall of the Guelph Party. Look how the three figures fill the circle. The Virgin, in her wide red robe, reads a book. The Child twists round toward Joseph. They are so solid they seem to press out of the frame. The museum says this tondo served as a model for a whole generation of artists, from Michelangelo and his Doni Tondo onward.

Then go through to the next room, Room A32, for a second Signorelli Madonna, from around 1490. Vasari says it was made for Lorenzo de' Medici, most likely Lorenzo di Pierfrancesco, the Medici who may appear in Filippino's Adoration. Look at the small nude figures behind the Virgin. They may stand for humanity before the law of Moses.

From Room A32, if the doorway into Room A33 is open, walk on through A33 and Room A34 to the corridor. If not, go back the way you came, through A31, A29, A28, A27, A26 and A25. In the corridor, turn left: Room A35 is the doorway just past Room A34, with the Baptism of Christ and its two kneeling angels.

[conspiratorial] Before 1769, visitors came into the gallery through a small door in this corridor. When the Grand Duke opened it to the public that year, the route was turned round and a large new entrance was built, the one still in use today.

Take a last look back along the Western Corridor, with its ancient statues standing in pairs, facing one another, under painted ceilings. This is the end of the second floor.

[lightly] Before you go down, the panoramic terrace is at this end of the floor, with the museum's café. The route doesn't come back up here, so if you'd like the view, go now.

[pause] Now the most important direction in this guide. Take the Lanzi staircase down *one* floor only, to the first floor, not out to the street. There's also a lift by the stairs. The staircase carries on down to a street exit on the ground floor. Don't follow it. Once you're out in the street, you can't come back in, and the cloakroom and audioguide desk are on the other side of the building.

[warmly] The first floor holds a great deal more. A private collection given to the state, a long run of artists' self-portraits, and then the sixteenth century, with Bronzino, Titian and the painters who came after Raphael and Michelangelo.

Go down one floor only and leave the stairs on the first floor, by the Info Point. Turn into the hall of the Contini Bonacossi collection, Room B1, beside the Info Point. The first room off it, Room B2, has a big gold altarpiece of the Madonna of the Snow.

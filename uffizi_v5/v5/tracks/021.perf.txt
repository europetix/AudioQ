[lightly] Before he became Grand Duke, Ferdinando de' Medici was a cardinal in Rome. When he came home to rule Tuscany, he brought a whole ceiling with him.

It's the dark coffered ceiling above you, by Jacopo Zucchi, moved here from Rome in one piece. Vasari had designed this space as an open loggia, looking out over the city. Ferdinando closed it in and turned it into a portrait of his state. Ludovico Buti painted the maps, from a new survey by the cartographer Stefano Bonsignori. Look for Tuscany, its sea and its islands, with around twelve hundred place names lettered in gold.

Next door, in the Room of Mathematics, Room A15, Ferdinando kept scientific instruments, a collection his father Cosimo had begun. Around 1600, Giulio Parigi painted its ceiling with ancient and modern inventions, and with Pythagoras, Ptolemy, Euclid and, above all, Archimedes. The room was restored in 2025, with new light on that ceiling, and small bronzes in the niches.

Until April 2027, one of them, Willem Tetrode's bronze Venus, is on loan to the Ashmolean Museum in Oxford.

Go back into Room A13 and through Room A15, the small Room of Mathematics, to the Tribune, Room A16: red velvet walls and a dome of mother-of-pearl.

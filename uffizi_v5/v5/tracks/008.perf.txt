[warmly] If Florence chose weight, Siena chose grace. This room is Siena's answer, from the same decades as Giotto.

Siena was Florence's great rival, and its painters kept faith with gold, flowing line and jewel-bright colour. Two of the finest were brothers, Pietro and Ambrogio Lorenzetti. Ambrogio alone painted the famous frescoes of Good and Bad Government in Siena's town hall. [quietly] Both brothers vanish from the records in 1348, the year of the Black Death. They probably died in the plague.

[pause] Find Ambrogio's Presentation in the Temple, the tall panel with a church interior. It's signed and dated 1342, and it was made for an altar in Siena Cathedral. He shows the temple inside and out at the same time, with columns and starry vaults. The lines of the coloured marble floor run back into depth. Then come closer. [amused] The women are wearing earrings. And the baby Jesus is sucking his thumb.

Pietro's Madonna, from about 1340, is quieter. Mother and son exchange a long, tender look. The Child strokes her face and holds on to her fingers.

Pietro has one more painting nearby, his Saint Humility, in Room A6.

Stay in Room A5 for Simone Martini's Annunciation, with the angel's words written in gold across the air.

[curious] Most painters show a voice by painting an open mouth. Simone Martini wrote the words themselves across the air, in raised gold.

"AVE GRATIA PLENA DOMINUS TECUM." Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you. The angel Gabriel has just arrived. His cloak is still fluttering and his wings are spread. The greeting runs from his lips towards the Virgin, so your eye follows the path of the sound.

[pause] Now look at Mary. This isn't calm acceptance. She is distressed. She draws back and wraps herself in her dark blue cloak. She had been reading, and her book is still in her hand. It's a very human flinch, at the most solemn moment in Christian art.

Simone was a star of his time, a friend of the poet Petrarch, who later worked for kings and for the popes in Avignon. He painted this in 1333 for an altar in Siena Cathedral, together with Lippo Memmi. They shared a workshop, and they were brothers-in-law, because Simone married Lippo's sister. Both names are in the signature. Critics give most of the conception and the painting to Simone, and the two side saints are often assigned to Lippo.

One of those saints is Ansanus, a patron saint of Siena, holding the city's banner. [wry] The other is a puzzle. She may be Maxima, his mother, or Saint Margaret. The name written at her feet says Judith, and that is wrong.

Between angel and Virgin stands a vase of lilies, for her purity. The angel holds an olive branch, a sign of peace. And up in the frame, four prophets in small roundels hold scrolls that point to this very moment.

[warmly] Almost seven hundred years on, the greeting still hangs in the air.

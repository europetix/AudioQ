[curious] A document of July 1481 records that Leonardo had taken on this altarpiece, and promised to finish it within thirty months. He never finished it.

It was for the high altar of San Donato a Scopeto, a monastery of the Augustinians just outside the walls. That September he was still at work on it. Then he left Florence for Milan, and the court of Ludovico Sforza. The Augustinians finally hired Filippino Lippi. His Adoration hangs in Room A28, back along this floor.

What's left is charcoal, ink and oil, straight on the wood. The restoration showed that, unusually for his day, he worked the composition out directly on the panel.

Look at the centre. The Virgin and Child sit calmly, and the kings kneel with their gifts. Everyone else leans in, reaches out, turns in wonder. Compare Gentile da Fabriano's orderly procession, earlier on this floor. Here a whole crowd reacts at once.

Behind them, the picture boils over. On the left, a ruined building with two staircases climbing to nothing. On the right, horsemen clash.

[pause] For a long time it looked almost brown all over. In 2011 it went to the conservators of the Opificio delle Pietre Dure. Earlier restorers had coated it in glue, varnish and egg white, and those layers were pulling the paint away from the panel. The cleaning took off only what was not original, and under a tinted old varnish, a landscape appeared, typically Leonardo in its depth and atmosphere.

[warmly] It came back to the Uffizi in 2017. Look into the background now: visitors before then could hardly make it out.

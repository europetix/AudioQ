[warmly] This is where the route ends. Before the look back, the way out.

Go down the Magliabechi staircase to the ground floor. If you'd rather not take the stairs, there is a lift close by. At the bottom you pass the cloakroom and the audioguide desk, so collect anything you left there. Once you are through the exit, you can't come back in for it. The way out is at the back of the building, on the Palazzo Vecchio side.

[pause] Now think back to where you started. You climbed to the top floor of a building that Vasari designed as offices for the Medici state. That's all "Uffizi" means. You began with three gold Madonnas, where the gold was the light. You end in rooms where the light is a single beam in the dark, or a newborn child, or the face of an old man.

Halfway along, you passed the Tribune, the little octagonal room with the red walls and the mother-of-pearl dome. That was where it started. A grand duke set his finest treasures in one room, to be admired. In time, the whole floor became a gallery. And on the 24th of June 1769 it opened to the public, as one of the world's first museums created with a modern concept, in the museum's own words.

[quietly] It's all still here because of a promise. When the Medici line died out, the last of the family, Anna Maria Luisa, agreed with the new rulers that nothing would leave the capital and the State. That is why you could stand in front of the Primavera today, in the city that made it.

[warmly] Outside, the Ponte Vecchio is a short walk away. Take one picture with you as you go, the one that stopped you. You'll find it's the one you remember. Thank you for walking the Uffizi with me.

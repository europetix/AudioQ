[curious] Gentile da Fabriano signed this painting with the month as well as the year: May 1423. Perhaps he knew it was something special.

It was ordered by Palla Strozzi, a banker and refined lover of art, one of the richest men in Florence, for his family's chapel in the sacristy of Santa Trinita. [pause] And it is a performance of wealth.

Read it like a story. Up in the three arches at the top, the journey of the Magi unfolds in three separate episodes. Then the procession winds down through the hills into the foreground. It's a crowd from many lands, with exotic animals to show how far the kings have come. At the left, the eldest king kneels at the Child's feet. The youngest, in the most dazzling clothes of all, waits while his golden spurs are taken off.

Come close to those spurs. Gentile embossed the metal leaf so that it stands up in relief from the surface, on the spurs and on a sword hilt. [wry] And the Strozzi themselves may be somewhere in that crowd.

Now drop your eyes to the row of small scenes along the base: the Nativity, the Flight into Egypt and the Presentation in the Temple. Here the gold sky gives way to blue. The Nativity is a night scene, and the light seems to come from the Child. [conspiratorial] One more thing. The Presentation, the last scene on the right, is a modern copy. The original is in the Louvre.

The museum calls this the most important example of International Gothic painting in Italy. [quietly] It's also a farewell. In the next room, the gold starts to come down.

Go back into Room A4. Room A8 is the other doorway on A4's far side, next to the one you came through. Look for Masolino and Masaccio's Saint Anne, with the Virgin on her lap.

After the Macchiaioli, Italian painting stopped agreeing with itself. These last rooms show it heading off in several directions at once, and you can walk them at a brisker pace.

Room seventeen opens with portraits, the faces of a prosperous new middle class. In Room nineteen, look for one more Fattori, a quiet portrait known as La cugina Argia, his cousin Argia, painted around 1861. It's a nice farewell to the painter you've been following.

Toward the end of the century, painters split. Some turned to Symbolism: moody, half-mystical pictures full of dreams and personifications. Others took up Divisionism, the Italian answer to French Pointillism, laying small strokes of pure colour side by side so that they shimmer when you stand back. Watch for both.

Then, in the final rooms, the twentieth century arrives, some of it given to the gallery by private collectors. Take these rooms at your own pace.

That's the end of the Gallery of Modern Art. Follow the signs back to the landing, then go along the corridor to the right. A staircase of two short flights takes you into the Museum of Fashion and Costume, on this same floor. Play its track at the entrance.

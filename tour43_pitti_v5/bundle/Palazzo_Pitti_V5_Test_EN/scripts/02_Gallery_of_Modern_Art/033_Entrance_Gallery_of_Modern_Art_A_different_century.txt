When this gallery opened in 1924, the word modern meant something rather different. There's no Picasso up here. No Matisse. What there is, across thirty numbered rooms, is Italian painting and sculpture from the late seventeen hundreds to the early twentieth century.

And at its heart, one of the richest collections anywhere of the Macchiaioli. They were Tuscan painters who took their easels outdoors and built pictures out of patches of light and colour, in the years when Italy was becoming a single country.

The rooms themselves belong to the story. These were family rooms of the Lorraine grand dukes.

The loop runs roughly in order of time. Neoclassicism and Napoleon's years come first. Then the grand history paintings. In the middle of the loop, the Macchiaioli, with portraits and landscapes around them. And in the final rooms, later Tuscan painting, Symbolism and the early twentieth century.

Watch the century change as you walk. It starts with marble gods and emperors. By the middle of the loop, it's painting soldiers, woodcutters and women sitting in the shade by the sea.

Room one is where the loop begins. The first two rooms hold the Neoclassical work, and a very famous face waits for you in Room two.

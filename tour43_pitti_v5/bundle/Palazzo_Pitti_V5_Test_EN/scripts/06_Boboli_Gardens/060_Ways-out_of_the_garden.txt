A garden this big has more than one front door. Boboli has four official gates, and each one leaves you somewhere different.

The main gate is the palace itself. Go back through the Ammannati Courtyard and you come out on Piazza Pitti, a few minutes from the Ponte Vecchio and the river.

On the western side, the Annalena gate opens onto Via Romana, in the Oltrarno, the old artisans' quarter south of the Arno. The name has a story. Annalena Malatesta was a noblewoman living in Florence whose husband was murdered in 1441. After his death she founded a convent on this street, and her name has stayed with it ever since.

At the far south-western corner, right beside the Isolotto, is the Porta Romana gate. It lets you out by the old city gate of the same name, on the road that once led south toward Rome.

And at the very top of the hill, the Forte di Belvedere gate leads out beside the hilltop fortress, where old lanes run down toward the river. The Bardini Garden, with one of the loveliest views in Florence, is reached from that side. It's a separate garden, with its own entrance.

One idea for another day. Some visitors who come back start at the bottom, at Porta Romana, and climb the garden in reverse. Up the cypress avenue, past the statues, and down at last to the palace. Early morning and late afternoon, when the light is long, are the loveliest times to try it.

Wherever you come out, there's one last short track to play once you're through the gate.

You're standing on a piece of a war. In 1529 Florence was under siege. The armies of the Emperor and the Pope surrounded the city, to put the Medici back in power over a briefly restored republic. The republic threw up ramparts along these southern walls, and tradition says Michelangelo himself had a hand in the defences. This garden sits on top of one of those bastions.

The Medici came back, of course. And the rampart built to keep them out became their garden. Florentine history rarely ends tidily.

Inside the walls, everything is on a small scale. Low box hedges, roses planted inside them, and in the middle, the Fountain of the Monkeys, with little bronze monkeys around its basin. If you've come in late spring, the roses fill the air.

The building is the Casino del Cavaliere. At the end of the sixteen hundreds, Cosimo the Third rebuilt it for his younger son, Gian Gastone, who would become the last of the Medici grand dukes. Today it houses the Porcelain Museum, which is closed for refurbishment.

Look out over the wall, too. The view here turns away from the city, toward the hills south of Florence. It's softer and quieter than the view from the Kaffeehaus.

Now look down. Below this garden, at the head of the central axis, stands a colossal woman carved in marble. That's Abundance. Walk down to her, and play her track at her feet.

There were two famous Berenices, and they're easy to mix up. One was a queen of Egypt who, the legend says, gave her hair to the gods, and it became a constellation. This ceiling is about the other one.

This Berenice was a princess of Judaea. The man she loved was Titus, son of a Roman emperor and later emperor himself. Their story didn't end well, because Titus chose Rome over her and sent her away. Duty over love.

Giuseppe Bezzuoli painted that parting in 1820, for the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes. It isn't a happy subject for a palace ceiling, but look at what surrounds it. In the lunettes, the curved spaces around the edge, are the four cardinal virtues. Prudence, Justice, Fortitude and Temperance. The story sits in the middle, and the lesson runs all the way around it.

Find the two lovers first, then follow the virtues around the room, one by one. For a family of rulers, it reads almost like a sermon on what a good prince gives up.

As for the Egyptian Berenice and her hair, they're still up in the night sky. Just not on this ceiling.

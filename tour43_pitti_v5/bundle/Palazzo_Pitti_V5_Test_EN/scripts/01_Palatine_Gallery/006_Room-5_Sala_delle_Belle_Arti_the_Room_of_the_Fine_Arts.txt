Around the time this ceiling was painted, Florence was getting its art back.

Napoleon's armies had carried some of the city's greatest treasures off to Paris. Raphael's Madonna della Seggiola, which hangs further along this route, spent the years from 1799 to 1815 in France. After Napoleon's fall, the paintings and statues came home. So did the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes.

Now look up. Domenico Podestà painted this ceiling in 1817. It's usually described as Jupiter, king of the gods, sending Iris, his messenger, and Minerva, the goddess of wisdom, down to earth with the Arts. The Arts descending from heaven, on the orders of the highest authority there is. It may be pure coincidence that this subject went up in this palace two years after the homecoming. If so, it's a beautifully timed one.

When this wing last reopened, this room was also home to a Risen Christ by Rubens. If it's hanging when you visit, it's the painting to find here. You'll meet Rubens properly later on the route.

The Sala di Ercole is the next room along. Play its track there.

Under the Medici, this was a room for soldiers, the guard room of the German Guard. Then it was handed over to a hero.

The change began under Napoleon's sister, Elisa Baciocchi, who ruled Tuscany as grand duchess and had the room remodelled. But the paintings came after the French had gone. Once Grand Duke Ferdinando the Third was back on his throne, he gave these walls to Pietro Benvenuti, a painter from Arezzo. Benvenuti worked here from 1817 to 1829. Twelve years, for one room.

Look at the four big scenes on the walls. Hercules as a baby, strangling the serpents sent to kill him. Hercules at the crossroads, choosing the hard road of virtue over the easy one. Hercules bringing Alcestis back from the dead to her husband, Admetus. And Hercules fighting the Centaurs. Up on the vault, among ten monochrome scenes, is his reward, his marriage to Hebe. The museum calls this room one of the high points of international neoclassicism.

And amid all this restored grand-ducal glory stands a present from Napoleon. The two-metre porcelain vase was made at Sèvres in 1784. Napoleon sent it to this palace as a gift for the King of Etruria, a king he had created himself.

The Sala dell'Aurora is the next room along. Play its track there.

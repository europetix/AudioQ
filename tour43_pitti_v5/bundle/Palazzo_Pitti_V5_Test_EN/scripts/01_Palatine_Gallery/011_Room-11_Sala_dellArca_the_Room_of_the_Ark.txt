In the other rooms of this wing, the story is over your head. In this one, it walks around you.

Luigi Ademollo painted these walls in 1816 as one long procession. King David is bringing the Ark of the Covenant into Jerusalem. In the Bible the Ark was the sacred chest that held the tablets of the Law, the sign that God was with his people. The book of Samuel says David danced before it as it came into the city. So walk the room slowly, the way the crowd moves along the walls. For a minute, you're part of the parade.

Ademollo was one of a team. In the years after Napoleon, the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes had this wing redecorated by some of the best painters in Florence. Podestà had the Fine Arts. Benvenuti had Hercules. Martellini had the dawn, Bezzuoli had Berenice, and Collignon had Psyche. Room by room, they wrapped a seventeenth-century apartment in the newest style of their day.

You'll meet Ademollo again later, in the Palatine Chapel on the ground floor.

To leave the wing, go on through the Sala della Musica and the long Poccetti Corridor to the Sala di Prometeo. Play its track there.

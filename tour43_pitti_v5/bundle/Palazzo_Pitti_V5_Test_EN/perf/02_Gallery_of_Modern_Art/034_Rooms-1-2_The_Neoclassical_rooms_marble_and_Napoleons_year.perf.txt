[lightly] The first figure in this gallery is a girl who's been left behind. In Room one, Pietro Tenerani's marble Psyche has just been abandoned by Cupid, as the old myth tells it. He carved her around 1819, and she is about as calm as heartbreak gets.

That calm is the whole point. Neoclassical artists were reacting against the swirl and drama of the Baroque. They looked back to ancient Greece and Rome for something cleaner. Clear lines. Balanced compositions. Cool colour. Feeling, yes, but held in check.

[pause] Then Room two, and the mood changes. Suddenly it's all about one man.

The great canvas here is by Pietro Benvenuti, painted in 1812. It shows the Saxons swearing an oath to Napoleon after the Battle of Jena. In these first rooms hangs Giuseppe Bezzuoli's portrait of Napoleon's sister, Elisa Bonaparte Baciocchi. [curious] Remember Bezzuoli's name, by the way. He turns up again a few rooms on, in a story about one of his students.

These rooms are about the Napoleonic years in Tuscany, and about the people who paid for art here, before and after the emperor fell. Grand history and family portraits hang side by side.

And in Room two there's also a colossal marble head of Napoleon himself, far bigger than life. It has quite a story, including how it ended up in Florence at all. Stand in front of it to hear it.

[warmly] This room began as a place for parties. When Cosimo the Third married in 1661, it was the grand central hall of the couple's summer apartment, a room for receiving guests. A century later the palace had new masters. In 1766 the Habsburg-Lorraine Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo, the reforming ruler who later became emperor, turned the hall into the court chapel.

The paintings came a generation after that. Luigi Ademollo, born in Milan, spent his working life in Florence, and he decorated this chapel in the early 1790s for Grand Duke Ferdinand the Third. [pause] Thirty years later he came back to finish one of the choir lofts. On the vault is the Assumption of the Virgin. Along the walls run scenes from the New Testament, among them Christ's entry into Jerusalem and the Crucifixion.

[quietly] Now find the altar. It's Florentine commesso, hard stones cut and fitted together like a mosaic. Some of its pieces were made for one of the grandest Medici projects, the altar of the Chapel of the Princes at San Lorenzo, which was never finished. So stone meant for the family's great mausoleum ended up here, in a converted reception hall.

Through the wall, Orthodox icons. Here, a Catholic court chapel. Two traditions of Christian devotion, side by side under one roof.

[pause] When you're ready, step back out into the Ammannati Courtyard. The gardens behind the palace open beyond it, and the first garden stop is at the back of the courtyard, where the garden begins.

[amused] So. Were they parrots?

They were eagles. The silk in that room is French, woven around 1810, and the bird in each medallion was meant to be Napoleon's imperial eagle. When the Lorraine grand duke Ferdinand the Third came home to Florence in 1814, after Napoleon's fall, he brought the hangings with him. [pause] The Florentines took one look at the proud imperial bird and called it a parrot. The name stuck.

That one room is the whole apartment in miniature. Nobody here ever started from scratch. Each family moved into the last one's rooms and changed only what it needed.

The Medici left their mark where you'd least expect it. Ferdinando's alcove, his private bedchamber, became a chapel under the Lorraine. Even so, its white and gold stucco, with mottoes for Ferdinando and his wife Violante, survived almost untouched. The Lorraine brought their silks and the little Oval Cabinet, all Rococo curves. The Savoy brought a throne under their own coat of arms. And Queen Margherita made a home in rooms hung with blue and yellow brocade. [wry] She's the queen the pizza is said to be named after, though that story may be better than it is true.

[quietly] Then, in 1919, King Victor Emmanuel the Third handed the whole palace to the state. Three dynasties, one set of rooms, and almost nothing thrown away.

[pause] From here, take the main stairs or a lift down to the courtyard. The Russian Icons and the Palatine Chapel are on the ground floor, opening off it. Follow the signs for the Palatine Chapel.

[curious] This young saint was painted from a living model. And yet, when people look at him, many of them think of a statue.

Andrea del Sarto painted him around fifteen twenty-three, for a wealthy Florentine banker, Giovanni Benintendi. Benintendi wanted to honour Saint John and the sacrament of baptism. So he hired several painters to make pictures for a decorated wooden setting in the antechamber of his palace. Pontormo painted an Adoration of the Magi for it. Andrea's John was its centrepiece.

Tradition gave painters two ways to show John: a baby playing with the infant Christ, or the grown prophet of the desert, gaunt and wild-haired. Andrea chose neither. His John is a youth, lit hard against the dark, wrapped in bright red drapery. He holds a scroll with the Latin words Ecce Agnus Dei, Behold the Lamb of God. It's John announcing Christ, and pointing ahead to the cross.

[pause] Now look below him. Down there are the signs of his hard life in the wilderness. There's the simple terracotta bowl he used to baptise Jesus, a rock, and the camel hair he wore. And there's a humble cross made of cane.

Andrea liked to work from live models, and you can see it in the face. The museum also notes that he looks a little like Michelangelo's David. [quietly] Look at the skin. Andrea was a master of warm flesh, the kind that looks as if blood is moving under it. Watch how the light moves over his face.

Thirty years later, in fifteen fifty-three, the painting was given to Duke Cosimo the First, and so it entered the Medici collections.

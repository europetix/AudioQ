[conspiratorial] This was the Grand Duke's private hot room.

It began as an open gallery. In the seventeenth century it was closed in and turned into a stufa, a bath heated the way the ancient Romans heated theirs. It was reserved for Grand Duke Ferdinando the Second, beside his bedroom.

To decorate it, he brought in Pietro da Cortona, the painter of the Planet Room ceilings. This is where their partnership began. Cortona painted the Four Ages of Man, from Ovid, one on each wall. The Golden and Silver Ages came first, in 1637. He returned for the Bronze and Iron Ages in 1641.

[warmly] Start with the Golden Age, the happiest of the four. People live in peace, the earth gives freely, and nobody carries a weapon. It's flattery, of course. The scene celebrates Ferdinando's peaceful rule, and his marriage that same year to Vittoria della Rovere. Her family name means "of the oak", and her family's heraldry is worked into this happy world.

Then turn through the other ages and watch it all go wrong. By the Bronze and Iron Ages, war has arrived. [pause] These walls brought a lighter, more theatrical style of painting to Florence. Ferdinando liked what he saw. Soon Cortona was at work on the ceilings of the state rooms.

The route continues through the door into the atrium of the Moor's Staircase, and on into the Sala dell'Iliade. Its track starts there. If the Iliad Room is closed when you visit, staff will send you back through the rooms you've just seen to the Room of Jupiter. In that case, just play each room's track as you walk in.

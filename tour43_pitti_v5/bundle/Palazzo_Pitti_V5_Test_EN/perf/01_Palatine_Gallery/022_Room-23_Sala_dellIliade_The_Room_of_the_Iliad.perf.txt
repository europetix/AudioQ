[curious] Here's a puzzle. This room is named after the most famous war in literature. Yet under the Medici, this was no room for heroes or ambassadors.

In the grand dukes' day, important guests walked through the Planet Rooms the other way round. They started at Venus, and the sequence ended next door, in Saturn, the first of the Grand Duke's own private rooms. So this room lay beyond it, on the private side of the court. [lightly] One account even says it held a billiard table.

The war came later. In eighteen nineteen, after Napoleon's fall, Luigi Sabatelli began painting the ceiling for the Lorraine grand dukes, who had taken over from the Medici. Look up, and the story of Troy runs right across the vault. Below it, the walls are hung two and three deep, Florentine fashion, the big pictures centred and the smaller ones fitted around them.

[pause] In the middle stands the calmest thing in the room. It's Lorenzo Bartolini's Charity, a marble mother with her children, carved in the eighteen-twenties. She has stood here since eighteen sixty-one.

Three works here have their own track. Andrea del Sarto's great Assumption of the Virgin. Bartolini's Charity. And Artemisia Gentileschi's Judith and her Maidservant. Artemisia's Mary Magdalene hangs in this room as well.

Start with the great altarpiece of the Virgin rising into heaven above a crowd of apostles. That's Andrea del Sarto's Assumption.

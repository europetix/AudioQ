[warmly] This room is a crossroads, and it sits between two very different worlds of the palace.

One way lies the Volterrano Wing, a run of smaller rooms that was once the private apartment of a grand duchess, Vittoria della Rovere, in the sixteen-hundreds. Far ahead, at the very end of the route, are the state rooms of her husband, Grand Duke Ferdinando the Second. Those are the Planet Rooms, which Pietro da Cortona frescoed for him in the 1640s. Private life on one side, power and ceremony on the other. And this room in between.

It takes its name from the painter Giuseppe Castagnoli, who decorated these walls in the early eighteen-hundreds. That was when the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes were turning the old palace rooms into a picture gallery. [lightly] He isn't the reason anyone comes in here. [pause] The reason is standing in the middle of the floor.

The Table of the Muses has been in this room since 1855. It's one of the great showpieces of Florentine stone inlay, and it took around twenty years, and a small army of craftsmen, to make. Before you walk up to it, look at it from a few steps back. At this distance it reads like a painting. Up close, you'll see it's something much stranger.

Walk up to the table and play its track there.

[curious] Almost every painting in the rooms ahead once belonged to the Medici. But it wasn't the Medici who hung them here. [pause] It was the family that came after them.

The Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes created this gallery between the end of the seventeen-hundreds and the early eighteen-hundreds. They chose around five hundred works from the great Medici collections and put them in the palace's state rooms. And they hung them in a deliberately old-fashioned way, the way picture galleries were hung in the sixteen-hundreds. Frame against frame, wall to wall, with sculpture, vases and stone-inlaid tables in between.

[lightly] So don't expect one painting on a white wall. Here the rooms are part of the show. You're meant to feel the richness first, and find the masterpieces second.

[pause] One tip before you start. Every room has its name written above the door, in Italian. Sala di Saturno, Sala di Giove, and so on. Every track in this part of the guide starts with that same name. So if you ever lose your place, look up at the door, and play the tracks that carry its name. The route keeps the five great Planet Rooms, with ceilings by Pietro da Cortona, for the very end.

Walk the length of the statue gallery to the room marked Sala del Castagnoli, with a great round stone table in the middle. Play its track there.

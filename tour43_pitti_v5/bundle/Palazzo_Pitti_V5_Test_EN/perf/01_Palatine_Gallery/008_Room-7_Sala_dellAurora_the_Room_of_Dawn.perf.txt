[lightly] There's a sunrise on this ceiling, and someone has put a grand duke in it.

The painter was Gaspero Martellini, and his subject is Aurora, the goddess of the dawn. Here she shares the sky with the Arts and with Time. And among them, as the scene is usually described, is a portrait bust of Ferdinando the Third, the Habsburg-Lorraine grand duke who came back to Florence after Napoleon.

[pause] You don't need a caption to read that. The long night is over, the rightful ruler is home, and the morning comes with him. Nobody walking through these rooms at the time could have missed the point.

[quietly] Not much is written about this room, and I won't pad it out. So give it a minute of your own. Find the grand duke's bust first. Then find Time, and the Arts, and see how the painter has arranged them all around the new day.

Martellini wasn't finished with this idea. He also painted the ceiling of the Sala di Ulisse, further along the route, where a hero's homecoming tells a very similar story.

The Sala di Berenice is the next room along. Play its track there.

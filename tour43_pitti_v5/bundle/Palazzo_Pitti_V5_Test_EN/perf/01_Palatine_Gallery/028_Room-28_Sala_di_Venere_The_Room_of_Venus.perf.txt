[warmly] You've reached the last room of today's route. Under the Medici, this was where it all began.

You've walked the prince's life backwards. [pause] And now, at last, you've reached the beginning.

In the Medici story, this was the very first lesson. Before a young prince could be taught by Apollo, hardened by Mars and crowned by Jupiter, he had to learn to turn away from Venus. From comfort, and from the pleasures of the senses. Look up. Pietro da Cortona built this ceiling around that idea.

[amused] Which makes this a slightly mischievous room to finish in. Because the walls are full of exactly the kind of beauty the prince was meant to resist.

Four works here get their own track. At the centre stands a marble Venus by Antonio Canova. And around the walls hang three paintings by Titian, made across some thirty-five years of his life. There's a young man's picture, the Concert. There's a portrait of his ferocious friend, the writer Pietro Aretino. And there's a woman in a blue dress, known simply as La Bella.

Begin with the marble woman in the middle of the room. Walk over to Canova's Venus, and play her track beside her.

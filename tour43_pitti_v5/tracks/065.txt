 Italian Haute Couture & the Sala Bianca — Room 8
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 8, the birth of Italian high fashion in the nineteen-fifties, tied to the Sala Bianca shows, with the
    grand-ducal jewels of Ferdinand III.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The 1950s Florence fashion shows that launched Italian couture on the world, plus historic grand-ducal
    jewellery.
    LISTEN FOR
    How Italian fashion was born in this very palace, and why the Sala Bianca matters.

 Room eight is the heart of the whole museum, because what it commemorates happened in this very
 building.

 On the twelfth of February, nineteen fifty-one, a Florentine businessman named Giovanni Battista
 Giorgini staged a fashion show in his own home for a small group of American department-store
 buyers. It went well enough that the next shows moved into the grandest room available, the Sala
 Bianca, the White Hall, of the Palatine Gallery here in Palazzo Pitti. Those Sala Bianca shows, through
 the nineteen-fifties, are where Italian fashion was born as an international force, where the world first
 saw that Italy, not only Paris, could lead. The houses shown there, among them Schubert, laid the
 foundation of everything Milan would later become.

 The room also holds historic grand-ducal jewels, including pieces associated with Ferdinand the Third
 of Lorraine, a reminder that this palace dressed its rulers in splendour for centuries before it dressed the
 world.

 When you've looked enough, continue to room nine, the nineteen-sixties and ready-to-wear. Play the
 next track when you arrive.

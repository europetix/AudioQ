TRACK R33 · ROOM 1                                 R-INTRO                             216 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Neoclassical Rooms — Batoni, Canova, and
 Napoleon
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the first rooms of the Modern Art Gallery, rooms 1 through 4, devoted to late-18th- and early-19th-century
    Italian and European neoclassicism.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Four rooms hung with late-18th- and early-19th-century painting, Pompeo Batoni's Grand Tour portraits and
    French Revolution-and-Empire works, plus small sculpture by Antonio Canova and his followers. Room 2
    holds Canova's marble bust of Napoleon.
    LISTEN FOR
    What neoclassicism was, who Pompeo Batoni was painting, and which sculpture in Room 2 gets its own
    track.

 Walk slowly through this cluster. The atmosphere is restrained — calm light, smaller paintings, smaller
 sculptures — appropriate to the moment in European art it represents.

 Neoclassicism dominated late eighteenth-century European painting and sculpture as a deliberate
 reaction against the swirling exuberance of the Baroque and Rococo that preceded it. Artists looked
 back to Greek and Roman antiquity for models of clarity, restraint, and moral seriousness. Lines were
 clean. Compositions were balanced. Colours were cooled.

 On the walls, look for Pompeo Batoni's portraits. Batoni was the most fashionable portraitist in Rome in
 the seventeen sixties and seventies, and his studio was a near-mandatory stop for English aristocrats on
 the Grand Tour. He painted them in front of Roman ruins, holding fragments of antique sculpture,
 looking thoughtful and educated. His portraits are documents of how eighteenth-century European elites
 wanted to be seen — as inheritors of classical civilisation rather than as the regional nobles they actually
 were.

 In Room 2 you'll find the sculpture that gets its own track here: Antonio Canova's marble bust of
 Napoleon. You met Canova earlier in the Sala di Venere, where his Venus Italica stands at the centre of
 the room. Here he appears as a portraitist of the most powerful man in Europe. Find the bust and play
 the next track.

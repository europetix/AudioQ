 Russian Icons — Cosimo III's Summer Apartment
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the first of four ground-floor rooms now housing the Museum of Russian Icons, also part of the original
    summer apartment of the palace.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A series of four rooms with seventeenth-century frescoed walls and ceilings, recently restored, now hung
    with Russian Orthodox icons — small to medium panels in gold and saturated colour, with descriptions in
    Italian, English and Russian.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why a Russian Orthodox collection is in a Medici palace, how old it is, and what to look for as you walk
    through.

 This is a museum that didn't exist when most guidebooks of Pitti were written. The four rooms you're
 now walking into opened to the public in January of twenty twenty-two — never previously part of a
 Pitti visit, used as offices for decades. The restoration uncovered the seventeenth-century frescoes you
 see on the walls and ceilings, and the museum now hangs the icons in front of those frescoes, letting
 both speak at once.

 How did Russian icons end up in Florence? In sixteen sixty-one the future Grand Duke Cosimo the
 Third married Marguerite Louise d'Orléans of France, and these very rooms were decorated as their
 summer apartment. Two icons were already in the palace by then, kept in the household chapel by the
 previous Grand Duchess Vittoria della Rovere — you'll see those in a moment. After the Medici line
 died out in seventeen thirty-seven, the new Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes who succeeded them kept
 adding to the collection. By the time it was catalogued, it ran to seventy-eight pieces — the oldest
 collection of Russian icons anywhere outside the lands where they were made.

 The icons themselves are small or medium-sized — devotional panels for private rooms or for travel,
 not church-sized altarpieces. And the gold backgrounds aren't decoration. In the Orthodox tradition
 they're the light of heaven, and the figures inside that light are meant to be present, not pictured.

 One pair of works gets its own track. Find the small Beheading of Saint John the Baptist — or, just
 nearby, the Mother of God of the long title. When you're in front of one of them, play the next track.

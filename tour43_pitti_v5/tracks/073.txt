 Salotto Celeste & Cappella — Blue Room and
 Chapel
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Salotto Celeste (Blue Room) and the Cappella (Chapel).
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The Blue Room, hung with Justus Sustermans's Medici portraits and an eagle chimneypiece, and the Chapel,
    the one room keeping its original Medici Baroque decoration and pietre dure furniture.
    LISTEN FOR
    Sustermans and the Medici family likeness, and why the Chapel is the only undisturbed Medici room in the
    apartment.

 Two rooms, very different in mood.

 First the Salotto Celeste, the Blue Room, named for the sky-blue silk on its walls. Stop in the middle
 and turn slowly: the faces watching you are mostly the seventeenth-century Medici grand dukes, painted
 by one man, Justus Sustermans, the Flemish-born court painter who held the post for sixty years. He
 painted everyone who mattered at the Medici court, and a row of his portraits shows you the family
 likeness running across five generations, the long Medici jaw, the watchful eyes. Look too for the eagle
 chimneypiece by Francis Harwood. Under the Medici this had been the room of musical instruments;
 the Lorraine turned it into a dining room.

 Then the Cappella, the Chapel. This is the one room in the whole apartment that keeps its original
 Medici decoration. It began as the Alcova, the alcove bedchamber, and its seventeenth-century fittings
 survive: look closely at the ebony furniture inlaid with coloured-marble pietre dure from the
 grand-ducal workshop, the famous Opificio delle Pietre Dure. When the room was later adapted as a
 private chapel, that older decoration was left in place. Everywhere else in the apartment you read layers
 of change; here you read the Medici, undisturbed.

 When you're ready, continue to the Parrot Room and the Queen's rooms. Play the next track.

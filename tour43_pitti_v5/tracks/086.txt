 Giardino del Cavaliere — The Knight's Garden and
 the Roses
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Giardino del Cavaliere, the walled garden at the top of the Boboli hill, built on a defensive bastion. The
    Casino del Cavaliere — now the Porcelain Museum — sits at its centre, and the rose garden lies on the slopes
    below.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small walled garden laid out on top of a defensive rampart designed by Michelangelo in 1529 during the
    Siege of Florence. Box-hedged beds of rare and fragrant plants, the small Fountain of the Monkeys (Fontana
    delle Scimmie), and panoramic views toward the Chianti hills. The box-hedged rose garden below is at its
    peak in May. The Casino del Cavaliere here houses the Porcelain Museum.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why this garden sits on a fortress wall, who designed that wall, and what's flowering around you if you've
    come in spring.

 You've reached the highest, quietest, and arguably loveliest corner of the whole garden.

 The Giardino del Cavaliere — the Knight's Garden — is a small walled enclosure, and what it sits on is
 the surprising part. In fifteen twenty-nine, Florence was under siege by the combined forces of the Holy
 Roman Emperor and the Pope, trying to force Medici rule back onto the briefly revived Florentine
 Republic. The Republic put Michelangelo himself in charge of the city's fortifications, and he designed
 defensive bastions along the southern walls. This garden is laid out on top of one of them. You're
 standing on a rampart built to keep the Medici out, which later became a pleasure garden for the Medici
 once they'd won. Florentine history rarely resolves cleanly.

 Inside the walls: low box hedges, beds of rare and fragrant plants, and the small Fountain of the
 Monkeys — la Fontana delle Scimmie — with its little bronze monkeys around the basin. The Casino
 del Cavaliere at the centre now houses the Porcelain Museum, which we'll come to later.

 If you've come in spring — and especially in May — the box-hedged rose garden on the slopes below is
 in full bloom, and the scent reaches the terrace. And the view from up here opens south and east toward
 the Chianti hills, away from the city — a softer, more rural view than the Kaffeehaus gives.

 When you've taken it in, look toward the very top of the central garden axis, where a single colossal
 female statue stands against the sky. Walk toward her and play the next track.

 Sala Verde & Sala del Trono — Green Room and
 Throne Room
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala Verde (Green Room) and the Sala del Trono (Throne Room).
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The Green Room, with a Luca Giordano ceiling-canvas (a rare Medici-era survival), and the Throne Room,
    where the Savoy throne stands under a canopy amid Flemish tapestries.
    LISTEN FOR
    The Luca Giordano allegory, and how one room served as Medici audience hall, Lorraine chamberlains'
    room, and Savoy throne room in turn.

 Two of the grandest rooms in the apartment.

 First the Sala Verde, the Green Room, named for the deep green silk on its walls. Look up: set into the
 ceiling is a canvas by Luca Giordano, the prolific Neapolitan painter, an Allegory of Peace between
 Florence and Fiesole, one of the very few original Medici-era furnishings left in these rooms. Under the
 Medici this was the guardroom of Grand Prince Ferdinando's apartment; everything decorative you see
 is a later overlay on those old bones.

 Then the Sala del Trono, the Throne Room. This is where the Savoy layer takes over completely. The
 walls are hung with red ornamental fabric, a fine set of Flemish tapestries and full-length portraits;
 under a canopy, ringed by a low gilded balustrade, stands the throne of the King of Italy, a gilded
 wooden armchair to which the House of Savoy simply added its coat of arms. In Medici times this was
 the audience room; the Lorraine called it the Chamberlains' Room; the Savoy made it the throne room
 when Florence was briefly the capital. Three uses, one room.

 When you've taken both in, continue to the Blue Room and the Chapel. Play the next track.

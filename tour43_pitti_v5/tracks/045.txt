 Sala di Psiche — Cupid and Psyche
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Psiche, with a ceiling fresco of Psyche and Cupid by Giuseppe Collignon.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small room with a Giuseppe Collignon ceiling fresco of Psyche being received among the gods on Mount
    Olympus, painted in the early nineteenth century. Walls hung with Salvator Rosa landscapes — wild, stormy
    mountain scenes with tiny figures — and other seventeenth-century Italian paintings.
    LISTEN FOR
    The story of Psyche behind the ceiling, who Salvator Rosa was, and why his stormy landscapes are unusually
    well represented in this room.

 Look up first.

 The ceiling shows the climax of the myth of Cupid and Psyche — one of the great love stories of
 classical mythology, told by the Roman writer Apuleius in The Golden Ass. Psyche, a mortal princess
 of extraordinary beauty, falls in love with Cupid and, after a long series of trials imposed by Cupid's
 jealous mother Venus, is finally received as an immortal among the gods on Mount Olympus. The
 fresco shows that final moment. Jupiter has consented to the marriage. Psyche, draped in white, is being
 welcomed into divinity.

 Giuseppe Collignon painted this around eighteen twenty for the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes. He
 was a French-born painter who had settled in Florence and become one of the more successful court
 frescoists of the period.

 On the walls — and worth crossing the room for — are usually several paintings by Salvator Rosa. Rosa
 was a seventeenth-century Neapolitan painter, poet, satirist, and general troublemaker, famous in his
 time for wild stormy landscapes populated by tiny figures of bandits, hermits, soldiers, and
 philosophers. His landscapes anticipate the Romantic taste for the sublime by a hundred years. The
 Medici grand dukes collected him enthusiastically in the seventeenth century, and the Pitti holds one of
 the best Rosa collections anywhere.

 Look for thunder, ruins, and tiny human figures lost in the landscape. When you're ready, continue to
 the Sala di Flora, the Room of Flora. Play the next track.

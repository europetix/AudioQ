 Anticamera & Sala delle Nicchie — The Approach
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the first two rooms of the apartment, the Anticamera (Antechamber) and the Sala delle Nicchie (Hall of
    Niches).
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The entrance antechamber and the niche-hall, where antique and Renaissance sculpture sits in wall niches as
    a small private gallery.
    LISTEN FOR
    How the apartment opens, and why the Hall of Niches is a domestic-scale echo of the great Palatine
    collection.

 Two rooms to open the apartment.

 The Anticamera, the Antechamber, is where the sequence begins, the first room a visitor crossed on the
 way in. Under the Medici and Lorraine it was a waiting room and a filter: you were admitted this far,
 and how much further depended on who you were.

 Next door is the Sala delle Nicchie, the Hall of Niches, named for the rectangular niches cut into its
 walls, each holding a piece of sculpture, antique Roman marbles, Renaissance copies, small busts. In the
 eighteenth and nineteenth centuries it worked as a small private gallery within the apartment, a place
 where the family could keep favourite pieces close at hand without going down to the main Palatine
 collection. Look at the ceiling and the proportions: a room built to display, on an intimate domestic
 scale, the same princely taste that fills the great gallery next door.

 Together these two rooms set the key for everything that follows. The grandeur is real, but it's
 residential grandeur, a home that happened to be run by grand dukes and kings. Walk through both, then
 continue to the Green Room and the Throne Room. Play the next track.

 Sala dell'Aurora — Dawn on the Ceiling
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala dell'Aurora, named for the early nineteenth-century ceiling fresco of Aurora.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small room with a ceiling fresco of Aurora — the Roman goddess of the dawn — driving her chariot across
    the sky to bring the morning, while Night flees before her. Walls hung with smaller paintings rotating by
    season.
    LISTEN FOR
    What the Aurora fresco is doing and why dawn is on so many neoclassical ceilings.

 A small room. Don't spend long. Look up.

 The ceiling shows Aurora — the Roman goddess of the dawn — driving her chariot across the sky,
 while Night flees ahead of her in a dark cloud. Aurora was one of the most popular subjects for early
 nineteenth-century ceiling frescoes throughout Europe. The image carried an obvious symbolic load: the
 dawning of a new age, the light of reason driving away the darkness of ignorance, the rebirth of order
 after the chaos of revolution.

 For the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes returning to Florence after Napoleon, an Aurora was almost
 obligatory. They were presenting their own restoration as a dawn — the morning that followed the long
 night of French rule. Half the palaces of restored Europe acquired an Aurora ceiling in the eighteen tens
 or twenties.

 The fresco here is competent rather than extraordinary, and the walls carry rotating small paintings.
 Walk through. Continue to the Sala di Psiche — the Room of Psyche. Play the next track when you
 arrive.

 Sala di Apollo — The Room of Music and Light
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Apollo, the fourth Planet Room on your route. The ceiling shows Apollo with his lyre.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A grand reception room with a Pietro da Cortona ceiling fresco — begun by Cortona, completed by Ciro Ferri
    — of Apollo, the god of music, poetry, and light. Walls hung with sixteenth-century Italian paintings, with a
    heavy concentration of Titian and the relocated Doni portraits from Saturn's recent rearrangement.
    LISTEN FOR
    Apollo's role in the Education of the Prince, what's currently in this room, and the two works that get their
    own track.

 You're now in the room of poetry, music, and civilisation — Apollo's room. In the programme of the
 Planet Rooms, this stage of the prince's education comes after Venus's temptations and before Mars's
 hardening. Apollo is the instructor: rationality, learning, the arts.

 The ceiling was begun by Pietro da Cortona but left unfinished when he left Florence in sixteen
 forty-seven. His most talented pupil, Ciro Ferri, came down from Rome and completed the cycle in the
 sixteen sixties. If you look carefully you can sometimes see the seam — Ferri's figures are a touch more
 brittle than Cortona's, the colours slightly cooler — but the programme reads as a single work.

 The most important permanent resident of this room is one of Titian's greatest devotional paintings: his
 Mary Magdalene, of about fifteen thirty-one. Find it — a half-length figure of a young woman with long
 red-gold hair cascading over her shoulders, looking upward with an open expression. Play the next
 track.

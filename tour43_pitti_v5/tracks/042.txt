 Sala dell'Arca — Luca Giordano and the Ark
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala dell'Arca, named for the ceiling fresco of the Ark of the Covenant.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small room with a ceiling fresco by Luigi Ademollo (early nineteenth century) of the Transportation of the
    Ark of the Covenant — the moment in the Hebrew Bible when the Levites carry the sacred chest into
    Jerusalem. Walls hung with seventeenth-century paintings, including a Caracciolo and works by other
    Neapolitan masters.
    LISTEN FOR
    What the Ark of the Covenant scene is, and why Ademollo (whose work you already saw in the Palatine
    Chapel) is here too.

 You're back in the company of Luigi Ademollo — the same painter whose frescoes covered the Palatine
 Chapel walls and ceiling on the ground floor. By the early eighteen hundreds Ademollo was the busiest
 fresco painter in Habsburg-Lorraine Florence, and he was given the commission to redecorate this
 whole stretch of the Volterrano Wing ceilings.

 Here he painted the Transportation of the Ark of the Covenant — the moment from the second book of
 Samuel when King David brings the sacred chest, the visible sign of the presence of God in the Hebrew
 Bible, into Jerusalem. The Levites carry it on poles. David himself dances in front of it. Crowds gather.
 The subject was a common one for Italian Baroque and neoclassical painters — it gave them an Old
 Testament scene with crowd movement, religious devotion, and an air of solemn procession all at once.

 Look at how Ademollo handles the composition. The Ark is at the centre, raised on its poles. Below it,
 figures lean and gesture; above it, the painted ceiling opens into the painted sky. The same illusionism
 you saw in the Palatine Chapel runs here, though the room is smaller and the effect more intimate.

 Take a walk. When you're ready, continue to the next small room — the Sala di Ercole, named for
 Hercules. Play the next track when you arrive.

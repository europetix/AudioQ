 Raphael — Madonna del Baldacchino
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Saturno, in front of Raphael's Madonna del Baldacchino, a large altarpiece of the enthroned
    Virgin and Child beneath a canopy, flanked by saints, with two music-making angels below.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A large, unfinished oil-on-panel altarpiece, around 1507-1508. The Virgin sits enthroned under a canopy, the
    Christ Child on her lap, with Saints Peter, Bernard, James and Augustine around her and two angels reading
    at the foot of the throne.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why this is the only altarpiece Raphael painted in Florence, why it was left unfinished, and how it bridges his
    Florentine and Roman careers.

 Step back first to take in the scale. After the small, intimate Madonnas you've just seen, this is Raphael
 working big.

 The Madonna del Baldacchino is the only altarpiece Raphael ever painted in Florence. He began it
 around fifteen-oh-seven for the Dei family chapel in the church of Santo Spirito, just across the Oltrarno
 from this palace. The Virgin sits enthroned beneath a great canopy, the baldacchino that gives the
 painting its name, with the Christ Child on her lap. Four saints stand around her: Peter and Bernard,
 James and Augustine. Two small angels at the foot of the throne read from a scroll.

 Look at how the figures occupy the space. This is Raphael learning, in real time, from the two giants
 whose work surrounded him in Florence. From Leonardo he took the soft modelling and the pyramid
 composition. From Fra Bartolomeo, the Florentine friar-painter, he took the grand architectural setting
 and the monumental calm. He was twenty-four, absorbing everything Florence could teach him about
 how to build a large, balanced, dignified holy scene.

 And then he left. In fifteen-oh-eight Pope Julius the Second called him to Rome to paint the papal
 apartments, and Raphael went, leaving the Baldacchino unfinished. Look closely and you can see it: the
 upper part, the architecture and the canopy, was completed later by other hands. The figures are his; the
 grandeur above them is a careful completion. The painting was never installed in Santo Spirito. It passed
 instead into the Medici collections and eventually here.

 So this is a hinge. On one side, the young Raphael of the small Florentine Madonnas, the Gravida and
 the Granduca you've already met. On the other, the Raphael who would walk into the Vatican and paint
 the School of Athens within two years. The Baldacchino is the last thing he made in Florence and the
 first time he painted truly large. Stand in front of it and you're standing at the turn.

 When you've looked enough, move on to the next room, the Sala dell'Iliade, the Room of the Iliad. Play
 the next track when you arrive.

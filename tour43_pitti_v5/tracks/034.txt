 Sala di Ulisse — The Room of Odysseus
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Ulisse, named for the ceiling fresco of Odysseus returning to Ithaca.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small to medium room with a Habsburg-Lorraine-era ceiling fresco showing Odysseus returning home to
    find Penelope. Walls hung with sixteenth and seventeenth-century paintings, including a major Raphael
    Madonna — the Madonna dell'Impannata — and works by Andrea del Sarto and the Mannerist generation.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why Odysseus is on the ceiling here, and the Raphael Madonna that gets its own track.

 The ceiling here was painted in the early eighteen hundreds by Gaspare Martellini, on the theme of
 Odysseus's return home. After twenty years away — ten at the Trojan War, ten wandering — the hero
 finds his wife Penelope still faithful, his son Telemachus grown, his household besieged by suitors. The
 Habsburg-Lorraine programmers of this room wanted the new ruling family to feel like they were the
 rightful returning king of Tuscany after Napoleon's annexation. The fresco was a political claim painted
 as a Greek myth.

 The walls hold a strong sixteenth-century Italian collection. There's a particularly fine Andrea del Sarto
 here, a Pontormo, and at least one excellent late Raphael — the Madonna dell'Impannata, named for the
 oiled-paper windowpane visible in the background of the painting. That's the one that gets its own track.

 Find the Raphael Madonna — a Virgin holding the Christ Child with Saint Anne, the infant John the
 Baptist, and Saint Elizabeth grouped around them, with a small soft-toned window behind the group.

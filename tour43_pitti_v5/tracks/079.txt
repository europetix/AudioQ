 Boboli Gardens — The Ammannati Courtyard and
 First View
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Ammannati Courtyard at the back of the palace, where the far side opens onto the gardens. The
    Artichoke Fountain marks the threshold; beyond it the ground rises in terraces toward the amphitheatre and
    the Egyptian obelisk.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The threshold of the Boboli Gardens — 45,000 square metres of formal Italian garden climbing the hill
    behind the palace, begun in 1549 and developed across four centuries by the Medici, Habsburg-Lorraine, and
    Savoy. Currently mid-way through the Boboli 2030 masterplan, a multi-year restoration of the whole garden.
    LISTEN FOR
    What Boboli is, how old it is, the honest state of the gardens right now, and one access restriction to plan
    around.

 You're at the threshold of one of the most important formal gardens in Europe. Take it slowly. The
 gardens cover forty-five thousand square metres, and the central axis alone runs six hundred and fifty
 metres and rises sixty metres in height.

 Boboli was begun in fifteen forty-nine by Eleonora di Toledo — the Spanish-born wife of Cosimo the
 First, whose funeral dress you saw in the Costume Museum. The original design was by Niccolò
 Tribolo, who died before finishing it; the work passed to Ammannati and Buontalenti. The Boboli that
 emerged became the prototype for every Italian Baroque garden that followed — and through them, for
 Versailles itself.

 One honest note before you set off. The whole garden is mid-way through a long restoration — a
 fifty-million-euro plan called Boboli Twenty-Thirty, meant to return it to its Medici-era condition over
 several years. You'll see scaffolding, fenced-off fountains, and works-in-progress as you walk. It's a
 garden being actively healed, worth knowing rather than being surprised by.

 The architectural logic is the axis. Stand at the back of the courtyard and look out: a single sight line
 runs through the Artichoke Fountain in front of you, up through the amphitheatre, past the Neptune
 Fountain, all the way to the Statue of Abundance at the top of the hill.

 And one access restriction. The Buontalenti Grotto opens its interior only at eleven, one, three, and four
 in the afternoon. Time your visit if you want inside. Thirteen tracks for Boboli. First, walk to your right
 toward the Bacchus Gate — there's a fat little figure astride a turtle you shouldn't miss. Play the next
 track.

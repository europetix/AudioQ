 The Belle Epoque — Room 5
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 5, the Belle Epoque, with society portraiture and the celebrated Franca Florio gown.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Turn-of-the-century elegance, the S-bend silhouette, and dresses worn by the famous beauties painted by
    Boldini and Corcos.
    LISTEN FOR
    The look of the Belle Epoque, and the story of one legendary gown.

 Room five is the Belle Epoque, the beautiful era, those few glittering decades before the First World
 War when European high society dressed with a confidence it would never quite recover.

 The silhouette now is the S-bend, the corset pushing the bust forward and the hips back into a long,
 sinuous curve, the body shaped into the line of an Art Nouveau ornament. This room ties dress to
 portraiture: the painters Giovanni Boldini and Vittorio Corcos made their names painting exactly these
 women in exactly these gowns, all swept brushwork and shimmering silk.

 Look for the gown associated with Franca Florio, the Sicilian noblewoman who was one of the great
 beauties and hostesses of the age, a woman Boldini painted and the era half-worshipped. To see her
 dress is to see what the Belle Epoque thought a goddess should wear.

 When you're ready, continue to room six, Fortuny and the Jazz Age. Play the next track when you
 arrive.

 Sala delle Belle Arti — The Room of the Fine Arts
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala delle Belle Arti, a smaller room in the Volterrano Wing.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A modest-sized room with a ceiling fresco of the Fine Arts — personified figures of Painting, Sculpture, and
    Architecture, painted in the early nineteenth century. Walls hung with seventeenth-century Italian and
    Flemish paintings, with rotation depending on the season.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes added a Room of the Fine Arts, and what's worth looking at on the
    walls.

 Move at your own pace through these smaller rooms. They reward attention, but they're less essential
 than the Planet Rooms. The Volterrano Wing was redecorated in the Habsburg-Lorraine period — that
 is, after the Medici extinction of seventeen thirty-seven — with new ceilings on neoclassical themes.

 This room takes its name from its ceiling: a personified group of the Fine Arts — Painting with her
 palette and brushes, Sculpture with her chisel and a marble head, Architecture holding a compass and a
 plan, all clustered around an idealised allegory of Inspiration. The fresco was painted in the early
 eighteen hundreds when this whole wing was being given a thematic overhaul.

 What's on the walls. The hanging here is more eclectic than in the Planet Rooms: seventeenth-century
 landscapes, a few Flemish still lifes, occasional portraits. Look for the small Salvator Rosa landscapes if
 they're hanging — he's a Neapolitan painter who specialised in wild, stormy mountain views with tiny
 human figures dwarfed by the landscape, and he's a counterweight to the serene Italian classicism that
 dominates the rest of the Palatine. The Pitti owns several Salvator Rosas and they rotate through these
 smaller rooms.

 When you've finished, continue into the Sala dell'Arca — the Room of the Ark — named for its ceiling
 fresco of the Ark of the Covenant being carried by the Levites. Play the next track when you arrive.

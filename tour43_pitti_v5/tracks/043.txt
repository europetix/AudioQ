 Sala di Ercole — The Labours on the Walls
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Ercole, a small room dominated by a series of monumental painted vases telling the story of
    Hercules.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small room with a ceiling fresco of Hercules and walls lined with a remarkable set of monumental
    hard-paste porcelain vases — the Ginori porcelain Labours of Hercules series, made at the Doccia factory
    near Florence in the early nineteenth century. Each vase shows one of the twelve Labours.
    LISTEN FOR
    What Doccia porcelain is, why a set of vases takes the place of paintings in this room, and what the Labours
    of Hercules are.

 The Sala di Ercole is unlike any other Palatine room you've walked through. Instead of paintings on the
 walls, you're surrounded by a set of monumental porcelain vases — each one almost as tall as a person,
 white and blue, with scenes painted around the body.

 These are Doccia porcelain. The Doccia factory was founded by the Florentine Marquis Carlo Ginori in
 seventeen thirty-five in a small town just outside Florence, and it became the most important Italian
 porcelain manufactory of the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Italy lagged behind Meissen and
 Sèvres in the development of hard-paste porcelain — the technical knowledge was guarded almost as a
 state secret in Germany and France — but by the early eighteen hundreds, Doccia had caught up.

 The set in this room shows the twelve Labours of Hercules — the cycle of twelve impossible tasks the
 hero of Greek mythology had to perform in penance for killing his own children in a fit of madness sent
 by the goddess Hera. The Nemean lion. The Hydra. The Erymanthian boar. The stables of Augeas. Each
 vase carries one Labour, painted as a miniature classical scene on the white porcelain ground.

 Walk slowly around. Some of the vases are more elaborately painted than others. The best of them are
 extraordinary objects of early nineteenth-century craft. Take your time, then walk into the next small
 room — the Sala dell'Aurora. Play the next track.

 Space Age & Ready-to-Wear — Room 9
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 9, the Space Age and the rise of ready-to-wear, the fashion of the nineteen-sixties.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The mini-skirt, geometric Space Age design, and the shift from made-to-measure couture to ready-to-wear.
    LISTEN FOR
    How the sixties broke fashion open, and what ready-to-wear changed.

 Room nine is the nineteen-sixties, when fashion stopped taking its orders from the couture houses and
 started taking them from the young.

 The defining garment is the mini-skirt, hemlines climbing to the thigh in a way that would have been
 unthinkable a decade earlier. The look was clean, geometric, futuristic, the so-called Space Age style,
 all bold blocks of colour and new synthetic materials, designers imagining what people might wear on
 the moon. Just as important, and less visible on a mannequin, was a structural change: the rise of
 ready-to-wear, clothes made in standard sizes for shops rather than sewn to measure for one body.
 Fashion was becoming democratic, fast, and, for the first time, a little disposable.

 Look for the bold sixties pieces and the early ready-to-wear gowns here, including work by designers
 who would soon become household names.

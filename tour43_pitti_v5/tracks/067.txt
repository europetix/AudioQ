 Black & White Minimalism — Room 10
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 10, the black-and-white minimalism of late-twentieth-century fashion.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A room built on the discipline of black and white, with pieces by Valentino, the Italian tailoring houses, and
    Jean Paul Gaultier.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why designers keep returning to black and white, and what pure restraint can do.

 Room ten is a study in restraint, a gathering of black-and-white pieces from the later twentieth century.

 Black and white is fashion's hardest test. Strip out colour and everything depends on cut, proportion, and
 line, the architecture of the garment laid bare. The designers here used that discipline as a statement:
 Valentino, who could do opulence but knew the power of pure white; the precise, sculptural tailoring of
 the Italian houses; the provocations of Jean Paul Gaultier, who dressed Madonna and turned underwear
 into outerwear. A single column of perfect black can say more than a room full of colour.

 Walk slowly here. The drama is in the silhouette, not the palette.

 When you've looked enough, continue to room eleven and the great age of the Italian designer. Play the
 next track when you arrive.

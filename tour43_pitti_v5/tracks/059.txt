 18th-Century Court Fashion — Room 2
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 2 of the Museum of Costume and Fashion, the room of eighteenth-century court dress.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Baroque and Rococo court gowns, men's silk suits and accessories from the seventeen hundreds, the age of
    the grand-ducal and Lorraine court in Florence.
    LISTEN FOR
    How an eighteenth-century court dressed, and why the clothes were as much architecture as fashion.

 Room two takes you into the eighteenth century, the age of the Baroque and the Rococo, when
 European court dress reached a kind of structural extreme.

 Look at the women's gowns first. Court dress was built over a rigid framework: a boned bodice
 narrowing the torso, and panniers, hoops worn at the hips, that pushed the skirts out sideways,
 sometimes so far that a woman entering a doorway had to turn. The fabric is the point, heavy silks
 brocaded with silver and gold thread, woven in Lyon or in Italy, so expensive that a single court gown
 could cost more than a house. Men were nearly as elaborate: embroidered silk coats, waistcoats stiff
 with gold thread, lace at the throat and cuffs.

 This was clothing as rank made visible. You did not dress for comfort or movement; you dressed to
 show, at a glance, exactly where you stood in the hierarchy of the court.

 When you've looked enough, walk on to Room three, neoclassical and Empire dress. Play the next track
 when you arrive.

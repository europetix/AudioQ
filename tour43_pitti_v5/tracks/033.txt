TRACK R24 · ROOM 22                                 R-INTRO                             257 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Sala della Stufa — Pietro da Cortona's First
 Florentine Walls
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala della Stufa, a small room near the end of the Volterrano Wing.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small room with frescoes by Pietro da Cortona on all four walls — not on the ceiling but on the upper walls
    — of the Four Ages of Man: Gold, Silver, Bronze, Iron. Painted between 1637 and 1641, before Cortona
    began the Planet Rooms. The room is more intimate than the Planet Rooms and the frescoes are at eye level.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why this small room is one of the most important in the entire Palatine for art history, what the Four Ages of
    Man programme is, and what to look at first.

 This room is more important than it looks. Pietro da Cortona — the painter of the Planet Room ceilings
 — painted these wall frescoes between sixteen thirty-seven and sixteen forty-one, before he began the
 Planet Rooms. This is where the Florentine collaboration started. Grand Duke Ferdinando the Second
 saw what Cortona could do here and commissioned him to scale up the same approach across the
 reception suite.

 The frescoes show the Four Ages of Man — a classical programme drawn from Hesiod and Ovid. The
 Golden Age, the original peaceful world without labour. The Silver Age, still pastoral. The Bronze Age,
 when humans first made war. The Iron Age, the present — cities, greed, the troubles of the modern
 world. Cortona painted each age on a separate wall, building a moral cycle that runs around you as you
 stand in the room.

 Look at the Golden Age first — the most lyrical of the four. A young population in loose draperies
 gathers under trees, the earth gives food without effort, no one is armed, animals come close. Then
 move clockwise and watch the world degrade. By the Iron Age the figures are in armour, the
 architecture is hard, the colour has gone cool and grey.

 The room is called the Sala della Stufa — the Room of the Stove — because for centuries it housed a
 stove for heating this corner of the apartment. Walk around. When you've taken in all four walls,
 continue to the Sala di Ulisse, the Room of Ulysses. Play the next track.

 Stage & Screen Costumes — Room 13
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 13, the final room, stage and screen costumes from the Piero Tosi donation and the Tirelli house.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Costumes for cinema, theatre and opera, including work tied to designer Piero Tosi and the Tirelli costume
    house.
    LISTEN FOR
    Where fashion meets film, and a closing thought before the route turns to the Royal Apartments.

 The last room turns from fashion to costume, the clothes made not to be worn in life but to be seen on
 stage and screen.

 The heart of it is the donation associated with Piero Tosi, the great Italian costume designer who dressed
 the films of Luchino Visconti and Federico Fellini, together with the holdings of the Tirelli house,
 which built costumes for opera, theatre, and cinema across the twentieth century. These are garments
 designed to carry under stage light and the camera's eye, to make a character real at fifty feet. Look
 closely and you'll see the difference from couture: the construction is bolder, the detail calibrated for
 distance, the whole thing a kind of wearable acting.

 It's a fitting place to end a fashion museum, with a reminder that all clothing is, in a sense, costume, a
 way of telling the world who we are.

 That's the Museum of Costume and Fashion. From here, head back into the main palace for the Imperial
 and Royal Apartments, the fourteen state rooms along the south wing. Remember they need a timed
 reservation, if you booked one, this is the moment to use it. Play the next track when you reach their
 entrance.

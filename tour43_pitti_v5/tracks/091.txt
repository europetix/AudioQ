 Leaving the Garden — The Annalena and Porta
 Romana Gates
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Walking toward the southern exits of the Boboli — the Annalena gate on Via Romana and, further south, the
    Porta Romana gate at the garden's far corner.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The southern exits of the Boboli. The Annalena gate opens onto Via Romana in the Oltrarno; the Porta
    Romana gate sits at the garden's southwest corner. Both are quieter than the main palace entrance, and many
    returning visitors prefer to enter from here and climb the gardens in reverse.
    LISTEN FOR
    Where you've arrived, why these quieter gates are worth knowing, and what's left of the tour.

 You're nearing the southern edge of the garden. Take the last stretch slowly.

 Two gates open on this side. The Annalena gate leads onto Via Romana, in the heart of the Oltrarno —
 the south bank of the Arno, the artisan quarter. It's named for a fifteenth-century Florentine
 noblewoman, Annalena Malatesta, who founded a Dominican convent on this street after the violent
 death of her husband. Further south, the Porta Romana gate sits at the garden's far corner, by the old city
 gate of the same name.

 Why mention the exits. Because both are much quieter than the main palace entrance, and many people
 who visit Boboli a second time come in from this side and climb the gardens in reverse — starting at the
 Isolotto, walking up the Viottolone, ending at the palace. If you ever come back — and Boboli rewards
 repeat visits, especially early morning and late afternoon when the light is long — try it from here.

 That's the end of the garden tour. Two more corners of the Pitti are on the same ticket if you'd like to
 wander them on your own: the Porcelain Museum in the Casino del Cavaliere at the top of the hill, and
 the Bardini Garden a few minutes east — smaller and quieter, with one of the loveliest views in
 Florence. Neither is covered on this tour, but both reward the walk. When you're ready to finish, make
 your way back through the gardens to Piazza Pitti, where you began. Play the final track when you get
 there.

 Neoclassical & Empire Style — Room 3
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 3, neoclassical and Empire-style dress, roughly 1800 to 1830.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The high-waisted Empire silhouette, light muslins and classical lines that swept away the rigid court dress of
    the previous century.
    LISTEN FOR
    How the French Revolution and a craze for antiquity changed the female silhouette almost overnight.

 After the heavy silks and hoops of the eighteenth century, room three is a shock of lightness.

 Around eighteen hundred, fashion did something it almost never does: it changed completely, and fast.
 The French Revolution had swept away the old court and, with it, the old court dress. In its place came a
 look borrowed from ancient Greece and Rome, which Europe was rediscovering with passion. The
 waistline jumped up to just under the bust, the so-called Empire line, named for Napoleon's empire.
 Skirts fell straight and narrow to the floor. The heavy brocades gave way to fine white muslin and
 cotton, so thin it was nearly transparent, draped to suggest a classical statue.

 It was the most radical change in the silhouette in centuries, and one of the few moments when women's
 dress became genuinely simple. Look at how little structure there is here compared with the room
 you've just left.

 you arrive.

 Sala di Giove — The Throne Room
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Giove, the second Planet Room on your route through the Grand Duke's apartments. The ceiling
    shows Jupiter crowning a young prince.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A grand reception room with a Pietro da Cortona ceiling fresco of Jupiter — the king of the gods — crowning
    a young prince with a wreath. Walls hung with sixteenth-century Italian paintings, with works by Raphael,
    Andrea del Sarto, Perugino, and Fra Bartolomeo.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why this was the throne room, what's happening on the ceiling, and the small Raphael painting you should
    not leave the room without seeing.

 You're in what was, under the Medici, the throne room. The five-room reception sequence of the Grand
 Duke's apartment was built to deliver the most important visitors to exactly this room, where the Grand
 Duke himself received them under a canopy of state.

 The ceiling tells you that directly. Pietro da Cortona, working in the late sixteen forties, painted Jupiter
 at the centre — king of the gods, ruler — placing a laurel crown on the head of a young prince. The
 prince is the ideal Medici heir at the moment of his ascension to power. Around him: allegories of
 Justice, Strength, and Heroic Virtue. The whole programme of the Planet Rooms peaks here. Saturn
 next door is old age and eternity, but Jupiter is the apex — the moment of being crowned.

 The room was reopened in March of twenty twenty-six after its own period of maintenance, so what you
 see on the walls now may be a slightly different selection from older guidebooks. The core of the room
 is Italian sixteenth-century painting, with Raphael's Veiled Lady — La Velata — among the most
 important.

 Two works in this room get their own track. Find first the Veiled Lady — a portrait of a young woman
 in a luminous white silk dress, with a transparent veil over her hair. Play the next track in front of her.

TRACK R18 · ROOM 4                                  R-INTRO                             300 WORDS · ~2 MIN

 Sala delle Allegorie — Five Painted Virtues
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala delle Allegorie, the second room of the Volterrano Wing — also possibly under restoration in
    spring 2026.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small room with five separate ceiling fresco panels, each one a Volterrano allegorical scene of a virtue —
    Faith, Hope, Charity, and two others — painted around 1658 for Vittoria della Rovere. Walls hung with
    smaller seventeenth-century paintings.
    LISTEN FOR
    What the five Volterrano allegories are, why a grand duchess wanted them on her ceiling, and what to look
    for as you walk through.

 Five ceiling panels here, each one an allegory — a personified virtue, painted by il Volterrano around
 sixteen fifty-eight, the year of his last major commission for Vittoria della Rovere. The five virtues are
 the standard set a seventeenth-century Catholic noblewoman would commission for her private
 apartment: the three theological virtues — Faith, Hope, and Charity — together with two of the
 cardinals, Prudence and Justice.

 Volterrano was a master of what was called quadro riportato — the painted easel-style panel embedded
 in a frescoed ceiling, designed to look as if a framed painting had been hoisted up and stuck to the
 plaster overhead. Each of the five panels reads as a discrete small painting. The framing is illusionistic.
 The figures inside the frames are warm, fleshy, Baroque — soft drapery, idealised female faces, small
 attendant putti.

 The function of the room was, technically, a passage room — the Grand Duchess walked through it to
 reach her more private chambers. But every Florentine visitor she received was meant to read the
 allegories overhead and see what kind of woman she presented herself as.

 If you're lucky enough to be inside the room with the works visible, take a slow walk and look up at
 each of the five panels in turn.

 When you've finished, walk through into the next set of small rooms — the Volterrano Wing continues
 with the Sala delle Belle Arti, the Sala dell'Arca, the Sala di Ercole, the Sala dell'Aurora and the Sala di
 Psiche, along with the smaller rooms named for their ceilings — the Sala della Fama, the Sala della
 Musica, the Sala della Giustizia and the Sala dei Putti (the Room of Cupids) — each named for its
 principal ceiling fresco. Play the next track when you reach the Sala delle Belle Arti.

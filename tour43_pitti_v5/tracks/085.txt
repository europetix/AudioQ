 The Kaffeehaus, the Ganymede Fountain, and the
 Best View in Florence
    WHERE YOU ARE
    At the Kaffeehaus pavilion on the upper terrace — a green-and-cream rococo structure with a copper dome
    — and the nearby Fountain of Ganymede.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A rococo pavilion designed by Zanobi del Rosso in 1775 for Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo, in
    Habsburg-Lorraine green and cream. Reopened in 2022 after about twenty years closed, as the first phase of
    the Boboli 2030 restoration — now with a café, restrooms, and panoramic terrace. It also houses the original
    marble dwarf sculptures, including the real Morgante. The Fountain of Ganymede stands nearby.
    LISTEN FOR
    Why a grand duke built a coffee house in his garden, what's actually inside it now, the view to look for, and
    the small fountain beside it.

 Stop at the terrace edge first. Before anything else, look at the view.

 You're seeing central Florence from above. Brunelleschi's Duomo — finished in fourteen thirty-six, still
 the largest masonry dome ever built — rises in the middle distance. Just to the right, Giotto's bell tower.
 To the left, the Arno. Beyond, the Tuscan hills, all the way to Fiesole. This is widely held to be the best
 single view of the city you can get.

 Turn around. The pavilion is the Kaffeehaus, built in seventeen seventy-five by Zanobi del Rosso for
 Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo. He'd grown up at the Habsburg court in Vienna, where coffee was the
 fashionable drink of European nobility, and he wanted a place to take it in the Viennese style — an
 informal pavilion away from the palace. The green-and-cream is the Habsburg-Lorraine colour scheme,
 straight from Vienna.

 It reopened in twenty twenty-two after about twenty years closed — the first finished piece of the
 Boboli Twenty-Thirty restoration. There's a café here again now. And inside, the Kaffeehaus has
 become the home of the original marble dwarf sculptures from the garden — including the real
 Morgante, whose copy you saw on the turtle down by the gate. If it's open, the original is worth a look.

 Just nearby is the Fountain of Ganymede — the boy carried off to Olympus by Jupiter's eagle to become
 cupbearer to the gods. A fitting little subject for a terrace built around the serving of drinks.

 From here the route climbs to the walled Knight's Garden. Follow the path up. Play the next track when
 you reach a small enclosed garden with low box hedges and roses.

 Sala dei Pappagalli & The Queen's Rooms
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala dei Pappagalli (Parrot Room) and the Queen's rooms, the Salotto della Regina and the Camera
    della Regina.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The Parrot Room, with Napoleonic eagle-silks the Florentines nicknamed parrots, and Queen Margherita of
    Savoy's sitting room and bedroom.
    LISTEN FOR
    The eagles-into-parrots joke, and Queen Margherita's honestly furnished royal bedroom of the 1890s.

 Three rooms now, beginning with the one with the best story.

 The Sala dei Pappagalli, the Parrot Room. Look at the walls first. The fabric is French, woven around
 eighteen ten, when Napoleon was annexing every Italian state he could reach. The pattern shows a
 heraldic bird in an oval medallion, and the bird is meant to be an eagle, Napoleon's imperial symbol.
 When Napoleon fell and the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes returned to Florence in eighteen fifteen,
 they took the rooms over with the fabric already on the walls and didn't strip it. The Florentines, being
 Florentines, looked at the heraldic eagles, decided they looked more like parrots, and the name stuck, a
 quiet joke at the expense of fallen French ambition. Under the Medici, two centuries earlier, this had
 been the antechamber to Grand Prince Ferdinand's bedroom.

 Then the Queen's rooms: the Salotto della Regina, her sitting room, and the Camera della Regina, her
 bedroom. These were Queen Margherita of Savoy's rooms, the first modern celebrity queen of Italy, the
 one the Margherita pizza was named for in eighteen eighty-nine. The bedroom keeps its blue-on-yellow
 silk hangings, mounted in eighteen forty-four, and a softly upholstered, slightly over-furnished comfort.
 It's not the most beautiful room in the apartment. It's one of the most honest: this is what a real royal
 bedroom of the eighteen-nineties was like.

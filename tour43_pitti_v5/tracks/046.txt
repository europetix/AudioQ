 Sala di Flora — The Last of the Volterrano Wing
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Flora, a small room at the end of the Volterrano Wing, named for a ceiling fresco of Flora.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A small intimate room with a ceiling fresco of Flora — the Roman goddess of flowers and spring —
    surrounded by attendant figures. Walls hung with seventeenth-century paintings, including the small
    Caravaggio Sleeping Cupid that is one of the marquee works of the Palatine.
    LISTEN FOR
    What the room contains and the one painting in it you should not leave without looking at.

 You've reached the last of the small rooms of the Volterrano Wing. The ceiling shows Flora, the Roman
 goddess of flowers and spring, surrounded by garlanding attendants and putti, a decorative
 early-nineteenth-century fresco for a room the grand duchesses used for informal afternoons.

 These last rooms of the wing are easy to walk past, but each is named for the scene on its ceiling, and
 together they round out the Grand Duchess's private quarter of the gallery. If they're open as you pass,
 glance up.

 And that's the Palatine Gallery, all twenty-eight rooms of it. From here the route goes up: leave the
 Volterrano Wing and take the staircase to the second floor and the Gallery of Modern Art. Play the next
 track when you reach the gallery entrance.

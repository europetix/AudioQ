 Sala di Berenice — The Volterrano Wing
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala di Berenice, the first room of the Volterrano Wing — the private apartment of the
    seventeenth-century Medici grand duchesses. Possibly restoration-closed in spring 2026.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A smaller, more intimate room than the Planet Rooms. The ceiling carries frescoes by Baldassarre
    Franceschini, known as il Volterrano, painted in the 1650s for Grand Duchess Vittoria della Rovere —
    episodes from the legend of Berenice, the Egyptian queen who offered her hair to the gods.
    LISTEN FOR
    What the Volterrano Wing was, the story on the ceiling, and how to look at a room that's about a wife's
    loyalty.

 If you've made it into this room, the wing is open. The Volterrano Wing has been undergoing
 maintenance through spring twenty twenty-six, and this room and the next — the Sala delle Allegorie
 — were the first to be worked on. If you're here, you're lucky.

 These rooms were the private apartment of the seventeenth-century Medici grand duchesses. Vittoria
 della Rovere — wife of Ferdinando the Second, the same grand duke who commissioned the Planet
 Rooms — lived here. So did her descendants. The last Medici, Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici, the
 Electress Palatine who bequeathed the Medici collections to Florence in seventeen forty-three, died in
 these rooms.

 The wing takes its name from the painter whose ceiling frescoes dominate it: Baldassarre Franceschini,
 called il Volterrano after his hometown. He painted these rooms for Vittoria della Rovere in the sixteen
 fifties.

 The ceiling here tells the story of Berenice — the Greek queen of Egypt who, when her husband
 Ptolemy went off to war, vowed her famous hair to the gods if he returned safely. He did. She cut off her
 hair and placed it in the temple. The next morning the hair had disappeared — turned, according to
 legend, into the constellation Coma Berenices, which you can still see in the northern sky.

 The choice of subject is not accidental. Vittoria della Rovere is presenting herself, in fresco, in the
 figure of a loyal wife — devoted, sacrificing, rewarded by the heavens. Continue into the Sala delle
 Allegorie. Play the next track when you arrive.

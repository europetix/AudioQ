 1930s–40s Elegance — Room 7
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 7, the elegance of the nineteen-thirties and forties, with work by Elsa Schiaparelli.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The long bias-cut glamour of the thirties and the inventive, Surrealist-tinged designs of Elsa Schiaparelli.
    LISTEN FOR
    How thirties dress recovered length and glamour, and why Schiaparelli was fashion's great provocateur.

 Room seven moves into the nineteen-thirties and forties. After the boyish twenties, the silhouette
 lengthened again and turned sleek and grown-up. The great innovation was the bias cut: fabric cut on
 the diagonal of the weave so that it skimmed and clung to the body in a long, liquid line, the look of
 nineteen-thirties Hollywood glamour.

 The name to know here is Elsa Schiaparelli, the Italian-born designer who, working in Paris, became the
 most inventive and provocative couturier of the age. She collaborated with Surrealist artists, Salvador
 Dali among them, on dresses printed with lobsters, hats shaped like shoes, a colour she trademarked as
 shocking pink. Where her rival Coco Chanel offered restraint, Schiaparelli offered wit and shock. Look
 for her pieces here: fashion treated as art and as joke at once.

 you arrive.

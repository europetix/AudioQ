 Fortuny & the Jazz Age — Room 6
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In Room 6, Mariano Fortuny and the Jazz Age, with the famous Delphos tunic.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Fortuny's revolutionary pleated-silk Delphos gown and the liberated dress of the nineteen-twenties.
    LISTEN FOR
    How one Spanish-Venetian designer freed the body from the corset, and the look of the Jazz Age.

 Room six is one of the highlights of the museum, and it turns on a single revolutionary dress.

 Around nineteen-oh-seven, a Spanish artist living in Venice, Mariano Fortuny, invented the Delphos: a
 column of finely pleated silk that hung straight from the shoulders to the floor, clinging to the body with
 no corset, no structure, nothing underneath. He named it for an ancient Greek bronze, the Charioteer of
 Delphi, and he guarded the secret of how he set the pleats so closely that it has never been fully
 reproduced. Worn by dancers and free spirits, the actress Eleonora Duse among them, the Delphos was
 scandalous and liberating at once, a dress you could actually move and breathe in.

 It points straight to the nineteen-twenties, also gathered here, when the female silhouette finally dropped
 the corset for the loose, straight, knee-skimming line of the flapper, and Coco Chanel made simplicity
 itself the height of luxury.

 next track when you arrive.

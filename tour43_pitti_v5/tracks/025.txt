 Sala dell'Iliade — The Room of the Iliad
    WHERE YOU ARE
    In the Sala dell'Iliade, the first large reception room of the Grand Duke's apartment. A sculpted female figure
    stands in the centre, surrounded by paintings hung salon-style on every wall.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    A large rectangular room with a high ceiling carrying frescoes of scenes from Homer's Iliad. Walls hung floor
    to ceiling with paintings in heavy carved frames. The centre of the room holds Lorenzo Bartolini's marble
    Charity — a seated female figure with children.
    LISTEN FOR
    What this room was used for in the Medici court, what's on the ceiling, and which three paintings get their
    own track.

 Take in the room before you focus on anything in particular. The ceiling shows scenes from Homer's
 Iliad — painted in eighteen nineteen by Luigi Sabatelli for the Habsburg-Lorraine restoration after
 Napoleon — which is what gives the room its name. Underneath, the walls are covered in paintings two
 and three deep, Florentine fashion, with the larger works centred and smaller ones fitting around them.

 Under the Medici, the five rooms following this one — Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Apollo, Venus — formed
 the Grand Duke's reception suite. This antechamber was where visitors waited before being shown
 deeper in. The most important people went all the way to the Jupiter Room, which was the throne room.

 The sculpture in the centre is Lorenzo Bartolini's Charity, finished in eighteen thirty-five — a
 Tuscan-born sculptor working in the long shadow of Canova. Bartolini wanted Italian sculpture to
 recover from what he saw as the chilly perfection of neoclassicism and return to warmer Renaissance
 models. His Charity is meant to feel almost like a Madonna without the religious framing — a seated
 mother tending to children, simple, grounded.

 Four paintings in this room get their own track. We'll take them roughly in the order they hang. Look
 first for a small portrait of a pregnant young woman, hand resting on her belly — that's Raphael's La
 Gravida. Find it and play the next track.

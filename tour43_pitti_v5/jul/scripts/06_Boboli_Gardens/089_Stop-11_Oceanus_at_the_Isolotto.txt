Circle the whole pool before you fix on the giant in the middle. The fountain works from every angle, and there's more here than him. Giambologna, born Jean Boulogne in Flanders in fifteen twenty-nine, was the dominant sculptor of late sixteenth-century Florence. He came to Italy in his twenties, stayed for life, and became official sculptor to the Medici grand dukes. The equestrian bronze of Cosimo the First in the Piazza della Signoria is his. The Rape of the Sabines under the Loggia dei Lanzi is his. So is this. He cast the Oceanus between fifteen seventy-one and fifteen seventy-five for Francesco the First. Oceanus, in classical cosmology, was the great river encircling the whole flat earth, the source of all other waters. He stands tall, bearded, holding a long staff. Around the base of his plinth, three reclining figures: the personified rivers Nile, Ganges, and Euphrates, Africa, Asia, and the ancient Near East. The four waters together stood for the whole world, with Medici Tuscany imagined at its commercial centre. Now look around the pool, not just at the middle. The Vasca dell'Isolotto, built in sixteen eighteen, is the largest body of water in the garden, and its rim and approaches hold a scatter of statue groups. Look for Perseus on horseback, rising up out of the water as if swimming his horse across, and Andromeda nearby. And look for the smaller, warmer groups, peasants, and children at play, set lower down. After all the gods and emperors, the garden lets in a few ordinary people too. Note: the Oceanus you see is a high-quality copy. The original was moved indoors for conservation in nineteen ninety-eight and is now in the Bargello in central Florence, where you can see Giambologna's actual hand.

You've reached the highest, quietest, and arguably loveliest corner of the whole garden. The Giardino del Cavaliere, the Knight's Garden, is a small walled enclosure, and what it sits on is the surprising part. In fifteen twenty-nine, Florence was under siege by the combined forces of the Holy Roman Emperor and the Pope, trying to force Medici rule back onto the briefly revived Florentine Republic. The Republic put Michelangelo himself in charge of the city's fortifications, and he designed defensive bastions along the southern walls. This garden is laid out on top of one of them. You're standing on a rampart built to keep the Medici out, which later became a pleasure garden for the Medici once they'd won. Florentine history rarely resolves cleanly. Inside the walls: low box hedges, beds of rare and fragrant plants, and the small Fountain of the Monkeys, la Fontana delle Scimmie, with its little bronze monkeys around the basin. The Casino del Cavaliere at the centre now houses the Porcelain Museum, which we'll come to later. If you've come in spring, and especially in May, the box-hedged rose garden on the slopes below is in full bloom, and the scent reaches the terrace. And the view from up here opens south and east toward the Chianti hills, away from the city, a softer, more rural view than the Kaffeehaus gives. When you've taken it in, look toward the very top of the central garden axis, where a single colossal female statue stands against the sky. Walk toward her and play the next track.

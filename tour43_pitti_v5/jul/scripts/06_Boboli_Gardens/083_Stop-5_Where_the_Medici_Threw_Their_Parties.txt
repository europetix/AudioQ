Stop here a moment. Look around the curve of the amphitheatre. You're inside one of the great spaces of Italian theatrical history. It was built from fifteen ninety-nine for the most spectacular Medici wedding ever held. Maria de' Medici, daughter of Grand Duke Francesco the First, was being married by proxy to Henri the Fourth of France, and Florence was determined to outshine every previous royal celebration. The amphitheatre was carved into the hillside as a permanent theatre for what the Medici called intermedi, elaborate combinations of music, dance, and stage machinery, and was later used for tournaments, mock naval battles, and operatic performances. When flooded for the naval shows, the whole performance space became a lake. Six tiers of grass step down to the central oval. In Medici times those tiers carried wooden benches. Several thousand spectators could watch, with the Grand Duke and his closest courtiers in the highest tier. The obelisk in the middle is older than everything else in this garden by a wide margin. It was carved in Egypt around thirteen hundred BCE for the pharaoh Ramesses the Second. The Romans brought it to Italy in the first century. Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo brought it to Florence in seventeen eighty-nine, where it has stood at the centre of the amphitheatre ever since, a three-thousand-year-old Egyptian monument anchoring a Renaissance Italian theatre.

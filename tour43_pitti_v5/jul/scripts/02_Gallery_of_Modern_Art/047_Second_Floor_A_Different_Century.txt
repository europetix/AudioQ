The Gallery of Modern Art opened in nineteen twenty-four. The word 'modern' in the title is a mid-twenties usage, it means, roughly, painting from the eighteenth century forward. There is no Picasso here. No Matisse. No twentieth-century abstraction. What there is, in thirty rooms, is one of the best collections anywhere of Italian painting between the late seventeen hundreds and the early nineteen hundreds, and in particular, the most concentrated collection in existence of the Tuscan Macchiaioli, the nineteenth-century Italian movement that anticipated Impressionism by a decade. The rooms themselves are part of the show. These were the everyday family rooms of the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes, less formal than the Palatine, less ceremonial than the Royal Apartments. Frescoed ceilings, stucco mouldings, windows on both sides looking out over Florence and the Boboli Gardens. The thirty numbered rooms run in a loop around the courtyard, and the layout is roughly chronological. Neoclassicism in rooms one to four. Romanticism and history painting in five to seven. Then the great Macchiaioli and mid-nineteenth-century band in eight to sixteen. The donated collections in seventeen to twenty-one. The Italian schools and Symbolism in twenty-two to twenty-six. And the early twentieth century in twenty-seven to thirty. Off to one side sits the grand Ballroom, the Sala da Ballo, and a mezzanine called the Andito degli Angiolini that holds temporary shows.

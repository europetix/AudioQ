Two rooms to open the apartment. The Anticamera, the Antechamber, is where the sequence begins, the first room a visitor crossed on the way in. Under the Medici and Lorraine it was a waiting room and a filter: you were admitted this far, and how much further depended on who you were. Next door is the Sala delle Nicchie, the Hall of Niches, named for the rectangular niches cut into its walls, each holding a piece of sculpture, antique Roman marbles, Renaissance copies, small busts. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries it worked as a small private gallery within the apartment, a place where the family could keep favourite pieces close at hand without going down to the main Palatine collection. Look at the ceiling and the proportions: a room built to display, on an intimate domestic scale, the same princely taste that fills the great gallery next door. Together these two rooms set the key for everything that follows. The grandeur is real, but it's residential grandeur, a home that happened to be run by grand dukes and kings.

Two of the grandest rooms in the apartment. First the Sala Verde, the Green Room, named for the deep green silk on its walls. Look up: set into the ceiling is a canvas by Luca Giordano, the prolific Neapolitan painter, an Allegory of Peace between Florence and Fiesole, one of the very few original Medici-era furnishings left in these rooms. Under the Medici this was the guardroom of Grand Prince Ferdinando's apartment; everything decorative you see is a later overlay on those old bones. Then the Sala del Trono, the Throne Room. This is where the Savoy layer takes over completely. The walls are hung with red ornamental fabric, a fine set of Flemish tapestries and full-length portraits; under a canopy, ringed by a low gilded balustrade, stands the throne of the King of Italy, a gilded wooden armchair to which the House of Savoy simply added its coat of arms. In Medici times this was the audience room; the Lorraine called it the Chamberlains' Room; the Savoy made it the throne room when Florence was briefly the capital. Three uses, one room.

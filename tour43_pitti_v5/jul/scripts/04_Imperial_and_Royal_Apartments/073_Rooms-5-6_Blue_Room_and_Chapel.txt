Two rooms, very different in mood. First the Salotto Celeste, the Blue Room, named for the sky-blue silk on its walls. Stop in the middle and turn slowly: the faces watching you are mostly the seventeenth-century Medici grand dukes, painted by one man, Justus Sustermans, the Flemish-born court painter who held the post for sixty years. He painted everyone who mattered at the Medici court, and a row of his portraits shows you the family likeness running across five generations, the long Medici jaw, the watchful eyes. Look too for the eagle chimneypiece by Francis Harwood. Under the Medici this had been the room of musical instruments; the Lorraine turned it into a dining room. Then the Cappella, the Chapel. This is the one room in the whole apartment that keeps its original Medici decoration. It began as the Alcova, the alcove bedchamber, and its seventeenth-century fittings survive: look closely at the ebony furniture inlaid with coloured-marble pietre dure from the grand-ducal workshop, the famous Opificio delle Pietre Dure. When the room was later adapted as a private chapel, that older decoration was left in place. Everywhere else in the apartment you read layers of change; here you read the Medici, undisturbed.

Reservation is compulsory and entry is by timed slot; if you're here without one, that's why you'd be turned away. Book a slot before you visit. What you're about to walk are fourteen ceremonial rooms that, over the centuries, served three different ruling families. The oldest layer is Medici: in the late seventeenth century these were the everyday apartment of Grand Prince Ferdinando de' Medici, the brilliant, short-lived son of Cosimo the Third. The second layer is Habsburg-Lorraine, who from seventeen thirty-seven redecorated throughout, adding the white-and-gold stucco ceilings and, later, the silk wall-hangings. The third is Savoy: when Florence became capital of a united Italy in eighteen sixty-five, King Victor Emmanuel the Second moved in, and the family used the rooms until nineteen nineteen, when they handed the palace to the Italian state. The tour is a single enfilade, and the official route runs in order: you check in at the Sala di Bona, then pass through the Antechamber and the Hall of Niches, the Green Room and the Throne Room, the Blue Room and the Chapel, the Parrot Room and the Queen's rooms, the two little Cabinets, and finally the King's rooms and the Red Room. As you go, watch for the layering: a Medici ceiling over a Lorraine wall-silk over a Savoy bed. Six tracks here, one per cluster of rooms.

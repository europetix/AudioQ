The last rooms of the apartment, five of them, ending the enfilade. First the two little Cabinets. The Gabinetto Ovale, the Oval Cabinet, and beside it the Gabinetto Rotondo, the Round Cabinet, were added by Maria Teresa of Austria between seventeen sixty-three and seventeen sixty-five, ahead of the arrival in Florence of her son Pietro Leopoldo as the new grand duke. The architect, Ignazio Pellegrini, chose an oval and a circle, shapes that suited the international Rococo taste of the moment. They're small, jewel-like rooms, a deliberate change of scale after the great state halls. Then the King's rooms. The Camera del Re, the King's Bedroom, was used by Umberto the First; look for the canary-yellow walls and a Madonna and Child with the young Saint John by a young Andrea del Sarto, the same painter whose work you met across the Palatine. Beside it, the Studio del Re, the King's Study, the working room of the apartment. And finally the Salotto Rosso, the Red Room, named for its hangings, which closes the enfilade. That's the end of the Royal Apartments, and nearly the end of the upstairs. From here head back down to the ground floor and the Cortile dell'Ammannati. One last part of the palace waits just off it: the Museum of Russian Icons and the Palatine Chapel, in Cosimo the Third's old summer apartment.

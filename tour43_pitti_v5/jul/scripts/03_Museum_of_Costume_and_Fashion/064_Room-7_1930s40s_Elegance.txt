Room seven moves into the nineteen-thirties and forties. After the boyish twenties, the silhouette lengthened again and turned sleek and grown-up. The great innovation was the bias cut: fabric cut on the diagonal of the weave so that it skimmed and clung to the body in a long, liquid line, the look of nineteen-thirties Hollywood glamour. The name to know here is Elsa Schiaparelli, the Italian-born designer who, working in Paris, became the most inventive and provocative couturier of the age. She collaborated with Surrealist artists, Salvador Dali among them, on dresses printed with lobsters, hats shaped like shoes, a colour she trademarked as shocking pink. Where her rival Coco Chanel offered restraint, Schiaparelli offered wit and shock. Look for her pieces here: fashion treated as art and as joke at once.

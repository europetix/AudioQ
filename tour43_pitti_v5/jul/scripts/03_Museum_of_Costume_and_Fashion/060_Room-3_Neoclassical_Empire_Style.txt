After the heavy silks and hoops of the eighteenth century, room three is a shock of lightness. Around eighteen hundred, fashion did something it almost never does: it changed completely, and fast. The French Revolution had swept away the old court and, with it, the old court dress. In its place came a look borrowed from ancient Greece and Rome, which Europe was rediscovering with passion. The waistline jumped up to just under the bust, the so-called Empire line, named for Napoleon's empire. Skirts fell straight and narrow to the floor. The heavy brocades gave way to fine white muslin and cotton, so thin it was nearly transparent, draped to suggest a classical statue. It was the most radical change in the silhouette in centuries, and one of the few moments when women's dress became genuinely simple.

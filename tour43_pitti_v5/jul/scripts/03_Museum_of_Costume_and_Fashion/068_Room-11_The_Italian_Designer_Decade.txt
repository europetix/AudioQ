Room eleven belongs to the labels that made made-in-Italy the most powerful phrase in fashion. From the nineteen-eighties on, a handful of Italian designers, most of them based in Milan, came to define how the world dressed. Giorgio Armani softened the suit into something fluid and powerful, the uniform of a new kind of professional. Gianni Versace did the opposite, loud, glamorous, gold and colour and rock-star excess. Prada, an old Milanese leather house, reinvented itself around a cool, intellectual restraint. Gucci swung from heritage to scandalous glamour and back. Together they turned Italian fashion from the upstart of the Sala Bianca days into a global empire. Look at the pieces here as a kind of family argument: four very different ideas of what Italian luxury should be, all of them right.

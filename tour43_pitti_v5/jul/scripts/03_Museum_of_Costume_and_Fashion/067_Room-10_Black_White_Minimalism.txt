Room ten is a study in restraint, a gathering of black-and-white pieces from the later twentieth century. Black and white is fashion's hardest test. Strip out colour and everything depends on cut, proportion, and line, the architecture of the garment laid bare. The designers here used that discipline as a statement: Valentino, who could do opulence but knew the power of pure white; the precise, sculptural tailoring of the Italian houses; the provocations of Jean Paul Gaultier, who dressed Madonna and turned underwear into outerwear. A single column of perfect black can say more than a room full of colour. Walk slowly here. The drama is in the silhouette, not the palette.

Room nine is the nineteen-sixties, when fashion stopped taking its orders from the couture houses and started taking them from the young. The defining garment is the mini-skirt, hemlines climbing to the thigh in a way that would have been unthinkable a decade earlier. The look was clean, geometric, futuristic, the so-called Space Age style, all bold blocks of colour and new synthetic materials, designers imagining what people might wear on the moon. Just as important, and less visible on a mannequin, was a structural change: the rise of ready-to-wear, clothes made in standard sizes for shops rather than sewn to measure for one body. Fashion was becoming democratic, fast, and, for the first time, a little disposable. Look for the bold sixties pieces and the early ready-to-wear gowns here, including work by designers who would soon become household names.

Room eight is the heart of the whole museum, because what it commemorates happened in this very building. On the twelfth of February, nineteen fifty-one, a Florentine businessman named Giovanni Battista Giorgini staged a fashion show in his own home for a small group of American department-store buyers. It went well enough that the next shows moved into the grandest room available, the Sala Bianca, the White Hall, of the Palatine Gallery here in Palazzo Pitti. Those Sala Bianca shows, through the nineteen-fifties, are where Italian fashion was born as an international force, where the world first saw that Italy, not only Paris, could lead. The houses shown there, among them Schubert, laid the foundation of everything Milan would later become. The room also holds historic grand-ducal jewels, including pieces associated with Ferdinand the Third of Lorraine, a reminder that this palace dressed its rulers in splendour for centuries before it dressed the world.

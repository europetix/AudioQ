Room four is the swing back. The light classical line of the Empire didn't last. Through the eighteen thirties, forties and fifties the waist dropped back to its natural place and tightened, and the skirt began to grow, and grow. By the eighteen fifties it had become the crinoline, a vast bell-shaped skirt held out by a cage of steel hoops, sometimes wide enough to fill a doorway again, as in the eighteenth century. Look at the day dresses, the wedding gowns, the court wear gathered here. The fabrics are rich, the construction elaborate, the message one of prosperity and propriety, the values of the rising nineteenth-century middle class as much as of the court. This is the world of the Romantic novel made visible: sentiment, modesty, and an enormous amount of cloth. When you've finished, continue to room five and the Belle Epoque.

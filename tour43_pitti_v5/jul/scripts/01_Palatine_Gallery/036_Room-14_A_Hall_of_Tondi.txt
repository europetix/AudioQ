You're now standing in a room of tondi, round paintings, the format that flourished in Florence in the late fourteen hundreds and early fifteen hundreds. Tondi were almost always commissioned for weddings or the birth of a child, hung in the bedroom or main living room of the new household, and depicted the Virgin and Child or the Holy Family. The round format was associated with the Florentine domestic interior the way the rectangular panel was associated with churches. Take a slow walk around the room. You'll see tondi by Sandro Botticelli, by Domenico Ghirlandaio, by Lorenzo di Credi, most of them small and intimate, painted for specific Florentine families whose names sometimes survive in the inventories and sometimes don't. This is one of the densest concentrations of Florentine fifteenth-century tondi anywhere outside the Uffizi. One of these gets its own track. Find the tondo by Filippo Lippi, the elder Lippi, father of Filippino, showing the Virgin and Child with episodes from the life of the Virgin in the background. It's sometimes called the Tondo Bartolini after the family that commissioned it. Find it and play the next track.

A small room. Don't spend long. Look up. The ceiling shows Aurora, the Roman goddess of the dawn, driving her chariot across the sky, while Night flees ahead of her in a dark cloud. Aurora was one of the most popular subjects for early nineteenth-century ceiling frescoes throughout Europe. The image carried an obvious symbolic load: the dawning of a new age, the light of reason driving away the darkness of ignorance, the rebirth of order after the chaos of revolution. For the Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes returning to Florence after Napoleon, an Aurora was almost obligatory. They were presenting their own restoration as a dawn, the morning that followed the long night of French rule. Half the palaces of restored Europe acquired an Aurora ceiling in the eighteen tens or twenties. The fresco here is competent rather than extraordinary, and the walls carry rotating small paintings.

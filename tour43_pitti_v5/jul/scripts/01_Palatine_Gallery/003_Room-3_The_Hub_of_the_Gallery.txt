You're standing in the practical hub of the Palatine. Two routes leave this room. Through the doorway on your right is the Volterrano Wing, the private apartments of Grand Duchess Vittoria della Rovere and the Habsburg-Lorraine grand duchesses who followed her, with smaller rooms named for their ceiling subjects. Straight ahead through the Hall of Music are the Grand Duke's reception rooms, the famous Planet Rooms. We'll do the Volterrano Wing on the way back; for now, we head straight to the Planet side. The ceiling frescoes here are by Giuseppe Castagnoli, painted in the early eighteen hundreds, and they show the two ruling families this palace served, the Medici and the Habsburg-Lorraine, receiving allegorical tributes. Castagnoli was a competent court painter rather than a great one, and the frescoes function more as decoration than as argument. The room takes his name partly because of him, partly because the curators needed a name for the room and there it was on the ceiling. The walls are court portraits of the Medici and Habsburg-Lorraine families, keep your eye out for the small full-length of Gian Gastone de' Medici, the last male Medici grand duke, who died in seventeen thirty-seven and ended the dynasty. He stands solemnly in his curled seventeenth-century wig, with the city of Florence dissolving in stormy haze behind him. The painter knew what he was doing.

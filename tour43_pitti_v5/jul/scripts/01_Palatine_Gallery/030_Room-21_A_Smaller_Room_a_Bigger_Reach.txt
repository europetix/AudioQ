You've left the grand reception suite. From here on, the rooms are smaller and more private, these were the corridors and antechambers connecting the Grand Duke's ceremonial rooms to the more intimate parts of the apartment. The ceiling here shows the infant Jupiter, the same king of gods you saw crowning a prince two rooms back, now painted as a baby. The legend was that the infant Jupiter, hidden from his father Saturn who ate his children, was raised on a hidden island by nymphs and nursed by a divine goat called Amalthea. The fresco shows the moment of his nursing. The iconographic logic for the Medici was clear enough: even the king of the gods had to be a child first, hidden and protected. The room has rotated through different hangings over the centuries, and today it holds a more eclectic group than the Planet Rooms. Two works in this room get their own track. One is a small Sleeping Cupid by Caravaggio, low on the wall, we'll come to it in a moment. The other, and the work this room is best known for, is Cristofano Allori's Judith with the Head of Holofernes. Look up at Jupiter and Amalthea first. Then find the Allori, a beautiful young woman, calm as anything, holding a man's severed head. It gets its own track. Play the next track in front of it.

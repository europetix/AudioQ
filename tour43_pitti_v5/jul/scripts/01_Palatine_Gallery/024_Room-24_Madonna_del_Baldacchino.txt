Step back first to take in the scale. After the small, intimate Madonnas you've just seen, this is Raphael working big. The Madonna del Baldacchino is the only altarpiece Raphael ever painted in Florence. He began it around fifteen-oh-seven for the Dei family chapel in the church of Santo Spirito, just across the Oltrarno from this palace. The Virgin sits enthroned beneath a great canopy, the baldacchino that gives the painting its name, with the Christ Child on her lap. Four saints stand around her: Peter and Bernard, James and Augustine. Two small angels at the foot of the throne read from a scroll. Look at how the figures occupy the space. This is Raphael learning, in real time, from the two giants whose work surrounded him in Florence. From Leonardo he took the soft modelling and the pyramid composition. From Fra Bartolomeo, the Florentine friar-painter, he took the grand architectural setting and the monumental calm. He was twenty-four, absorbing everything Florence could teach him about how to build a large, balanced, dignified holy scene. And then he left. In fifteen-oh-eight Pope Julius the Second called him to Rome to paint the papal apartments, and Raphael went, leaving the Baldacchino unfinished. Look closely and you can see it: the upper part, the architecture and the canopy, was completed later by other hands. The figures are his; the grandeur above them is a careful completion. The painting was never installed in Santo Spirito. It passed instead into the Medici collections and eventually here. So this is a hinge. On one side, the young Raphael of the small Florentine Madonnas, the Gravida and the Granduca you've already met. On the other, the Raphael who would walk into the Vatican and paint the School of Athens within two years. The Baldacchino is the last thing he made in Florence and the first time he painted truly large. Stand in front of it and you're standing at the turn.

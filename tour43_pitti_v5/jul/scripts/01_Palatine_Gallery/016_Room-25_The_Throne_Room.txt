You're in what was, under the Medici, the throne room. The five-room reception sequence of the Grand Duke's apartment was built to deliver the most important visitors to exactly this room, where the Grand Duke himself received them under a canopy of state. The ceiling tells you that directly. Pietro da Cortona, working in the late sixteen forties, painted Jupiter at the centre, king of the gods, ruler, placing a laurel crown on the head of a young prince. The prince is the ideal Medici heir at the moment of his ascension to power. Around him: allegories of Justice, Strength, and Heroic Virtue. The whole programme of the Planet Rooms peaks here. Saturn next door is old age and eternity, but Jupiter is the apex, the moment of being crowned. The room was reopened in March of twenty twenty-six after its own period of maintenance, so what you see on the walls now may be a slightly different selection from older guidebooks. The core of the room is Italian sixteenth-century painting, with Raphael's Veiled Lady, La Velata, among the most important. Two works in this room get their own track. Find first the Veiled Lady, a portrait of a young woman in a luminous white silk dress, with a transparent veil over her hair. Play the next track in front of her.

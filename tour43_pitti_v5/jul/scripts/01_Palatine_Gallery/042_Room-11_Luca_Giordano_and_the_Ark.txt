You're back in the company of Luigi Ademollo, the same painter whose frescoes covered the Palatine Chapel walls and ceiling on the ground floor. By the early eighteen hundreds Ademollo was the busiest fresco painter in Habsburg-Lorraine Florence, and he was given the commission to redecorate this whole stretch of the Volterrano Wing ceilings. Here he painted the Transportation of the Ark of the Covenant, the moment from the second book of Samuel when King David brings the sacred chest, the visible sign of the presence of God in the Hebrew Bible, into Jerusalem. The Levites carry it on poles. David himself dances in front of it. Crowds gather. The subject was a common one for Italian Baroque and neoclassical painters, it gave them an Old Testament scene with crowd movement, religious devotion, and an air of solemn procession all at once. Look at how Ademollo handles the composition. The Ark is at the centre, raised on its poles. Below it, figures lean and gesture; above it, the painted ceiling opens into the painted sky. The same illusionism you saw in the Palatine Chapel runs here, though the room is smaller and the effect more intimate. Take a walk.

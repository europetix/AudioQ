Originally the figure stood in a softly lit interior, with a window and a glimpse of landscape behind her. Sometime in the late seventeen hundreds or early eighteen hundreds, the background was overpainted black, probably to focus attention on the figures in the manner fashionable at the time. The overpaint has been examined many times, and the current curatorial decision is to leave it, partly because removing it would risk damaging the rest, partly because the dark has become part of how the painting works on us. It feels older and more sacred for being veiled. That's an accident of history, but the accident is beautiful.

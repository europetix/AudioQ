This room is more important than it looks. Pietro da Cortona, the painter of the Planet Room ceilings, painted these wall frescoes between sixteen thirty-seven and sixteen forty-one, before he began the Planet Rooms. This is where the Florentine collaboration started. Grand Duke Ferdinando the Second saw what Cortona could do here and commissioned him to scale up the same approach across the reception suite. The frescoes show the Four Ages of Man, a classical programme drawn from Hesiod and Ovid. The Golden Age, the original peaceful world without labour. The Silver Age, still pastoral. The Bronze Age, when humans first made war. The Iron Age, the present, cities, greed, the troubles of the modern world. Cortona painted each age on a separate wall, building a moral cycle that runs around you as you stand in the room. Look at the Golden Age first, the most lyrical of the four. A young population in loose draperies gathers under trees, the earth gives food without effort, no one is armed, animals come close. Then move clockwise and watch the world degrade. By the Iron Age the figures are in armour, the architecture is hard, the colour has gone cool and grey. The room is called the Sala della Stufa, the Room of the Stove, because for centuries it housed a stove for heating this corner of the apartment. Walk around.

You're now in a transitional zone. These two small rooms used to be part of the personal apartment of the Habsburg-Lorraine grand duchesses, places where the royal family lived rather than received. Then Napoleon happened. In eighteen-oh-seven, when Napoleon annexed Tuscany into the French Empire, he had this particular small room fitted out as a bathing chamber for his sister Elisa Baciocchi, whom he had installed as Grand Duchess of Tuscany. Elisa never actually used the bath; the room was prepared for an imperial visit Napoleon never made. After his fall in eighteen fifteen, the returning Habsburg-Lorraine grand dukes left the room exactly as it was. The white marble bath and the neoclassical detailing are preserved as a small accidental monument to the brief French occupation. Next door, the Sala dell'Iride is more decorative, a ceiling fresco of Iris, the Greek goddess of the rainbow, painted in the early eighteen hundreds. The wall paintings rotate; what hangs when you walk through depends on the season's curatorial choices. Walk slowly through both rooms. When you've crossed them, you reach the Castagnoli Room, the hub you passed through earlier. From there you'll now turn into the Volterrano Wing, the private rooms of the seventeenth-century Medici grand duchesses. One honest note: parts of the Volterrano Wing have been under restoration through spring twenty twenty-six, and the next two rooms, the Sala di Berenice and the Sala delle Allegorie, may still be partly closed.

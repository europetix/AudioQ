Take in the room before you focus on anything in particular. The ceiling shows scenes from Homer's Iliad, painted in eighteen nineteen by Luigi Sabatelli for the Habsburg-Lorraine restoration after Napoleon, which is what gives the room its name. Underneath, the walls are covered in paintings two and three deep, Florentine fashion, with the larger works centred and smaller ones fitting around them. Under the Medici, the five rooms following this one, Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Apollo, Venus, formed the Grand Duke's reception suite. This antechamber was where visitors waited before being shown deeper in. The most important people went all the way to the Jupiter Room, which was the throne room. The sculpture in the centre is Lorenzo Bartolini's Charity, finished in eighteen thirty-five, a Tuscan-born sculptor working in the long shadow of Canova. Bartolini wanted Italian sculpture to recover from what he saw as the chilly perfection of neoclassicism and return to warmer Renaissance models. His Charity is meant to feel almost like a Madonna without the religious framing, a seated mother tending to children, simple, grounded. Four paintings in this room get their own track. We'll take them roughly in the order they hang. Look first for a small portrait of a pregnant young woman, hand resting on her belly, that's Raphael's La Gravida. Find it and play the next track.

Look at the figure standing in the centre of the room. Antonio Canova carved this marble, the Venus Italica, between eighteen-oh-four and eighteen-twelve, on a direct commission from Napoleon. Napoleon had just stripped the Pitti of one of its most famous antique sculptures, the so-called Medici Venus, an ancient Roman copy of a Greek original, and shipped it to Paris. Canova, the most celebrated sculptor in Europe, was commissioned to replace it. He didn't make a copy. He made a modern Venus, a younger and more vulnerable figure than the antique one, and placed her in the same spot. After Waterloo the original Medici Venus came home from Paris. It's now in the Tribuna of the Uffizi. The Canova replacement stayed at the Pitti because by then the Florentines had grown fond of her. Two more works here get their own track.

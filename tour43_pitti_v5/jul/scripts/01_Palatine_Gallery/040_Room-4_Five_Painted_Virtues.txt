Five ceiling panels here, each one an allegory, a personified virtue, painted by il Volterrano around sixteen fifty-eight, the year of his last major commission for Vittoria della Rovere. The five virtues are the standard set a seventeenth-century Catholic noblewoman would commission for her private apartment: the three theological virtues, Faith, Hope, and Charity, together with two of the cardinals, Prudence and Justice. Volterrano was a master of what was called quadro riportato, the painted easel-style panel embedded in a frescoed ceiling, designed to look as if a framed painting had been hoisted up and stuck to the plaster overhead. Each of the five panels reads as a discrete small painting. The framing is illusionistic. The figures inside the frames are warm, fleshy, Baroque, soft drapery, idealised female faces, small attendant putti. The function of the room was, technically, a passage room, the Grand Duchess walked through it to reach her more private chambers. But every Florentine visitor she received was meant to read the allegories overhead and see what kind of woman she presented herself as. If you're lucky enough to be inside the room with the works visible, take a slow walk and look up at each of the five panels in turn. When you've finished, walk through into the next set of small rooms, the Volterrano Wing continues with the Sala delle Belle Arti, the Sala dell'Arca, the Sala di Ercole, the Sala dell'Aurora and the Sala di Psiche, along with the smaller rooms named for their ceilings, the Sala della Fama, the Sala della Musica, the Sala della Giustizia and the Sala dei Putti (the Room of Cupids), each named for its principal ceiling fresco.

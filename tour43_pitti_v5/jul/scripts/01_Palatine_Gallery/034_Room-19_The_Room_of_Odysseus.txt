The ceiling here was painted in the early eighteen hundreds by Gaspare Martellini, on the theme of Odysseus's return home. After twenty years away, ten at the Trojan War, ten wandering, the hero finds his wife Penelope still faithful, his son Telemachus grown, his household besieged by suitors. The Habsburg-Lorraine programmers of this room wanted the new ruling family to feel like they were the rightful returning king of Tuscany after Napoleon's annexation. The fresco was a political claim painted as a Greek myth. The walls hold a strong sixteenth-century Italian collection. There's a particularly fine Andrea del Sarto here, a Pontormo, and at least one excellent late Raphael, the Madonna dell'Impannata, named for the oiled-paper windowpane visible in the background of the painting.

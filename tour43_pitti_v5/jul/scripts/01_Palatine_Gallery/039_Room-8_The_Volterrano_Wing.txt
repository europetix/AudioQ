If you've made it into this room, the wing is open. The Volterrano Wing has been undergoing maintenance through spring twenty twenty-six, and this room and the next, the Sala delle Allegorie, were the first to be worked on. If you're here, you're lucky. These rooms were the private apartment of the seventeenth-century Medici grand duchesses. Vittoria della Rovere, wife of Ferdinando the Second, the same grand duke who commissioned the Planet Rooms, lived here. So did her descendants. The last Medici, Anna Maria Luisa de' Medici, the Electress Palatine who bequeathed the Medici collections to Florence in seventeen forty-three, died in these rooms. The wing takes its name from the painter whose ceiling frescoes dominate it: Baldassarre Franceschini, called il Volterrano after his hometown. He painted these rooms for Vittoria della Rovere in the sixteen fifties. The ceiling here tells the story of Berenice, the Greek queen of Egypt who, when her husband Ptolemy went off to war, vowed her famous hair to the gods if he returned safely. He did. She cut off her hair and placed it in the temple. The next morning the hair had disappeared, turned, according to legend, into the constellation Coma Berenices, which you can still see in the northern sky. The choice of subject is not accidental. Vittoria della Rovere is presenting herself, in fresco, in the figure of a loyal wife, devoted, sacrificing, rewarded by the heavens. Continue into the Sala delle Allegorie.

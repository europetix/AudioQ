[warmly] If you had time for only one room in this palace, many people would choose this one. In here, you can watch Raphael grow up.

He came to Florence as a young man in his early twenties, and some of the portraits and Madonnas he made there hang on these walls. So do pictures he painted later in Rome, as the pope's own painter. Raphael died at thirty-seven. A great deal of that short career fits into this one room.

The pictures arrived by many routes. A Medici pope's commission. A grand duke's purchase. An altarpiece bought for a chapel in a small Tuscan cathedral, and later bought again by a Medici prince.

[pause] Now look up. The ceiling is the work of Ciro Ferri, a pupil of Pietro da Cortona. It finishes a story that runs through all five Planet Rooms. Each room stands for one age in the life of an ideal prince. Turning away from pleasure in Venus. Learning in Apollo. War in Mars. Good government in Jupiter. And here, at the end, old age. The prince, his earthly life nearly over, is led up into the heavens. Saturn, the god of Time, waits for him, and Fame stands ready with a crown.

Under the Medici, guests began at Venus, and the sequence ended here, in Saturn, the first of the Grand Duke's private rooms, just beyond the throne room. [wry] Today the route runs the other way, so you meet old age first and finish with youth and Venus.

Six tracks in this room are given to Raphael. Start with the round painting of a mother hugging her child, the Madonna della Seggiola.

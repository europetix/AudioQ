[curious] This building is named after a spot of sunlight. More exactly, after the line it travels along.

In 1699, Vincenzo Viviani, a pupil of Galileo, made a meridian in the vestibule of Grand Prince Ferdinando de' Medici's apartment. It lies under a vault painted by Anton Domenico Gabbiani with an Allegory of Time and the Arts. In that painted vault there's a tiny hole. At noon the sun shines through it, and a small bright spot falls on a line in the floor, at a different point for every day of the year. [pause] Time painted on the ceiling. Time measured on the floor.

The little palace itself came later. It was begun for Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo, and only finished in 1830, under Leopold the Second. The Lorraine and later the Savoy lived here.

[warmly] Today it holds Italy's first state museum devoted to the history of fashion, and to what clothes say about the people who wear them. The collection runs to more than fifteen thousand pieces, from the eighteenth century to today. Silk and lace don't like light, so only a part is shown at a time, and the displays change roughly once a year.

One thing never changes. The oldest and rarest garments here are always on show: the clothes three Medici were buried in, more than four hundred years ago. Look for them first.

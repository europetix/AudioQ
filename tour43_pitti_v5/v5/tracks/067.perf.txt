[curious] The little man on the turtle had a real name. He was Braccio di Bartolo, born in the countryside near Bologna, and he had dwarfism. He was sold by his parents to the court of Cosimo the First and Eleonora. [pause] The court renamed him Morgante, after a giant in a popular comic poem. It was a joke about his size, and he carried it all his life.

He served Cosimo from 1541, as jester and companion, and became one of the best-known faces at court. Cosimo eventually gave him a farm of his own near Arezzo. The painter Bronzino portrayed him in a famous double-sided portrait. Some years later the sculptor Valerio Cioli carved him in marble, in the 1560s.

[quietly] It's hard to look at this honestly without holding two thoughts at once. A disabled man was made into entertainment, carved naked and turned into a fountain. That was the casual cruelty of the age. And yet the carving seems to come from someone who looked at him closely, as a person, not a type.

Now look at the details. The turtle isn't random. A turtle with a sail on its back was Cosimo's personal emblem, with the motto festina lente, make haste slowly. So Morgante is riding the duke's own badge. And the raised arm is a gesture of command. He's asking for silence before he speaks, just like the ancient bronze of the emperor Marcus Aurelius in Rome. [amused] A naked jester on a turtle, calling the crowd to order like an emperor.

What you see here is a modern copy. The original marble is kept indoors, safe from the weather.

[warmly] The joke is sharp, and the carving is affectionate. Both are true, and that's why he's worth the stop. The Grotta Grande is just a few steps along this same wall. Play its track in front of its façade.

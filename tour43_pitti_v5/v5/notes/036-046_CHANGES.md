# 036–046 CHANGES: Mars, Apollo, Venus (Palatine end segment)
Writer pass, 4 Oct 2026. All tracks rewritten for the V5 standard and for the route direction Saturn → Jupiter → Mars → Apollo → Venus → Sala delle Nicchie → exit. Every old "next Planet Room" cue pointed against the flow; all of them have been replaced. Official pages were re-fetched today via WebFetch: Canova, Concert, Magdalene, Aretino, La Bella, the Consequences of War video page, and the Palatine history page.

## 036 (old 011) Sala di Marte, R26
- Route: "one room deeper into the reception suite" is replaced by the backwards-story framing. Medici guests went Venus→Saturn, today's route runs the other way (G6 N36). The Venus/Apollo/Mars programme is kept, framed as the Medici scheme.
- Fact: ceiling date "late 1640s" changed to "mid-1640s" (G1 B-011; uffizi.it/en/palatine-gallery/history gives 1640–47).
- Script fix: "Three works get their own track" is now correct, because the Van Dyck Bentivoglio is held out of the build. The Bentivoglio is no longer mentioned.
- Placement hedge: the Rubens are "the room of Rubens" (traditional, not officially confirmed for 2026). The English Gentleman is "usually counted among this room's treasures, though he has also hung next door, in the Room of Apollo" (G1 A/F: room unverified, older guides put it in Apollo).
- Cut: "It hangs at eye level" (unverified hang, G1 E-011).

## 037 (old 012) Rubens, Four Philosophers
- Fact: positions corrected, left to right: Rubens standing far left, Philip, Lipsius at the book, Woverius on the right. Old script had Lipsius on the right and Woverius on the left (G1 B-012; Wikipedia).
- Fact: Philip "died in Italy" is now "died in Antwerp" (G1 B-012; Wikipedia Philip Rubens).
- Hedge: the bust is now "believed in Rubens's day to show Seneca" (G1 B-012; pseudo-Seneca).
- Hedge: "two tulips … tulip mania" is cut. The text now says only "a few tulips", with the memento-mori reading framed as "often read as" (G1 B-012: count unverified).
- Cut: "the open book is one of Lipsius's editions of Seneca" (not confirmed). Kept the sourced point that Lipsius edited Seneca.
- Cut: Rubens "looking out at us" (gaze not verified). Kept: he stands a little apart, as a witness.
- Hedge: "two years after he came home" is now "a few years" (he returned in 1608).
- Cue: "find the much larger Rubens on the opposite wall" is now "look for the other Rubens" (G1 E-012: wall unverified).

## 038 (old 013) Rubens, Consequences of War
- Fact: "sent as a gift to Ferdinando II … at the Pitti ever since" is replaced. Sustermans asked Rubens for it in 1637. Rubens sent it with a letter dated 12 March 1638. Grand Prince Ferdinando bought it in 1691 (uffizi.it Consequences of War video page; G1 B-013). This is the new "turn".
- Fact: Sustermans is no longer "the Florentine painter who would soon hang it". He is the Flemish Medici court painter who commissioned and owned the picture (same source).
- Hedge: "spent his diplomatic career trying to stop it" is softened to "had spent years as a diplomat, working for peace".
- Kept: the figures (Mars, Venus, Alecto, mother and child, architect, lute player, Europe in black, Janus temple) and the Thirty Years' War. G1 confirms the Thirty Years' War, Europe and Janus. The other figures come from the old script and are not contradicted.
- Fact/cue: "bearded man in a black hat" is now "young man in black". He is bareheaded (G1 B-013). The cue now includes a fallback: if he isn't here, look in the Room of Apollo.
- Cut: the opening command "Step back to see it whole", because tracks must not open with a command.

## 039 (old 014) Titian, English Gentleman
- Fact: he is bareheaded ("Not even a hat"), and the gloves come from the findings (G1 B-013, card-014).
- Hedge: "called the English Gentleman since at least the seventeenth century" is now "no one knows when it began; first documented in the Medici collections by 1698" (G1 B-014).
- Hedge: "around 1545, perhaps fifty-five" is now "early 1540s, in his fifties" (c.1540–45; Titian born 1488/90).
- Hedge: "in his late career painted with pittura di macchia, as his contemporaries called it" is now "already moving toward the free, broken brushwork of his old age". The unsourced term is dropped (G1 B-014).
- Fact: "two hundred years before the Impressionists" is now "three centuries" (1540s to 1870s).
- Cut: the claim that each eye focuses at a different distance (unverifiable).
- Cue: "one more painting in this room, a Van Dyck" is replaced, because the Van Dyck is held out of the build. The new cue is "walk on into the Room of Apollo, unless you're already standing in it". This covers the unverified room.

## 041 (old 009) Titian, Magdalene
- Fact: "painted around 1531 for Federico Gonzaga, who asked for a Magdalene 'as tearful as possible'" is replaced. It was probably painted in the early 1530s for Francesco Maria della Rovere, Duke of Urbino, and came to Florence with Vittoria della Rovere's inheritance in 1631 (uffizi.it saint-mary-magdalene; G1 B-009).
- Cut: "Critics complained at the time. Federico Gonzaga didn't." (G1 B-009).
- Fact: "one of at least seven Magdalenes" is replaced by "Titian and his workshop copied it again and again" (official).
- Hedge: "the earliest of the type and the one most scholars consider the best" is now "generally considered the earliest of the type" (G1 B-009).
- Added (official): the crystalline tears, the signature on the jar at the bottom left, and Vasari seeing it at Urbino in 1548 and calling it "a rare thing".
- Accuracy: "in the Gospels, the woman who anointed Christ's feet" is now "legend merged her with the sinful woman who anointed Christ's feet".
- Added: the Vittoria della Rovere setup line, which pays off in 046.
- Cue: "one more Titian in this room, Aretino" is now the Sala di Venere, the last room. Aretino is in the Venus Room (G1 A-010).

## 042 (old 005) Sala di Venere, R28
- Route: "the first of the Planet Rooms … the original beginning of the route" is now "the last room of today's route; under the Medici, where it all began" (G6 N44).
- Hedge: the ceiling subject (Venus pulling the prince) is unverified (G1 F; Minerva vs Venus). The text now says only that the first lesson was to turn away from Venus, and that Cortona built the ceiling around that idea.
- Fact: the Napoleon commission and the "Medici Venus from the Pitti" are removed here. The corrected story lives in 043 so it is not told twice.
- Added: the four works in the room. Canova, plus three Titians spanning about 35 years: Concert 1510–11, La Bella 1536–38, Aretino 1545 (all official pages, all "Venus Room").
- Cue: "Walk to the Canova and play the next track" is now a self-contained cue.

## 043 (old 006) Canova, Venus Italica
- Fact: "commission from Napoleon" is now commissioned in 1802 by King Ludovico of Etruria, confirmed by the queen regent, and paid under Napoleon through Elisa Baciocchi (25,000 francs) (uffizi.it canova-venus-italica; G1 B-005/006).
- Fact: the Medici Venus was taken from the Uffizi Tribune to the Louvre, not from the Pitti (same).
- Fact: "placed her in the same spot … stayed at the Pitti because the Florentines had grown fond of her" is replaced. The Canova went into the Tribune in April 1812 on a revolving base. After Napoleon fell and the antique returned, she was "ousted" and moved to the Pitti (official).
- Added (official): drying herself after the bath, the ointment vase, life-size.
- Cut: Canova's anecdote about finishing the surface with abrasives and wax (no source).
- Hedge: "For more than a century" the Medici Venus had been the pride of Florence. The old card implied longer; she was in the Tribune from the 1670s to 1802.
- Cue: the old cue "one of them turning to look out at us" is cut (G1 E-006, wrong).

- Hedge: "attributed to Giorgione for about three centuries; twentieth-century scholarship moved it" is now "bought by Cardinal Leopoldo in 1654 as Giorgione; today unanimously Titian" (official; G1 B-007).

- Cue: "continue to the next Planet Room, the Sala di Apollo" (against the flow) is replaced by "find Aretino in this room".

## 046 (NEW) Titian, La Bella
- New track, from the official page https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/portrait-of-a-lady-la-bella. The page gives: Venus Room; 1536–38; oil on canvas, 89 × 75.5 cm; acquired in Venice by Francesco Maria della Rovere, Duke of Urbino. His letter to Titian about delivery calls it "that portrait of the woman wearing the blue dress", with no name. The page reads it as an ideal of feminine beauty and describes the dress and features. The painting came to Florence in 1631 with the Vittoria della Rovere collection. The epithet "La Bella" dates from the late 18th century.
- Not used: the Venus of Urbino / same-model link and the Eleonora Gonzaga identification. Neither is on the official page.
- Not used: the letter's year. The official page says the letter is "dated in 1535", but gives the acquisition as "around 1536–38", which is inconsistent. The spoken text avoids the year.
- Exit cue (PLAN 046 / G6 N46): end of the Palatine. Out through the Sala delle Nicchie to the entrance atrium, where the Royal Apartments meeting point is. Otherwise stairs or lift to the second floor and the Gallery of Modern Art.

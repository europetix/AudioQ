[curious] Overhead, a thief is at work. Prometheus, the Titan who stole fire from the gods and gave it to humankind, is caught in the act on this ceiling. Giuseppe Collignon painted him between 1809 and 1814. Those were the years when Napoleon's sister Elisa ruled Tuscany. Governments came and went. The myths stayed on the ceilings.

Now look at the walls, and notice how many of the paintings are round. A round painting is called a tondo, and Florence loved them in the fifteenth century and the early sixteenth. Tondi were often linked to marriages and births. They hung in private homes rather than churches, and they usually showed the Virgin and Child or the Holy Family.

[warmly] So this room feels a little like a gathering of family pictures, taken down from Renaissance bedrooms and living rooms and hung together. Around you are tondi and panels by Botticelli and his circle, by Signorelli, Beccafumi and Pontormo. Some of the families who ordered them are still known by name. Many are not.

[pause] One family did leave its name behind. Look for Filippo Lippi's tondo. The Virgin sits in front with the Child on her knees, and behind her, tiny figures move through deep, open rooms. It was painted for the Bartolini family, and it's worth standing right in front of it. Its track starts there.

[curious] Imagine having a name that's also a compliment, as the woman who lived in these rooms did. She was called Vittoria. [pause] Victory.

This wing was her private apartment in the sixteen-hundreds. Vittoria della Rovere was the wife of Grand Duke Ferdinando the Second, and around 1658 she had this ceiling painted by Baldassarre Franceschini. He came from Volterra, so everyone called him il Volterrano, and the whole wing still carries his name.

[lightly] Now look up. The figures overhead, as they're usually read, play on her name. At the centre is Victory herself, with Fame and Strength beside her. Around them are Modesty, Peace, Truth and Wisdom. It's a portrait of a woman with no portrait in it. Anyone she received here was meant to read the message.

When this wing last reopened, this room also held Volterrano's best-known easel painting, the Prank of the Pievano Arlotto, a country priest's practical joke. If it's back on the walls, find it, because it shows the same painter in a much lighter mood.

One practical note. These rooms are sometimes closed for restoration. When they are, the route carries on to the Sala di Prometeo instead, and the guide picks up again there.

[warmly] If the wing is open, the Sala delle Belle Arti is the next room along. Play its track there.

[warmly] Even the king of the gods was a baby once.

Look up, and you'll see him. The god Saturn had been warned that one of his children would overthrow him, so he swallowed them, one after another. When Jupiter was born, his mother hid him away. He was raised in secret on an island, looked after by nymphs, and nursed by a divine goat called Amalthea. That's the scene on the ceiling, painted by Catani in 1819. A god in hiding, fed on goat's milk, waiting to grow up.

[amused] He did grow up, of course. He overthrew his father and took charge of Olympus. You'll meet him later on this route, fully grown, crowning a young prince on the ceiling of the Sala di Giove, the throne room. You're getting closer to the grand reception rooms now, and this is the first chapter of his story.

[pause] Now the walls. Two paintings in this room have their own tracks. One is Caravaggio's Sleeping Cupid, a small dark canvas of a sleeping winged child, which usually hangs here. We'll come to it in a moment.

The other is the painting this room is best known for. Look for a beautiful young woman in a shimmering dress, calm as anything, holding a man's severed head by the hair. That's Cristofano Allori's Judith. Its track starts in front of it.

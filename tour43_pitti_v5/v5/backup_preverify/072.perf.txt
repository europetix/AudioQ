[warmly] This is the view people climb the hill for. The whole of central Florence is spread out below you. In the middle distance rises Brunelleschi's dome, finished in 1436 and still the largest masonry dome ever built. Beside it stands Giotto's bell tower. Beyond the rooftops, the hills climb toward Fiesole.

[pause] Now turn around. The pavilion behind you is the Kaffeehaus, begun in 1774 for Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo by the architect Zanobi del Rosso. It was a resting place for the court on its walks through the garden. The name promises coffee. [amused] The museum's own description says they came here for hot chocolate. With its odd, turreted roof, Florentines nicknamed it the Chinese pavilion.

It was restored in 2022, the first project of the garden's long restoration. And in recent years, the original marble of the little man on the turtle, down by the palace, has been kept indoors here, in the Kaffeehaus.

Close by is the small Garden of Ganymede, with its fountain of the boy carried off by Jupiter's eagle to serve as cupbearer to the gods. [lightly] A fitting neighbour for a building made for drinks.

From here, follow the path uphill to the walled Knight's Garden at the top of the hill. Play its track once you're inside the walls.

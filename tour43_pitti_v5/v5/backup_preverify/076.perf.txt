[curious] The giant in the middle of the island is a stand-in. The real Oceanus left over a century ago. Around 1910 or 1911, the original was moved indoors, to the Bargello museum across the river, and this copy took his place.

The original was carved in marble by Giambologna, in the 1570s, for Grand Duke Francesco the First. [pause] Giambologna was born in Flanders and came to Italy as a young man. He never left. He became the Medici's own sculptor, and some of Florence's most famous public sculpture is his. The bronze Cosimo on horseback in the Piazza della Signoria. The Rape of the Sabine Women in the Loggia dei Lanzi. And this.

Oceanus, for the ancient Greeks, was the great river that circled the whole world, the source of every other water. At his feet recline three more rivers, the Nile, the Ganges and the Euphrates. Africa, Asia and the ancient East, gathered around one fountain. It's the whole world, with the Medici at its centre.

The fountain didn't start here. It was first set up higher on the garden's main axis, and came down to this island when the pool was made, in 1618.

[warmly] Now look around the rim, not just at the middle. Perseus rides his horse up out of the water, and Andromeda waits nearby. Lower down are livelier groups, peasants playing their rough village games. After all the gods and emperors, the garden lets in some ordinary people. The island itself is closed for restoration, so enjoy them from the outer ring. And notice the lemon trees in their big terracotta pots. They have a winter home.

Two ways on from here. The Porta Romana gate is right beside this pool. To see that winter home, the Lemon House, walk back north along the Via Romana side of the garden, toward the palace. Play its track when you reach the long, low building.

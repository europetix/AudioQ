This wide square is younger than it looks. Until the Civil War, a dense neighbourhood stood here. The broad avenue in front of the cathedral was opened afterwards, after the war, over almost twenty years.

[quietly] The cathedral's neighbourhood was one of the worst hit by the bombing. The bombs fell on the streets around you. In the little square of Sant Felip Neri nearby, on 30 January 1938, 42 people died, 20 of them children.

[pause] The old stone house beside the cathedral is the Casa de l'Ardiaca, the archdeacon's house. The city's archive says it was built from the 12th century, "taking advantage of part of Barcelona's old Roman wall". Look at it: the house simply moved into the wall.

At the end of the 15th century the archdeacon Lluís Desplà rebuilt it in the Gothic style, "with some Renaissance features". You'll meet him again at the end of the tour, kneeling in a painting.

[lightly] Across the square, the line drawings on the modern College of Architects are by Picasso. In October 1960 he telephoned to say the drawings were ready. The artist Carl Nesjar sandblasted them into the concrete, raising a formidable cloud of dust.

Now turn to the front of the cathedral. Stand far enough back to see it whole, from the door up to the spires: The facade · Drawn in 1408, built in 1887.

[warmly] If you came looking for the Sagrada Família, you're in the right city, and standing in front of a different church. You're in good company: plenty of visitors arrive with the same idea. The Sagrada Família is a basilica, dedicated by the Pope in 2010. This one is Barcelona's cathedral, the seat of its bishops: the Cathedral of the Holy Cross and Saint Eulàlia. The city simply calls it la Seu.

Christians have worshipped on this corner of the old Roman town for seventeen centuries. What stands here now is a Gothic church, built around a girl who became the city's own saint. And out in its cloister, you'll meet geese.

[pause] Here's the walk. We start out here on the square, with the front of the cathedral. Inside, we'll stand under the lantern tower over the door, and pause at the chapel of the Christ of Lepanto. Then on to the lift, and up onto the roof. Back down, the choir, and the crypt of Santa Eulàlia. Then behind the high altar, into the cloister and its geese, and the Chapter Hall museum. We leave by the cloister's own door, onto Carrer del Bisbe. On the shorter tours, some of these stops are skipped.

A few practical things. Headphones, please: this is a working church, and people come here to pray. Inside, keep your voice low. Please don't take photos during services. The roof is reached by a small lift, so there may be a wait. And this guide works offline, so you won't need a signal.

[lightly] The full tour takes about an hour and a half, the essential about an hour, the highlights about half an hour, walking included, plus any wait for the lift to the roof. Play the stops in order. The numbers on your phone may jump, but every stop tells you where to go next.

Now walk along the square to the end where two Roman towers stand, and turn to look back at the cathedral: The Pla de la Seu · The bishops never moved.

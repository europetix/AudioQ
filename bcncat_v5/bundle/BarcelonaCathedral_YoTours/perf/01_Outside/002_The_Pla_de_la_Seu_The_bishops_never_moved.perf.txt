When Barcelona began, it was tiny. The city's history museum describes Roman Barcino as "a small fortified city covering just ten hectares". It was founded in the time of the emperor Augustus, in 10 BC.

You're standing at its edge. These towers belong to the Roman town's walls. The museum says there were two walls, "one dating from the 1st century and the other from the 3rd". Here was a gate, where the town's main street, the decumanus, came in from the countryside.

[warmly] Look down at the foot of the wall, where the Roman aqueduct once reached the city. Find the big letters standing there. They're a visual poem by Joan Brossa, and together they spell Barcino, the city's Roman name.

[pause] Now look back at the cathedral. When the Christians of the late Roman town built their first official church, the seat of their bishop, they put it in a corner of the walled town. That corner is where the cathedral stands now. The bishops never moved.

Somewhere beneath the cathedral lies that first church. It has never been dug. Scholars place it under the floor, but without an excavation, nobody can show it.

The name came before any building you can see. In 599 a document records the church's dedication to the Holy Cross. The Holy Cross came first.

[pause] Then came Almanzor. In 985 his army raided Barcelona, destroyed the city and wrecked the cathedral. From the cathedral's archive, about a hundred documents came through.

Turn now to the old stone house built into the Roman wall beside the cathedral, the Casa de l'Ardiaca: The Pla de la Seu · A square from after the war.

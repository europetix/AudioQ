"The most venerated image in Barcelona." That's how the cathedral describes the crucifix in this chapel. This is the Christ of Lepanto chapel, also the chapel of the Blessed Sacrament.

Tradition says it sailed into battle at Lepanto, in 1571, aboard the flagship of Don John of Austria. And the story goes that when a Turkish cannonball came, the Christ swerved aside. That, they say, is why the body bends.

[curious] Now the evidence. A historian, Stefan Hanß, found that no archival document of 1571 seems to mention the crucifix at all. In the sources he cites, the cannonball story first turns up in the confraternity's statutes, written down in 1762, almost two hundred years after the battle. As he puts it, "it cannot be proven that the object actually was on the ship of Don John".

[pause] Then the bigger surprise. The cathedral now says the carving is "not from the 16th century, as was believed". It is older than the battle it's famous for.

If you can see it from here, notice how dark it is: candle smoke and wax, and varnish added later. Restorers have cleaned it, and saved paint that was beginning to flake.

The room itself is older than the legend. Arnau Bargués built it in 1407 as the canons' meeting room, their chapter house. They met here until the 1670s. It's not the Chapter Hall museum at the end of the tour. Beneath the crucifix lies a bishop-saint, Oleguer.

Most of the year it hangs here. In Lent, it's moved.

[warmly] The battle made it famous. It was old long before the battle.

If you're on the full tour, stay at the doorway for one more short story: The Lepanto chapel · Extra · The bishop under the crucifix. Otherwise, the next main stop is the marble screen at the end of the choir, the wooden enclosure in the middle of the nave. Stand back a little to see its carved panels: The choir screen · The sculptor who never came back.

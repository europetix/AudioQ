[warmly] From up here, the old town starts to make sense. Its streets press in tight around the cathedral, on the corner of the Roman town where you began.

Within a few streets of this roof, the cathedral counts its neighbours. The Bishop's Palace. The Palau de la Generalitat, the seat of the Catalan government. Barcelona's City Hall. The Casa de l'Ardiaca, the archdeacon's house. The old royal palace. The Frederic Marès museum. And the Archive of the Crown of Aragon. Bishops, kings, the city and the government, all packed round one corner. From where you stand, you may pick out a tower or a roof line of one or two of them.

[pause] Now look down into the cloister. That's the garden you'll walk round towards the end of the visit, with its palm trees, and somewhere in it, the pond where the geese live.

[lightly] On a clear day, look far off on the horizon for the spires of the other great church in this city, the one people so often take this cathedral for.

When you've seen enough, take the lift back down. Then go to the choir, the enclosure in the middle of the nave, and go in at its entrance. If the choir is closed, listen at its entrance: The choir · Oak from Bruges.

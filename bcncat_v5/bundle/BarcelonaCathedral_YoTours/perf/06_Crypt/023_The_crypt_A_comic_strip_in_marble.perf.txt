[curious] This tomb tells its story the way a comic strip would. Eight carved scenes run round the box, and they follow her life as the medieval Catalan versions of the Golden Legend told it.

[pause] Start at the narrow end where she talks with her parents, and then walks off alone. Along the long side, the governor Dacian puts her on trial and condemns her. At the other narrow end, she dies on the cross. If it's hard to make out from where you are, the scenes are still there.

[amused] The best story is carved on the back. When her body was carried in procession, the story goes, it grew too heavy to move. A cleric had stolen a finger from her right hand. Only when he put it back could the procession go on.

The two masters behind this crypt and its tomb turn up together in one document of 1327. The Pisan sculptor, Lupo di Francesco, signed as a witness while the Mallorcan master, Jaume Fabre, set a Greek slave free.

[wry] That same year the city's councillors wrote to the bishop. He wanted to send to Pisa for a marble altar, and they thought it could be made here for less. Their letter mentions the master of Santa Eulàlia's tomb.

If you went down, climb back up to the top of the stair. Turn to face the high altar, and walk round its left side, as you face it, into the walkway behind it. Stop where you can look up at its tall windows: Behind the high altar · They started at this end.

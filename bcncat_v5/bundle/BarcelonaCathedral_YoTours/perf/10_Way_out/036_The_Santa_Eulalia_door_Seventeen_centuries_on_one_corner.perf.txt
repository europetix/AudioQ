Seventeen centuries of history, on one corner of the Roman town. The first Christians of Barcelona built their church in this corner of the walled town. And the bishops never moved.

They finished the front you came in by on the feast of Santa Eulàlia. She's the city's own saint, by tradition a girl of thirteen, martyred for her faith. Her feast is still kept every twelfth of February. And she is co-patroness of the cathedral, the archdiocese and the city.

In the summer of 1936, while churches burned across Barcelona, this one was protected, and it came through intact. Much of what you've seen today survives because of that.

Behind you, in the cloister, the thirteen geese keep their pond, as geese have here for at least four hundred years.

This is the Santa Eulàlia door, named for her, and it is also the cathedral's step-free way in. It carries the arms of a bishop, Sapera, from the years the cloister was being built. Step through it and you're in Carrer del Bisbe, the Bishop's Street, in the heart of the old town.

Out in that street, the little stone bridge everyone photographs looks medieval. It was built in 1928.

[warmly] Thank you for walking it with me: the Roman corner and the Gothic church, the girl in the crypt, the egg and the geese, the saint in spectacles. It is still a working church, and the city's own.

[quietly] One corner of a Roman town, and seventeen centuries of people coming back to it.

Carrer del Bisbe is just outside. Go gently.

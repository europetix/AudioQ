The pillars of this cloister carry a Bible in stone. Twenty-one carved bands run along the tops of the pillars. Their scenes come from the Old Testament, and from a legend of the Holy Cross, the cross this cathedral is named for. A study of the cloister reports that they have been read as a great Bible of the poor in stone, a picture Bible promoted by Bishop Sapera.

[curious] If you can find the chapel of the Apostles, look at the pillar in front of it. Its capitals tell the first chapters of Genesis, and they are attributed to Dello Delli, a sculptor who worked in this cloister. The chapel was designed in 1408 by Carlí, the master who drew the cathedral's front.

The Pietat door was built at the end of the 1300s. Its carving of the Pietà, the dead Christ with his mother, is attributed to Michael Lochner, the German carver who began the choir's canopy of spires. The canon who paid for it, Berenguer Vila, kneels at the lower right. The original relief, the Catalan encyclopedia says, is kept indoors by the cathedral, so the one above the door may be a copy.

The cloister's chapels were first dedicated to the patron saints of guilds and institutions. Inside the church, the shoemakers' confraternity had a chapel of its own, Saint Mark's. In 1437 it hired the painter Bernat Martorell for its altarpiece. And the painter himself was buried somewhere under this cloister.

Walk round to the chapel of Santa Llúcia, at a corner of the cloister, on the same walk as the Chapter Hall museum: The cloister · Santa Llúcia, older than the cathedral.

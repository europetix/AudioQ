This doorway may be older than the cathedral around it. The cathedral describes it as "Romanesque, made of white marble". And it adds: "It is said to have come from the former Romanesque Cathedral." That was the church of the count and countess whose painted coffins hang by the sacristy.

The Catalan encyclopedia's Romanesque survey says the marble is Italian, and the carving late Romanesque, from the 1200s. Was the door saved from the old cathedral, or carved a little later by Italian craftsmen? It treats both as open questions.

[curious] Look at its five pointed arches, and at the capitals beneath them. On one, Job, with figures at prayer. On the last, the Annunciation. And above another, a warrior fights a winged dragon with three bodies.

Then turn to the garden, with its palms, its magnolias and an orange tree.

A sale of 1078 already places a cloister here. Round it stood the canons' world: their chapter house, their dining room, their houses, and two cemeteries. Canons lived and were buried around this garden.

Chapels open off three sides of the cloister. The fourth side has none. It holds the Chapter Hall museum and the old chapel of Santa Llúcia, where the cloister walk ends.

And above this door rises the cathedral's second bell tower, the one with eleven bells for the services.

Now turn to the small vaulted pavilion over a fountain, close to the door you've just come through, with a figure of Saint George on top: The cloister · The egg the accountant wrote down.

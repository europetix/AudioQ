For centuries, this carried the Host through Barcelona's streets at Corpus Christi. It's the cathedral's great monstrance, made in the late 1300s: a two-tier, six-sided tower of gold and gilded silver, set with pearls and gems, eighty-eight centimetres high. While it was still being made, a bishop ordered a sapphire and a ruby to be added.

It stands on a silver seat called King Martin's throne, which has been the monstrance's base since the middle of the 1400s. Tradition says it was the throne of King Martin the Humane. The Catalan encyclopedia's art history calls that a late tradition. In fact, it explains, the seat is a folding bishop's chair of gilded silver, and it carries Barcelona's hallmark.

Barcelona was one of the first cities in the Christian world to hold a Corpus procession, in 1320. It's the same feast as the dancing egg on the cloister fountain.

Look also for a stone font with lobed sides. It's from the 11th century, from the Romanesque cathedral that stood here before this one.

Two more works to find: Jaume Huguet's altarpiece of Saint Bernardino and the Guardian Angel, from the late 1460s, and a terracotta Santa Eulàlia by Giuliano di Nofri. She once stood over the Santa Eulàlia door, the one you'll leave by.

Leave the museum, and walk to the cloister's door to Carrer del Bisbe, the Santa Eulàlia door. Stop just inside it, out of the way of people leaving: The Santa Eulàlia door · Seventeen centuries on one corner.

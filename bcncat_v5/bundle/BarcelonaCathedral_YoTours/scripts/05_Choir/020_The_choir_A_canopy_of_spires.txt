A German woodcarver came to Barcelona and never left. Michael Lochner was working here by 1483, with a workshop in the Carrer de Mercaders, and he died in the city seven years later.

Look up at the spires over the upper seats, a forest of carved pinnacles. Lochner began them in 1483, probably to a design drawn up back in Sanglada's day. After his death his assistant, Joan Frederic, another German, carried on alone, and by 1497 the first phase was done. That's almost a century after the seats beneath them.

So when you read that Lochner carved this choir, it's only part of the story. The stalls are three generations' work: Sanglada's team for the upper seats, Bonafè for the lower ones, and Lochner and Frederic for the canopy over them all.

Now the wooden pulpit beside you. Sanglada carved it, around 1400. It has six sides, with fifteen full-length figures, and the painter Lluís Borrassà decorated it.

Presiding over the whole choir, the Catalan encyclopedia notes, is a bishop's chair of the 1300s.

Leave the choir and walk towards the high altar. Stop at the top of the stair that leads down to the crypt, in front of the altar. If the crypt is closed, stay at the top of its stair: the tomb is in view below. Either way, start here: The crypt · Thirteen, according to tradition.

Some of the liveliest carving in this choir is hidden under the seats. On the little ledges called misericords, tucked beneath the canons' folding seats, the carvers let the everyday world in.

There are monkeys aping people. There are musicians and dancers, animals and games. There's a siren holding up a mirror, a figure of vanity. The Catalan encyclopedia's art history describes these scenes, in Catalan, as "details of daily life, of work and of fun".

The city adds that somewhere in these stalls there's a game with sticks and a ball. Its guide to the cathedral calls finding it the best test for the keen-eyed.

Look at the carving under the raised ledge in front of you. Who is it, and what are they doing? Then find one more along the row, and compare the two.

Now look up, above the seats, at the row of painted shields: The choir · Europe's most exclusive club.

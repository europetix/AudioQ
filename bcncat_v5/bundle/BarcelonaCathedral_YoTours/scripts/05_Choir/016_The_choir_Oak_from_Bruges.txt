These seats began with a journey to Flanders. The chapter's old choir book records it, in old Catalan: "Anglada was sent … to Bruges to buy oak for the said choir." Anglada is Pere Sanglada, the Barcelona sculptor in charge, sent to buy the wood himself.

This is the canons' choir, with its choir stalls, set in the middle of the nave, in front of the crypt. Here the canons sang the hours, enclosed in the middle of the church.

Work began in the 1390s, under Bishop Ramon d'Escales. Sanglada directed the upper stalls, carved mainly in Flemish oak, and he didn't carve them alone. The Catalan encyclopedia's art history calls the stalls, in essence, a team effort, with a workshop of carvers around him. On the fifth of December 1399 they were formally declared finished.

You've met his work already: the alabaster figure of the bishop under the Lepanto crucifix.

The lower row of seats came half a century later. A carver called Macià Bonafè won that job, against a rival, Joan Claperós.

Now look at the upper row, the tall seats at the back. Follow the carving on the seat backs and along the armrests, one seat to the next. That is the work of Sanglada's team, in oak that came from Bruges.

If you're on the full tour, find a seat whose folding ledge is already raised, and look at the carving underneath. Please don't lift one yourself: The choir · Extra · Under the canons' seats. Otherwise, the next main stop is above the seats, at the row of painted shields: The choir · Europe's most exclusive club.

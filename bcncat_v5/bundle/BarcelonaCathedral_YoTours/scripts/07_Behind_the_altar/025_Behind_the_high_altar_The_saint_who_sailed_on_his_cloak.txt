The story goes that Ramon de Penyafort once crossed the sea from Sóller, on Mallorca, all the way home to Barcelona, sailing on his own cloak. The cathedral's page, which tells it as a legend, calls it his blanket.

He was a Dominican friar. He died in Barcelona in 1275, at the convent of Santa Caterina, and was made a saint in Rome more than three centuries after his death.

So why is his tomb here, and not in his convent? In 1835, a year of unrest in the city, a great fire damaged the convent, and it was left all but abandoned. His tomb was brought here. Soon afterwards the convent was pulled down, and explosives were used to bring down its church's vault.

Tradition ties him to something that began in this cathedral. In 1218, the story goes, the bishop of Barcelona founded here an order to ransom captives, the Order of Mercy. The Catalan encyclopedia gives the day, the nineteenth of August, and the names. The bishop gave the white habit to Pere Nolasc and thirteen knights, with Ramon de Penyafort advising and King Jaume the First helping. Where it happened, the encyclopedia doesn't say. Here, says tradition.

If a sign beside a chapel names him, find his tomb there.

Carry on round to the right side of the high altar, as you face it. By the sacristy door, look high on the wall for two painted wooden boxes: The sacristy door · The founders who were excommunicated.

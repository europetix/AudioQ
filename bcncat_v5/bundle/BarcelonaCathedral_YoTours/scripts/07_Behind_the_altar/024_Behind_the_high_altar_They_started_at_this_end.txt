Who drew the first plan of this cathedral? Nobody can say for sure. Historians still ask whether the first design was kept, or reworked by the first master we can name. His name was Jaume Fabre. In 1317 the cathedral hired him from Mallorca, where he had been running the works of a Dominican convent in Palma. He was made master of the works for the rest of his life. The crypt you've just left was his finest work.

This is the ambulatory, the walkway behind the altar. And this is where the building began. The first builders started here, at the far end, and worked their way towards the main door. In the first phase they raised this whole end: the apse, the chapels round this walkway, the sanctuary of the high altar, and the crypt beneath it. Most of it rose under one bishop, Ponç de Gualba. The new high altar was consecrated in the 1330s.

Now look up at the tall windows. The oldest stained glass in the cathedral is here, above these chapels. Each of the earliest windows shows the saint of the chapel beneath it. The cathedral dates the first of them by a coat of arms, Bishop Ponç de Gualba's. One study of the glass places them a little later in the 1300s. Two windows carry only the arms of the royal house of Barcelona, perhaps because the king paid for them.

Find the central window, high above the apse, straight behind the altar. On it is the bishop's own shield, beside the arms of Barcelona. The same shield is on his tomb. He lies in one of these chapels, the chapel of the Patrocini. So the bishop who started this end of the church lies under his own window.

Twelve of the cathedral's old windows were restored in a project finished in 2024.

Walk slowly on round the walkway, past its chapels, reading the names beside them, to the tomb of Sant Ramon de Penyafort: Behind the high altar · The saint who sailed on his cloak.

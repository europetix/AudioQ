Her name means "the well-spoken one", the city's popular-culture pages explain. Eulàlia. And in her legend she is all words.

According to tradition, she was a girl of thirteen from Sarrià, who left her parents' house to face the governor, Dacian, in the persecution of the year 304. In the story he threatens her, and she answers him with words and reason.

Thirteen tortures, the story says, one for each year of her life. The old written account of her martyrdom names three: the whips, the cross and fire. She was crucified on an X-shaped cross. You'll find that cross in images of her all over this cathedral.

Did she live at all? The same pages answer it: "Saint Eulàlia of Barcelona's existence as a historical figure is doubtful." The Spanish city of Mérida has a girl martyr of the same name, with an almost identical story. Experts, they add, see "a single legend claimed by two different places".

A century and more ago, Barcelona's own historians doubted her too. Others answer with the early Christian graves found under Santa Maria del Mar, the church where her body was found, and argue for an early cult there. A study published in 2023 still treats the question as open. Historians argue whether Barcelona's Eulàlia and Mérida's were two girls or one story.

What the record does show is devotion. Her cult is documented by the end of the sixth century, and her written story dates from the seventh.

Today she is co-patroness of this cathedral, of the archdiocese and of the city. Look down the stair: the tomb below is hers.

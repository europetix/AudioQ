From up here, the old town starts to make sense. Its streets press in tight around the cathedral, on the same corner of the Roman town where the bishops never moved.

Within a few streets of this roof, the cathedral counts its neighbours. The Bishop's Palace. The Palau de la Generalitat, the seat of the Catalan government. Barcelona's City Hall. The Casa de l'Ardiaca, the archdeacon's house. The Palau Reial Major, the old royal palace. The Frederic Marès museum. And the Archive of the Crown of Aragon. From where you stand, some of them may hide behind their neighbours' roofs. So pick out the towers and the roof lines the view gives you, and let the rest be a list of who lives next door.

Now look down into the cloister. That's the garden you'll walk round towards the end of the visit, with its palm trees, and somewhere in it, the pond where the geese live.

And if the day is clear, look far off on the horizon for the spires of the other great church in this city, the one people so often mistake this one for.

When you've seen enough, take the lift back down. Then go to the choir, the wooden enclosure in the middle of the nave, and in at its entrance. If the choir is closed, listen at its entrance: The choir · Oak from Bruges.

Here's a piece of the Renaissance, set into a Gothic church. This white marble wall is the trascor, the choir screen. It was begun by Bartolomé Ordóñez, a sculptor who came to Barcelona in 1517.

Look at the four scenes of Santa Eulàlia's martyrdom, two on each side of the door. The carving is in very low relief, almost drawn into the stone. One art historian praises its delicacy, and the virtuosity of its small details. Find the flames, and see how little depth he needed for them.

The story behind it is short. An inventory of 15 October 1519 lists what he'd done: two large reliefs of Eulàlia, finished, and two finished statues, one of them Eulàlia herself. Then he left for Carrara, in Italy, the marble town. He never came back. By 1521 a payment record already calls him, with the Latin word quondam, "the late" Ordóñez. Others finished the screen later, among them Pedro Villar.

So two of these four were finished when he left. Which two, the inventory doesn't say.

The girl on these panels lies in the crypt ahead, under the high altar.

In this room, the Jews of medieval Barcelona speak through their paperwork. The Old Archive, finished in 1535, shows a sample of original documents, in Latin, Hebrew and Catalan: sales, rentals and wills from the Call, the Jewish quarter. They are, in the cathedral's words, "written testimonies that give a voice to the inhabitants of medieval Barcelona."

In August 1391, after preaching against the Jews that had begun in Seville, the Call was attacked. The city's history museum records the deaths of about three hundred Jews. Those who survived converted to Christianity or fled.

After 1391, Jewish gravestones were reused as building stone in the streets around here. The places where they have been found are outside the cathedral.

Until 1969 this room held the chapter's archive. The archive itself, now kept elsewhere, holds about forty thousand parchments, and nine papyri, found hidden in the endpapers of one old book.

Look for a line of Hebrew among the documents on display.

Now go to the Chapter Hall museum, next to the chapel of Santa Llúcia. Inside, find the painting of the dead Christ across his mother's lap: The Chapter Hall · The saint in spectacles.

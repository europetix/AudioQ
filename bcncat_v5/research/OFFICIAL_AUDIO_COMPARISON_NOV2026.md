# Barcelona Cathedral: official audio offer vs competitors (research as of Nov 2026, DATED)

**Status:** COMPLETE, 14 Nov 2026. **DATED:** every finding is as of 14 Nov 2026 (access date of all pages unless noted). Tickets, guides, works, Mass times and rules change. Re-check before launch.
**Purpose:** what the official audio guide, guided visits and rooftop offer at Barcelona Cathedral (Catedral de la Santa Creu i Santa Eulàlia), what commercial competitors offer, and where a Yo Tours audio guide can beat them.
**Method:** WebSearch and WebFetch only, one page at a time per site, no bursts. No curl, no browser, no CAPTCHA, no sign-in. The official ticket shop (tickets.catedralbcn.org) is JS-only and was not fetched. Tripadvisor, Google Maps and GetYourGuide product pages were not fetched directly (bot-protection risk); Tripadvisor appears only as search-result titles. Review content comes from reseller review pages (Viator partner, Headout, Klook, Civitatis), Wanderlog (Google-review excerpts) and Foursquare. One site (eurovistatrips) showed a bot-verification page and was abandoned.
**Grades:** **OFF** = official page (catedralbcn.org, its PDFs, the Archdiocese's tourism site, Ajuntament de Barcelona). **SEC+** = Turisme de Barcelona's own ticket shop (thisisbarcelona.com) or a reseller page whose named provider is the cathedral itself. **SEC** = other secondary page fetched and read (reseller, producer GVAM, press, blog, aggregator). **SNIP** = search-result snippet or title only.
**Recommendations contain no prices** (binding). Prices that appear in the source log are facts as found, for context only.
**Full quotes and URLs for every point are in Appendix A (source log).** The sections below give the URL in-line the first time and the short source afterwards.

Reviews rarely separate the official guide from the reseller's wrapper. The direct evidence is thin but mostly negative about **delivery**, not content:

- "the audio was very disappointing - it wouldn't play after the first few short files"; "The audio does not work at all"; "didn't work well" without WiFi; no starting point and no pacing guidance, so the listener kept catching up to it (paraphrase) (Viator partner d562-374952P6, May–Sep 2026, SEC, https://avionero.partner.viator.com/tours/Barcelona/Cathedral-of-Barcelona-entrance-ticket-with-access-to-the-rooftop/d562-374952P6). That product says "Digital Audio Guide in 8 languages", so it may be wrapping another audio file or the official one.
- Positive: it "made it easy for us to understand the cathedral's interior" (Klook, Apr 2026); "insightful commentary" (Klook, Oct 2025) (SEC, https://www.klook.com/en-US/activity/110209-barcelona-cathedral-ticket-audio-guide-barcelona/); "the perfect balance of independence/freedom and insights" (Viator, May 2026).
- The Google-review excerpts on Wanderlog (2026) do not mention the audio guide at all. Visitors remember the roof, the cloister and the geese (SEC, https://wanderlog.com/place/details/19487).

### 1.4 What the ticket covers (official list) and what sellers get wrong
The official "Visitable spaces" (OFF) and the Ajuntament agenda (OFF, https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/lgtbi/en/detall/92086000934) list six items: **temple and cloister; the roof (lift); the choir "with the golden fleece choir stalls"; the Chapter Hall museum; "Jewish memory in the Cathedral" (Old Archive of 1535); the Holy Christ of Lepanto chapel.**
- **The crypt is not on the official list**, but visitors do reach it ("Tomb of Saint Eulalia", Klook review, Feb 2025; Rick Steves "Descend the steps beneath the altar"). Barcelona Lowdown and Lonely Planet say the altar and crypt are closed during Mass. **Status to confirm in FACTS.**
- **Museum location (official):** "under the cloister gallery, next to the Saint Lucia chapel"; originally a soup kitchen for the poor; 11th-c. font, terracotta Saint Eulàlia, Huguet and Bermejo paintings (the Desplà *Pietat*, per thisisbarcelona), late-14th-c. monstrance (OFF, Visitable spaces). No photography inside (Barcelona Lowdown, SEC).
- **Seller errors:** Headout lists "Acceso al Museo Diocesano de Barcelona" and Klook lists "Diocesan Museum of Barcelona" and "Sant Sever Church" as included. These are not on the official list (the Museu Diocesà is a separate venue). Klook gives hours "08:30-19:30, Mon-Sun" (wrong) (SEC).

### 1.5 Official rules a guide must get right (DATED)
- **Cultural-visit hours:** weekdays 9:30–18:30 (access closes 17:45); Saturdays and eves 9:30–17:15 (access closes 16:30); Sundays and religious holidays 14:00–17:00 (access closes 16:30) (OFF, Visiting hours). A reviewer says "the rooftop closes at 4:30 PM" (Wanderlog, Aug 2026, SEC; confirm).
- **Three gates:** Main gate on Pla de la Seu; **Gate of Santa Eulàlia (Carrer del Bisbe), "access for people with reduced mobility"**, which leads through the cloister; Gate of Sant Iu (OFF, Visiting hours). Which gate ticket holders use is **not stated** officially.
- **Masses during visiting hours:** weekday Masses at 10:00 (not Jul–Aug), 11:00 and 12:00 are said **in the Chapel of the Holy Sacrament**. Saturday 18:00 and 19:15 and all Sunday Masses are at the **High Altar** (OFF, https://catedralbcn.org/en/worship-and-prayer/masses/). Free entry is for worship only: "Free admission applies during worship hours, when only the temple can be visited" (SEC+, thisisbarcelona).
- **The Lepanto Christ hangs in that same Mass chapel:** "Chapel of the Blessed Sacrament" most of the year, the Chapel of Saint Severus in Lent (OFF, https://catedralbcn.org/en/worship-and-prayer/saints/holy-christ-of-lepanto/). The official rule is "Please do not enter or take pictures during the celebrations" (OFF, Visitable spaces).
- **The lift:** "can be accessed though the chapel of the Innocent Saints" (OFF), "beside the Sant Iu door" (SEC, https://barcelonaurbana.com/en/blog/barcelona-cathedral-tickets-prices-free-hours ; Bus Turístic). Reviews say "only 6 people is allowed in elevator each time", with a wait of about 20 minutes, and that the stairs are an alternative but "fairly steep" (Wanderlog, Aug 2026, SEC).
- **Dress and conduct (thisisbarcelona, SEC+):** "Hats and caps must be removed inside the nave or museum"; "No see-through clothing."; "No shorts or short skirts."; shoulders and stomach covered; "No flip-flops."; "speak in a low voice"; "**To maintain silence, groups must use radio guides.**" Barcelona Lowdown: "You may be turned away if you are unsuitably dressed, and your ticket will not be refunded" (SEC). A shawl vendor works outside (Barcelona Hacks, SEC).
- **Timed slot:** "The ticket is valid only for the selected tour time slot." No changes or refunds (SEC+, thisisbarcelona).
- **Works (DATED):** organ concerts from Oct to Dec 2026 have moved to the Capella del Santíssim "because of restoration work on two vaults in the transept area" (OFF, https://catedralbcn.org/en/). The 2024 "Obert per obres" programme covers the cloister stone, the roofs (new stairs, railings and "a modernized lift"), the bell tower, the stained glass and **the choir ("recuperar les talles de fusta i la policromia")** (OFF, https://catedralbcn.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Obert-per-obres-Catedral-de-Barcelona.pdf). **Re-check before launch what is screened off.**

### 1.6 Official internal conflict (must resolve in FACTS)
- **Lepanto Christ date:** the Visitable spaces page says "16th-century Holy Christ of Lepanto", but the official Lepanto page says "the crucifix could be from the 13th century" and "not from the 16th century, as was believed". Its presence at Lepanto in 1571 is "According to tradition", and both explanations of its curved torso (it dodged a Turkish cannonball; it covered a hole in the hull) are "traditional explanations" (OFF).
- **Santa Eulàlia:** the official page says she left home at 13 "according to tradition" and was martyred in 304 under Dacian. Her tomb is a "polychromed marble sarcophagus" (not alabaster) inaugurated in 1339 by a sculptor from the Pisa region. **The official page says nothing about the geese** (OFF, https://catedralbcn.org/en/worship-and-prayer/saints/saint-eulalia/).

**Bottom line on the official offer:** the ticket bundles a 2019 web guide in six languages with two tours (Essentials, which includes a 360º choir view, and a longer tour), a VR film and an AR rooftop viewer. The guide's stop count, running time and narration are undocumented. It locates spaces by photographs, not numbers. It needs a working phone connection at the door (QR or voucher link), and reviews report it failing to load or play. **No official product offers a timed, numbered walking order through a crowded nave that changes around Masses, and none publishes its length.**

**Pattern:** the official guided visit lasts 75 minutes, is offered in three languages (no German or Italian), and spends part of that time on a VR film. No seller publishes an outline that names the crypt, the choir, the Chapter Hall museum or the Lepanto chapel.

### 2.2 The rooftop
- The rooftop visit **reopened in full in March 2025** after restoration ("launched on Tuesday along with the reopening of the cathedral's roof", Catalan News 28 Mar 2025, SEC). New stairs, railings and a modernized lift are part of the works programme (OFF, Obert per obres).
- **Access:** by lift from the Chapel of the Holy Innocents, next to the Sant Iu door (OFF; SEC). The view takes in "the two bell towers, the lateral pinnacles, the dome topped by the Holy Cross, the cloister" and "a spectacular panoramic view of Barcelona" (OFF, Visitable spaces).
- **On the roof:** the La Talaia AR device (45 points of interest) is included (SEC, Catalan News; GVAM 2025).
- **Practical (reviews):** the lift takes 6 people, with a wait of about 20 minutes; the stairs are steep; the roof closes about 16:30 (Wanderlog, 2026, SEC). Rick Steves' walk has **no rooftop chapter**, because it predates the reopening (SEC, voicewalks mirror).

## 4. Common gaps and mistakes, and what our guide must do better

### 4.1 Evidence (paraphrased; exact quotes and URLs in Appendix A)
1. **The 13 geese are presented as fact.** Their link to "13 = Eulàlia's age" is stated as fact by the Bus Turístic, Lonely Planet, Barcelona Lowdown, Barcelona Hacks, Audiala, the Audible podcast, barcelona-tickets and El Debate (SEC). Only Barcelona Secreta and the free-tour script call it a legend (SEC). **The official cathedral pages give no reason for the geese**, and even her age of 13 is "according to tradition" (OFF). Rick Steves adds "for at least 500 years" (unverified). One seller promises to reveal "the secret behind the 13 courtyard geese" (GuruWalk p13743).
2. **The Lepanto Christ legend.** Rick Steves says it was "carried into battle in 1571" and repeats the cannonball story ("They say"). The Bus Turístic says it "leaned to dodge a bullet" (hedged). The official page calls Lepanto and both curvature stories "tradition" and dates the crucifix possibly to the 13th century, "not from the 16th century, as was believed". Its own Visitable spaces page still says 16th century (OFF conflict).
3. **People confuse the cathedral with the Sagrada Família.** "I bought the wrong ticket. I wanted to visit the Sagrada Familia Cathedral." (Headout, Apr 2024); "We thought we were purchasing tickets for La Sagrada Família but ended up with tickets for Barcelona Cathedral" (Viator, May 2026); "All very misleading" (Viator, Jun 2026). Sellers now print warnings: "This program is NOT for the La Sagrada Familia!" (Viator); "Please note: this is not the Sagrada Familia." (GuruWalk). Foursquare in 2012: "Barcelona Cathedral is NOT, as many think, the Sagrada Familia." Wanderlog in 2026: "within walking distance of La Sagrada Familia" (SEC).
4. **No directions or pacing.** The official guide uses photographs, "so the signs have no numbers" (GVAM). One listener had no starting point and no pacing guidance and kept catching up to the recording (Viator, Sep 2026). The guided tour "felt too short" and one group "didn't show us the terrace" (Civitatis). Weekday Masses at 10, 11 and 12 occupy the Holy Sacrament / Lepanto chapel during visiting hours, and the choir closes during Mass (GVAM; Lonely Planet). **No product tells visitors what to do when a Mass is on.**
5. **The crypt, choir, museum and Jewish-memory room are skipped.** No guided-tour outline names the crypt, choir, Chapter Hall or Lepanto chapel (thisisbarcelona, GuruWalk, Civitatis). The crypt is missing even from the official Visitable spaces list. Rick Steves has no museum. Headout and Klook mislabel the museum as the "Diocesan Museum". Lonely Planet still describes a separate choir fee.
6. **Delivery failures.** QR codes or voucher links depend on signal: "trouble connecting" (2023); "wouldn't play after the first few short files"; "didn't work well" without WiFi (2026). "make sure you have internet access on your phone" (tourscanner) (SEC).
7. **Facts are wrong or stale.** Old free-hours models still circulate: "free other times" (Foursquare 2013), "Free outside of 1-5:30pm" (2019), Tripadvisor title "Free before midday and after 6pm", "free before noon" (Viator review 2026). Barcelona Hacks gives free weekday worship windows (8:30–12:30, 17:45–19:30) that overlap the official paid hours, and a reader comment says it "is no longer free at any time". Lonely Planet places Eulàlia in the "4th century BC" and calls the building "less than 200 years old". Klook gives wrong hours. Audio languages are stated as 6, 8 or 15 (SEC).
8. **Languages are missing.** The official guided visit runs in EN, ES and FR only, with no German or Italian. Civitatis offers English only; reviewers ask for Catalan. The official audio guide has no PT or Asian languages (only La Talaia adds Korean).
9. **Thin or tiny products.** A 5-minute podcast; a 45-second sample; city walks that pass by outside. No competitor publishes a stop list and running time for the interior.
10. **Practical gaps.** The six-person lift and its queue; steep stairs; the roof closing about 16:30; coin-operated toilets; the coin-operated crypt light (Barcelona Hacks); dress refusals ("cover knees (as well as shoulders)"); "queue for ages, despite having tickets" (Viator).

### 4.2 Concrete list: what the Yo Tours guide must do (no prices)
1. **Track 0, before arrival: "This is not the Sagrada Família."** One sentence on which building this is (the Gothic seat of the archbishop in the Barri Gòtic). Name the full name once. Explain that the ticket is a timed cultural-visit slot.
2. **Make it offline-first.** Download the whole guide before arrival. Track 0 says so, and so does the booking page. Use numbered tracks with landmark cues ("stand with the main door behind you, the choir screen ahead"). Never depend on GPS, the door QR code or signal inside thick stone walls.
3. **Give a fixed, numbered walking order with directions at every step:** facade and square, then the nave, side chapels, choir, crypt steps, high altar, Lepanto chapel (when free), the lift via the Holy Innocents chapel by the Sant Iu door, the roof, the cloister, the geese, the Santa Llúcia chapel, then the Chapter Hall museum, the exit, and how to find the official route if it differs. Each track ends with "next, walk to…".
4. **Make the route Mass-aware.** Before the weekday 10/11/12 Masses in the Holy Sacrament chapel, a "Mass on?" branch says to skip the Lepanto chapel and come back, or listen from outside without photos, as the official rule asks. Sunday visits run 14:00–17:00. The choir may be closed: offer a "choir closed" variant that describes what can be seen through the screen.
5. **Separate fact from legend in plain words.** The geese: say that they are there and that 13 is the number kept, and that the "13 = Eulàlia's age" and "13 tortures" links are a popular explanation, not a documented one (V5 hedge). The Lepanto Christ: give the tradition and both stories as stories, and the official redating ("could be from the 13th century"). Eulàlia's age is "according to tradition". Never give the "500 years" figure without a source.
6. **Cover what everyone skips:** a real crypt track (the marble sarcophagus of 1339 and its relief scenes), the choir (stalls, Golden Fleece arms, pulpit, retrochoir reliefs), the **Chapter Hall museum** (where it is, the Bermejo *Pietat*, no photos inside), the Jewish-memory archive room and the Lepanto chapel.
7. **Do the roof properly.** Give the lift location, the six-person lift and the queue, the steep-stairs alternative, and a warning that the roof closes earlier than the cathedral. Then a rooftop "what you are looking at" track that complements, not duplicates, the La Talaia device.
8. **Honest lengths:** 30 / 60 / 90 minutes, stated up front, with the real track count, which no competitor publishes. The 30-minute path still reaches the nave, the choir view, the crypt, the cloister and the roof queue decision.
9. **Make it practical and current.** Give the dress code (shoulders, stomach, knees, no see-through clothing, hats off, no flip-flops; refusal is possible). Say that free entry is for worship only and that old "free hours" guides are out of date. Name the step-free Carrer del Bisbe (Santa Eulàlia) gate. Point to toilets, silence and the rule that groups must use radio guides. Add a line on works screens (transept vaults in late 2026; choir restoration).
10. **Languages:** EN, ES, FR and DE from day one. The official guided visit lacks German, so a German self-guide adds real value.

## 5. What visitors most want to know, and the common confusions on site

### 5.1 What they want (from FAQs, reviews and guides)
- **Is it free?** No for sightseeing. The cultural visit is ticketed and free entry is for worship only (SEC+, thisisbarcelona; SEC, https://www.trvlogue.com/en/barcelona-cathedral-tickets-2026, upd. 19 Oct 2026). Many old sources say otherwise (§4.1-7).
- **How long:** "Entre 45 y 75 minutos para la mayoría", about 90 with the audio and the roof (barcelona-tickets, SEC); "at least an hour" with the roof and cloister (trvlogue); "at least 1.5 hours" (Klook review); "about 40 minutes there" (Viator review).
- **The highlights they remember:** the roof views, the cloister and geese ("Don't miss the goose garden"), the choir stalls, Eulàlia's tomb (Wanderlog; Klook; Headout reviews).
- **When to go:** quieter at about 9:30 or from about 16:00 (Barcelona Lowdown); busiest from mid-morning to early afternoon, at weekends and in Jun–Aug (barcelona-tickets); "before the 11:00 wave" (Barcelona Urbana) (SEC).
- **Do I need to book?** Not essential, "unlike the Sagrada Familia", but the on-site ticket queue "can be quite long" (trvlogue, SEC).

# AUDIT V5: facts (Barcelona Cathedral, 36 tracks + 3 bridge files), 14 Nov 2026

This is an independent fact audit of `v5/tracks/001–036.perf.txt` (the spoken body after `---`, plus @what and @sources) and of `v5/bridges/*.txt`. I did not write these tracks and I did not edit any of them.

**Method**
- **Baseline.** I read FACTS_NOV2026, FACTS_GAPS_NOV2026 (rows and the "Changes proposed to FACTS rows" table), the route file, PLAN §1, §4 NEVER, §6 DATED and the lead's decisions, and the three writer notes. I then checked every quotation, every date and number in the brief, and every claim that looked like a gloss against the cited pages myself.
- **Web.** I used WebFetch and WebSearch only, one page at a time and in sequence: 51 fetches and 2 searches. I used no curl, no browser and no CAPTCHA, and I never relied on Wikipedia. Every page I fetched could be read. The dialnet PDF (Mercader 2008), which the Park Güell audit could not reach, opened this time.
- **Quotes are "as relayed".** WebFetch returns a model summary and caps verbatim excerpts at about 125 characters, so every quote below is as relayed. Where a long quotation was relayed in pieces, I say so.
- **One correction to my own expectation.** I expected Gabriel Blancafort to have died before the 1994 work on the organ. The GEC says otherwise (d. 24 Aug 2001), so 011 stands. I mention it because it is the kind of thing a later checker may "correct" wrongly.

**Counts: 2 MUST, 13 SHOULD, 21 NICE.**
- **Quotations:** 40 quoted strings in the spoken text, plus 2 glosses ("the late", "Do not touch me"). All 40 are confirmed (V) as relayed, and none is misattributed. One caveat: 034's signature spelling is still to be checked on the panel (O21).
- **Bridges:** all 3 files are directions only, with no factual claims. They are OK.
- **NEVER list.** No breach in the spoken text, with two caveats:
  - The gargoyle animals in 013 sit between PLAN §2 (which plans them, attributed) and §4 (which bars them until O7). This is S13 below.
  - Three paragraphs are conditional on site checks but are written in unconditionally: the 1493 plaque (010, O14), Sança's tomb (008, O4/O5) and the Pont del Bisbe (036, O23). The writers flag all three; see N6 and N21.
- **Banned phrases: none found.** In detail:
  - no 878, 1046, "three queens", "alabaster" for her tomb, "hockey", "mermaid", "anarchist", "Diocesan Museum" or "scarlet";
  - no Louis II by name, and no "Charles presided in person";
  - no "massacre" in our voice, and no 1391 figure but "about three hundred";
  - no "bombed cathedral"; the Pont del Bisbe is never called medieval;
  - the ou com balla is "at least to 1636", not "began 1637"; Santa Llúcia is not called the Romanesque survivor, and 032 corrects that cliché;
  - the legends are told as legends: the cannonball, the finger, the thirteen tortures and the age of thirteen, the goose-girl, Penyafort's cloak, Honorata, Helena, the Mercy "here", the coffins and al-Bakri.
- **Tense for the dead:** correct everywhere except al-Bakri ("says", 026: S12 below).
- **DATED items are current on 14 Nov 2026.**
  - O2, today: 13 geese, "at least to 1636", the 3 May blessing.
  - O8, today: Lent at Saint Severus.
  - The dean's words: Catalan News, 11 Feb 2025. The dean is not named.
  - The 2024 roof works and the 12 windows: unchanged.

## 1. Findings

### MUST

**M1. 021: "Barcelona has never had any doubt." is false, and it contradicts the two sentences before it.**
- **Track text:** "A century and more ago, Barcelona's own historians doubted her too. Others answer with the early Christian graves found under Santa Maria del Mar … A study published in 2023 still treats the question as open. Historians argue whether Barcelona's Eulàlia and Mérida's were two girls or one story. Barcelona has never had any doubt."
- **Sources:**
  - Barcelona's own popular-culture pages, A9 (https://barcelona.cat/culturapopular/en/node/1720, read today): "St Eulàlia of Barcelona's existence as a historical figure is doubtful."
  - A10 (https://barcelona.cat/culturapopular/en/noticia/463606, 1 Feb 2026, read today): "everything seems to suggest that Saint Eulalia de Merida actually existed, whereas Saint Eulalia de Barcelona did not."
  - GS14: Barcelona historians Sampere i Miquel and Carreras i Candi rejected her existence. The track itself says so one sentence earlier.
- **Problem.** The line comes from FACTS 7.8's suggested wording, but it is untrue as spoken. The city, the city's historians and a 2023 study have all doubted her. What has never wavered is the devotion, and the next paragraph already makes that point.
- **Replace with:** "Barcelona's devotion to her has never wavered."
  - Or simply cut the sentence. The paragraph then ends on "two girls or one story", and the next paragraph ("What the record does show is devotion…") carries the point.

**M2. 026: the count and countess did not consecrate the cathedral.**
- **Track text:** "Soon after, in November 1058, the count and countess consecrated their new cathedral, here, on this site."
- **Sources:**
  - O1 via FACTS 2.3: "was consecrated on 18 November 1058 by the Archbishop Wilfred of Narbonne."
  - S1 (Enc. Cat.): "La catedral romànica fou consagrada el 1058 per l'arquebisbe de Narbona, durant el bisbat de Guislabert."
  - S2: the consecration act names "els comtes Ramon Berenguer I i Almodis, junt amb el bisbe Guislabert" as the builders.
- **Problem.** Only a bishop consecrates a church; the counts were its builders and patrons. The sentence gets the roles wrong in our voice.
- **Replace with:** "Soon after, in November 1058, their new cathedral was consecrated, here, on this site."
  - Or: "…the count and countess saw their new cathedral consecrated, here, on this site."

### SHOULD

**S1. 018: "the knights paid for their own coats of arms" goes beyond the receipts.**
- **Track text:** "The receipts show the money came from the order's own treasurers: the knights paid for their own coats of arms."
- **Source:** Carbonell i Buades, S12 (https://revistes.uab.cat/locus/article/download/v5-carbonell/106-pdf-ca/531, read today, offset 100000). Borgonya's shield paintings were paid "dels tresorers de l'orde, no del capítol"; "la tresoreria del Toisó va pagar a Carbonell" 280 ducats.
- **Problem.** The order's treasury is not the knights, so the second clause is our gloss. FACTS 9.20 offers it only as an "optional" line, with no source.
- **Replace with:** "The receipts show the money came from the order's own treasurers. The order paid for its own coats of arms."

**S2. 034: "This is his last known work." contradicts the encyclopedia, and 010.**
- **Track text:** "This is his last known work. When he died, nobody knows."
- **Sources:**
  - MNAC M2 (https://blog.museunacional.cat/en/bermejo-a-fifteenth-century-rebel-genius, read today) does say "his last known work, Canon Lluís Desplà's Pietà (1490)".
  - The Catalan encyclopedia, S14 (https://www.enciclopedia.cat/lart-gotic-a-catalunya/bartolome-de-cardenas-el-bermejo, read today), says: "El 1495 Bermejo dibuixà el cartó d'un vitrall que feu Gil de Fontanet per a la finestra de la capella baptismal". It also records further stained-glass designs in 1500 and 1501, now lost.
- **Problem.** Track 010 tells the listener that the baptistery window (1495) was made from his design. An attentive listener hears the guide contradict itself.
- **Replace with:** "This is his last known painting. When he died, nobody knows."
  - Or attribute it: "The national art museum calls it his last known work."

**S3. 009: "for more marble" is not in the source.**
- **Track text:** "Then he went to Carrara, in Italy, for more marble. He never came back."
- **Source:** S12 (read today). His earlier trip to Italy, Oct 1517 – Apr 1518, was "contractant marbre i ajudants". For the 1519 departure the article gives no reason; a 1519 document only foresees his heirs being paid "if he died in Carrara", and his will was "redactat a Carrara".
- **Replace with:** "Then he left for Carrara, in Italy, the marble town. He never came back."
  - Or simply: "Then he left for Carrara, in Italy, and never came back."

**S4. 011: the identification of the two Antoni Carbonells is not sourced, and the source knows of two men with that name.**
- **Track text:** "Carbonell also worked for the knights of the Golden Fleece, in the choir."
- **Sources:**
  - S12 (read today) distinguishes an Antoni Carbonell "major", "el fuster oficial de la seu", from an Antoni "menor", also a "fuster" on the choir. It does not mention the 1538 organ.
  - Orgues Blancafort, S21 (read today), links "Antoni Carbonell" to "all the carving and cabinet work of the case".
  - FACTS 11.17 makes the link ("the same Carbonell") without a source.
- **Replace with:** "A joiner of the same name, Antoni Carbonell, had worked on the choir for the knights of the Golden Fleece."
  - Or cut the sentence; 018 and 019 do not need it.

**S5. 024: "the arms of Barcelona" are the royal arms, not the city's.**
- **Track text:** "On it is the bishop's own shield, beside the arms of Barcelona."
- **Source:** Cañellas, GS11 (https://core.ac.uk/download/39093939.pdf, read today): "la vidriera central porta l'escut del bisbe Pons de Gualba amb el del casal de Barcelona". *Casal de Barcelona* is the ruling House (the royal arms). The study lists the city's arms separately, on other windows.
- **Problem.** The track itself says "the royal house of Barcelona" one paragraph earlier. A listener will hear the city's arms here.
- **Replace with:** "On it is the bishop's own shield, beside the royal arms."

**S6. 025: the move of the tomb is out of order, and "that year" is not in the source.**
- **Track text:** "In 1835, a year of unrest in the city, a great fire damaged the convent. Soon afterwards it was pulled down, and explosives were used to bring down its church's vault. By then the convent was all but abandoned, and so his tomb was moved here that year."
- **Sources:**
  - GO1 (https://catedralbcn.org/en/worship-and-prayer/saints/saint-raymond-of-penyafort/, read today): "In 1835 the convent of Saint Catherine was practically abandoned. As a result, the tomb of Saint Raymond of Penyafort was moved to the Cathedral of Barcelona." No year is given for the move.
  - GS12: demolished December 1836 – February 1837.
- **Problem.** "By then" points after the demolition, while "that year" points back to 1835. FACTS_GAPS 10.24's "tomb to the cathedral in 1835" is a reading of GO1, not a statement in it.
- **Replace with:** "In 1835, a year of unrest in the city, a great fire damaged the convent, and it was left all but abandoned. His tomb was brought here. Soon afterwards the convent was pulled down, and explosives were used to bring down its church's vault."

**S7. 029: "the first place in Barcelona" is the cathedral's claim, spoken in our voice.**
- **Track text:** "This cathedral was the first place in Barcelona to present the dancing egg, going back at least to 1636."
- **Sources:**
  - O2 (https://catedralbcn.org/en/the-cathedral/traditions/, read today): "The Cathedral of Barcelona was the first to present the dancing egg in Barcelona, going back at least to 1636."
  - The city's page, A3 (read today), gives "since at least 1636" and the accounts, but does not say "first".
- **Problem.** "First" is a superlative of the kind the NEVER list keeps attributed. The words are the cathedral's.
- **Replace with:** "The cathedral says it was the first place in Barcelona to present the dancing egg, going back at least to 1636."

**S8. 033: "It holds" puts forty thousand parchments in this room.**
- **Track text:** "Until 1969 this was the chapter's archive. It holds about forty thousand parchments, and nine papyri…"
- **Source:** O9 (https://catedralbcn.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Cata%CC%80leg-de-lArxiu.pdf, read today): the 1535 room "ha s[er]vit d'Arxiu Capitular fins al 1969". The archive holds "unes 40.000 unitats" of "documents en pergamí". The papyri were found "dins una de les guardes del ms. 120".
- **Problem.** The room stopped being the archive in 1969, so the present tense misleads.
- **Replace with:** "Until 1969 this was the chapter's archive. The archive, kept elsewhere in the cathedral today, holds about forty thousand parchments, and nine papyri…"

**S9. 023: "The best story, the cathedral says" attributes our judgement to the cathedral.**
- **Track text:** "The best story, the cathedral says, is carved on the back."
- **Source:** O3 (https://catedralbcn.org/en/?p=2421, read today). The page describes the rear reliefs, including the stolen finger that makes the body too heavy to move, but it ranks nothing.
- **Replace with:** "On the back, the cathedral says, is carved the best story of all." (The judgement is then ours.)
  - Simpler: "The best story is on the back, where the cathedral describes this scene."

**S10. 004: the quotation is credited to "a University of Barcelona exhibition", but the exhibition was not the University's.**
- **Track text:** "A University of Barcelona exhibition put it plainly: 'The lack of budget in medieval Barcelona prevented this project' from being built."
- **Source:** S25 (https://www.ub.edu/web/ub/en/menu_eines/noticies/2020/02/028.html, read today). It is the UB's news item about an exhibition at the Casa de la Pia Almoina, "organized by the cathedral and the Diocesan Museum" (Feb–Apr 2020), as part of a Vallmitjana Year co-organized by the UB and the Seminari. The page also says the 1408 original was under restoration, and a reproduction was shown.
- **Replace with:** "The University of Barcelona put it plainly: 'The lack of budget in medieval Barcelona prevented this project' from being built."
  - 005 already says "The University of Barcelona says it plainly". To avoid the echo, 004 could use "A University of Barcelona report put it bluntly".

**S11. 026: Hercules. The traveller does not say where the tomb was.**
- **Track text:** "Four centuries ago, a traveller wrote that he was shown an old tomb here."
- **Source:** Mercader 2008, GS5 (https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/5744790.pdf, read today). Joly wrote: "Un viejo resto de sepulcro se enseña; algunos dicen que es de Hércules, otros de Raimundo, conde de Barcelona." The relay says Joly does not say where the tomb was; the placement beside the sacristy is the author's, supported by Ponz in 1787.
- **Problem.** "Here", spoken under the coffins, turns the author's identification into Joly's.
- **Replace with:** "Four centuries ago, a traveller wrote that he was shown an old tomb in this cathedral." The rest of the paragraph stays.

**S12. 026: al-Bakri is spoken in the present tense.**
- **Track text:** "An Arab chronicler, al-Bakri, says the count hired the Muslim fleet of Tortosa to carry her off from her husband."
- **Rule.** PLAN §1 Conventions: "Past tense always: every medieval … person". (The Park Güell audit graded the same issue SHOULD, S3 Bassegoda.)
- **Source:** GS6 Epistolae (https://epistolae.ctl.columbia.edu/woman/25529.html, read today): "A Muslim historian, al-Bakri, gives a romantic reading of the event, reporting that Raymond had hired the Saracen fleet of Tortosa to spirit Almodis away from her then husband". The content is correct.
- **Replace with:** "An Arab chronicler, al-Bakri, wrote that the count hired the Muslim fleet of Tortosa to carry her off from her husband."

**S13. 013: the gargoyle animals, PLAN §2 against §4 (for the lead).**
- **Track text:** "The city counts two hundred on the roofs and pinnacles. And among the monsters, it says, there's a bull, a unicorn and an elephant."
- **Source:** A4 (https://www.barcelona.cat/en/conocebcn/pics/la-catedral-92086000934, read today): "On the roofs and pinnacles there are 200 gargoyles keeping watch from on high," / "but it is surprising to find a bull, a unicorn and an elephant among the monsters and demonic beings." The text is correct and attributed.
- **Problem.** PLAN §4 NEVER bars "gargoyle animals (F4.16) … until the matching ONSITE item finds them". §2's row for 013 plans exactly this line. This is not a factual error, but one of the two rules has to give.
- **Recommendation.** Keep the line: it is attributed and heard in the queue, with no "look for".
  - If the lead holds to §4, cut "And among the monsters … elephant." (−17 words).

### NICE

**N1. 001: "worshipped … for seventeen centuries".** The source O6 says "més de mil set-cents anys d'història" (history), and 036 deliberately says "history". Worship from the 4th-century see is roughly right, so this is OK.
- **Optional:** "Christians have gathered on this corner of the old Roman town for seventeen centuries."

**N2. 003: "built from the 12th century".** A6 (read today) says "built around the 12th century".
- **Replace with:** "The city's archive says it was built around the 12th century, 'taking advantage…'".

**N3. 004: "Nine years after Carlí's visit".** No source calls Carlí a visitor: he was the "mestre tracista", and in 1408 he also designed the cloister chapel of the Apostles (S33, 031).
- **Replace with:** "Nine years after Carlí's drawing, they closed it with a plain wall…"

**N4. 004: the title says "built in 1887", but building took 1887–1890.**
- **Title.** Nothing in the spoken text is wrong. Optionally, change the title to "Drawn in 1408, begun in 1887". Leave it as it is if the 1887 title is already fixed in the PLAN or the lint.
- **Lantern year.** COAC S24 (read today) gives the cimbori as "beginning in 1906 and finishing in 1912". The official 1913 used in the track is the right choice (FACTS 5.16).

**N5. 005: confirmed, and stronger than spoken.** COAC S24 (read today): "Girona himself had drawn the proposed design, endorsed by Josep Oriol Mestres, the cathedral architect and a personal friend". Martorell's design was "far more ornate, majestic, and vertically emphasised", which supports "taller and richer".
- **Optional:** "But the man paying the bills had drawn it himself", attributed to the architects' college. As written, the line is OK.

**N6. 010: the 1493 plaque.** Keep it only if O14 finds the plaque, which is the writer's own flag; PLAN §4 bars it otherwise. El Debate (S34, read today) quotes "los primeros seis indios venidos de América", says only "la capilla", and places the doubt in the king's stated presence ("estando presente el Rey Fernando") while he lay recovering at La Murtra.
- **Replace "six men" with:** "six people Columbus brought back from the Caribbean".

**N7. 011: the organ's date.** The GEC entry for Gabriel Blancafort (read today) says he built the cathedral's organ "(1994, a partir de la façana i la caixa originals del 1538)". The cathedral (O6) says "restored". The track's "made around 1540 … restored it in the 1980s and 1990s" follows the cathedral and is acceptable.
- **More exact:** "Its case was made around 1540…; Gabriel Blancafort rebuilt the instrument inside it, in the 1980s and 1990s."

**N8. 013: the 1606 firecrackers rest on a weak source.** The betevé page (S30, read today; 30 Apr 2021, modified 29 Apr 2022) also contains three errors:
- Almanzor in "997";
- the ou com balla arriving in "1637";
- a 1938 bomb that "partly destroyed the cathedral".

The line is attributed ("Barcelona's public television tells"), as PLAN requires, so keep it. Do not cite S30 for anything else. The 025 line ("Here, says tradition") also leans on S30: "l'Orde de la Mercè es fundés el 1218 dins la Catedral, a l'altar major de Santa Eulàlia". That is the right register.

**N9. 014: "the two tall Gothic pinnacles that flank the church".** O6 says only "els dos pinacles laterals", and "Gothic" is the writer's word. The side spires belong to the 1887–1913 front and lantern works, so they are neo-Gothic.
- **Replace with:** "There are the two tall pinnacles that flank the church."

**N10. 016: "to choose the wood himself".** S9 (read today) says only that he was sent "per comprar fusta de roure".
- **Replace with:** "He travelled all the way to Flanders to buy the wood."

**N11. 018: "Among the arms are those of distant kings" (plural) follows lead's decision 5.** Only one king is sourced: Louis II of Hungary, from a chapter title (GS4); the others are UNVERIFIED (G20). Decision 5 explicitly allows this sentence, so it stays.
- **Optional:** "Among the arms are a king's."

**N12. 020: "carried on alone".** S10 says only that Joan Frederic worked "fins el 1497, data en què va acabar la primera fase". "Alone" is our word.
- **Replace with:** "After his death his assistant, Joan Frederic, another German, carried on, and by 1497 the first phase was done."

**N13. 021: "In the story he attacks her".** A9 (read today) says she opposed Dacian "with words and reason".
- **Replace with:** "In the story he threatens her, and she answers him with words and reason."

**N14. 021: "You saw that cross on the choir screen".** This assumes one of the four trascor panels shows the X-shaped cross. O24 should confirm this; if not, change it to "You'll find that cross in images of her all over this cathedral."

**N15. 024: the crypt and Ponç de Gualba.** The track lists the crypt in the first phase and adds "Most of it rose under one bishop, Ponç de Gualba". Bracons (S4, read today) has the crypt begun on 29 June 1337 and completed 1338–39, under Ferrer d'Abella; Ponç died in 1334. "Most of it" saves the sentence. It would be cleaner to end the list at "the sanctuary of the high altar" and keep "and the crypt beneath it" for 022–023.

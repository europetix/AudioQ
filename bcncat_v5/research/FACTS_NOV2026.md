# Barcelona Cathedral (Catedral de la Santa Creu i Santa Eulàlia): verified fact base (as of 14 Nov 2026)

Yo Tours English audio guide v5. Scope per `USER_DECISIONS.md` (14 Nov 2026): everything on the cultural-visit ticket. That means the facade and the Pla de la Seu outside, the nave, side chapels, high altar and crypt, the choir, the cloister with its geese and fountain, the rooftop terraces by lift, the chapter-house museum, plus the Santa Llúcia chapel, the Santíssim (Christ of Lepanto) chapel, the Porta de Sant Iu, the bell towers, the organ and the cimbori. This file holds facts, stories, quotes and things to look at. **No prices or hours belong in scripts.** Route and on-site placement belong in `VISIT_ROUTE_AND_SPACES_NOV2026.md` (a separate file, written in parallel; not cross-checked against this one). The official plan (map) is printed on p. 39 of the cathedral's own magazine [O11] but its numbering could not be extracted (§17).

## 0. Method and keys

- **Tools.** WebSearch and WebFetch only. One page at a time per site, no bursts. No curl, no browser, no CAPTCHA. All reads were done on 14 Nov 2026.
- **WebFetch caveat.** WebFetch returns a model summary, and verbatim excerpts are capped at about 125 characters. So every "verbatim" quote is **as relayed**. **Re-check the exact wording against the page before quoting on air.** The original language is marked: (EN), (CA), (ES), (LA).
- **Grades.**
  - **OFF**: official. That means the cathedral itself (catedralbcn.org, run by the Chapter, "Santa Església Catedral Basílica Metropolitana de Barcelona"), the Holy See (vatican.va), the Ajuntament de Barcelona (barcelona.cat, incl. **MUHBA**, the city history museum, the Arxiu Històric and Cultura Popular pages), the Diputació, Barcelona Turisme (a public consortium; its ticket page is a seller's page), and **MNAC** (Museu Nacional d'Art de Catalunya). **OFF** pages contain slips; those are flagged in §18.
  - **SEC**: a named secondary source read directly. That covers peer-reviewed papers (*Matèria*, *Locus Amoenus*, *Pedralbes*, *Forum Kritische Archäologie*), UB theses, the Enciclopèdia Catalana family (*Gran Enciclopèdia Catalana*, *Catalunya Romànica*, *L'art gòtic a Catalunya*, *Del romà al romànic*), the National Gallery London, the COAC architecture catalogue, and reputable press (betevé, Catalan News, Time Out, El Debate).
  - **SNIP**: a search-result snippet or title only.
  - **WIKI**: used as a lead only, never as sole support. No row below rests on Wikipedia.
- **Verdicts.**
  - **OK**: safe to state as written.
  - **HEDGE**: true in substance, or sources conflict; use the safe wording.
  - **LEGEND**: tradition or tour lore; script only as "the story goes…".
  - **UNVERIFIED**: do not script.
  - **ERROR**: a claim found in circulation, or on an official page, that is wrong or contradicted.
  - **DATED**: a 2024–2026 fact that may change. Re-check before release.
- **Sources** are listed in §22 as [O…] (cathedral / Holy See), [A…] (Ajuntament / MUHBA / Diputació / Barcelona Turisme), [M…] (MNAC) and [S…] (secondary).

## 21. Story bank (strongest, all from rows above)

1. **The girl and the geese** (7.3–7.8, 12.4–12.7): thirteen years, thirteen tortures, thirteen geese, "according to tradition". Geese documented here since the 16th century; nobody knows why geese. And the honest turn: historians argue whether Barcelona's Eulàlia and Mérida's are one story.
2. **The finger that weighed a ton** (7.14, 8.13): the body grows too heavy until the stolen finger is returned. See it on the sarcophagus.
3. **Two kings and their queens on the crypt steps** (8.5–8.7, 8.14): July 1339; the tomb shows its own inauguration on the lid; marble, not alabaster.
4. **Two masters and a freed slave** (8.9–8.12): the Mallorcan Fabre and the Pisan Lupo meet in one document of 1327.
5. **The stalls from Bruges oak** (9.3–9.5): Sanglada travels to Flanders for wood; under the seats, monkeys, musicians, a siren with a mirror.
6. **Europe's most exclusive club meets in the choir** (9.14–9.26): a duke's order, a pagan fleece swapped for a biblical one, the young King Charles in March 1519, and the shields painted for the day. Charles saw almost what you see.
7. **Ordóñez's unfinished marble** (9.28–9.30): a Renaissance sculptor carves Eulàlia, goes to Carrara, never returns.
8. **The Christ older than its battle** (10.4–10.11): the cannonball story appears 191 years later; the cathedral now says the carving may be 13th-century; the darkness is candle smoke.
9. **A facade drawn in 1408, built in 1887** (5.1–5.13): the Rouen master's twelve parchments; a banker who paid when the state wouldn't; unveiled on Santa Eulàlia's day.
10. **Gaudí lost here** (5.6–5.8, 5.20): he drew Martorell's rejected design; the winners were accused of borrowing from it.
11. **The stopgap roof that lasted 485 years** (3.14, 4.9, 5.16): Gual goes to Valencia in 1421; the cimbori is finished in 1913 over the entrance, not the altar.
12. **The archdeacon in two places** (6.4, 14.2–14.8): Desplà rebuilt the Casa de l'Ardiaca outside; inside he kneels in Bermejo's Pietà beside a saint wearing spectacles.
13. **A Saracen head that spat sweets** (11.22–11.23): fun, and a story the city now calls racist. Tell both.
14. **The bell the king punished** (11.7–11.9): Honorata after 1714, as the city tells it; Eulàlia strikes the hours.
15. **The egg the accountant wrote down** (12.15–12.19): 1636, eggs bought for the fountain at Corpus; Saint George above in the keystone.
16. **A bishop's silks in Cleveland** (13.9–13.10): Arnau de Gurb's tomb in Santa Llúcia; "very mediocre" carving, by one historian's verdict.
17. **The Christ, the archive and July 1936** (15.7–15.10): the government guards the cathedral; the archive is carried out to safety; back in 1939.
18. **The founders who were excommunicated** (2.12–2.13, 10.14): Ramon Berenguer and Almodis; the countess murdered by her stepson; their painted coffins high on the wall, with doubts about who is inside.
19. **Blessing the city from the roof** (11.28, 17.5): every 3 May, from where you stand.

[curious] Under the crucifix lies a bishop who helped bring a whole city back to life. Sant Oleguer was made bishop of Barcelona in the early 1100s. When Tarragona was won back, he became its archbishop too. He died in 1137, and was made a saint in 1675.

If you can see in from the doorway, look for his tomb. It's Baroque, and on it lies something older: a medieval figure of the bishop in alabaster, carved by Pere Sanglada in 1406. It's the only alabaster among his surviving works, and it was kept when the new tomb was made.

[lightly] Remember this alabaster when you reach Santa Eulàlia's tomb in the crypt.

Once a year, on his feast, the sixth of March, his body in bishop's robes is shown in a glass case in a chamber of the cathedral.

[pause] Next door, more alabaster. A noblewoman, Sança Ximenis de Cabrera, bought the chapel beside the way into this one. Everyone calls the alabaster tomb in it hers. But an art historian argues, from a payment of 1442 and the coats of arms, that it was meant for her mother.

Now walk to the white marble screen at the end of the choir, the enclosure in the middle of the nave. Stand back a little to see its carved panels: The choir screen · The sculptor who never came back.

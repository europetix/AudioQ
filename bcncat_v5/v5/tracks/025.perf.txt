The story goes that Ramon de Penyafort once crossed the sea from Sóller, on Mallorca, all the way home to Barcelona, sailing on his own cloak. The cathedral's page, which tells it as a legend, calls it his blanket.

He was a Dominican friar. He died in Barcelona in 1275, at the convent of Santa Caterina, and was made a saint in Rome more than three centuries later. He is the patron saint of Catalan lawyers, and his feast falls on the seventh of January.

So why is his tomb here, and not in his convent? In 1835, a year of unrest in the city, a great fire damaged the convent. Soon afterwards it was pulled down, and explosives were used to bring down its church's vault. The cathedral says that by then the convent was all but abandoned, and so his tomb was moved here that year.

[pause] Tradition ties him to something that began in this cathedral. In 1218, the story goes, the bishop of Barcelona founded here an order to ransom captives, the Order of Mercy. The Catalan encyclopedia gives the day, the nineteenth of August, and the names. The bishop gave the white habit to Pere Nolasc and thirteen knights, with Ramon de Penyafort advising and King Jaume the First helping. Where it happened, the encyclopedia doesn't say. Here, says tradition.

Look for his name on the sign beside the chapel, and find his tomb.

Carry on round to the right side of the high altar. By the sacristy door, look high on the wall for two painted wooden boxes: The sacristy door · The founders who were excommunicated.

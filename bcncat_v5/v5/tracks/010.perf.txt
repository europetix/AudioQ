[curious] In 1968, under the cathedral floor, archaeologists found the font where Barcelona's first Christians were baptised. It was a pool inside its own small building, later rebuilt with eight sides. You can't see it today. It lies under the floor, out of sight.

This chapel is the baptistery, and its font is from the 15th century. Look up at its tall window. It shows Saint Michael and the scene called Noli me tangere, Latin for "Do not touch me". Gil de Fontanet made it in 1495, from a design by the painter Bartolomé Bermejo, and it's still in the place it was made for. The cathedral calls it "the most important of all the Cathedral windows". Bermejo also painted the Pietà you'll see in the Chapter Hall museum.

[warmly] Now look lower, for a carved stone washing cupboard from the early 1400s. It's the first surviving work of the sculptor Antoni Canet, older than the chapel's vault above it. It rests on stone brackets carved as human heads. One is a girl, with a low neckline and a necklace. The other is a man in a hood, with a generous beard. Find the girl.

[pause] A plaque here remembers the baptism of six people Columbus brought back from the Caribbean, in April 1493. The date on it may not be right.

Walk up this left-hand aisle, as you face the high altar, past its side chapels, to the side door near its far end, the Sant Iu door, with the great organ high above it. Stand near the door, out of the way: The Sant Iu door · A Saracen under the organ.

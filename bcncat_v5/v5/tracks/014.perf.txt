On top of the lantern tower stands a woman holding a cross. She is Saint Helena, carved in 1910 by the sculptor Eduard Alentorn, and she crowns the lantern you stood under when you came in.

[curious] Why her? Helena was the mother of the emperor Constantine, and the story goes that she found the cross of Christ itself, in Jerusalem. This is the cathedral of the Holy Cross. And there's a family reason too. The cathedral says that the family of the banker who paid for the front were devoted to Saint Helena, and that many of them bore her name.

[pause] Now turn slowly and take in the roof around you. There are the two bell towers, each with eight sides: one stands over the Sant Iu door and carries the clock, the other stands over the door into the cloister. There are the two tall Gothic pinnacles that flank the church. And down below lies the cloister, its garden open to the sky.

[warmly] Once a year, this roof has a job to do. Every year in early May, on the feast of the Holy Cross, the whole city is blessed from up here. In Catalan it's called the Benedicció del Terme, the blessing of the city's territory. It's given from the cross on the roof over the apse, at the far end of the church, behind the high altar. Look along the roof for that cross.

As you walk, keep an eye on the gargoyles at the edges of the roof, the carved stone spouts that throw the rainwater clear of the walls. Look for the beasts among them.

Walk on along the terraces to a spot where you can see over the old town and down into the cloister: The roof · The old town from above.

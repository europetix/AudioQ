Modern sculpture, "a stone's throw from the Monastery of Sant Miquel". That's how the Poble's own page sets the scene, and it's a fair description of this odd and lovely corner.

This is the Sculpture Garden, opened in 2004. It holds thirty-six sculptures by twenty-seven contemporary artists, whom the Poble describes as "Spanish artists of the 20th century". The Catalan encyclopedia lists the garden with the Fran Daurel foundation, the same collection as the museum down on the Plaza de la Iglesia.

The ground here moves in what the Poble calls, in Spanish, leves pendientes, gentle slopes. There are two giant slides, which the Poble says are for "children and adults alike". Its guide for schools even sends classes here to eat under the trees.

Pick one sculpture, any one, and find its label. Read the artist's name, then walk round the work and look at it again from the other side.

Further on, the Poble promises "a unique view over the city of Barcelona" from "the vantage point at the end of the garden".

Leave the garden the way you came in, and take the steps from the Baluard up to the monastery. It's the Romanesque church with the square bell tower, at the top of the hill: Monastery of Sant Miquel · A monastery in pieces.

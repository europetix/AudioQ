By 1990 the village had a weak spot. That year the Catalan magazine El Temps described it as the tourist attraction that, "at night, never quite worked".

The answer was found inside these towers. Their interior was redesigned as a night venue, the Torres de Ávila. The magazine wrote, in Catalan: "There was a lot of envy around the affair, everyone wanted the last word. But it was Arribas and Mariscal who were the heroes of the feat." Alfredo Arribas and Javier Mariscal, two designers.

They divided it into the Sun and the Moon. "The woman's part, that is the Moon," the magazine wrote, "has a giant moving wall lined with optical fibres." And "at the very top there is the terrace", where, it said, "the towers take on their least-known dimension".

Look up at the top of the towers, among the battlements. That's where the magazine found the terrace.

And Mariscal is this village's one real thread to the 1992 Olympics. He created the mascot for Barcelona's 1992 Games: Cobi, a "humanised Pyrenean mountain dog", as the Olympic movement describes him.

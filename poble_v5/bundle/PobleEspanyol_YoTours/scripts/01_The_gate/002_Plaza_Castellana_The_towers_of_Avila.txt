In 1929, the Exhibition's official visitors were photographed right here. The Poble's own history says so: "Several official photos were taken in front of the Torres de Ávila." The towers of Ávila. The King and Queen, Alfonso the Thirteenth and Victoria Eugenia, came to the village's opening.

What were they standing in front of? The Poble calls this gate "a representation" of the Puerta de San Vicente, a gateway in the medieval walls of Ávila, in Castile. The city's photo archive has a picture captioned: "Main entrance to the Poble Espanyol site. Gate of San Vicente of the wall of Avila. 1928."

Now look at it. A Catalan heritage catalogue describes the gate, in Catalan, as "flanked by two great cylindrical towers crowned with merlons and joined to each other by a bridge". Find the merlons, the square teeth along the top. Then find the walkway that bridges the gap between the two towers.

This is also where the hill begins. The village is entered at its lowest point and climbs from here. An architect's study of the village says, in Spanish, that "our visit obliges us to enter through the gate of San Vicente", and that this is what makes it the main entrance.

Around you is Plaza Castellana, with palaces from Extremadura. The Poble's own list places the Casa del Sol, from Cáceres, at house 3. Round this square it also places more noble houses from Cáceres, one from Plasencia and a bishop's palace from Miajadas. They're pink on the plan, for the Centre.

People sometimes think the village was built for the 1992 Olympics. It's sixty-three years older.

This gate was a stage entrance for the Exhibition. It is still the way in, and at the end of this walk it will be your way out.

If you're on the full tour, there's a short Extra here, on what these towers became in 1990. The next main stop is the start of Calle Caballeros, the street that climbs away from Plaza Castellana between houses 115 and 2. Stop where it begins: Calle Caballeros · Four men and a Hispano-Suiza.

Almost every house in this village comes from somewhere else. In the Poble's own words, these are "117 buildings that are actually full-scale recreations of other existing ones". Life-size, not miniatures.

Welcome to the Poble Espanyol, the Spanish Village, on Montjuïc. It was built for the great Barcelona International Exhibition of 1929. Building began in January 1928, and the village opened about sixteen months later. The Poble's own history says that "the initial idea was for it to be demolished". It never was.

So what is it? Not a museum of copies. A 1961 architecture journal put the aim, in Spanish, as "interpretation more than the reproduction of particular models". Nor, later researchers add, was it about "making a collection of monuments". It's an invented town, built from real pieces. So this guide tells you, house by house, where the original stands and what the builders changed.

On the official plan the houses come in four colours: North green, Centre pink, South yellow, Mediterranean purple. Many stops name a house by its number on that plan, so pick one up at the entrance.

Our walk is one loop, uphill first. Up Calle Caballeros to Aragon's brick tower, then through Catalonia to the monastery at the top, with its view of Barcelona. Then down through the white streets of Andalusia and the Santiago Steps to the Plaza Mayor, and out through this gate.

The full tour takes about an hour and a half, walking included, nearer two hours if you also go round the Fran Daurel art museum. The Essential version takes about an hour and a quarter, the Highlights about three quarters of an hour. In the shorter versions the numbers may jump. Every stop tells you where to go next.

The official plan marks a step-free route. Ask for it at the entrance, and this guide warns you where there are steps.

Craftspeople work in the workshops here: glassblowing, pottery, stained glass. If a door is open, look in.

Stay in Plaza Castellana and face the gate you came in by, its two round towers with battlements, joined by a walkway: Plaza Castellana · The towers of Ávila.

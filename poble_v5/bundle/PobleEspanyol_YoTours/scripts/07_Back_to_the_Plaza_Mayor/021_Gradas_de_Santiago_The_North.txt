This is the least documented corner of the village, and we'd rather say so than make something up.

The Poble calls these steps the Gradas de Santiago, the Santiago Steps, and its family trail ties them to the Camino de Santiago, the pilgrims' road. The city's film office calls them "the bleachers of Santiago". What they were modelled on, if anything, no source we've found says.

Round these steps, the Poble's own list includes houses from Galicia, from Cambados and Betanzos among others. From Asturias, Cangas de Onís. And from Aragon, arches from Sos del Rey Católico.

Here is the honest part. The historians who found the makers' notebooks name no Galician models. We know these towns from the Poble's own list, not which front is which.

The journey did come this way. In September 1927 the first trip crossed Galicia, Asturias and Cantabria. Among the photographs that survive is Francesc Folguera's of the pre-Romanesque church of San Miguel de Lillo, near Oviedo.

If you have the official plan, look at the houses round the top of these steps: they're green, the plan's colour for the North. And the plan's North is generous. It takes in Aragon and Navarre too.

Go down the Gradas de Santiago. At the bottom, the corner the plan calls Rincón del Triste is beside you. Walk on into the Plaza Mayor and find a place on its steps where you can see the whole square and the arcaded town hall: Plaza Mayor · A square from Riaza.

On the first trip, in 1927, the team stopped in the main square of Riaza, in Segovia, and Ramon Reventós wrote a note in Spanish: "Riaza, a sublime house, with extra-barbarous Ionic columns." They christened it La Casona, the big house.

This is the Plaza Mayor, the main square, and the Poble's own page calls it, in Spanish, a "replica of the Castilian square of Riaza". The historians who found the team's notebooks are more exact. The makers wanted a square with changes of level, and that, the historians write, "they would find in the main square of Riaza". Riaza's square has similar features, they add, "despite being round".

Look round. On the side toward Plaza Castellana, the historians list town halls from Graus and La Fresneda in Aragon, Sangüesa in Navarre and Sigüenza in Castile. On the opposite side, they note, the plan for that row grew from five models to seven, and one of them is Riaza's La Casona itself. See if you can find a front with columns. Alquézar, in Huesca, is on the Poble's list for this square too.

And remember Santillana del Mar, the famous town that let them down? It got in anyway. The Poble's own list has two Santillana houses on this square, the Casa de Águila and the Casa Barreda.

Now look at the slope, and the steps that cross the square. That's Riaza's idea. The square covers about two thousand square metres, and it can hold more than five thousand people for a concert. In 1968, a local history journal records, the village decided to hold a festival each year dedicated to one town.

The big arcaded building, house 21, is the town hall, modelled on the one in Valderrobres, in Aragon.

A square from Riaza, then, but only its shape. Its faces were borrowed from towns across the north and the centre of Spain.

Now walk over to the town hall, the big building with the arcade along its front, house 21. The Poble shows its photographs of the building site inside, if the door is open: Town hall · Valderrobres, and a building site.

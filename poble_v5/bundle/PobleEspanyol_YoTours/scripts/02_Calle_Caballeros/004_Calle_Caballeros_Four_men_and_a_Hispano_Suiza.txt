In July 1927 Miquel Utrillo, the engineer and painter leading the project, wrote to Lluís Plandiura, one of the Exhibition's leaders. He wrote, in Spanish, that the journey was "so indispensable that a good part of the work done this month will, logically, be changed". Changed "by the living things that the architects and our friend Nogués will see".

That September four men set off in one car, a Hispano-Suiza: Utrillo, the painter Xavier Nogués, and two architects, Francesc Folguera and Ramon Reventós. A month on the road. They passed through almost seven hundred towns, and stopped to study a hundred and thirty-seven. Two more trips followed. Across all three, they passed through something like sixteen hundred places.

They had a method. In small towns Nogués sketched the outline while the two architects stood ready with the camera. In the important towns they split into two teams, an architect and a painter in each, and then they voted on every find, marks out of ten. Colours were matched against scales of tone that Nogués had drawn up.

It wasn't all smooth. Near Zaragoza the car broke down, and Utrillo recorded the moment in a caricature. On the second trip, that November, a letter was written while they waited for the Zaragoza express. It says, in Catalan, "we have the car in Tudela being repaired, because the clutch burned out".

Did the trip matter? Of the models in the first plan, only nine survived into the final village, and only three unchanged.

Then the record of the journey went into a drawer. More than a hundred photographs and sketches, in five travel notebooks, lay hidden there, in a desk in a Reventós family office, until the researchers found them. They wrote: "These notebooks, which have remained hidden until today, constitute unpublished material." The city's photo archive also holds about a thousand negatives from that year.

Look up the street: this is what all that voting produced.

Walk on up Calle Caballeros, slowly, and play the next stop as you go. Look at the doorways and the coats of arms above them: Calle Caballeros · Coats of arms.

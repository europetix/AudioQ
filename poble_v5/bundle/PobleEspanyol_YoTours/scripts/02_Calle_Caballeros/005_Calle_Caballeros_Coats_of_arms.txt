On this street, a coat of arms over a door was more than decoration. The village's own family trail puts it, in Spanish: "the coats of arms on the façades showed the families' social class."

By the Poble's own list, this street is mostly Castile. There's the Casa del Doncel from Sigüenza, palaces from Ayllón and El Burgo de Osma, and houses from Segovia, Toro and Soria. All pink on the plan, for the Centre.

Two surprises interrupt the pink. Just off the start of the street, one row back, the plan shows a little purple, the Mediterranean colour. The Poble's list places two Mediterranean houses here, from Jérica, in Castellón, and the Desclergue house of Montblanc, in Catalonia. It also lists a northern one, green on the plan, from Cantabria: the house of the Marqués de Santillana.

Look closely at the walls as you pass. The Poble says the builders used "casts of real stones" to copy the textures and colours of each region. See if you can tell a cast from a real stone.

Then look up at the roofs. The Poble says "800,000 old tiles were brought from across the country".

You may pass workshops here: a blacksmith, stained glass, wooden toys. If a door is open, look in.

Keep going to where Calle Caballeros becomes Calle Príncipe de Viana, at houses 102 and 103, with the Travesía del Maestrazgo going off beside house 41. Stop there a few minutes: Calle Príncipe de Viana · The men who made it.

[amused] On the first trip, in 1927, the team stopped in the main square of Riaza, in Segovia, and Ramon Reventós wrote a note in Spanish: "Riaza, a sublime house, with extra-barbarous Ionic columns." They christened it La Casona, the big house.

This is the Plaza Mayor, the main square, and the Poble's own page calls it, in Spanish, a "replica of the Castilian square of Riaza". The historians who found the team's notebooks are more exact. The square they had in mind, with its changes of level, "they would find in the main square of Riaza", they write. Riaza's square has similar features, they add, "despite being round". So the layout and the levels came from Riaza. The fronts came from all over.

[warmly] Look round. On the side toward Plaza Castellana, the historians list town halls from Graus and La Fresneda in Aragon, Sangüesa in Navarre and Sigüenza in Castile. On the opposite side, they say, the row grew from five models to seven, and one of them is Riaza's La Casona itself. See if you can find a front with columns. Alquézar, in Huesca, is on this square too.

[wry] And remember Santillana del Mar, the famous town that let them down? It got in anyway. The Poble's own list has two Santillana houses on this square, the Casa de Águila and the Casa Barreda.

Now look at the slope, and the steps that cross the square. That's Riaza's idea. The square covers about two thousand square metres, and on concert nights more than five thousand people fill it. From 1968, a local history journal records, the village held a festival each year dedicated to one town.

The big arcaded building, house 21, is the town hall, modelled on the one in Valderrobres, in Aragon.

[pause] A square from Riaza, then, but only its shape. Its faces were borrowed from towns across the north and the centre of Spain.

Now walk over to the town hall, the big building with the arcade along its front, house 21. If its door is open, the photographs of the building site are inside: Town hall · Valderrobres, and a building site.

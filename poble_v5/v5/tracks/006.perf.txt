[amused] Nobody has ever quite agreed whose idea this was. The writer Carles Soldevila joked, in Catalan, that working out who deserved the glory was "as complicated as finding out the origins of the Great War".

The Poble's own history names four men. Miquel Utrillo, "engineer, painter and project leader". In the early 1920s he was already pitching a Spain-in-one-place idea to the mayor. He called it *Iberiona*. General Primo de Rivera's coup in 1923 froze it, and three years later Utrillo brought it back.

He had done something like it before, at Maricel in Sitges, for the American Charles Deering. A 1961 architecture journal called Maricel, in Spanish, "a kind of first Spanish Village, built with fairly authentic elements". [pause] Utrillo also gave his surname to the son of his Paris friend Suzanne Valadon: the painter Maurice Utrillo.

Xavier Nogués was the painter and draughtsman. Then two young architects. Francesc Folguera, who later laid out S'Agaró. And Ramon Reventós, who also designed the Teatre Grec on Montjuïc, in an old quarry. Reventós remembered, in Spanish, that "what really changed with our involvement was that the vague ideas about the layout of the streets became concrete." Look at the street ahead as it curves away. That layout is theirs.

The village was the last of the Exhibition's projects to be awarded, and it got a leftover plot.

[wry] In December 1929 a gossip column in the Catalan magazine *Mirador* began: "Now it turns out that the author of the Poble Espanyol was also, if not the first, one of the first excavators of Montjuïc." It meant Utrillo.

Reventós outlived the others. The 1961 journal called him "an inexhaustible archive of the history and the ups and downs" of the village.

You may read that Josep Puig i Cadafalch had the idea. He drew the Exhibition's overall plan. The village was the work of these four.

Carry on up Calle Príncipe de Viana, the curving street that climbs toward Aragon's brick tower. Play the next stop as you walk, and look at the houses on both sides: Calle Príncipe de Viana · Navarre and the Basque hills.

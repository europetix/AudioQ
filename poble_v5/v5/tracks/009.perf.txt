[curious] On paper, this was going to be a Baroque church. Then four men drove through Aragon, and the plan changed.

You heard the recipe at the tower. Here you can see it.

The researchers who studied the trip describe the result, in Spanish, as una simbiosis, a symbiosis. The church, they write, joins "the façade of the church of the Carmelites of Alcañiz" with "the tower of Utebo and a façade taken from Torralba de Ribota". Three towns, all in Aragon: Alcañiz in Teruel, Utebo near Zaragoza, and Torralba de Ribota near Calatayud.

[pause] So look at the church front, and then up at the brick tower. The Poble's own list places the Carmelite church of Alcañiz on this square. The Torralba front is close by, though no source we trust says exactly which wall it is. That's two of the three towns, perhaps all three, in a single glance.

Plaza Aragonesa, where the tower rises, the Plaza del Carmen, and this one, the Plaza de la Iglesia, or Church Square, are three small squares around a single church.

[warmly] A church that exists nowhere else, made from three real ones.

[curious] This house tells a story about a girl and a king.

The Tablao de Carmen has been "putting on an authentic Flamenco show since 1988", the Poble says. It was "created to pay tribute to the legendary flamenco dancer Carmen Amaya". The city's film office passes on the house's story. When the Exhibition opened in 1929, it says, "a very young Carmen Amaya danced" for King Alfonso the Thirteenth, in its courtyard. That, the story goes, is why it's called the Patio de Carmen.

[pause] It's a lovely story, and that listing is the only place we have found it. The King and Queen certainly opened the village that year.

About her, more is certain. A biography from Barcelona City Council calls her a Catalan bailaora, a flamenco dancer. It follows her "from her birth in the Somorrostro shantytown to her death in a village on the Costa Brava in 1963". In between came "her success in Argentina" and "her boundless fame in New York and Hollywood". The book calls her "the most famous Romani flamenco dancer ever".

[warmly] Look at the house: the Tablao de Carmen. The street itself comes from Arcos de la Frontera.

Walk on down Calle Arcos, which has steps. At its foot you come out by the brick tower, where the little Plaza del Carmen opens: Plaza del Carmen · War, prisoners and a folk museum.

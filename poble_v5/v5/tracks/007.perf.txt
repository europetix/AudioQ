[curious] The village's family trail sets a little game on this stretch. It asks families to compare three regions, in Spanish: "Castilla, Navarra y País Vasco". Castile, Navarre and the Basque Country. Castile was the pink you've just walked through. From here, on the plan, the houses turn mostly green, for the North.

And this is Navarre, the region of the burnt clutch. On the second trip, in November 1927, the car ended up in a repair shop in Tudela, a Navarrese town.

By the Poble's own list, the Navarrese houses along here include the palace of San Cristóbal, from Estella, and the Casa de las Cadenas, from Corella. There's a house from the Roncal valley, and carved pre-Romanesque reliefs too.

Then the Basque Country: a farmhouse, a caserío, from Bizkaia, and a house from Gipuzkoa. Look at the houses on both sides as you climb.

[pause] Keep an eye out for an arch. Architects who studied the village record, on this street, an arch from Amaiur, Maia in Basque, in the Baztán valley. You may read that it's Basque. In fact Amaiur is in Navarre.

If you have children with you, one side street is worth knowing about. The Poble's school guide says, in Catalan, that "eleven small sculptures are waiting for you" on the Bajada de Cervantes. It's a street of steps, off our route.

At the top, the street opens into Plaza Aragonesa, with the tall brick tower rising over it. Find a spot where you can see the whole tower: Plaza Aragonesa · The tower they liked better.

[curious] On the first of October 1927, near the end of a month on the road, the team's diary reads, in Catalan: "We set off on the last stage: Alcañiz, Valderrobres, Tortosa."

Valderrobres, Vall-de-roures in Catalan, is a hill town in Aragon. Its town hall is a Mannerist building, finished in 1599. That date is carved on the coat of arms on its front, and the coat of arms has two griffins, a male and a female. Look up: does the Poble's version carry a dated coat of arms too?

[pause] Because this isn't Valderrobres as it stands. The historians who found the team's notebooks write, in Spanish, that the reproduction is "clearly larger than the original", and that it stands on its own, where the original sits on a corner of its square. It was given a staircase from the monastery of Orihuela, and a ceiling on the main floor with the same interlaced pattern as the Audiencia of Valencia. The team even went to Valderrobres to check details and take measurements.

If the door is open, inside is "A photographic journey. The construction of Poble Espanyol": photographs, letters and drawings from the trips, the Poble says.

[warmly] The contract went to a company called Material y Obras, "with several votes against", as a Catalan study puts it. The budget was about two and a half million pesetas. Work began in January 1928. More than a thousand workers from across the country built it, the Poble says, and it opened the following May, about sixteen months later. A Madrid architecture journal later wrote that the structures and fronts were built "on an entirely provisional basis". A temporary set, then, but solid enough to survive. And, in the Poble's own words, "the first Exposition venue that could be visited, even before the event began."

Remember the notebooks that lay hidden in a drawer? They, and about a thousand negatives in the city's photographic archive, are how this story came back.

Come back out onto the Plaza Mayor and find somewhere to sit for a few minutes. The square's steps will do: Plaza Mayor · An irremediably trivial idea.

[curious] Someone from Rupit once stood where you're standing and did something very simple. They counted.

This is house 83, Can Banús, the Poble's version of a house in Rupit, a village in the province of Barcelona. Rupit gave the Poble eight houses in all. Four more, Can Betes, Can Vázquez, a house put together from several, and Can Lo, are numbers 79, 80, 81 and 82 on Carrer Mercaders, just above.

The writer, in Rupit's own local journal, put it like this, in Catalan: "In the Poble Espanyol the stair has seven steps, half as many as here in Rupit, which has fourteen."

[wry] Half a stair. And you'd never know, unless someone from Rupit told you.

So count them, from the bottom step to the top.

[pause] That small number is the best answer to anyone who calls this village a set of exact replicas. You saw the brick tower, cast in plaster and a little smaller than the real one in Utebo. Here the stair is halved. Each house is the Poble's version of its original, not a copy.

The same writer noticed something else. Little signs once named the houses and their towns. In the article's words, "over the years, the signs gradually disappeared." If you can't find one here today, now you know why, and you know its name anyway.

[warmly] Seven steps. Keep that number for the rest of the walk.

Now walk up Carrer Mercaders, the street of Rupit house fronts. Stop at house 81, where you may see, and hear, a glass furnace at work: Carrer Mercaders · The glassblower's house.

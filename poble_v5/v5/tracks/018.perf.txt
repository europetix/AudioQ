[curious] Andalusia, the region so many people picture when they think of Spain, was left off the map on purpose.

In 1929 Seville was holding its own Ibero-American Exhibition. So when the four makers planned their first long journey, the historians write, they left out Andalusia and Murcia, so as not to compete. The islands were left out at first, too.

Then the King took an interest. Alfonso XIII, write the researchers who found the team's notebooks, in Spanish, "asked about the Andalusian representation" and "made it plain how much it would be missed". Exactly when he said it, the sources don't agree.

[pause] In May 1928, with building already under way here, the team set off again: a third trip, twenty-one days in the south. An Andalusian quarter was added and moved within the plan, with what the historians call "whitewashed walls, and interiors grouped round courtyards". Murcia came in too.

[warmly] Look round you. The Poble's own description, in Spanish, promises "typical streets with little white houses and blue flowerpots". See if you can spot the blue pots. Then look for a front with three balconies. From the Casa-Palacio García de Veas, in Arcos de la Frontera, "the façade and the three balconies have been recreated", the Poble says. The two side ones have enclosed railings, the middle one a railing of wrought iron. On the original, in Cádiz, the front carries seven family names, García and Veas among them.

The Poble's list adds Úbeda, Córdoba, Écija and Ronda: five Andalusian provinces in all. So it's the Andalusian quarter, never the Sevillian one.

Since February 2024 the Vidre art gallery at 67 and 68, "the iconic former glassblowing workshop" in the Poble's words, has shown contemporary art.

[pause] The south came last. It came because a king asked where it was.

There's a short Extra on Calle Arcos, at the flamenco house, the Tablao de Carmen, house 61, number 9 on the street: go through Plaza Peñaflor and down Calle Arcos. The next main stop is the Plaza del Carmen, at the foot of the brick tower: Plaza del Carmen · War, prisoners and a folk museum.

[curious] Picasso, Miró and Dalí, in a village of plaster casts and borrowed fronts. And the man who gathered them came from the business of bottling Coca-Cola.

The name on the door, Fran Daurel, is short for its founder, Francesc Daurella i Franco. His family's company, Cobega, held the Coca-Cola concession, and in time he became its president. His foundation opened here, in the Poble Espanyol, in December 2001.

The museum's own page counts more than three hundred works by more than eighty artists. Among them: Picasso, Miró, Dalí, Tàpies, Barceló and Chillida.

[pause] It singles out a few. From Picasso, twenty ceramic pieces, with two etchings and an engraving from the 1960s. From Dalí, a bronze from the 1970s called Alice au pays des merveilles, Alice in Wonderland, and an ink drawing, Cavalier avec son aurige, a horseman with his charioteer. From Miró, a selection of lithographs and engravings from his last years. What hangs on the walls can change, but the Poble's guide for school groups still promises, in Catalan, "works by Tàpies, Dalí or Miró". The same foundation also has a sculpture garden, up by the monastery at the top of the hill.

Now look at the museum's front. On the official plan the houses are coloured by region: green, pink, yellow or purple. This block is grey on the plan, outside the four region colours.

[warmly] And here's the thing to hold on to. The village itself is twentieth-century work. Its houses were chosen on the road, marked out of ten and sketched in notebooks, and the whole place went up in about sixteen months. The tower was cast in plaster. So the modern art is less out of place than it looks. It sits in a village that is, itself, a modern work.

From here, go back along the church to Plaza Aragonesa and start up Carrer de la Davallada, the street that climbs into the Catalan quarter. Stop at house 83, a few doors up, before Carrer Mercaders comes in. It's the house with an outside stair up its front: Carrer de la Davallada · Seven steps, not fourteen.

[curious] There's a glass furnace inside a house from Rupit. And the house itself is a blend.

Rupit's own local journal places a composite Rupit house here, at number 81 on Carrer Mercaders. It was put together from pieces of several houses in Rupit: Can Rovira and Can Sallent, and the doorway of a third, Ca l'Estragués. Even here, in a village of versions, this is a version of several.

[pause] Today it's the glassblower's house. The Poble's own page describes the glassblower as "a professional with over 45 years of experience in the craft." If the door is open, look in. Watch for the glow of the furnace and the long blowpipe, and listen to the work.

This quarter is where the village works. "Every day," the Poble says, "more than 15 artisans hone their craft in the enclosure's workshops." Its family trail says it too, in Spanish: "In the Catalonia area there's a great concentration of artisans." The crafts on the Poble's current list include pottery on the wheel, stained glass, guitar making, blacksmithing, embroidery and wooden toys.

[warmly] Crafts were part of the idea from the start. A 2011 architecture study says the first idea risked looking, in its Spanish phrase, "like a sordid archaeological exhibition". The makers moved toward something else: a modest, living village, one that could hold crafts.

So the furnace isn't decoration. It's the living village they hoped for.

Carry on up Carrer Mercaders to the square at the top, Plaça de la Font, with its fountain: Plaça de la Font · The Mediterranean.

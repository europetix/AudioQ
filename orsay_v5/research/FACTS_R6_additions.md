**Works whose artwork pages say "Rez-de-chaussée, Salle 11" (OFF):** *Une moderne Olympia*, *Le Christ aux limbes*, *Les Accessoires de Cézanne. Nature morte au médaillon de Philippe Solari*, *La Maison du docteur Gachet à Auvers*, *Dahlias dans un grand vase de Delft*. Source: musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/une-moderne-olympia-1478, …/le-christ-aux-limbes-136066, …/les-accessoires-de-cezanne-nature-morte-au-medaillon-de-philippe-solari-1484, …/la-maison-du-docteur-gachet-auvers-1479, …/dahlias-dans-un-grand-vase-de-delft-1481 (OFF).
Recommended pair: **Le Christ aux limbes** (the earliest one, from the family house) + **Une moderne Olympia** (Auvers, first Impressionist show).

#### Cézanne — *Le Christ aux limbes* (Christ in Limbo)
- c. **1869** · oil on plaster, **transferred to canvas** · **168.5 × 100.5 cm** · inv. no. not seen. Room 11 (OFF).
- Origin (OFF, works-in-focus "christ-in-limbo-19038"): in the 1860s Cézanne painted murals to decorate the family house, the **Jas de Bouffan** near Aix-en-Provence. This one was in the **salon** of his father's house. **A year after the artist's death** the new owner had some of the paintings taken off the walls and put on canvas, meaning to give them to the State. **The Musée du Luxembourg refused the gift.** The pieces were then gradually scattered.
- What you see (OFF): it copies an **engraving after Sebastiano del Piombo** (painting in the Prado). Christ goes down into Limbo, where the righteous of the Old Testament wait for Redemption. The figures **bottom left are probably Adam and Eve**.
- Hooks: a wall painting cut off a wall; a gift the State refused; the young Cézanne copying an old master from a print.
- Related OFF video: "Quand Paul Cézanne voulait peindre les murs" (musee-orsay.fr/en/node/276905), not read.

#### Cézanne — *Une moderne Olympia* (A Modern Olympia)
- **1873–1874** · oil on canvas · **46.2 × 55.5 cm** · **gift of Paul Gachet, 1951** · Room 11 (OFF, artwork page 1478). Inv. no. not seen.
- Story (OFF): an earlier version (**1870**) already answered **Manet's Olympia**, the scandal of the **1865 Salon**. This second version was painted while Cézanne stayed with **Doctor Gachet at Auvers-sur-Oise**: "in the heat of a discussion" he picked up a brush and made this coloured sketch.
- What you see (OFF): bright, dazzling colour and a brilliant touch that recalls **Fragonard**. The naked woman is uncovered by her **Black servant**. A **man in black**, who looks strangely like **Cézanne himself**, watches like a spectator. The scene is erotic and theatrical.
- Reception (OFF): shown at the **first Impressionist exhibition, 1874**, it was "scorned by both public and critics".
- Hooks: an argument at the doctor's table turned into a painting; Cézanne puts himself in the audience of his own Olympia; a reply to Manet (whose Olympia the listener may already have heard about in room 14).
- Note: this is from 1873–74, not the 1860s. The room's theme is "débuts", so say "early" and not "1860s".

#### Other early Cézanne facts (OFF, usable as context; rooms not confirmed)
- *L'Avocat (l'oncle Dominique)*, **1866**, oil, 65 × 54.5 cm: his maternal uncle **Dominique Aubert, a bailiff**, posed as a lawyer. In 1866 Cézanne painted a series of him with paint laid on thick, in ridges, often with the palette knife according to general literature (the "knife" detail is NOT confirmed). Cézanne later called this manner **"couillarde"** ("ballsy"). Source: artworks/lavocat-loncle-dominique-78698 + "Portraits by Cézanne" exhibition page (OFF). Room not given.
- *Achille Emperaire* (painting, 1867–1868): **refused by the Salon in 1870**, because it broke the time-honoured rules of portraiture of the late Second Empire (OFF, "Portraits by Cézanne" exhibition text). Room NOT found; the room-11 hits are other works. Do not place it in room 11.

#### Degas — *Portrait de famille*, also called *La Famille Bellelli* (The Bellelli Family)
- **1858–1869** · oil on canvas · **201 × 249.5 cm** · **RF 2210** (pop.culture.gouv.fr Joconde 000PE003801, OFF). The **largest finished canvas Degas ever painted** (OFF, restoration video page 219295).
- Provenance (OFF, works in focus 7168): bought by the **Musée du Luxembourg** at the **first Degas studio sale, Galerie Georges Petit, Paris, 6–8 May 1918**, "with the help of the count and countess of Fels and thanks to René de Gas" (his brother). Luxembourg → **Louvre 1929** → **Jeu de Paume 1947–1986** → **Orsay 1986**. (Joconde notes a sale "Hôtel Drouot, April 1918"; the Orsay commentary's Georges Petit May 1918 version is preferred.) Joconde also gives a showing at the **1867 Salon** (OFF).
- Sitters (OFF): painted while Degas finished his training in Italy, **aged 22 to 26**. His **father's sister Laure**, her husband **Baron Gennaro Bellelli (1812–1864)**, an **Italian patriot banished from Naples and living in exile in Florence**, and their daughters **Giulia and Giovanna**. Laure **wears mourning for her father, Hilaire** (Degas's grandfather). **His portrait hangs in a frame on the wall, close to her face.**
- What you see (OFF): the mother "impressively dignified", with a slightly stern authority; the father turned away and distant. Big size, **sober colours**, and open perspectives make the room feel oppressive. The picture "evokes the family tensions isolating each member". It recalls Flemish family portraits, **van Dyck** above all.
- Restoration (OFF, node 287266 / article 219295): no major treatment for 40 years. **Four large old tears**, mended with patches on the back and filler. Cleaning brought back light and colour. It was shown restored beside **Manet's *Le Balcon*** in "Manet/Degas" (2023).
- Studio sales (OFF): Degas had asked that works not worth keeping be burned. Instead **eight sales** (1918–1919) revealed how much he had kept, above all his drawings.
- **LOAN RISK (HIGH):** press for the Tokyo Metropolitan Art Museum show "What a Wonderful World: Masterworks from the Musée d'Orsay" (**14 Nov 2026 – 28 Mar 2027**, tobikan.jp, SEC1) are mixed in search extracts with press for the earlier National Museum of Western Art show "Impressionist Interiors" (2025–26), which named the Bellelli as **"shown for the first time in Japan"** (asianews.network / artprice, SEC2 for that earlier show). R3 also found a japantravel snippet naming the Bellelli for the Nov 2026 show. **Assume it may be away from 14 Nov 2026.** Write the track so that it can run on another room-13 work (Sémiramis or Pagans) with a Bellelli section that can be cut.
- Hooks: the dead grandfather watching from the wall; a family split in two by the composition (father apart); the painting Degas kept rolled up in his studio until his death [only "stayed in Degas's possession until the 1918 sale" is verified; "rolled up" is NOT verified].

#### Back-up works in room 13 (OFF)
- *Sémiramis construisant Babylone*: begun c. **1860**, oil, **151.5 × 258 cm**, **stayed in Degas's studio until his death**. The queen of Assyria, founder of Babylon, stands on the Euphrates bank watching the city go up. The chariot at right and her hairdo seem taken from **Assyrian reliefs newly arrived at the Louvre**. **Piero della Francesca** (seen in **Arezzo**, late 1850s) is a more likely model. Figures drawn **nude first, then draped**, the Ingres way. Source: works in focus "semiramis-building-babylon" (OFF). The young Degas trying to be a history painter.
- *Lorenzo Pagans et Auguste de Gas*: **1871–1872**, oil, **54.5 × 39.5 cm**. Degas's father, a banker, held musical **"Mondays"** in the 1860s, favouring early music (**Bach, Rameau, Gluck**). Here he listens to the Spanish tenor **Lorenzo Pagans (1838–1883)**. Degas's sister **Marguerite** was a fine singer; Opéra orchestra players came too (OFF, artwork page 9996).

### (c) Level 2 · room 51 "Salle des fêtes"

- Location: **"Niveau médian, Salle 51 – Salle des fêtes"** (OFF, artwork pages of the Fritel décor).
- The hotel (OFF, history page node/60, "La gare"): Laloux wrapped the station on two sides with a **luxury hotel of 400 rooms**, with a **restaurant and a salle des fêtes**. Station and hotel were **inaugurated 14 July 1900** for the Exposition universelle. The décor mixes all the styles of French classicism, **Louis XIV to Louis XV**: stone, staff (plaster), ornamental cast iron. Hotel names on Orsay pages: **"Hôtel Terminus"** and **"hôtel du Palais d'Orsay"**.
- **De Gaulle (OFF + SEC2):** "On **19 May 1958**, it was in the salle des fêtes of the hotel that General de Gaulle held the press conference announcing his return to power" (Orsay history page, OFF). The Fondation Charles de Gaulle and INA (SEC2) call the place the **"Palais d'Orsay"**. **About 500 journalists** (INA/Fondation). Aim: to explain his declaration of **15 May** that he was ready to take on "the powers of the Republic", and to reassure people who feared a dictatorship (Fondation, SEC2).
  - The famous line, as INA titles it ("Charles de Gaulle et la carrière de dictateur") and the Fondation gives it in English: **"Why do you want me, at 67, to start a career as a dictator?"** (SEC2, INA ina.fr/ina-eclaire-actu/video/i00012921 + charles-de-gaulle.org). Usual French wording: « Pourquoi voulez-vous qu'à 67 ans, je commence une carrière de dictateur ? » The French text was NOT seen verbatim in a snippet, so give it in English as reported speech, or check INA before quoting it.
  - Nuance: he did not "return to power" that day. He spoke as a would-be leader; he was invested later (1 June 1958 speech at the Assemblée, assemblee-nationale.fr, SEC1). Say "the press conference that opened his return to power".
- Hotel closed **1 January 1973** (OFF).
- **Restored, not rebuilt (OFF):** "an authentic room from the old hotel, **not reconstructed but restored**". It evokes the life of a château and the splendour of the **Galerie des Glaces at Versailles**. Period rooms were otherwise excluded from the museum design; the ballroom is the exception. The museum photographer Jim Purcell recorded it **before restoration** and during the **floor repair (c. 1985)** (OFF, artwork records 155575, 127028).
- **What the visitor sees (OFF):** mirrors, **chandeliers** (Purcell record "Lustres de la salle des fêtes"); **painted décor by Pierre Fritel (1853–1942), 1900**, ordered by the **Compagnie des chemins de fer d'Orléans**, oil on canvas fixed to plaster: four **trapezoid panels on the east and west walls** (e.g. south-west ***La Musique***; themes include Music, Poetry and Dance) and four **triangular panels on the north and south walls** (e.g. north-west ***L'Été***). One panel is about **250 × 320 cm** (top width). Acquired/registered **1986**. **Adrien Moreau-Néret** painted ***Amours et guirlandes de fleurs*** in the **antechamber** (c. 1900). Gabriel Ferrier's ***Les Quatre Saisons*** is the ceiling of the **dining room** (now the Restaurant), not of the ballroom.
- Sculptures shown in the room in 2026: NOT found. During school holidays the room hosts family workshops (OFF). Check on site.
- Hooks: a ballroom inside a railway station; Versailles mirrors for train travellers; 500 journalists and one quip about dictatorship in the same gilt room.

### (d) Level 5 · room 46 "Le cabaret du Chat noir"

- Since the **10 September 2019** reopening of the **nine renovated post-Impressionist rooms on level 5**, "the performing arts are represented with a choice of **zinc silhouettes that were projected at the Cabaret du Chat Noir**, where creators from all horizons met" (OFF, "Salles post-impressionnistes" article 17592). The plan-guide names the room 46 (OFF).
- The cabaret: founded by **Rodolphe Salis** in **November 1881** at **84 boulevard Rochechouart**, at the foot of Montmartre. Later moved to a grander house on **rue de Laval**, with a **shadow theatre** (BnF Gallica "Le Chat noir, une sombre histoire" + data.bnf.fr, SEC1 BnF; Orsay agrees on Salis and "foot of Montmartre", OFF). The Orsay 1992 exhibition is titled "Le Cabaret du Chat Noir (**1881–1897**)" (OFF). Its weekly journal ran **1882–1897** (BnF). Salis created, ran and hosted the cabaret (OFF).
- Three things it is famous for (OFF, 1992 exhibition text): its **journal** (more than ten years, a key link in the history of comic strips); the **chanson** of the chansonniers, "really born at the Chat Noir"; and the **shadow theatre**, where **Henri Rivière** took the technique to its peak, with **45 plays**.
- Key object: the **cut and painted zinc silhouettes, 1886–1896** (OFF). Example: Rivière's silhouette of himself, "Henri Rivière (1864–1951), dessinateur, fondateur et directeur du théâtre d'ombres" (artwork 123419); also Henry Somm, Victorien Sardou. Which silhouettes hang in room 46 now: not found.
- How the show worked (OFF, "Cinq choses à savoir sur Henri Rivière", node 282042): Rivière was **22** when Salis gave him the new shadow theatre (**1886**). Moving zinc figures were lit onto a screen against **coloured backdrops made with colour filters**, with texts read to music and **wind and smoke effects**. Orsay has Rivière's **photographs, 1887–1894**, of stagehands sliding **zinc figures for Caran d'Ache's "L'Épopée"** behind the screen (artwork 22094; gift of Henriette Guy-Loë and Geneviève Noufflard, **1986**). In **March 1889** Rivière photographed the **Eiffel Tower** from its top with Chat Noir friends (OFF).
- Hooks: a cabaret run by a showman, with a cinema before cinema; Caran d'Ache's Napoleonic epic in zinc; the stagehands photographed behind the screen.

### (e) Level 5 · room 47 "Cinéma (1895–1914)"

- Official text (OFF, "Le cinéma dans le parcours permanent / Cinema in the Orsay collections", node 413): since the museum opened in **1986** a room has been devoted to the **birth of the cinématographe in 1895**. Display cases hold **thaumatropes, phenakistiscopes, stereoscopes, a magic lantern and a projection lantern, a praxinoscope-théâtre**, next to **chronophotographs by Étienne-Jules Marey**, the **zinc silhouettes of the Chat Noir**, and a **"Lumière column"** where visitors can view **autochromes** (Lumière colour photographs).
- **Since 2019 (OFF):** about **fifteen films or excerpts** are projected on **three screens**, with **"sound showers"** (directional sound). Themes taken from the history of the medium will change, to show the main stages of its invention, **1895–1914**.
- **Location conflict:** that same page still says the space is at the "median level, rue de Lille side" (the 1986 layout); the 2019 text says "southern rooms". The **summer 2026 plan-guide puts it on level 5, room 47** (OFF). Follow the plan-guide; check on site what is in which case.
- Key object: not named in any official extract. Safest choice: the **three screens of early films**. Optical toys and the Lumière autochrome column are confirmed as being in the space but not confirmed as on show in 2026.
- Context (OFF): Orsay holds Lumière material (cinematographic plates, contact prints, "photogrammes"). The 2021 exhibition "Enfin le cinéma ! Arts, images et spectacles en France (1833–1907)" featured Lumière, **Alice Guy** and **Méliès**.
- Hooks: a museum that ends its 19th century on moving pictures; the shadow theatre next door as the step before film.

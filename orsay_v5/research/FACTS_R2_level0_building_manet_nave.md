### 001 · The building: Gare d'Orsay → Musée d'Orsay (welcome)
- **Station.** Built for the **1900 Exposition universelle**. Victor Laloux, who had just finished the Tours town hall, was **chosen in 1898**. The station and its hotel were built in two years and **inaugurated 14 July 1900** (OFF, history node/60, the-architecture page). 138 m long; 12,000 t of metal (AUDIT A1, OFF).
- **The disguise.** Laloux used a metal structure but **hid it behind an envelope of cut stone** in an eclectic style, so it would sit well beside the Louvre and the Tuileries (OFF).
- **Verbatim quote (OFF, history page):** the painter Édouard Detaille wrote in 1900: **"The station is superb and looks like a Palais des beaux-arts…"**. Use only this half. The joke that often follows it was **not** found verbatim.
- **Terminus 1900–1939** for the south-west lines. From 1939 it served only the suburbs, because electrification and longer trains had made its platforms too short (OFF).
- **Later life (OFF, history page):**
  - During the war it was a dispatch centre for parcels to prisoners.
  - At the Liberation, from **April 1945**, it was the arrival point for prisoners, deportees and STO workers coming back from Germany (OFF; the museum has held an exhibition on "Gare d'Orsay in spring 1945").
  - It was a film set, including **Orson Welles's The Trial** (after Kafka).
  - It was a temporary home for the **Renaud-Barrault theatre company**.
  - It housed **auctioneers while the Hôtel Drouot was rebuilt**.
- **Near-demolition.** A building permit for a big international hotel was **refused in February 1971**. The station's hotel **closed on 1 Jan 1973**. The station was put on the supplementary list of Historic Monuments on **8 March 1973**. The demolition of Baltard's Halles (1971) changed official attitudes to 19th-century buildings (OFF, De la gare au musée PDF + history page; Britannica for the Halles link → SEC2).
- **The decision.** An interministerial council decided on the museum on **20 Oct 1977**, "at the initiative of" **Valéry Giscard d'Estaing**. In 1978 the building was **classified** as a historic monument and the public body for the museum was created (OFF). The idea of Pompidou as originator is not official (AUDIT A1: hedge or drop).
- **Architects.** **ACT-Architecture** (Renaud Bardon, Pierre Colboc, Jean-Paul Philippon) won the competition in **1979**, chosen from six proposals. Their brief was to respect Laloux while adapting the building. The **interior design** was led by **Gae Aulenti** (OFF; "six proposals" from Britannica → SEC1/OFF-consistent). The year of Aulenti's appointment (often given as 1980) was **not** confirmed.
- **Opening.** **Inaugurated 1 Dec 1986** by President François Mitterrand; **opened to the public 9 Dec 1986** (OFF). An archive photo shows Françoise Cachin, first director, greeting the first visitor (OFF, artwork 151917).
- **2026.** It is the 40th anniversary year ("Orsay à 40 ans", OFF magazine). Building work runs 2026–28 and the museum stays open (AUDIT A1: no ticket or entrance talk in the audio).
- **Hooks:** (1) a station dressed as a palace, and Detaille's line; (2) the railway terminus that was almost knocked down for a hotel in 1971; (3) in 1945 this hall saw returning deportees before it held paintings.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/node/60 (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/history-of-the-museum/the-architecture.html (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/sites/default/files/2021-07/De_La_Gare_Au_Musee.pdf (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/whats-on/exhibitions/gare-dorsay-spring-1945-scene-return-rapatriates (OFF) · https://www.britannica.com/topic/Musee-dOrsay (SEC)

### 002 · The nave: how the building works (levels 0 / 2 / 5)
- **Layout (OFF, the-architecture page):** the museum is organised on **three levels**. On the ground floor, galleries sit on either side of the **central nave**. The nave is overlooked by the **terraces of the median level**, which open onto more galleries. The great hall is the **"main artery of the visit"**.
- **Materials:** **Buxy stone** in the nave; **travertine** to match the façade stone (OFF).
- **What sits where (OFF, as compiled in AUDIT G):**
  - Level 0: central sculpture aisle; academic painting on the Lille side; Realism and Manet on the Seine side; the Opéra space, "Paris, capitale" and the Gates of Hell at the far end.
  - Level 2: sculpture terraces, Art Nouveau, Nabis, and now "Painting in Europe around 1900" (room 69).
  - Level 5: Impressionists (rooms 29 onwards), Van Gogh (36–37) and the Galerie Françoise Cachin.
- **Far-end axis:** since 24 May 2022, Rodin's Gates of Hell stand **between the two towers at the far end of the nave, on the axis of the central aisle**, visible as you come in (OFF via AUDIT G/A2).
- **Hooks:** the platforms became the sculpture aisle; standing in the aisle you can see the Gates of Hell at the far end.
- Sources: as for 001; https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/articles/new-presentation-gates-hell-202408 (OFF, via audit)

### 014 · Manet — *Olympia*
- **1863** · oil on canvas · **130.5 × 191 cm** · **RF 644** (OFF).
- **Provenance:** shown at the **Salon of 1865**, where it caused a scandal. In **Nov 1890** a group of subscribers **led by Claude Monet** gave it to the State, and it went to the Musée du Luxembourg. **"Nearly a hundred people"** subscribed: artists, collectors, dealers, writers, politicians (OFF). It later went to the Louvre and then to Orsay in 1986. The Louvre date (1907) was not re-checked this round.
- **Display:** **room 14, level 0** (OFF via AUDIT A1/G). No 2026 loan found. Not in the published Tokyo highlights; full list not seen.
- **What the visitor sees (OFF commentary):**
  - A nude, the model **Victorine Meurent** (1844–1928), looking straight at the viewer, "calculating".
  - A **black servant holding a bouquet** at the centre.
  - The **black cat** pushed to the right, where Titian had a dog. The museum's description, verbatim: "probably the most famous cat in the history of painting".
  - Manet quotes **Titian's Venus of Urbino**, **Goya's Maja desnuda** and the odalisque-with-servant tradition, but turns it into contemporary Paris prostitution. "Venus has become a prostitute."
- **Context (OFF):** Orsay's own commentary notes the servant's "invisibility" in the old reading. The 2019 exhibition "Le modèle noir" gave the model, **Laure**, back her name (AUDIT A1, OFF).
- **Quotes:** "female gorilla" and Baudelaire's "first in the decrepitude of your art" were confirmed by AUDIT A1 as consistent with the record, but the exact source wording was not re-checked here. Use with attribution and no other embellishment.
- **Hooks:** (1) the cat the museum calls the most famous in painting; (2) Monet passing the hat round, about 100 donors, to buy Olympia for France in 1890; (3) Laure's name restored in 2019.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/olympia-712 (OFF) · https://m.musee-orsay.fr/en/works/commentaire_id/olympia-7087.html (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/whats-on/exhibitions/hundred-years-ago-olympia (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/program/whats-on/exhibitions/accro-chat-ge (OFF)

### 015a · Manet — *Le Fifre* (The Fifer)
- **1866** · oil on canvas · **160.5 × 97 cm** · **RF 1992** · **bequest of Count Isaac de Camondo, 1911** (OFF).
- **Display:** **room 14, level 0** (OFF via AUDIT G).
- **What the visitor sees (OFF):** an **ordinary, unnamed boy from a military band**, treated "with the dignity of a Spanish grandee". Flat areas of colour and thick streaks of white. There is no setting: the figure stands against a plain ground.
- **Story (OFF):** Manet first went to Spain and the Prado in **1865**. There he was struck by **Velázquez's Pablo de Valladolid**, which he called to Fantin-Latour **"the most astonishing piece of painting ever done"** (OFF, as given in the museum's English text; it is a translation, so frame it as "he wrote to Fantin-Latour that…"). Back in Paris he painted Le Fifre. It was **rejected by the 1866 Salon jury**, and it is one of the works that fired **Zola's** enthusiasm for Manet (OFF).
- **Hooks:** a nobody painted like a king; rejected in 1866, and its rejection made Zola a champion.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/le-fifre-709 (OFF) · Manet/Velázquez exhibition page (OFF)

### 015b · Manet — *Émile Zola*
- **1868** · oil on canvas · **146 × 114 cm** · given in **1918 by Mme Émile Zola, reserving a life interest** (OFF). The RF number was not found.
- **Display:** **room 14, level 0** (OFF via AUDIT G). Shown at the **1868 Salon** (OFF).
- **Why it exists (OFF):** Manet offered the portrait **to thank Zola for his written defence**. The sittings took place in Manet's studio, **rue Guyot**.
- **What the visitor sees (OFF commentary):**
  - Zola is seated at his work table holding a book, probably **Charles Blanc's Histoire des peintres**. There is an inkwell and a quill.
  - On the wall: a **reproduction of Olympia**, which Zola thought Manet's best work. Behind it, a print after **Velázquez's Bacchus**, and a **Japanese print of a wrestler by Utagawa Kuniaki II**.
  - A **Japanese screen** on the left.
  - On the table, Zola's **pamphlet on Manet with a blue cover**. It **carries Manet's signature**, which serves as the signature of the painting (OFF).
- **The pamphlet:** an 1867 brochure reprinting Zola's article defending Manet (OFF). The museum's English extract names the original journal as "La Revue du XXe siècle", which is evidently a typo for the 19th century. Do **not** name the journal or date the article; say "a pamphlet Zola wrote defending Manet".
- **Fantin link (OFF):** a Zola line is quoted on the Fantin page (see 016).
- **Hooks:** (1) a thank-you painting; (2) find the blue pamphlet, which carries Manet's name; (3) Olympia hangs inside the painting, as a picture within a picture, in the same room as the real one.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/emile-zola-713 (OFF) · works-in-focus "emile-zola-3060" (OFF)

  - **Manet** at the easel.

The painting's title was not captured; check before naming it. Orsay also holds the oil sketch and drawn studies, one of 1866 annotated by Fantin about Scholderer's pose (OFF).

  - 1871 · 144.3 × 163 cm · state purchase 1891 (OFF).
  - Home: **room 69, level 2**, "La peinture en Europe autour de 1900", with Fantin-Latour (OFF).
  - **On loan:** Tate Britain 21 May – 27 Sep 2026, then Van Gogh Museum, Amsterdam **16 Oct 2026 – 10 Jan 2027** (SEC2). **Not on view in Paris until at least mid-Jan 2027, and then on level 2, not level 0.**

### 017 · MNR room 10b — « À qui appartiennent ces œuvres ? »
- **Opened 5 May 2026**, **room 10b, level 0, at the back of the nave** (OFF).
- **What MNRs are (OFF):** works recovered in Germany and Austria after WWII. Most were sold by the State in the early 1950s; **about 2,200** were entrusted to the national museums. These are the MNR (Musées Nationaux Récupération).
  - Ministry figures (AUDIT A2, OFF): about 100,000 works spoliated in France; about 60,000 returned to France; about 45,000 restituted; about 13,000 sold; 2,027 MNR today.
- **At Orsay (OFF):** **15 restituted in 30 years**; **225 still held**. That is mostly because the provenance is incomplete. Some have been shown **not** to be stolen, so there is nothing to restitute.
- **Display (OFF):** **13 works** at present, 12 paintings and 1 sculpture (AUDIT A2). The room is designed for **rotating displays**, so do not hard-wire the list. The backs of some paintings are visible (AUDIT A2, OFF).
- **Works named in sources (check against the current display):**
  - Rodin, *Éternel printemps* (OFF).
  - Degas, *Souper au bal*, 1879, a free copy after Menzel, MNR 226, once owned by Fernand Ochsé. Ochsé and his wife, the sculptor **Louise** Ochsé, were deported in July 1944 (AUDIT A2: SEC2).
  - Alfred Stevens, an 1891 beach scene acquired for Hitler's planned Linz museum (AUDIT A2: SEC2).
- **Hooks:** the question in the title; the visible backs (labels, stamps) as evidence; "not every gap means a theft".
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/node/345503 (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/magazine/2026-07-10/comprendre-les-oeuvres-mnr-en-5-minutes (OFF, title only) · AUDIT A2 §14

### 019 · Carpeaux — *La Danse* (stone original)
- **1869** · **Échaillon stone** · **420 × 298 × 145 cm**, **18,000 kg** (OFF). The inventory number was not found.
- **Display:** **level 0, "Allée centrale des sculptures"** (OFF artwork page). **This is the original stone from the Opéra façade, not a plaster.** Orsay's related plasters are a sketch, a half-size model (RF 818) and the Génie de la Danse busts. None is a life-size group (AUDIT A2, OFF). The original plaster model is "non localisé"; a 1930 cast is in Valenciennes (OFF notice 5713).
- **Story (OFF):**
  - In **1863** Charles Garnier commissioned **four groups from four Prix de Rome winners** for the façade of the new Opéra. Carpeaux took Dance and worked through sketches and models for three years.
  - The result is a **swirling ring of women round the Genius of Dance**: six bacchantes and a putto (AUDIT A2: SEC2).
  - At the 1869 unveiling there was an outcry at its **realism and nudity**. **A bottle of ink was thrown at it on the night of 26–27 August 1869** (OFF).
  - The 1870 war, the fall of the Empire and Carpeaux's death in 1875 saved it from removal (OFF).
  - **In 1964** a copy replaced it on the façade (OFF: "by Paul Belmondo"; AUDIT A2: carved by **Jean Juge** under Belmondo). The original went to the **Louvre**, then **Orsay in 1986** (OFF).
- **Dates:** the Opéra opened 5 Jan 1875; Carpeaux died 12 Oct 1875 (AUDIT A2, OFF).
- **Hooks:** (1) the ink bottle; (2) the group on the Opéra today is a copy, and the original is here; (3) the Opéra space at the end of the nave lets the listener connect it with the Opéra model.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/la-danse-1704 (OFF) · works-in-focus la-danse-7092 (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/collections/catalogue-des-oeuvres/notice.html?nnumid=5713 (OFF) · https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/objects/la-danse-carpeauxs-sculpture-for-the-facade-of-the-opera-garnier (SEC)

### 021 · *Opéra de Paris, maquette de la coupe longitudinale* (Garnier cut-away model)
- Made in **1985** under Peduzzi's artistic direction by the **Gianese workshop in Rome** (Ercole Borsani, Amedeo Brogli, Giovanni Gianese). It is based on Garnier's drawing in *Le nouvel Opéra de Paris* (published **1880**), at **1/25 scale**. **H 240 × L 578 × D 110 cm**. Gift of 1989 (OFF).
- **Mediation panel (OFF):** since **19 June 2023**, a **5.60 m** universally accessible panel stands in front of the model. It has French and English texts, braille, and 18 tactile visuals. It is in four parts: the overall cross-section; façade, foyer and grand staircase; the auditorium; backstage. It is Orsay's **first** universally accessible station.
- **Purpose (OFF):** with the other works, it explains how Garnier's building works, shows decoration such as Carpeaux's La Danse, and, through Degas, the life of the corps de ballet.
- **⚠** The Peduzzi exhibition (see 020) is on until 17 Jan 2027.
- **Hooks:** a whole opera house sliced open lengthwise, from façade to backstage; its four sections follow the panel.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/opera-de-paris-maquette-de-la-coupe-longitudinale-89419 (OFF) · https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/articles/new-mediation-station-front-model-paris-opera-house-219776 (OFF)

### 043 · Van Gogh: rooms 36–37 (intro: Paris, Arles, Auvers)

- Sources: musee-orsay.fr/en/articles/van-gogh-new-presentation-artists-works-musee-dorsay-277393 (OFF); …/leglise-dauvers-sur-oise-vue-du-chevet-755 (OFF).

### 044 · Van Gogh — Portrait de l'artiste (Self-Portrait), 1889
- 1889, painted at **Saint-Rémy-de-Provence** (OFF extract). Oil on canvas, **65 × 54.2 cm** (OFF). Inventory: the extract did not show it. RF 1949 17 is commonly given (WEAK, unverified), so check it.
- Provenance: **collection of Dr Paul Gachet**, Auvers-sur-Oise. Accepted by the State in **1949** as a gift from **Paul and Marguerite Gachet**, for the Jeu de Paume (OFF: …/portrait-de-lartiste-747). Earlier guides said it was the portrait sent to his mother. **That is wrong** (AUDIT A2 15.2, OFF CONTRA). Under the gift terms it may be lent only to restricted institutions (AUDIT A2 15.2; wording not re-seen, so hedge).
- Display: room 36 (AUDIT G, OFF).
- What to look at (OFF commentary extract): a head-and-shoulders view; he wears a suit; the features are "hard and emaciated", with "green-rimmed eyes" that look anxious. The dominant colour mixes **absinthe green and pale turquoise**, set against the **fiery orange of the beard and hair**. "The model's immobility contrasts with the undulating hair and beard, echoed and amplified in the hallucinatory arabesques of the background."
- Quote (Artaud, as given on the Orsay Van Gogh/Artaud exhibition page): "Let anyone who once knew how to look at a human face take a look at the self-portrait of Van Gogh … Painted by an extra-lucid Van Gogh, that face of a red-headed butcher, inspecting and watching us, scrutinising us with a glowering eye." OFF: musee-orsay.fr/en/program/whats-on/exhibitions/presentation/van-gogh-artaud-man-suicided-society. Extract wording; check against the page. The extract does not say this refers to the Orsay canvas specifically, so present it as Artaud on "the self-portrait of Van Gogh".
- Van Gogh quotes, already verified (AUDIT A2 15.3, OFF vangoghletters.org):
  - Letter 805 (c. 20 Sept 1889): "look at it for some time … my physiognomy has grown much calmer, although the gaze may be vaguer than before".
  - Letter 800 (5–6 Sept 1889): "it's difficult to know oneself — but it isn't easy to paint oneself either".
  - Do **not** use "simpler, more truthful than a photograph" or "look at myself in the mirror …". Neither was found (A2).
- Hooks: (1) It came from the doctor's family, not his mother's (OFF). (2) Stillness against a background that moves (OFF). (3) Artaud's "red-headed butcher" (OFF exhibition page).

### 045 · Van Gogh — La Chambre de Van Gogh à Arles (Van Gogh's Bedroom in Arles), 1889
- 1889. Oil on canvas, **57.3 × 73.5 cm**, **RF 1959 2** (OFF extract).
- Provenance: entered in **1959** "by cession in application of the peace treaty with Japan" (OFF). Context: it belonged to the Japanese collector **Kojiro Matsukata** (1865–1950), a dockyard owner who bought about 2,000 works, mostly in France, from 1916, for a planned Tokyo museum. France sequestered the part left in France as enemy property at the end of WWII; it passed to the French state under the San Francisco treaty arrangements (1951). France returned most of it to Japan, where it founded the **National Museum of Western Art (1959)**, and kept a few works, including this one (SEC2: theartnewspaper.com 2024/04/19 article on the Matsukata collection + artsandculture.google.com "The Matsukata Collection: A One-Hundred-Year Odyssey" for the general story; the link of *this* canvas to Matsukata is OFF "peace treaty with Japan" + WEAK Wikipedia naming Matsukata). Say "a Japanese collector's painting, kept by France under the post-war treaty"; naming Matsukata is safe for the collection story.
- What it is (OFF commentary extract): painted **about a year after the original**, a **smaller version made for his family in Holland**. The larger version is in the **Art Institute of Chicago**.
- Letters (OFF vangoghletters.org, search extracts):
  - Letter 806 (28 Sept 1889) and the letters around it: he sends **smaller canvases for his mother and sister**, including reductions of Wheatfield and cypresses, Olive trees, Reaper and **Bedroom**, "along with a small portrait of himself". This is the tool's summary, not a quote.
  - Letter **705** to Theo, Arles, **16 Oct 1888**, on the first version (extract wording): the walls are "of a pale violet"; "the colour has to do the job here, and through its being simplified by giving a grander style to things, to be suggestive here of rest or of sleep in general." "In short, looking at the painting should *rest* the mind, or rather, the imagination." He also painted it "to take my revenge for the enforced rest that I was obliged to take." Colours listed in the letter: red floor tiles, butter-yellow bed and chairs, lemon-green sheets and pillows, scarlet blanket, green window, orange dressing table, blue basin, lilac doors.
  - Caution: letter 705 describes the **1888** canvas (Van Gogh Museum), not the Orsay copy. Say "When he first painted this room, he wrote…".
  - The Orsay commentary's own colour list ("pale lilac walls, broken and faded red floor, chrome-yellow chairs and bed … blood-red blanket … I wanted to express absolute rest") is a **different translation**. Don't mix it with 705's VGM wording.
- Display: rooms 36–37 assumed. **Room not confirmed** in any extract.
- Hooks: (1) A painting meant to rest the mind, made by a man who had been forced to rest (letter 705, OFF). (2) A copy made small for his mother and sister (OFF). (3) A Japanese collector's picture that France kept after the war (OFF + SEC2).

### 046 · Van Gogh — La Nuit étoilée (Starry Night over the Rhône), 1888 ⚠ Tokyo
- 1888, Arles. Oil on canvas, **73 × 92 cm**, **RF 1975 19**. Gift with life interest (usufruct) by **M. and Mme Robert Kahn-Sriber**, in memory of **M. and Mme Fernand Moch**, **1975** (OFF: …/la-nuit-etoilee-78696). Room **36** (OFF).
- What to look at (OFF commentary extract): a view of the Rhône where he "transcribed the colours he perceived in the dark". Blues dominate: **Prussian blue, ultramarine and cobalt**. The town's gaslights glow "an intense orange" and reflect in the water. The stars sparkle "like gemstones". A **couple of lovers** at the bottom make it "more serene".
- Letter 691 to Theo (c. 29 Sept 1888, VGM translation): "Against the green-blue field of the sky the Great Bear has a green and pink sparkle whose discreet paleness contrasts with the harsh gold of the gaslight." It was "actually painted at night, under a gas jet" (AUDIT A2 17.2–17.3, OFF).
- Shown at the **Indépendants, 1889**, no. 272, sent by Theo with the Irises (AUDIT A2 17.6, OFF). Fénéon, La Vogue, Sept 1889: "a diverting colourist even in eccentricities like his Starry Night" (AUDIT A2 17.7, OFF notes). **Do not use** the "too mad" quote, the L'Homme de bronze notice or the candle-hat legend as fact (A2).
- **Loan:** Tokyo, 14 Nov 2026 – 28 Mar 2027, named highlight (SEC2). Fallback: the track must work as "this wall usually holds…" or switch to another Arles work in room 36. The Arlésienne (…/larlesienne-14058) and La Salle de danse à Arles (…/la-salle-de-danse-arles-759) have Orsay pages (OFF existence only; rooms and loan status not checked).
- Hooks: (1) A painting made at night, by gaslight (OFF letter). (2) The lovers at the bottom (OFF). (3) Going to Tokyo for the museum's 40th year (SEC2).

### 047 · Van Gogh — L'Église d'Auvers-sur-Oise, vue du chevet (The Church at Auvers), 1890
- Painted **4–8 June 1890**. Oil on canvas, **93 × 74.5 cm**, **RF 1951 42** (OFF: …/leglise-dauvers-sur-oise-vue-du-chevet-755).
- Provenance: Dr Gachet, then his son Paul Gachet until 1951. **Acquired in 1952** "with the support of Paul Gachet [the son] and participation of an anonymous Canadian donation" (OFF extract). **It was not a pure gift.**
- Orsay: "the only painting representing in full the church in Auvers" (OFF). Room 36 (AUDIT G, OFF).
- Letter 879 to Willemien, **5 June 1890**: "it's almost the same thing as the studies I did in Nuenen of the old tower and the cemetery, only now the colour is probably more expressive, more sumptuous." The windows "look like ultramarine blue patches" against "a sky of a deep and simple blue of pure cobalt" (AUDIT A2 16.2–16.3, OFF). **The windows are blue, not black.**
- Hooks: (1) He linked it back to the church tower of his Dutch childhood village (OFF letter). (2) The only full view he painted of this church (OFF). (3) It entered the museum partly thanks to an anonymous Canadian donor (OFF).

### 048 · Van Gogh — Portrait du Docteur Gachet (Dr Paul Gachet), 1890, and the Gachet gift
- 1890. Oil on canvas, **68 × 57 cm** (106 × 94 cm framed), **RF 1949 16** (OFF: …/portrait-du-docteur-gachet-98570). Room: the extract did not give one. Room 37 is "the Paul Gachet collection" (OFF), so the portrait is presumably there or in 36. **Check.**
- Who Gachet was (OFF, node 277393): a doctor specialising in "melancholy" and nervous illnesses, who monitored Van Gogh's health. An amateur painter and engraver under the name **Paul Van Ryssel**. Close to the Impressionists; he bought works by **Cézanne, Guillaumin, Monet and Renoir**. One of the first collectors of Van Gogh. His heirs **Marguerite Gachet (1869–1949)** and **Paul Gachet junior (1873–1962)** gave the collection to the **Louvre between 1949 and 1954**. It stayed there until Orsay opened.
- Van Gogh came to Gachet **on Theo's advice** after leaving the hospital at Saint-Rémy (OFF commentary extract, works-in-focus "Dr Paul Gachet").
- What to look at (OFF commentary extract): a "melancholy pose", a "severe portrait brushed in cold colours". "The only touch of hope" is the **foxglove**, valued for its curative properties.
- Quote: in letter **RM23** to Gauguin (on or about 17 June 1890), he says he has "a portrait of Dr Gachet with the deeply sad expression of our time" (OFF vangoghletters.org, search extract; extract wording, so check the page). Orsay's commentary renders it as "the desolate expression of our time". Pick **one** translation and credit it.
- Letter 877 to Theo, 3 June 1890 (OFF extract) describes a Gachet portrait: "the head with a white cap, very fair, very light, the hands also in light carnation, a blue frock coat and a cobalt blue background, leaning on a red table on which are a yellow book and a foxglove plant with purple flowers." **Caution:** this is generally taken to describe the **first** version (not at Orsay; not verified here). Don't use it to describe the Orsay canvas without checking which version has books.
- Etching: at Gachet's house Van Gogh made **his first and only etching**, a portrait of Gachet. Orsay holds the copper plate, dated **25 May 90** (OFF commentary extract).
- Orsay exhibition: "A Friend of Cézanne and Van Gogh: Doctor Gachet (1828–1909)" (OFF page exists; date not seen. 1999 is commonly cited, so check). It gives Gachet's dates as 1828–1909.
- Hooks: (1) The doctor who painted under a false name, Van Ryssel (OFF). (2) The foxglove, a medicine plant, as the only hope in the picture (OFF). (3) His children kept everything for decades, then gave it in 1949–54; Marguerite died in 1949, the year of the first gift (OFF dates).

### 049 · Georges Seurat — Le Cirque (Circus), 1891
- 1890–91 (unfinished). Oil on canvas, **186 × 152 cm**, **RF 2511** (OFF extract: …/le-cirque-542. A3 cites …/le-cirque-3015, which may be a duplicate page). Room **38** (AUDIT A3, OFF).
- Provenance: **Paul Signac's collection from 1900 to 1924**, then **John Quinn**. Accepted by the State in **1927** as Quinn's bequest to the Louvre (OFF).
- Story: the **third** of his panels on modern night-time entertainments, after **Parade** and **Chahut** (OFF). Shown at the **Indépendants, 1891**. Seurat **died of diphtheria a few days after the Salon opened**, leaving it unfinished. It became "his artistic testament" (OFF). He was 31 when he died, in March 1891 (AUDIT A3).
- Theory: applies **Charles Henry**'s ideas on the psychological effect of line and colour, and the colour-mixing theories of **Chevreul and Rood** (OFF).
- What to look at (OFF): a **dark border painted directly on the canvas**, plus a flat frame in the **same shade of blue**, both part of the work. The **clown in the foreground** is seen from behind and takes the place of the musician in Chahut. Above him are a horsewoman and acrobats. Orsay holds studies: the clown, L'Écuyère, a watercolour and a painted sketch (OFF: articles/…-213296; …/lecuyere-etude-pour-le-cirque-265347; …/le-cirque-etude-265346). Whether they are displayed is not known.
- Quote (contemporary critic, as quoted by Orsay; critic unnamed in extract): "everything in Circus achieves harmony through analogy, through the conciliation of opposites, conspiring towards a sense of gaiety: ascending lines, successive tone contrasts, pronounced dominance of orange, highlighted by a frame which creates an opposition of tone and colour with the whole" (OFF extract wording).
- Corrections from A3: Seurat's term was "chromo-luminarisme", not "divisionnisme"; blue and yellow dots do **not** make green in the eye (A3 30.3–30.4).
- Hooks: (1) Signac owned it for 24 years (OFF). (2) He died days after it was hung (OFF). (3) Everything goes **up**: rising lines for gaiety, per Henry's theory (OFF).

### 050 · Signac and the Neo-Impressionists
- **Femmes au puits. Opus 238** (1892): oil on canvas, **194.5 × 130 cm**, **RF 1979-5**. Bought in **1979** from **Ginette Signac**, the artist's daughter. **The extract says "not currently exhibited"** (OFF: …/femmes-au-puits-opus-238-3017). Don't build the track on it unless it is seen on site. Story, if used: shown at the **1893 Indépendants** as "Jeunes Provençales au puits (décoration pour un panneau dans la pénombre)". It grew out of a sketch for the large "Au Temps d'Harmonie". The real Saint-Tropez landmarks (citadel hill, jetty, Maures, Esterel) are recombined (OFF commentary).
- **Recommended instead: Femme à l'ombrelle** (1893): oil on canvas, **81 × 65 cm**, **room 40** (OFF: …/femme-lombrelle-25640 and commentary 8852). The sitter is **Berthe Roblès (1862–1942)**, a distant cousin of Camille Pissarro. Signac met her in the early 1880s at the **Chat Noir** cabaret. She lived with him from then on and **married him on 7 Nov 1892**, a few months before this picture. Finished Neo-Impressionist portraits are rare in his work. This is the last of a series on family and friends, which includes **Félix Fénéon** (1890, **MoMA**). It takes up the Impressionist parasol theme, as in **Monet's two "Essai de figure en plein air"** at Orsay, "in a purely Neo-Impressionist manner" (OFF). Inventory and provenance were not seen.
- Other Signac works sit in room 39 (OFF extract; which work is not known). Cross, Luce and others: **not searched** (budget).
- Hooks: (1) Monet's woman with a parasol, redone in dots by the next generation (OFF). (2) A wedding portrait in all but name: she married him months before (OFF). (3) Signac as collector, who owned Le Cirque (049; OFF).

### 051 · Pont-Aven — Émile Bernard, Madeleine au Bois d'Amour, 1888
- 1888. Oil on canvas, **137 × 163 cm**, **RF 1977 8**, acquired in **1977** (OFF: …/madeleine-au-bois-damour-7992 + commentary 2278). Room **not found**.
- Story (OFF commentary): Bernard was **only twenty** when he painted this **life-size** portrait of his sister **Madeleine, then seventeen**. She lies in the **Bois d'Amour** on the edge of Pont-Aven, "a wood that Sérusier's Talisman had made famous" (useful link to 056). It is "not a realistic scene but a portrait with allegorical overtones" of the young Madeleine, "**with whom Gauguin had fallen in love**". Bernard and his sister were very close to Gauguin, leader of the "Impressionist and Synthetist" group at Pont-Aven, who recommended "painting in masses and solid areas of colour".
- Hooks: (1) A brother of twenty paints his sister of seventeen, life-size (OFF). (2) Gauguin was in love with the model (OFF). (3) The same wood as Le Talisman (OFF).

### 052 · Gauguin before Tahiti — La Belle Angèle (1889) / Les Alyscamps (1888)
**La Belle Angèle**
- 1889. Oil on canvas, **92 × 73.2 cm**, **RF 2617**. **Gift of Ambroise Vollard, 1927** (OFF: …/la-belle-angele-286 + commentary). Room not found.
- Story (OFF): the sitter is **Marie-Angélique Satre**, who kept a hotel at Pont-Aven and was thought one of the most beautiful women in the area. **Gauguin offered her the painting and she refused it.** **Degas** thought it a masterpiece, **bought it in 1891** and kept it until his studio sale in **1918**.
- What to look at (OFF): her portrait is **circled** and set on a mostly decorative background, with forms partitioned and outlined in a dark line, a method taken from **Japanese prints**. On the **left**, an anthropomorphic **pot in Peruvian style**, "an exotic version of a Breton idol".
- Hooks: (1) The model refused the gift; Degas bought it (OFF). (2) The circle, like a Japanese print (OFF).

**Les Alyscamps**
- 1888. Oil on canvas, **91.5 × 72.5 cm** (OFF extract). Inventory and provenance: **not found**. Room not found.
- Story (OFF commentary): in **October 1888** Gauguin answered Van Gogh's invitation to Arles. This is probably one of his first paintings there, in brilliant autumn colours. The Alyscamps was the Roman necropolis, made a Christian burial ground in the 3rd century by Saint Trophime, first bishop of Arles. Gauguin almost drops the historic site: fields, woods, a canal and **three walkers (two Arlésiennes and a man)**. Gauguin "found little charm in the women of Arles". When he sent it to Theo, he **ironically titled it "Landscape or Three Graces at the Temple of Venus"** (Orsay German commentary 2047, via extract).
- Hooks: the ironic "Three Graces" title; the link back to Van Gogh's yellow house, two rooms earlier (OFF).

### 053 · Gauguin — Arearea (Joyeusetés), 1892 ⚠ Tokyo · fallback Femmes de Tahiti
**Arearea**
- 1892. Oil on canvas, **75 × 94 cm**. **Bequest of M. and Mme Lung, 1961** (OFF: m.musee-orsay.fr commentary arearea-9767). Inventory not seen in the extract (RF 1961 6 is unconfirmed).
- Gauguin's first trip to Tahiti began in **April 1891** (OFF).
- What to look at (OFF): two women seated at the centre; a tree cuts across the composition; a **red dog**. In the background, women worship a statue: Gauguin "enlarged a small Maori motif to the size of a great Buddha and imagined a sacred rite".
- Story (OFF): shown with his Tahitian paintings in Paris in **November 1893**. The red dog provoked **much sarcasm**. Gauguin thought it one of his best works and **bought it back himself in 1895**, before leaving Europe for good.
- Ethics (AUDIT A2 18.3, SEC2): the "thirteen-year-old" figure comes from Gauguin's own semi-fictional Noa Noa. Attribute it to him if it is used at all.
- **Loan:** confirmed in Tokyo, 14 Nov 2026 – 28 Mar 2027, "back in Japan after 16 years" (SEC1 bijutsutecho + AUDIT G SEC2).

**Femmes de Tahiti (fallback)**
- 1891. Oil on canvas, **69 × 91.5 cm** (OFF commentary 16337). Inventory, provenance and room: **not found**. **Tokyo status unknown**: check before using it as the Arearea fallback.
- What to look at (OFF): typical of the start of his first stay: Tahitian women at simple daily tasks. "Heavy, hieratic figures", each in its own space. Faces like masks, "tinged with melancholy". Influence of **Manet**'s simplified forms. Gauguin made a variant in 1892, **Parau Api (Dresden)**, where a **flowered pareo replaces the strict mission dress** of the woman on the right (OFF).
- Gauguin's motives (OFF, quoted by Orsay): "to live there in ecstasy, calm and art"; to escape "this European struggle for money"; "free at last" (Orsay quoting Gauguin; extract wording).
- Hooks: (1) He bought Arearea back himself (OFF). (2) The red dog that made Paris laugh (OFF). (3) Femmes de Tahiti: the mission dress, swapped for a pareo in the later version (OFF).

### 059 · Café Campana and the great clock
Only audit-verified facts (no new searches possible):
- Café Campana is on **level 5**, "at the exit of the Impressionist gallery … behind one of the great exterior clocks" (AUDIT A3, OFF: musee-orsay.fr/fr/visite/restauration).
- It opened in **2011** with the renovated Impressionist gallery. The Pavillon Amont opened on **20 Oct 2011** (AUDIT G, culture.gouv.fr OFF).

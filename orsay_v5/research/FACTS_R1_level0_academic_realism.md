### 003 · Thomas Couture — *Romains de la décadence* (Romans of the Decadence)
- **Basics:** 1847 · oil on canvas · 472 × 772 cm · inv. **INV 3451** (OFF). Commissioned by the State on 25 Jun 1846 and shown at the Salon of 1847 (OFF; A1). The museum says it took Couture three years to paint (OFF).
- **On display:** "Rez-de-chaussée, Allée centrale des sculptures", the central sculpture aisle on level 0 (OFF via A1 S4). A 1986-era Jim Purcell photo is titled "Rez-de-chaussée, niveau transept, Lille", which is an archival position (OFF, archive photo). Gabriele Basilico photographed Carpeaux's *Ugolin* standing in front of the Couture, so the two works can be paired on the route (OFF, artwork 148470).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF commentary, summarised): an orgy in a classical hall, with statues of the virtuous ancestors looking down. It is a history painting, the noblest genre of the day, and it carries a moral message.
- **Story / context:**
  - Couture took his theme from Juvenal: the virtues of the ancients set against the depravity of later times (OFF).
  - In the 1847 Salon catalogue he quoted two lines of Juvenal. The museum's English: **"Crueller than war, vice fell upon Rome and avenged the conquered world"** (OFF, verbatim translation in the extract).
  - It was also a jab at France under the July Monarchy. Critics in 1847 quickly read it as "The French of the Decadence" (OFF). The museum calls it a "realist allegory" (OFF).
  - Manet studied in Couture's studio from 1850 to 1856 (A1, consistent).
- **Quotes usable:** the Juvenal lines (OFF); "The French of the Decadence" (OFF).
- **Hooks:** (1) A painting of ancient Rome that was really about Paris in 1847, and Paris got the joke. (2) The State commissioned it before anyone had seen it. (3) Carpeaux's starving *Ugolin* stands in front of it: decadence and hunger face to face.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/romains-de-la-decadence-9493 (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/painting/commentaire_id/romans-during-the-decadence-2105.html (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/ugolin-de-jean-baptiste-carpeaux-devant-les-romains-de-la-decadence-de-thomas-couture-148470 (OFF)

### 004 · Jean-Auguste-Dominique Ingres — *La Source* (The Spring)
- **Basics:** begun c.1820 in Florence, finished in 1856 in Paris · oil on canvas · 163 × 80 cm · inv. **RF 219** (OFF).
- **Provenance** (OFF):
  - 1857: owned by Count Charles-Marie Tanneguy Duchâtel, who bought it from the artist for 25,000 francs.
  - 1878: bequeathed by Countess Duchâtel to the Louvre.
  - 1986: sent to Orsay.
- **On display:** "Rez-de-chaussée, Salle 1" (OFF). An archive photo calls it "côté Lille, première salle", the first room on the Lille side (OFF, Purcell).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF):
  - A standing nude set in a vertical format "as if in an alcove".
  - Together with the pose, this gives the figure "the immobility of a marble statue". The influence of sculpture is "striking".
- **Story / context:**
  - Ingres finished it with the help of two pupils, **Paul Balze and Alexandre Desgoffe**, who did the accessories and background, "a common practice for large formats" (OFF).
  - At Duchâtel's home the picture was "surrounded by large plants and aquatic flowers so that the nymph of the spring looked even more like a real person" (OFF).
  - Ingres was born in 1780, so he was 76 in 1856, not 86 as the old guide said (A1).
- **Quotes:** none from Ingres verified. The "probity of art" line was not re-found in this round (see Contested).
- **Hooks:** (1) A painting that took 36 years and was finished by other hands. (2) Its first owner staged it among real water plants, like a live nymph. (3) A painted marble statue.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/la-source-525 (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/fr/oeuvres/rez-de-chaussee-cote-lille-premiere-salle-la-source-de-jean-auguste-dominique-ingres-acheve-laide-de-paul-balze-et-alexandre-desgoffe-127831 (OFF)

### 005a · Alexandre Cabanel — *Naissance de Vénus* (The Birth of Venus)
- **Basics:** 1863 · oil on canvas · 130 × 225 cm · inv. **RF 273** (OFF).
- **Provenance:** a great success at the **Salon of 1863**, where Napoleon III bought it for his private collection. A court decision assigned it to the national museums in 1879 (OFF).
- **On display:** "Rez-de-chaussée, salle 3" (OFF).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF): Venus born of sea-foam and carried ashore. The nude is idealised but "in a lascivious pose". The handling is virtuoso and polished, mixing references to Ingres and 18th-century painting in the eclectic Second Empire spirit.
- **Story / context:**
  - The theme let artists "introduce eroticism without offending public morality, under the pretext of representing a classical subject" (OFF).
  - It hung at the same 1863 Salon season as the Salon des Refusés. That timing is consistent, but the museum page does not link the two, so frame it as "the same year" only.
- **Quote:** Zola, as given by the museum: **"The goddess, drowned in a sea of milk, resembles a delicious courtesan, but not of flesh and blood – that would be indecent – but made of a sort of pink and white marzipan"** (OFF; the museum's English translation).
- **Hooks:** (1) The Emperor bought it. In the same year, Manet's picnic was laughed at in the Refusés (the Refusés fact is from A1/track 25 sources). (2) Zola's "marzipan" line. (3) Mythology as a licence for the nude.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/naissance-de-venus-98 (OFF); https://m.musee-orsay.fr/en/works/commentaire_id/the-birth-of-venus-7082.html (OFF)

### 006b · Pierre Puvis de Chavannes — *Le Pauvre Pêcheur* (The Poor Fisherman) ⚠ NOT EXHIBITED
- **Basics:** 1881 · oil on canvas · 154.7 × 192.5 cm · inv. **RF 506** (OFF).
- **On display:** the official page extract says it is **not currently exhibited** (OFF). Seurat's small *Paysage avec "Le pauvre pêcheur" de Puvis de Chavannes* is listed in "Niveau supérieur, Salle 38" on level 5. That could serve as a level-5 echo next to *Le Cirque* (OFF).
- **What the visitor would see** (OFF): a widower and his two children in a bleak landscape. The painting is synthetic, with no modelling or traditional perspective, in "greenish hues". The fisherman has "obvious Biblical resonances".
- **Story / context** (OFF):
  - Most critics were against it at the Salon of 1881. Huysmans compared it to a missal picture or dull, flat old frescoes.
  - Durand-Ruel showed it again, and the State bought it in **1887**, the first Puvis the State ever bought: "it took six years for a national museum to dare to show this radical painting".
- **Hooks:** (1) Six years before the state dared to buy it. (2) The younger generation, Seurat among them, copied it.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/le-pauvre-pecheur-1069 (OFF); https://m.musee-orsay.fr/en/works/commentaire_id/the-poor-fisherman-373.html (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/paysage-avec-le-pauvre-pecheur-de-puvis-de-chavannes-119389 (OFF)

### 007 · Jean-Baptiste Carpeaux — *Ugolin* (Ugolino)
- **Basics:** bronze, cast by Victor Thiébaut in Feb 1863 · 194 × 148 × 119 cm (OFF). Inventory number not found.
- **On display:** "Rez-de-chaussée, Allée centrale des sculptures" (OFF). The Basilico photo shows it in front of the Couture (OFF).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF): the father's pain and anguish. The museum points to Carpeaux's study of the antique *Laocoön*, of Michelangelo, and of Géricault's *Raft of the Medusa*.
- **Story / context** (OFF):
  - The subject is Dante, *Inferno*, canto XXXIII. In 13th-century Pisa, Ugolino della Gherardesca betrayed the Ghibelline party. Archbishop Ubaldini had him shut in a tower to starve to death.
  - Carpeaux reached Rome and the Villa Medici in 1856, almost two years late. That made his relations with the director, the painter Victor Schnetz, awkward from the start.
  - He worked on the group from 1857 to 1861, beyond the term of his stay. The plaster was finished in Rome in October 1861, and the Académie des Beaux-Arts judged it in Paris on 8 Mar 1862. The reaction did not live up to his hopes.
  - The Académie's remarks were published in the press. The State then offered a marble commission at a fee Carpeaux found too low, and he negotiated hard. The result was a bronze, shown at the Salon of 1863.
  - The bronze was installed in the Tuileries Garden as a pendant to the *Laocoön*, from 1863 to 1904, then went to the Louvre and later Orsay.
  - Another official extract says the State acquired it in 1862 "for a commission of 30,000 francs". Use the figure only with care; see Contested.
- **Hooks:** (1) A student who overstayed in Rome and defied his director. (2) A father in Dante's tower. (3) It stood in a public garden for forty years.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/ugolin-630 (OFF); https://musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/sculpture/commentaire_id/ugolino-7160.html (OFF)

### 008 · Honoré Daumier — *Les Célébrités du Juste Milieu* (caricature busts)
- **Basics:** 1832–1835 · unbaked clay painted in oil · small busts, for example *Charles Philipon* (1832) at H. 16.4 cm and *Guizot* (1833) at H. 22.5 cm (OFF).
- **The series:** about forty busts were made. **Thirty-six survive, and all are at Orsay** (OFF).
- **Provenance:**
  - Several busts were bought in **1980** with help from Michel David-Weill and the Fondation Lutèce (OFF).
  - In 1927 the sculptor Fix-Masseau repaired them before their owners of the time, the galerie Sagot-Le Garrec, cast them in bronze (OFF).
  - The Fondation BNP Paribas paid for a study and restoration, which led to the 2005 Orsay exhibition (25 May – 28 Aug 2005) (OFF).
- **On display:** individual bust pages give **"Rez-de-chaussée, Salle 4"** (OFF; the extract listed several busts there). Check on site how many are in the case.
- **Story / context** (OFF):
  - They were commissioned by **Charles Philipon**, founder of the anti-July Monarchy satirical papers *La Caricature* and *Le Charivari*.
  - Daumier used them as models for his lithographs.
  - The series is called "Celebrities of the Juste Milieu" rather than "Parliamentarians" because only 26 of the sitters actually held a parliamentary mandate.
- **Nicknames verified on official pages** (OFF): Philipon, "Le Rieur édenté" (the toothless laugher). Guizot, "L'Ennuyeux" (the bore). Dupin, "Le Prédicateur" (the preacher). Other nicknames that came up in one extract are not confirmed (see Contested).
- **Hooks:** (1) Thirty-six fragile clay heads that survived because nobody fired them in a kiln. (2) The man who ordered them, Philipon, is among them as the "toothless laugher". (3) The link back to Couture's 1847 attack on the same regime.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/program/whats-on/exhibitions/presentation/daumier-celebrities-juste-milieu (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/charles-philipon-6335 (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/francois-pierre-guillaume-guizot-656 (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/andre-marie-jean-jacques-dupin-6356 (OFF)

### 009 · Jean-François Millet — *Des glaneuses* (The Gleaners) ⚠ TOKYO

- **Provenance** (OFF): Binder; Paul Tesse (1865); Ferdinand Bischoffsheim (1867–1889); Mme Pommery, who gave it with reservation of usufruct in 1890.
- **Exhibited:** Salon of 1857, no. 1936 (OFF).
- **On display:** room 5, level 0, Seine side (OFF via A1). An archive photo describes the Millet, Rousseau and Corot room as the "last room before the transept" (OFF, Purcell).

- **What the visitor sees** (OFF): the painting caps "ten years of research on the theme of the gleaners". The women were allowed to go through the fields at sunset and pick up, one by one, the stalks of wheat the harvesters had missed.
- **Hooks:** (1) Ten years of studies for three bent backs. (2) A Champagne house widow gave it to the nation (Mme Pommery, OFF). (3) The Tokyo loan gives a "this painting is travelling" fallback.

### 010 · Jean-François Millet — *L'Angélus* (The Angelus) (+ *Le Printemps*)
- **Basics:** 1857–1859 · oil on canvas · 55.5 × 66 cm · inv. **RF 1877** (OFF). It is small, which is worth saying to set expectations.
- **Provenance** (OFF):
  - 1 Jul 1889: sold at the Secrétan sale, Sedelmeyer gallery, Paris, lot 63, to the American Art Association.
  - 1890–1909: owned by Alfred Chauchard.
  - Chauchard's bequest was accepted in 1910 and assigned to the Louvre, where it hung from 1909/1910 to 1986, when it went to Orsay.
- **On display:** no room was found. Other Millets are in room 5, level 0 (OFF). Not listed among Tokyo loans in the sources seen (NONE). Confirm on site.
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF): a man and a woman recite the Angelus, which commemorates the Annunciation. They have stopped the potato harvest, with fork, basket, sacks and wheelbarrow around them.
- **Story / context:**
  - Millet's own account (1865), as the museum translates it: **"L'Angélus is a painting I created thinking about how, when working in the fields long ago, my grandmother never failed, upon hearing the church bell ring, to make us stop our work to recite the Angelus for those poor souls"** (OFF). The museum stresses that it is a childhood memory, not religious exaltation; Millet was not a practising Catholic.
  - Its "extraordinary destiny": Dalí venerated it, a disturbed man slashed it in 1932, and it became a world icon in the 20th century (OFF).
  - At the 1889 Secrétan sale, the Director of Fine Arts, Antonin Proust, bid for France at 553,000 francs, but the government did not ratify the purchase. The painting went to the United States and toured there. Chauchard then bought it back (SEC: napoleon.org plus a historical directory; the price details are not official, use as "a record price" only).
- **Hooks:** (1) A grandmother who stopped the work when the bell rang. (2) France bid for it, failed to pay, lost it to America, and a department-store owner bought it back. (Chauchard founded the Grands Magasins du Louvre; that is SEC knowledge, not verified this round, so verify before use.) (3) It was slashed in 1932.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/langelus-345 (OFF); https://m.musee-orsay.fr/en/works/commentaire_id/the-angelus-339.html (OFF); https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/paintings/the-angelus (SEC)

### 011 · Gustave Courbet — *Un enterrement à Ornans* (A Burial at Ornans) ✔ BACK ON DISPLAY
- **Basics:** 1849–1850 · oil on canvas · 315 × 668 cm · inv. **RF 325** (OFF). Given by Mlle Juliette Courbet, the artist's sister, in **1881** (OFF).
- **On display:** room 7, level 0, its usual place (OFF).
  - The restoration was done in public, in a glazed enclosure in room 7, between May 2025 and Aug 2026. Visits to the restoration site ran 8 Jan – 30 Apr 2026, and the rehang was scheduled for **5 Aug 2026** (OFF).
  - France 3 Régions and Finestre sull'Arte report that it is back in the galleries (SEC2).
  - The restoration was supported by the Bank of America Art Conservation Project (OFF).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF): villagers at the burial of someone "of unknown identity", painted "as if it were a major historical event". The figures are **life-size**, "a privilege usually reserved for biblical and mythological figures", and are not idealised.
- **The restoration** (OFF):
  - The aim was to remove old varnish layers that had been "poorly applied, yellowed, and thickened".
  - The press adds that the original colours reappeared, along with several centimetres of paint at the edges that earlier remountings had hidden. It was the biggest restoration since the painting entered the national collections in 1881 (SEC2: France 3, Finestre).
- **Story / context:**
  - Courbet's 1849 medal exempted him from the Salon jury, so the *Burial* was shown without selection (A1, OFF Courbet biography).
  - The museum calls it a "manifesto of the realist movement" (OFF).
- **Hooks:** (1) Nobody knows whose funeral it is. (2) It is freshly cleaned, with colours not seen for decades and edges that had been folded out of sight, which suits the 40th anniversary. (3) No jury could stop it.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/un-enterrement-ornans-924 (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/magazine/2025-02-20/restoration-courbets-painting-burial-ornans (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/node/289948 (OFF); https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/bourgogne-franche-comte/doubs/besancon/le-retour-d-un-enterrement-a-ornans-apres-quinze-mois-de-restauration-le-chef-d-uvre-de-courbet-retrouve-les-galeries-du-musee-d-orsay-3413114.html (SEC); https://www.finestresullarte.info/fr/musees/le-musee-d-orsay-fete-ses-40-ans-un-courbet-restaure-une-grande-exposition-consacree-a-mary-cassatt-et-le-mum-orsay-itinerant (SEC)

### 012 · Gustave Courbet — *L'Atelier du peintre. Allégorie réelle déterminant une phase de sept années de ma vie artistique (et morale)* (The Painter's Studio)
- **Basics:** 1854–1855 · oil on canvas · 361 × 598 cm · inv. **RF 2257** (OFF).
  - The extract gives the subtitle as "…de ma vie artistique". Check on site whether the label adds "et morale".
- **Provenance** (OFF): kept by Juliette Courbet until the 1881 studio sale, then private collections, then Galerie Barbazanges (1919–20). Bought in **1920** with a public subscription and help from the Société des Amis du Louvre.
- **On display:** room 7, level 0 (OFF; A1).
- **Exhibited:** Pavillon du Réalisme, 1855 (OFF).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF):
  - Right side: the collector **Alfred Bruyas**, the philosopher **Proudhon**, the critic **Champfleury** on a stool, and **Baudelaire** absorbed in a book.
  - Left side, "everyday life": a priest, a merchant, a beggar girl, an unemployed worker, and **a hunter who "somewhat resembles Napoleon III"**.
- **Courbet's own words**, as the museum translates them: **"It's the whole world coming to me to be painted"**. On the right are "all the shareholders, by that I mean friends, fellow workers, art lovers". On the left is "the other world of everyday life, the masses, wretchedness, poverty, wealth, the exploited and the exploiters, people who make a living from death" (OFF).
- **Champfleury hated his portrait** (OFF): "I was appalled at this image of myself which made me look like the Jesuit General… This damned portrait is monstrous".
- **Restoration:** restored in public, in situ behind glass, about ten years before the *Burial*. About 900 crowdfunding donors raised €86,000 (OFF, node/415). Exact years not confirmed; the project was announced in Dec 2014.
- **Hooks:** (1) "The whole world coming to me to be painted." (2) A poacher who looks like the Emperor. (3) Champfleury: "this damned portrait is monstrous". (4) The public paid for it twice: by subscription in 1920 and by crowdfunding for its restoration.
- **Sources:** https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/latelier-du-peintre-927 (OFF); https://musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/painting/commentaire_id/the-artists-studio-7146.html (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/node/415 (OFF)

### 013 · Gustave Courbet — *L'Origine du monde* (The Origin of the World) (OPTIONAL)
- **Basics:** 1866 · oil on canvas · 46.3 × 55.4 cm · inv. **RF 1995-10** (OFF).
- **Provenance** (OFF):
  - Its first owner, who probably commissioned it, was the Turkish-Egyptian diplomat **Khalil-Bey** (1831–1879). He built a short-lived collection celebrating the female body and was ruined by gambling debts.
  - It later belonged to the psychoanalyst **Jacques Lacan**.
  - It entered Orsay in **1995** as a *dation* (payment of inheritance tax in kind; decree of 13 Jun 1995).
  - The museum: it "epitomised the paradox of a famous work that is seldom actually seen".
- **On display:** **room not found** (NONE). Courbet's *La Source* is listed in room 6, next to room 7 (OFF).
  - Loans: Centre Pompidou-Metz, Lacan exhibition, from Nov 2023 to before summer 2024 (SEC: Sortir à Paris). There it was **tagged in May 2024** (SEC: La Libre).
  - No 2026 loan was found. Confirm on site that it is hung, and where.
- **Story / context:**
  - The model was identified in 2018 as the Opéra dancer **Constance Quéniaux**. Claude Schopp found a letter from Alexandre Dumas fils to George Sand. Sylvie Aubenas of the BnF said she was "99 per cent certain" (SEC2: Apollo, The Art Newspaper). The museum's own position was not found.
  - Dumas's line, as reported in English translation, was that Courbet had painted "Ms. Queniault of the Opera, for the Turk…". It is crude; do not use it in audio.
- **Hooks:** (1) For 130 years almost nobody saw it: a private room, a hidden panel at Lacan's (the hidden panel is not verified, see Contested), then the museum. (2) The 2018 identification of the model. (3) It entered the nation as payment of a tax bill.

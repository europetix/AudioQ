### Gauguin — Le Cheval blanc (The White Horse)
- **1898**, oil on canvas, **140.5 × 92 cm**, **RF 2616**. Painted during the **second Tahiti stay**. In the collection of **Daniel de Monfreid** until **1927**, when the museum acquired it (OFF: musee-orsay.fr/en/node/21142; commentary "the-white-horse-3088").
- **Location:** "Niveau supérieur, Galerie Françoise Cachin" (OFF, artwork page). Room **44** is inferred from the plan-guide ("Gauguin à Tahiti"). Restored for the reopening of the post-Impressionist rooms on **10 Sep 2019**; the restoration "gave back to the painting its vibrant colours" (OFF, node/21142 extract).
- **Tokyo 14 Nov 2026 – 28 Mar 2027:** it is **not named** in any source found. Tobikan, art.nikkei.com/orsay2627, museum.or.jp and Tokyo Art Beat name The Gleaners, Starry Night over the Rhône, Renoir's Bathers and Arearea (SEC2). The full list of about 110 works could not be read (art.nikkei.com is blocked by the proxy). Status: **probably stays in Paris, unconfirmed.** It is a good alternate if Arearea goes.
- **What the visitor sees (OFF commentary):** an imaginary, "synthetic" Tahitian landscape, not a scene from life. A **white horse whose coat is tinged with the green of the vegetation** drinks, standing in a **stream that runs vertically** through the picture. **Behind it, two nude riders ride bareback into the distance.** Colours: greens "from grass green to emerald" and deep blues, set against **orange and pink notes** and the **coppery skin** of the riders. The horse is a "sacred animal". The solitary horse "probably" has a symbolic meaning linked to Tahitian beliefs about the soul's passage to another world: "in Polynesia, white is associated with death and worship of the gods" (OFF).
- **Pharmacist story: OFFICIAL.** The Orsay commentary says the pharmacist in Tahiti who commissioned the picture "did not appreciate Gauguin's daring use of colour" and refused it **because the horse was too green** (OFF, node/21142 and commentary 3088). Writers may use it, attributed to the museum ("the museum tells us…").
- **Hooks:** (1) A commission turned down because the horse was "too green" (OFF). (2) From Monfreid's collection to the museum in 1927 (OFF). (3) Peter Doig hung it beside a Courbet in "Orsay by Peter Doig" (17 Oct 2023 – 21 Jan 2024), to show affinities between the two pictures; the Orsay text says it "exudes a personal form of poetry", painted "as if lost in a dreamlike reverie" (OFF: musee-orsay.fr/en/articles/orsay-peter-doig-276003). The Courbet's title was not seen.
- Sources: musee-orsay.fr/en/node/21142 (OFF); musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/painting/commentaire_id/the-white-horse-3088.html (OFF); musee-orsay.fr/en/articles/orsay-peter-doig-276003 (OFF); tobikan.jp/en/exhibition/2026_orsay.html and art.nikkei.com/orsay2627 (SEC2, for what they do not list).

### Gauguin — Oviri
- **1894**, statuette in **partially glazed stoneware**, **H 75 × W 19 × D 27 cm, 20 kg**, **OAO 1114**. **Acquired in 1987** (OFF: works-in-focus commentary oviri-11308; artwork page oviri-19409). The seller or donor in 1987 was not found.
- **Location:** room **not found** on any official page. Gauguin's works are generally listed as "Niveau supérieur, Galerie Françoise Cachin". Room 44 ("Gauguin à Tahiti") is likely but **unconfirmed**; check on site. Tokyo: not named (see above).
- **Official story (OFF commentary):**
  - Gauguin **discovered clay with the ceramist Ernest Chaplet** and hoped to earn money from the pots he made in **1887**.
  - His ceramic work ended, during his **last stay in Paris**, with Oviri, **his largest piece**, a stoneware figure **fired in Chaplet's kiln in December 1894**.
  - He could not sell **"his Murderess"** (Gauguin's word, quoted by Orsay), so in **1900** he asked **Daniel de Monfreid** for it, **intending to put it on his tomb** in Tahiti.
  - **Theme:** "death, savagery, wildness". Oviri **lords it over a dead she-wolf and crushes the life out of her cub**.
  - **Christopher Gray** (*Sculpture and Ceramics of Paul Gauguin*, 1963) saw in it "the expression of Gauguin's profound disillusionment and discouragement" (quoted by Orsay).
  - Albert **Aurier** in 1891 on Gauguin's ceramics: "strange, barbaric, savage ceramics" (OFF extract; it is not certain this is the Oviri page).
- **What the visitor sees:** a standing figure over a dead wolf and a cub (OFF). No further official visual description was found. **Do not describe the face, hair or eyes from memory.**
- **Hooks:** (1) "My Murderess", the piece he could not sell (OFF). (2) He wanted it on his grave (OFF). (3) His largest ceramic, from Chaplet's kiln (OFF).
- Sources: musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/sculpture/commentaire_id/oviri-11308.html (OFF); musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/oviri-19409 (OFF, existence).

### Odilon Redon — Les Yeux clos (Closed Eyes), and Redon's Paul Gauguin
**Les Yeux clos**
- **1890**, signed "ODILON REDON 1890", **44 × 36 cm**. Medium: "oil on cardboard" in the extracts, but the commentary also says the canvas grain shows through, so the support is unclear (it may be canvas mounted on cardboard). Say "oil" only. Inventory **RF 2791** (pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/000PE004044 via search extract; one extract tied RF 2791 to a drawing, so **check before use**).
- **Provenance (OFF):** **chosen in 1904 from the artist's studio by Léonce Bénédite**, director of the Musée du Luxembourg. It was **the first work by Redon to enter the national collections**. It came to Orsay in 1986 (Joconde extract, SEC2).
- **Location:** the audit (A1, S7) gives "Galerie Françoise Cachin", upper level (OFF). Room **45 "Redon et les Nabis"** is inferred from the plan-guide; it is not on the artwork page.
- **What the visitor sees (OFF commentary):**
  - Probably a portrait of **his wife, Camille Falte**, remade from an earlier drawing.
  - The closed eyes suggest an **inner world, dream, absence or apparition**, the themes Redon explains in his journal ***À soi-même*** (published **1922**).
  - **Very diluted paint**, "almost immaterial", with the grain showing through. The **bust seems to float** in an undefined space.
  - The face recalls **Italian Renaissance busts, especially Francesco Laurana's marbles**, and **Michelangelo's *Dying Slave* (Louvre)**, which deeply moved Redon. In his journal he wrote of the strange charm of the "closed eyes".
  - Orsay calls it "an icon of Symbolism in painting".
- Redon's age: born 1840 (SEC2, per audit A1), so he was about 50 when he painted it, early in his turn to colour. Use "around fifty", not "in his sixties" (audit A1).

**Paul Gauguin (portrait by Redon)**: a strong partner to the Gauguin tracks
- **1903–1905**, oil on canvas, **65 × 54 cm**, **RF 1950 31** (OFF: musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/paul-gauguin-477). Room not given; listed with the Redon holdings.
- **OFF text:**
  - "In all likelihood" inspired by **Gauguin's death in the Marquesas on 8 May 1903**.
  - That **November**, Redon published an article praising the "refined, savage, grandiose and delicate" Gauguin.
  - It is the only time in his work that he made several tributes to one painter, whom he probably met in **1886**.
  - Gauguin's **dark profile stands against a medallion**, like a funerary relief or an ancient medal, with the **"Inca" nose and long hair** Gauguin was proud of.
- **Other Redon works whose pages say "Niveau supérieur, Galerie Françoise Cachin"** (OFF, page existence only): *Madame Odilon Redon* (1882), *Ève* (1904), *Baronne Robert de Domecy* (1900), *Portrait d'Arï Redon au col marin* (c. 1897), *Christ en croix* (c. 1910), *Plante verte dans une urne*, *Le Chemin à Peyrelebade*, *Figure*, two *Frise de fleurs* panels. Which of these hang in room 45 now: **unknown**.
- **Hooks:** (1) Eyes closed: the picture of a world inside the head (OFF). (2) The first Redon the French state ever took, picked in his studio in 1904 (OFF). (3) Redon's memorial to Gauguin, the "refined, savage" friend, links rooms 44 and 45 (OFF).
- Sources: musee-orsay.fr/en/collections/works-in-focus/painting/commentaire_id/closed-eyes-3022.html (OFF); m.musee-orsay.fr/fr/oeuvres/commentaire_id/les-yeux-clos-379.html (OFF); musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/les-yeux-clos-476 (OFF, per audit A1); musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/paul-gauguin-477 (OFF); pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/joconde/000PE004044 (SEC2).

### Henri Rousseau — La Charmeuse de serpents (The Snake Charmer)
- **1907**, oil on canvas, **169 × 189.5 cm** (OFF; one secondary source gives 167, so ignore it). Inventory RF number **not found**. **Bequest of Jacques Doucet, 1936** (OFF). Orangerie text: it "became part of Jacques Doucet's prestigious collection, alongside Picasso's *Demoiselles d'Avignon*" (OFF, Orangerie).
- **Where in 2026:**
  - The artwork page gives "Niveau supérieur, Galerie Françoise Cachin" (OFF, search extract, so level 5).
  - The summer 2026 plan-guide names **no Rousseau room**. The likely rooms are 42–45, but this is not confirmed. The only plan theme near it is 45 "Redon et les Nabis". **The room is unconfirmed; check on site.**
- **Travels 2025–26:**
  - Lent to the **Barnes Foundation, Philadelphia**, "Henri Rousseau: A Painter's Secrets", **19 Oct 2025 – 22 Feb 2026**. There it hung for the first time beside *The Sleeping Gypsy* (MoMA) and *Unpleasant Surprise* (Barnes) (SEC2: barnesfoundation.org press release).
  - Then shown at the **Musée de l'Orangerie**, "Henri Rousseau, l'ambition de la peinture", **25 Mar – 20 Jul 2026** (OFF Orangerie pages; artprice SEC2). It was away from Orsay when the summer plan-guide was printed.
  - It should have been back at Orsay since late July 2026. No official notice of its rehang was found.
  - It is **not named** among the Tokyo loans (none found).
- **What the visitor sees / official description (OFF commentary 8846):**
  - **Commissioned by Robert Delaunay's mother after a trip to India.** Delaunay was among Rousseau's most fervent admirers, with Jarry, Apollinaire, Picasso and Breton.
  - A **dark-skinned, androgynous figure** charms the snakes and the natural world with a **flute**: "a black Eve in a disquieting Garden of Eden, charming a snake as terrifying as the serpent in Genesis was seductive".
  - **Bright, dense colours, backlit**, which anticipate Magritte. Drawing "both precise and naïve". An **innovative vertical, asymmetrical composition**. Figures, animals and fantastic plants get the same uniform treatment.
  - Orsay: one of his "most fascinating and enigmatic" paintings, which **inspired generations of artists, especially the Surrealists**.
  - Strong contrast: one part very bright, the other very dark (OFF extract).
  - Rousseau made his jungle scenes from studies at the **Muséum (Natural History Museum) and the Jardin des Plantes greenhouses**, not from travel (OFF extract).
- **Exact quote (OFF, Orangerie):** Rousseau to Picasso: "We are the two greatest, [you] in the Egyptian style, me in the modern style." This is the Orangerie's wording (English extract). Check it on the page before using it.
- **Hooks:** (1) Painted for the mother of Robert Delaunay, after her trip to India (OFF). (2) A jungle that never left Paris: the Jardin des Plantes (OFF). (3) Just home from Philadelphia and the Orangerie (SEC2/OFF). (4) Owned by Doucet, beside the *Demoiselles d'Avignon* (OFF).
- Sources: m.musee-orsay.fr/en/works/commentaire_id/the-snake-charmer-8846.html and /fr/…/la-charmeuse-de-serpents-8846.html (OFF); musee-orangerie.fr/en/whats-on/exhibitions/henri-rousseau-painters-ambition (OFF); barnesfoundation.org/press/press-releases/barnes-foundation-presents-henri-rousseau-a-painters-secrets (SEC2); news.artprice.com/en/12743 (SEC2, weak-ish).

## Contested / unverifiable (do not use)
- **"Yadwigha on a Louis-Philippe sofa"**: this came up in one search summary for the Charmeuse. It describes Rousseau's *Le Rêve* (MoMA), **not** this painting. Do not use it.
- Oviri "exhibited at the 1895 Salon de la Société nationale": one search summary said this, but it is not seen in the OFF text and other accounts say it was refused. Do not use either version.
- Oviri on Gauguin's tomb in Atuona (a cast of it): not found in OFF. Say only that he *wanted* it on his tomb.
- Oviri: the meaning of the word ("savage"): not stated verbatim in the OFF extracts. Don't translate it.
- Les Yeux clos: support (cardboard vs canvas) and inventory RF 2791 are not settled.
- Charmeuse: the inventory number, and whether it is back on the wall now, are not confirmed.
- Charmeuse: whether Rousseau visited India or the jungle (he did not travel, according to the OFF Jardin des Plantes text). Never say he travelled.

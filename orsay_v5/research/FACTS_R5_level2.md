### 060 · Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec — *Jane Avril dansant* (Jane Avril Dancing)
- **Facts:** c. 1892 · oil on cardboard · 85.5 × 45 cm · **RF 1937-37** · bequest of Antonin Personnaz, 1937. Sources: Orsay page (OFF); the RF number comes from Louvre collections ark cl020228195 (SEC1).
- **Location: settled as far as a desk check can go.**
  - The official artwork page reads "**Niveau médian, salle 68**" (OFF, re-confirmed today).
  - Against that, the official plan-guides list a "Galerie Françoise Cachin" on the **niveau supérieur** (level 5) with "Van Gogh, Gauguin, **Toulouse-Lautrec**, Redon". The 2022–23 plan-guide snippets also list "Panneaux de la Goulue" there (OFF, plan-guide PDFs 2022-10, 2023-05, 2023-11). The spring 2026 plan-guide snippet carries a Lautrec heading, "Toulouse-Lautrec, spectacles et coulisses (Donation Philippe Meyer / Collection Max et Rosy Kaganovitch)". The search summary placed it on level 5, but the snippet does not show the level clearly (OFF, ambiguous).
  - **Verdict:** the work-specific evidence (the artwork page) says **level 2, room 68**, and that is the only OFF source naming this painting's room. The plan-guide evidence is about a *section*, not this painting. It may be stale or refer to other Lautrecs. Keep track 060 on level 2 as planned, but write the cue so it does not depend on a room number. Mark it **"on-site check before recording"**. If the painting turns out to be on level 5, the track can move into the level-5 Post-Impressionist run without rewriting.
- **On display / loans:** no loan found. It is not among the Tokyo works named in the searches (only partial lists were found).
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF commentary):
  - A tall, narrow cardboard. Jane Avril dances on her own in a light, thinned paint.
  - Lautrec used "the fluidity of oil paint diluted with turpentine" and sketched in broad strokes "the essential elements of the dancer's movement".
  - Behind her stand two figures: an unknown woman in a hat and, on her left, "the impresario Warner", the model for Lautrec's lithograph *The Englishman at the Moulin Rouge* (OFF).
- **Story** (OFF, "Qui était Jane Avril ?", musee-orsay.fr/en/node/288022):
  - Jeanne Beaudon, born 9 June 1868, died 17 January 1943, Paris.
  - Her mother beat her. She suffered from chorea, a nervous disease with convulsive movements, and was interned at **La Salpêtrière at 14**.
  - At a masked Mi-Carême ball there, carried away by a waltz, she danced; the audience made a circle and applauded, and "her vocation was born".
  - She danced at the Moulin Rouge, Jardin de Paris and Folies-Bergère; the artwork page also names Les Décadents and Le Divan Japonais. Contemporaries called her "the incarnation of dance" (OFF).
  - In **1911, aged 42**, she married the poster artist **Maurice Biais** and left the stage for a quiet life in Jouy-en-Josas (OFF).
  - The V4 claims "1905 / print-maker / 1929 crash" are CONTRA (A1).
- **Quotes:** "the incarnation of dance" (OFF wording, in quote marks on the page). No other quotes.
- **Hooks:**
  1. A girl in a hospital for nervous disorders discovers dance at a patients' ball (OFF).
  2. The Englishman in the background walks out of this picture into one of Lautrec's best-known prints (OFF).
  3. Painted fast and thin on cardboard: you can see the brown support through the paint (OFF on technique; "visible support" is an observation for the on-site check).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/jane-avril-dansant-7998 (OFF); /en/node/288022 (OFF); plan-guide PDFs /sites/default/files/2026-03/plan-guide ORSAY Europe printemps26.pdf, 2023-11 PLANGUIDE_ORSAY_FAE_automne23.pdf, 2022-10 PLANGUIDE ORSAY FAE automne22_0.pdf (OFF, snippet); collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl020228195 (SEC1, RF number).

### 061a · Toulouse-Lautrec — *Clownesse Cha-U-Kao* (The Clown Cha-U-Kao)
- **Facts:** 1895 · oil on cardboard · 58 × 43 cm (catalogue snippet) · **RF 2027** (catalogue snippet; this is the inventory number, not a date) · bequest of Count Isaac de Camondo, accepted 1911 for the Louvre. It was probably bought from the artist by Maurice Joyant in 1895 for 500 francs (OFF).
- **Location:** room not found in snippets (NONE). It is presumably shown with the other Lautrecs; confirm on site.
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF commentary):
  - A private view, not the stage: she is in her dressing room or a private room, trying to fasten a **large yellow ruffle** to the bodice of her costume.
  - The ruffle takes up a large part of the composition. It is echoed by the **yellow ribbon** that "almost ironically" ties her **white toupee**.
  - Above a small table, a portrait or **mirror reflects an elderly man**, who "could be a close friend, an admirer or a customer".
  - Green walls, a red sofa, lively brushstrokes over the whole surface.
- **Story** (OFF):
  - She was a dancer and clown at the **Nouveau Cirque and the Moulin Rouge**.
  - Her "Japanese-sounding" name is a phonetic spelling of **"chahut"**, an acrobatic dance derived from the cancan, plus **"chaos"**, the uproar when she came on stage.
  - Like La Goulue, she is a recurring figure in Lautrec's work. Her clowning and acrobatics put her closer to the circus, which also fascinated Lautrec.
- **Hooks:** the name that is a pun ("chahut-chaos"); the man in the mirror whom nobody can identify (OFF).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/clownesse-cha-u-kao-724; works-in-focus "the-clown-cha-u-kao-2996"; catalogue notice nnumid=724 (OFF).

### 061b · Toulouse-Lautrec — *Panneaux pour la baraque de la Goulue, à la Foire du Trône à Paris* (Panels for La Goulue's booth)
- **Facts:** 1895 · oil (on canvas) · 298 × 316 cm (snippet figure; check whether it applies to one panel or to the set) · inventory number NOT found.
- **Provenance** (OFF):
  - Bought from the fairground by the collector **Viau in 1900**, which saved them from the weather.
  - The **Hodebert gallery cut them up in 1926**.
  - In 1929 the national museums acquired eight pieces and fragments from **Hugues Simon** and assigned them to the Louvre. A fragment of Valentin le Désossé was given by **M. Auffray** in 1929.
  - **Reconstituted in 1930.** Now at Orsay.
- **Location:** NONE on the artwork page snippet. Plan-guides from 2022–23 list "Panneaux de la Goulue" under the level-5 Galerie Françoise Cachin (OFF, older). **Location unconfirmed for 2026**; on-site check needed. If they are not on level 2, track 061 should be Cha-U-Kao alone, or Cha-U-Kao plus a mention of the panels.
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF):
  - Two monumental scenes. **Left panel:** *La Danse au Moulin-Rouge, La Goulue et Valentin le Désossé*, the acrobatic dances that made the pair famous.
  - **Right panel:** *La Danse mauresque*, the belly-dance show La Goulue performed inside her booth, "loosely inspired by eastern choreographies".
  - The two panels hung outside, at the entrance to her booth.
- **Story** (OFF):
  - La Goulue, a famous cabaret dancer, wanted to perform in her own booth at the **Foire du Trône**. Lautrec accepted the commission for two decorative panels.
  - The panels stood "exposed to the elements".
- **Hook:** a masterpiece painted for a funfair stall, left out in the rain, sliced into saleable pieces by a dealer, then pieced back together by the State (OFF, all steps).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/panneaux-pour-la-baraque-de-la-goulue-la-foire-du-trone-paris-3007; /en/node/21420; m.musee-orsay.fr …/la-danse-au-moulin-rouge-23378 (OFF).

### 062 · Édouard Vuillard — *Jardins publics* (Public Gardens)
- **Facts:** 1894 · **distemper** (peinture à la colle) on canvas · inventory numbers not found.
- **Orsay panels** (OFF): *Fillettes jouant*, *L'Interrogatoire*, and the triptych *La Conversation, Les Nourrices, L'Ombrelle rouge*. Five panels in all.
- **Location:** **Niveau médian, salle 72** (OFF, triptych page). A Purcell photo shows a "salle consacrée à Vuillard" with the five panels hung in this left-to-right order: Fillettes jouant, L'Interrogatoire, Les Nourrices, La Conversation, L'Ombrelle rouge (OFF).
- **Story** (OFF):
  - A cycle of **nine panels** commissioned in 1894 by **Alexandre Natanson** for his mansion on the Avenue du Bois, now Avenue Foch.
  - Natanson directed **La Revue blanche**, the journal that published the Nabis.
  - The panels decorated a large room used as living and dining room. The set was **broken up in 1929**. Five panels are reunited at Orsay and **hung "around the corner of the room, as they were originally"**.
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF):
  - Separate scenes, each with its own subtitle, joined by continuous ground and sky, light and colour: "harmonies of beige, green and blue enlivened by a few splashes of red".
  - Distemper gives a **matt surface like fresco**; Orsay compares it to frescoes "from Piero della Francesca to Puvis de Chavannes". This was the first time Vuillard used it on this scale.
- **Hooks:**
  1. You stand at the corner of a dining room that no longer exists; the panels turn the corner just as they did in 1894 (OFF).
  2. Find the red parasol (OFF title, colour accents).
  3. Nine panels, scattered in 1929, five back together here (OFF).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/jardins-publics-la-conversation-les-nourrices-lombrelle-rouge-8015; …/jardins-publics-linterrogatoire-8285; …/jardins-publics-fillettes-jouant-8284; Purcell photo …-127975 (OFF).

### 063a · Pierre Bonnard — *Femmes au jardin* (Women in the Garden)
- **Facts:** 1891 · distemper on canvas · four decorative panels at Orsay: *Femme à la pèlerine*, *Femme à la robe à pois blancs*, *Femme assise au chat*, *Femme à la robe quadrillée* · acquired 1984 (search summary of the OFF pages; check the acquisition mode). Inventory numbers not found.
- **Location:** *Femme à la pèlerine*: **Niveau médian, salle 72** (OFF). The other three panels' locations were not shown in snippets.
- **Story / look** (OFF, Bonnard *Paravent* commentary):
  - Bonnard first treated the **screen format as a series of decorative panels** in these 1891 *Femmes au jardin* (Orsay; another part is in the Kunsthaus Zürich).
  - His friends nicknamed him **"le Nabi très japonard"**.
  - The Japanese influence shows in the tall screen-panel format, sober tones, chequered treatment of clothes, mottled surfaces, and flat layering without depth.
- **Hooks:** "the very Japanese Nabi" (OFF nickname); four women like a folding screen pulled apart (OFF on the screen origin).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/femmes-au-jardin-femme-la-pelerine-8024, -8025, -8022, -8023; commentary paravent-19797 (OFF).

### 065 · Hector Guimard and the Paris Métro style
- **Works on level 2** (OFF): all in **Niveau médian, salle 64**:
  - *Banquette de fumoir*, 1897: jarrah wood and carved metal (modern upholstery), H 260 × L 262 cm. It came from the billiard room of the Roy property at Gévrils (Loiret), fitted out by Guimard in 1897–98.
  - Armchair from the **Castel Val**, 1903: pear wood with original embossed leather.
  - Armchair, c. 1900: green-painted cast-iron base.
  - Vase and base, 1905–07: cast iron.
  - Stained-glass window, 1897.
- **Look / story** (OFF, bench page):
  - Guimard was a leader of French Art Nouveau, whose central claim is "the unity of architecture, furniture and decoration". He designed all the furniture for his **Castel Béranger** (16th arrondissement).
  - On the asymmetrical bench, "one leg … seems to be a trunk sprouting supple branches that curve like vines".
- **Métro:**
  - Orsay owns a real Guimard Métro entrance: *Entrée ouverte de Métropolitain : station Montparnasse-Bienvenüe*, 1910, cast iron, stone, enamelled lava and tinted glass.
  - It was **given to the State in 1961 by the RATP's director-general Jacques Courson**, and transferred to Orsay in 1978 from the Musée national d'art moderne (OFF).
  - The Compagnie générale du Métropolitain turned to Guimard "after an unsuccessful competition" (OFF).
  - **Display location of the entrance: NONE.** Do not tell visitors it is on level 2.
  - Orsay also holds Guimard's preparatory drawings, shown in the display "Hector Guimard et la genèse du Métropolitain", 16 Mar – 14 Jul 2024, now over (paris.fr, parisbouge: SEC2).
- **Hooks:**
  1. The style Parisians still walk under every day: the Métro entrances (OFF).
  2. A sofa that grows like a tree (OFF).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/banquette-de-fumoir-19205; fauteuil-provenant-du-castel-val-22697; fauteuil-154369; vase-et-socle-3212; vitrail-19236; entree-ouverte-de-metropolitain-station-montparnasse-bienvenue-19242 (OFF); paris.fr accrochage page (SEC).

### 066a · Émile Gallé — *La Main aux algues et aux coquillages* (Hand with Seaweed and Shells)
- **Facts:** 1904 · crystal, partly blown and hot-modelled, with inclusions, applications and wheel engraving · H 33.4, W 13.4 cm · **gift, 1990** · inventory number not found.
- **Location:** **Niveau médian, salle 63** (OFF).
- **Story** (OFF):
  - "Doubtless the last work of crafted glass produced by the master from Nancy".
  - Shown at Nancy in 1904 in a showcase called **"The Deep Sea"** (*Les Profondeurs de la mer*).
  - Gallé made it **knowing he was about to die**: a farewell piece, confident in the "radiant existence" awaiting him. Orsay devoted a focus display to it in 2004.
- **What the visitor sees** (OFF): a hand rising from the sea. The shells are rendered naturalistically, while the hand's "gelatinous texture evokes the body of a jellyfish rather than human skin".
- **Hooks:** a glassmaker's last work, a hand reaching up from the deep (OFF). It also links to the Café Campana décor on level 5, an aquatic homage inspired by Gallé (A3, OFF).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/la-main-aux-algues-et-aux-coquillages-22694; exhibition page hand-seaweed-and-shells-emile-galles-artistic-testament (OFF).

### 067 · François Pompon — *Ours blanc* (Polar Bear)
- **Facts:** stone · 163 × 251 × 90 cm · carved 1923–33 (page dates) · **bought after a State commission of 1927** · inventory number not found.
- **History** (OFF):
  - Shown at the inauguration of new rooms at the Musée du Luxembourg from 1929; assigned to Orsay in 1986.
  - Carved by Pompon's practitioner **Jean-Joachim Supéry** at Malakoff and **delivered 16 Feb 1929**.
- **Location:** current artwork page: "**Niveau médian, Terrasse des sculptures 1880-1910**" (OFF). A Basilico photo is captioned "sur la terrasse côté Lille" (OFF). The audits gave "room 62" from page 15530, which may be a different cast or version. Use "on the sculpture terrace" and confirm on site.
- **Story** (OFF):
  - Pompon was for years one of the most sought-after **practitioners** in Paris, carving marble for **Rodin and Camille Claudel**.
  - From 1905, reacting against Rodin's expressionism, he left the human figure for animals observed at the **Jardin des Plantes**.
  - The plaster *Ours blanc* at the **1922 Salon d'Automne** gave him his first public success, **at 67**.
- **Quote** (OFF, verbatim in the commentary, English rendering): "I keep a large number of details destined to disappear. I do the animal with almost all its trappings. And then little by little, I eliminate…"
- **Hooks:**
  1. Fame at 67, after decades carving other men's marbles (OFF).
  2. A bridge to Rodin and Claudel, the next two stops: Pompon worked for both (OFF).
  3. Pompon is the source of the dressing-gown story about *Balzac* (see 068).
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/ours-blanc-15496; m.musee-orsay.fr …/ours-blanc-365.html; Basilico photo …-148471 (OFF).

### 068 · Auguste Rodin — *Balzac* (plaster)
- **Facts:** 1898 · plaster · 285 × 125 × 132 cm, 220 kg · exhibited at the **1898 Salon of the Société nationale des beaux-arts** (OFF). Inventory number and provenance: NONE. One catalogue snippet mentions a Musée Rodin deposit of 1986, but it could not be tied to this plaster; do not use it.
- **Location:** **Niveau médian, Terrasse des sculptures 1880-1910** (OFF).
- **Story** (OFF):
  - In **1891 Zola**, newly elected president of the **Société des gens de lettres**, chose Rodin for a monument to Balzac, almost half a century after the writer's death.
  - At the 1898 Salon critics poured scorn on the "formless block". They compared it to "**a toad in a sack, a statue still wrapped, a block of salt caught in a shower**" and nicknamed it "**the menhir, the snowman**".
  - The Société refused the work, which broke with commemorative conventions and ignored the demand for a realistic likeness. It commissioned **Falguière** instead, for the avenue de Friedland.
  - **Rodin kept the statue, returned the money, and refused all offers to buy it.**
  - A bronze was finally inaugurated on the **boulevard Raspail in 1939** (OFF, À nos grands hommes database).
  - Pompon's account: Rodin "**trempa sa robe de chambre dans une grande bassine de plâtre et habilla ainsi son étude**" ("dipped his dressing gown in a big tub of plaster and dressed his study with it"). OFF, quoted in an Orsay entry; read it as Pompon's testimony, not a fact.
- **Quotes:** the critics' comparisons, as quoted by Orsay in English translation (OFF); Pompon's sentence (OFF).
- **Hooks:**
  1. The monument nobody wanted: insulted in 1898, cast in bronze 41 years later (OFF).
  2. A dressing gown dipped in plaster (Pompon, OFF).
  3. Orsay also owns Steichen's photograph *Balzac – The Open Sky* (OFF page balzac-open-sky-4292); mention only, it may not be on show.
- **Sources:** musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/balzac-15643; honore-de-balzac-7692; anosgrandshommes.musee-orsay.fr object 5541; balzac-open-sky-4292 (OFF).

### 069 · Camille Claudel — *L'Âge mûr* (Maturity)
- **Facts:** c. 1902 · bronze group in three parts · 114 × 163 × 72 cm. It is **the first bronze**, cast in the artist's lifetime, and **entered Orsay in 1982** (OFF). Inventory number not found.
- **Location:** "**Salle 55**" per the search summary of the official page; the level is not stated in the snippet (OFF, partial). G lists it on level 2 with its position unconfirmed. **On-site check needed.**
- **Story** (OFF commentary):
  - After the break-up, **Rodin tried to help Claudel through an intermediary** and obtained a **State commission (1895)** from the director of the Beaux-Arts. The group was exhibited in 1899.
  - The bronze was **never ordered** and the plaster never delivered. **Captain Tissier commissioned the first bronze in 1902.** Orsay holds Claudel's letters to Tissier, including one saying that **F. Rudier asked 6,000 francs** for the casting (OFF, archive items 266518, 266515).
  - Orsay's reading: the group evokes **Rodin's hesitation between his former mistress, "who was to prevail", and Claudel**, who bends forward to hold him. Claudel embodies herself in the figure she called **"L'Implorante"**.
  - "The man at the end of his maturity is vertiginously drawn by age while he extends a useless hand towards youth". Flying draperies speed the movement.
- **Quote** (OFF, as quoted by Orsay in translation): Paul Claudel: "My sister Camille, beseeching, humiliated on her knees, this superb, this proud woman, and do you know what is being torn from her, at this very moment, before your eyes, it is her soul."
- **Facts confirmed in A2 (OFF):** Claudel was interned in March 1913 aged 48 and died in 1943. The V4 claim that "Rodin tried to suppress the work" is contradicted by Orsay. Do not use it.
- **Hooks:**

### 072 · The way out: terraces, and the exit during the 2026–2028 works
- **Terraces** (OFF):
  - On level 2 the sculpture terraces run along both sides of the nave ("Terrasse Seine", "Terrasse Lille", plus the "Terrasse Rodin" on the plan) and **overlook the central aisle** below.
  - They show sculpture of about 1880–1910: Rodin (*L'Âge d'airain*, *Balzac*), Maillol, Bourdelle, Pompon, Joseph Bernard. The Seine terrace also has monumental Third Republic sculpture (Coutan's *Chasseurs d'aigles*, Deloye's *Saint Marc*; Purcell captions).
  - From the terraces the visitor sees the nave from above. Rodin's *Gates of Hell* stands at the far end of the nave, between the towers (A2/G, OFF).
  - A "light on the Seine" view from level 2 is NOT documented: do not promise a river view.
  - The Restaurant du musée, in the former Hôtel d'Orsay dining room, is on level 2 (G, OFF).
- **Works 2026–2028** (OFF, "Travaux au musée d'Orsay" and magazine 2 Mar 2026):
  - The museum stays **open throughout**, from **March 2026 to summer 2028**.
  - **Phase 1:** 10 Mar 2026 – Q1 2027. **Phase 2:** Q1 2027 – summer 2028, extending to the forecourt and the glass canopy (marquise).
  - **Entrance and exit are being separated.** A new **exit on the Seine side** will free about 500 m². A new **bookshop** goes in the **Montherlant hall**, the former foyer of the Hôtel d'Orsay, and visitors leave onto **quai Valéry-Giscard-d'Estaing**.
  - The **Quai entrance** serves visitors with timed tickets and Paris Museum Pass holders with a time slot.
  - "Entrances may be temporarily modified depending on the progress of the work".
  - Groups were not admitted **10 Jun – 5 Oct 2026** because of work in the reception area.
- **Generic wording for the audio:** "To leave, go back down to the ground floor and follow the exit signs; during the building work the way out may change, so trust the signs." Avoid naming entrances, numbers or the bookshop as fixed points; the arrangement will change again in 2028.

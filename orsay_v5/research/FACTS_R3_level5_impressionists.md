### 024 · The clock window over Paris (level 5)
- Building: the former Gare d'Orsay, built for the 1900 Exposition universelle. Victor Laloux was chosen in 1898 by the Compagnie des chemins de fer d'Orléans. Station and hotel were built in two years and inaugurated on 14 July 1900. — musee-orsay.fr/en/node/60 "Histoire du musée" (OFF)
- The conversion deliberately left traces of the station visible, "including the metal structure, clocks, and one of the seven domes". — node/60 (OFF)
- The museum was inaugurated by President Mitterrand on 1 Dec 1986 and opened to the public on 9 Dec 1986. The project was chosen in 1979 from six proposals. — node/60 (OFF)
- Café Campana is "at the exit of the Impressionist gallery … behind one of the great exterior clocks". — A3, restauration page (OFF)
- The Campana café sits in the Pavillon Amont, so the viewing clock at the gallery entrance is probably the downstream one. — A2 19.1 (SEC2 for Campana in the Pavillon Amont; the inference is unverified)
- The view through the transparent dial runs across the Seine to the Louvre and the Tuileries, and on a clear day to Sacré-Cœur. — fabulousmomlife.com, explorest.com (**WEAK**; check on site and describe only what can be seen)
- Archive photos titled "Restauration d'une des grandes horloges lumineuses donnant sur le quai" and "Réparation de l'horloge au moyen d'un bras télescopique installé sur le quai Seine" (Jim Purcell, musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/…-156259, …-156265, OFF). These show that the clocks face the quai and were restored during the conversion. Dates were not given in the snippets.
- Hooks: (1) a railway clock that once timed trains for the 1900 World's Fair now frames the Louvre opposite (node/60 OFF + view on site); (2) "a station turned museum that kept its clocks" (node/60 OFF).
- Do not use: the clock's diameter, whether it still runs, or Aulenti "making the dial a window". All are unverified (A2 19.2/19.4).
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/node/60 (OFF); https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/restauration-dune-des-grandes-horloges-lumineuse-donnant-sur-le-quai-156259 (OFF); A2, A3.

### 025 · Manet — *Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe* (Luncheon on the Grass)
- 1863 · oil on canvas · 207 × 265 cm · **RF 1668**. Provenance: Jean-Baptiste Faure (1878–1898) → Durand-Ruel (1898–1900) → Étienne Moreau-Nélaton (1900–1906) → given to the national museums in 1906 → at Orsay since 1986. — musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/le-dejeuner-sur-lherbe-904 (OFF)
- **Location:** "Rez-de-chaussée, Salle 18" per two OFF snippets of the artwork page; room 29, level 5, per A1 and G (SEC2/WEAK). **Conflict; check on site.** A 1986 archive photo, "Rez-de-chaussée, 'Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe' d'Edouard Manet" (Jim Purcell, …-151952 and …-128230), shows its first hang on the ground floor (OFF).
- Look: Manet "paid tribute to Europe's artistic heritage by borrowing from Titian's *Concert champêtre*" and took inspiration from Marcantonio Raimondi's engraving after Raphael's *Judgement of Paris*. Orsay's comment: it "can perhaps be considered as the departure point for Modern Art". — 904 (OFF)
- Story: shown at the Salon des Refusés of 1863, opened by Napoleon III's decree, under the title "Le Bain". — A1 Track 09 row 4 (OFF/SEC2, confirmed)
- Hooks: (1) the Monet fragments in room 29 were painted as "both a tribute and a challenge" to this painting (Monet page, OFF); (2) the first owner was the opera baritone Faure, who also owned Monet's *Coquelicots* (see 027) (OFF, both pages).
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/le-dejeuner-sur-lherbe-904 (OFF); A1.

### 026 · Monet — *Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe* (fragments)
- Orsay holds **two fragments**:
  - **RF 1987 12**, the central fragment: 248.7 × 218 cm, 97 kg with frame. Accepted by the State as a dation (payment of tax in kind) in 1987. — …/le-dejeuner-sur-lherbe-25651 (OFF)
  - **RF 1957 7**, the left fragment, hung to the left of RF 1987 12. Given by Georges Wildenstein in 1957. — same page (OFF snippet; check the RF number on the label)
  - A third fragment is not at Orsay. Its location was not looked up.
- Room: **Niveau supérieur, salle 29** (OFF).
- Story: begun in spring 1865. The whole canvas measured "over four metres by six" and was meant as "both a tribute and a challenge to Manet". It was abandoned in 1866, just before the Salon. Monet left it with his landlord as security for rent and found it mouldy when he got it back in 1884. He then cut it up and kept three fragments. — 25651 (OFF)
- Hooks: (1) rent security, then mould, then scissors; (2) the largest canvas of his youth, now in pieces.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/le-dejeuner-sur-lherbe-25651 (OFF); …/le-dejeuner-sur-lherbe-10737 and node/85245 (the second fragment's pages, OFF, details not read).

### 028 · Monet — *Femmes au jardin* (Women in the Garden)
- c. 1866 · oil on canvas · 255 × 205 cm (285 × 234.5 with frame). Bought from Monet by the State in **1921** for the Musée du Luxembourg; at Orsay since 1986. — …/femmes-au-jardin-807 (OFF)
- **Room: Rez-de-chaussée, salle 18 (ground floor), NOT level 5** (807 + node/290886, OFF).
- Story: painted in 1866 in the garden of a house Monet rented in the Paris suburbs. It was a "twofold challenge": painting outdoors, and on a format usually kept for history painting. He lowered the canvas into a trench with a pulley to reach the top. Camille posed for the three figures on the left. — 807 (OFF)
- Restoration: carried out by Orsay with the C2RMF. Old repairs had deteriorated and the varnish had yellowed. Cleaning brought back "dazzling whites" against bluish-grey shadows. Completed 2022 per the search summary (OFF snippet; check the year).
- Hooks: the trench and pulley; one model, three women.
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/femmes-au-jardin-807, https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/node/290886 (OFF).

### 031 · Monet — *La Gare Saint-Lazare*
- 1877 · oil on canvas · 75 × 105 cm · **RF 2775**. Caillebotte bought it from Monet on **10 March 1878 for 685 francs**. Part of the Caillebotte bequest, accepted by the State in 1896 for the Luxembourg. — …/la-gare-saint-lazare-10897 (OFF)

### 034 · Degas — *Petite danseuse de quatorze ans* (Little Dancer Aged Fourteen)
- **Which version:** a posthumous **bronze** cast by the lost-wax method by the founder **Adrien-Aurélien Hébrard**, **cast 1921–1931**, after the wax model of 1878–1881. Patinated bronze with a **tulle tutu, satin ribbon and wooden base**. H. 98, L. 35.2 cm (103.8 × 48.8 × 50 with base). Acquired in **1931** thanks to the generosity of Degas's heirs and the Hébrard family. — …/petite-danseuse-de-quatorze-ans-961 (OFF). The original wax is in Washington (A3 26.7).
- Room: **Niveau supérieur, salle 32** (OFF).
- Story: shown at the Impressionist exhibition of 1881. About 150 wax or clay sculptures were found in Degas's studio at his death in 1917. The bronze edition "tried to preserve the characteristics of the wax as much as possible". — 961 (OFF). The model was Marie van Goethem, born in Paris to Belgian parents (A3 26.1, SEC2).
- Do not use: the critics' quotes ("monkey", "Aztec") and the sister's arrest date (A3 26.2/26.3).
- Sources: https://www.musee-orsay.fr/en/artworks/petite-danseuse-de-quatorze-ans-961 (OFF).

### 035 · Degas — *Dans un café*, also called *L'Absinthe*
- 1875–1876 · oil on canvas · 92 × 68.5 cm · bequest of comte Isaac de Camondo, 1911. — m.musee-orsay.fr commentary dans-un-cafe-167 (OFF). RF number not seen; RF 1984 is unconfirmed.
- **Room: NONE.**
- Look: the woman sits on a banquette behind a marble table with a glass of absinthe. Man and woman sit side by side, "each immersed in silent isolation", with empty, sad gazes. - Hook: two real friends cast as strangers. The café is the same Nouvelle Athènes district Monet moved into for Saint-Lazare (031 OFF).

### 038 · Sisley — *La Barque pendant l'inondation, Port-Marly* (The Boat in the Flood)

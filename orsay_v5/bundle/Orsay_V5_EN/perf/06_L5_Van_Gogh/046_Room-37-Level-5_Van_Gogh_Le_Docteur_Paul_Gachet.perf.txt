[amused] Dr Paul Gachet had a second life, and a second name. He was an amateur painter and engraver, and he signed that work Paul Van Ryssel.

As a doctor, his speciality was melancholy and nervous illness. He was close to the Impressionists, and bought works by Cézanne, Monet, Renoir and Guillaumin. He was one of Van Gogh's first collectors. So when Van Gogh left the hospital at Saint-Rémy, Theo advised him to go to Auvers, where this doctor could keep an eye on his health.

[pause] A special bond grew between the two men. Within days of arriving, Van Gogh made his first and only etching, a portrait of Gachet. The museum still has the copper plate, dated the twenty-fifth of May, 1890.

[quietly] Then came this painting. Look at the pose, heavy with melancholy, and at the colours, all cold. In a letter to Gauguin, Van Gogh said he had painted Gachet "with the deeply sad expression of our time." Not just one man's sadness. A whole era's.

But find the foxglove. It's a plant doctors valued for its healing properties, and the museum calls it the only touch of hope in the picture. A doctor's flower, for a doctor who was himself, perhaps, a little melancholy.

[warmly] Long after the doctor's death, his children, Marguerite and Paul, gave his collection to the Louvre, between 1949 and 1954. This room, the self-portrait and the church next door all came from that house in Auvers.

Then leave the Van Gogh rooms for room 38. Look for Seurat's large circus scene, a clown seen from behind in the front, inside a painted dark border. The next story is waiting there.

[lightly] Here is a painting that, the story goes, was turned down for being the wrong colour.

Gauguin painted Le Cheval blanc, The White Horse, in 1898, during his second stay in Tahiti. By then he had left Europe for good. Years before, he had said he wanted to escape "this European struggle for money". So there is an irony in what comes next.

[conspiratorial] According to the story, this canvas began as a commission from a pharmacist in Tahiti. And the pharmacist refused it, because the horse was too green. It's a lovely tale. It also rests on very little evidence, so hold it lightly.

[pause] Now judge for yourself. Find the horse that gives the painting its title. Ask yourself how white it really is.

Whatever a client may have thought, Gauguin was not a painter who kept to the expected colours. In these rooms you've seen him circle a woman's face like a Japanese print, blow a small Maori motif up into a giant idol, and paint a dog red. A horse with green in its coat is in good company.

The painting entered the national collections in 1927. It was restored in 2019.

[wry] If the pharmacist really did say no, he turned down a Gauguin over a shade of green.

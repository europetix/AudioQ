[lightly] This quiet little flood scene began life in a household that would end up joined to Monet's. Its first owner was a man named Hoschedé, and his wife was called Alice. Years later, Alice Hoschedé married Claude Monet. The picture went its own way, to the collector Isaac de Camondo, who left it to the nation. You'll meet his name again with the cathedrals.

But the picture itself is about water. In the same spring that Renoir was painting his dancers, the Seine climbed out of its bed.

Sisley had moved to Marly-le-Roi two years earlier. When the river flooded Port-Marly in the spring of 1876, he went out to paint it, and not just once. He made six pictures of that flood, and this is one of them.

[curious] Look at how he splits the work in two. The sky and the water take up most of the canvas, and he paints them in light, broad strokes. Then the house. It stands at an angle, and its pink and yellow walls are painted with real precision. The boat of the title is out there on the flood.

[pause] Everything that moves is loose, and the one thing that stays put is firm. [quietly] Give it a minute after the noise of the dance. A small picture, and a whole river out of place.

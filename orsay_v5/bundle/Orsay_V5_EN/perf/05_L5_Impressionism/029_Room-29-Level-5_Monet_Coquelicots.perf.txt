[warmly] In eighteen seventy-four, a group of painters set up a company of their own and hung an exhibition in Nadar's studio in Paris. In the catalogue, this little picture was number ninety-five.

After that giant picnic, Monet's Coquelicots is almost pocket-sized, about half a metre high. He painted it the year before the show, at Argenteuil.

Look at the two figures in the foreground, a woman under a sunshade and a child beside her. They're probably Camille and their son Jean. So this is not just a field of poppies. It's very likely a family walk.

[curious] The poppies give the picture its title and most of its colour. Let your eye follow the red across the field. Then come back to the woman and the child, two small figures wading through all that colour.

That show in Nadar's studio was only the beginning. Monet would go on to show at five of the group's exhibitions.

[lightly] The picture reached the nation in nineteen oh six, as a gift from the collector Étienne Moreau-Nélaton. [pause] In that same year, he also gave Manet's Déjeuner sur l'herbe. So the picnic Monet tried to rival and his own little field of poppies became public property together.

[warmly] Eight men fill this studio, and seven of them are here for the one at the easel.

Henri Fantin-Latour showed the picture at the Salon of eighteen seventy. Take the friends from left to right. At the far left, Otto Scholderer, a German painter. Then Manet himself, at the easel, and Renoir, in a hat. Next come Zacharie Astruc, sculptor and journalist, and Émile Zola. Edmond Maître, who worked at the Paris city hall. Then Frédéric Bazille, whose own studio you'll see in room 18, and at the right, Claude Monet.

[pause] Why gather them around Manet? Many of these men lived in the Batignolles district of Paris, and Fantin casts Manet as the head of their school. Zola put it this way. [quietly] "Around the painter scorned by the public, a united front of painters and writers has formed, considering him their master."

Find Manet at the easel first. Then let your eye travel along the group, one friend at a time, and notice how they hold together around him.

[lightly] Now the least famous name here, probably Scholderer, at the far left. In early twenty twenty-six the museum acquired a painting by him, and calls it a masterpiece. Fame can be slow, sometimes a century and a half slow.

Manet's own great provocation is on this floor too. Find Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe, the Luncheon on the Grass, in room 18, and play its track there.

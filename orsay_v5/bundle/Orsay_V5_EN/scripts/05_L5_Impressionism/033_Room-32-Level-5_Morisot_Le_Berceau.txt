Of all the paintings in these rooms, this may be the quietest. A young woman watches over a sleeping baby, and that's all that happens. She is the painter's sister, Edma. The baby in the cradle is Edma's daughter, Blanche.

Berthe Morisot painted it in eighteen seventy-two. Two years later she sent it to the first exhibition the Impressionists put on for themselves, and she was the first woman to show with the group.

And yet the museum calls it, without hesitation, Morisot's most famous painting.

Look at where the attention goes. Not only to the baby, but to the watching. Morisot was painting her own sister in a private moment. You almost feel you should lower your voice.

The same year as that first exhibition, Berthe married Eugène Manet. So Édouard Manet, whose paintings you met on the ground floor, became her brother-in-law.

The picture stayed in the family for more than half a century. Then, in nineteen thirty, the Louvre bought it. And the person who sold it was Blanche. The baby in the cradle, all grown up.

When Paris first saw this girl, she wasn't made of bronze at all. She was wax, with real hair and real fabric.

That was in eighteen eighty-one, at the Impressionists' exhibition. Degas had been working on the figure for about three years. His model was Marie van Goethem, a young dancer born in Paris to Belgian parents. The title tells you her age, fourteen.

Her dancing career did not last. The following year, she was dismissed.

So what exactly are you looking at? The figure in front of you is not the one Degas showed. His wax original is in Washington. Degas never had this cast made.

When he died in nineteen seventeen, about a hundred and fifty sculptures in wax or clay were found in his studio. In the years that followed, the founder Adrien-Aurélien Hébrard cast a bronze edition by the lost-wax method. The museum says the aim was to keep the character of the wax as much as possible. This cast was made in that campaign, and the museum acquired it with the help of Degas's heirs and the Hébrard family.

So take a slow walk round her. The body is patinated bronze, but look at the skirt, because that's real tulle. And the ribbon is real satin, just as the wax had real fabric. She's just under a metre tall, smaller than many people expect.

Everything else here is a careful copy. The tutu and the ribbon are real cloth. Somehow, that's enough to make her feel alive.

In the same spring that Renoir was painting his crowd of dancers, Sisley was painting a river that had climbed out of its bed.

Sisley had moved to Marly-le-Roi two years earlier. When the Seine flooded Port-Marly in the spring of 1876, he went out to paint it, and not just once. He made six pictures of that flood, and this is one of them.

Look at how he splits the work in two. The sky and the water take up most of the canvas, and he paints them in light, broad strokes. Then the house. It stands at an angle, and its pink and yellow walls are painted with real precision. Everything that moves is loose, and the one thing that stays put is firm. The boat of the title is out there on the flood.

There's a small story in who owned it first. Its first owner was a man named Hoschedé, and his wife was called Alice. Years later, Alice Hoschedé married Claude Monet. So this quiet flood scene began life in a household that would end up joined to Monet's.

After that, it passed through the dealer Durand-Ruel to the collector Isaac de Camondo, who left it to the nation in his will. You'll meet his name again in the next room.

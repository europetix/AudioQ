One of the eight men in this painting had only a few months to live. Fantin-Latour showed it at the Salon in the spring of eighteen seventy. Before the year was out, Frédéric Bazille had fallen in the Franco-Prussian War. He was twenty-six.

Fantin painted his friends in a studio, and the museum names them for us, from left to right. At the far left, Otto Scholderer, a German painter. Then Manet himself, at the easel, and Renoir, in a hat. Next come Zacharie Astruc, sculptor and journalist, and Émile Zola. Edmond Maître, who worked at the Paris city hall. Then Bazille, and at the right, Claude Monet.

Why gather them around Manet? Many of these men lived in the Batignolles district of Paris, and Fantin casts Manet as the head of their school. Zola put it this way, in the museum's translation. "Around the painter scorned by the public, a united front of painters and writers has formed, considering him their master."

Find Manet at the easel first. Then let your eye travel along the group, one friend at a time.

One small twist. The least famous name here is probably Scholderer, at the far left. In early 2026 the museum acquired a painting by him, and it calls that painting a masterpiece. Fame can be slow, sometimes a century and a half slow.

Manet's own great provocation is nearby. Find Le Déjeuner sur l'herbe, the Luncheon on the Grass, in room 18 on this floor, and play its track there.

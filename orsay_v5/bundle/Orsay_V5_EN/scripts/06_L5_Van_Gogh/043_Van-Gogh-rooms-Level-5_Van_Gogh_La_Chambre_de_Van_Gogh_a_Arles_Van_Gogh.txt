Van Gogh painted his bedroom to get his revenge. That's his word, not mine.

When he first painted this room in Arles, in the autumn of 1888, he wrote to Theo about it. He had been made to rest, and he said he painted it "to take my revenge for the enforced rest that I was obliged to take." Then he explained what he wanted the picture to do. "Looking at the painting should rest the mind, or rather, the imagination."

Look at how he goes about it. Nothing here is subtle. Yellow for the bed and the chairs. Red for the floor. Pale lilac on the walls. He wanted the colour, simplified, to do the work of calm on its own.

This canvas is not that first one, and a larger repeat hangs in Chicago. About a year later, he painted this smaller version for his family in Holland. In his letters that autumn, he talks about sending smaller canvases home, for his mother and his sister, and the bedroom is among them. So this is the room he slept in, shrunk to fit a family wall.

It came into the French national collection in 1959, under the peace treaty with Japan that followed the Second World War.

A painting made to rest the mind, for a mother far away, by a man who had been told to rest, and painted instead.

In room 36, find his night view of a river: deep blue sky and water, stars, and the orange gaslights of the town reflected below. Play its track there, even if the painting is away on loan. The track will tell you where to stand.

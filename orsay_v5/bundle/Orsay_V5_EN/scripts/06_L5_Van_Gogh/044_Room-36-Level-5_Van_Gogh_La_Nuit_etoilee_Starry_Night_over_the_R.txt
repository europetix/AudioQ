This night sky was not painted from memory. Van Gogh painted it at night, with the stars overhead, by the light of the gas lamps.

A practical word first. From mid-November 2026 to the end of March 2027, this painting is on loan to Tokyo. If the wall is empty, go back to the Bedroom, or to the self-portrait, and listen there. The story of this autumn belongs to the Bedroom too.

It's September 1888, in Arles. Van Gogh writes to Theo that he has painted a starry sky, and that it was actually painted at night, under a gas jet. Then he describes what he saw. "Against the green-blue field of the sky the Great Bear has a green and pink sparkle whose discreet paleness contrasts with the harsh gold of the gaslight."

So look. The museum describes this as a painter setting down the colours he perceived in the dark. The blues do most of the work: Prussian blue, ultramarine and cobalt. The town's gaslights glow an intense orange, and their reflections spill into the Rhône. Above it all, the stars sparkle like gemstones.

Now look right down at the bottom. There's a couple, a pair of lovers. They're easy to miss. But they change the mood. The museum says they make the whole scene more serene.

The next year, Theo sent this canvas with the Irises to the exhibition of the Indépendants. One critic, Félix Fénéon, called Van Gogh "a diverting colourist even in eccentricities like his Starry Night." A backhanded compliment. It hasn't aged as well as the painting.

And if you're standing at the Bedroom instead, notice the timing. A couple of weeks after this letter, he described that room to Theo, a picture meant to rest the mind. First the night outside, then the place to sleep.

In this room, find the church at Auvers, its windows deep blue under a cobalt sky. Play its track there.

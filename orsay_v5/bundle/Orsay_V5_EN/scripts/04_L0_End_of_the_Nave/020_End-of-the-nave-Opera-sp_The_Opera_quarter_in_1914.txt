Under your feet, a whole quarter of Paris has stopped. The streets around the Opéra are frozen here as they stood in nineteen fourteen.

The model's full title says exactly that. Maquette du quartier de l'Opéra, arrêtée en l'état en 1914. The Opéra quarter, stopped as it was in 1914. It was made between nineteen eighty-four and eighty-six, as the museum was being prepared, by Rémi Munier and a small team. It's resin, under plexiglass, and it measures six and a half metres on each side. The whole space around it was laid out by Richard Peduzzi.

Why build Paris in miniature inside an art museum? The museum's answer is simple. It wanted to show the Opéra and its quarter, a part of the city "profoundly transformed by the prefect Haussmann's major public works." You've just seen Carpeaux's dancers from its façade. Here is the street plan the building belonged to.

So look down and find Garnier's Opéra among the blocks. Then follow the streets out from it, one by one. From up here, the plan of the quarter is easier to read than it ever is on foot.

And think about the year it stops. Nineteen fourteen is the year the war began. Whatever came after, this little Paris never sees it.

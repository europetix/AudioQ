This door was made for a museum that was never built. Rodin was commissioned to make a great doorway for a museum of decorative arts. The museum never happened. The door never hung on any building. Yet Rodin worked on it from eighteen eighty until nineteen seventeen.

What stands here is his original plaster, six metres thirty-five high. Not a bronze cast.

Think about where you've walked. You came down a long nave full of sculpture, past Carpeaux's dancers, past the side rooms of the Realists and of Manet. And at the far end, all that time, there was this door. Since May twenty twenty-two it has stood here, between the two towers, on the axis of the central aisle, placed so that you see it as you come in. It has been waiting at the end of your walk since the very first step.

So give it the time it asks for. Start from a little way back, where it fills the end of the aisle. Then come closer, slowly, and let the detail take over from the whole. Around it, the museum has added works lent by the Musée Rodin, so the doorway has company.

And if you're hoping for The Thinker, you won't find him at Orsay. The museum doesn't have one. What it has is this doorway, and it is enough.

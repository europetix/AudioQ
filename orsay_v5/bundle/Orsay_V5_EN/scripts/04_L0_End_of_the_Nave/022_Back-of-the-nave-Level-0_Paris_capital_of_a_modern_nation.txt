The name of these rooms is a big claim. Paris, capitale d'une nation moderne. Paris, capital of a modern nation. They opened in May 2022, and they set out to show how that capital was made.

The story is the transformation of Paris under the Second Empire. The museum tells it with paintings, drawings, models and sculpture, all in one place. You've already met two of its landmarks. Carpeaux's dancers came from the façade of the new Opéra. And the model under the floor showed the Opéra quarter, which the museum says was profoundly transformed by Haussmann's public works.

The centrepiece here is a large painting by Victor Navlet. Its title is a small adventure in itself. Vue générale de Paris, prise de l'Observatoire en ballon. A general view of Paris, from the Observatory, in a balloon, dated eighteen fifty-five.

Did Navlet go up himself? The title doesn't say, and we won't guess. But that is the viewpoint he gives you. Not a street corner, not a single monument, but the whole city at once, spread out below.

So take a moment with it. See how much of the city you can recognise from the air. Then remember the two halves of these rooms. One is the city seen whole, from above. The other is a single great building, Garnier's Opéra. Together they're a portrait of a capital rebuilding itself.

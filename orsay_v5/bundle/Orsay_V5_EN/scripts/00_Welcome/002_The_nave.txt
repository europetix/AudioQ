Everything in this museum hangs off one long spine, and you're standing at the top of it. The museum itself calls this great hall the main artery of the visit. Learn it once, and you won't get lost.

Down the middle runs the central aisle, lined with sculpture. On either side, at this ground level, galleries open off the hall. One side is called the Lille side, after the street behind it. The other is the Seine side, towards the river.

Along the hall, the terraces of level two look down over the nave and lead into more rooms. And above them is level five, the top floor of galleries. The nave is built in Buxy stone, with travertine chosen to match the stone of Laloux's façade.

Here's the plan for our walk. We begin on this floor. Academic painting first, on the Lille side, then Realism and Manet on the Seine side. Then the far end of the nave, for the Opéra and Rodin. After that, up to level five for the Impressionists, Van Gogh and Gauguin. Then down to level two for Toulouse-Lautrec, the Nabis, Art Nouveau and the sculpture terraces, and out.

Now look all the way down the aisle. At the far end, between the two towers, exactly on the line of this aisle, stands Rodin's Gates of Hell. Since twenty twenty-two it has stood there, in view from the moment you come in. It will be the last thing we see on this floor.

The first stop is in this aisle. Find Thomas Couture's enormous painting of a Roman feast, nearly eight metres wide, and play its track there.

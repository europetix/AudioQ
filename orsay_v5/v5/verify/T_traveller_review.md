# T — Traveller review, Orsay V5 (67 tracks)

Reviewer stance: first-time visitor, phone and earphones, no prior plan of the museum. I read PLAN.md, the spoken text of all 67 tracks (001–067) and the Orangerie reference track 046 (Soutine). No track was edited.

**Verdict:** It's good. It sounds like a warm, well-read friend most of the time, and roughly 15 tracks are as good as the Orangerie reference or better. Three things hold it back:
1. **Route on level 5.** There are long stretches of "look in the Impressionist rooms" with no room number, and there is no orientation at all on arrival on level 2.
2. **Repetition.** The clock speech is given twice almost word for word (024 and 054). "The museum says/calls…" appears **92 times**, and several anecdotes are told twice.
3. **Length.** 18,393 spoken words is about **2 h 20 of audio alone**. With walking and looking, the visit is closer to **3 h 30**, not 2 h 30.

---

## 1. Can I follow the route?

**Overall: yes on level 0, mostly yes on level 5, shaky on the descent to level 2.** Every track ends with a "find … play its track there" cue, and the visual descriptions in the cues are excellent: the "nearly eight metres wide" Couture, the "yellow hat chasing a red ball", the "glass hand rising out of the sea". These descriptions are what will actually get people there.

### Level changes
| Cue | Verdict | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| **023 → level 5** | Clear | "That's the end of the ground floor. Take the escalators or the lifts up to level 5. At the top, find the great clock window." Simple and correct. |
| **054 → level 2** | Weak | "Take the lift or the stairs down to level two. Find room 68." On level 2 I have no idea which way room 68 is: no side, no sign to follow, no landmark. This is the only place in the tour where I would stand still and look lost. There is a second problem. The Lautrec hedge ("older plans placed Lautrec on level 5") is only in 055's @where, and it isn't spoken. If Lautrec turns out to be upstairs, I have already left level 5. **Fix:** in 054, add one spoken line before the descent: "On level 2, follow the signs for Toulouse-Lautrec and the Nabis." Do the on-site check on Lautrec's level before recording. |

### Optional 012
The 011 cue is clear and tactful ("small and very explicit… Its track is optional. Otherwise, go on to Manet's Olympia in room 14"). 012's first sentence states that it is optional and gives the exit. Both cues land on room 14, so skipping costs nothing. **Pass.** One wrinkle: 011's "Either way, play its track there" has an ambiguous "its". Say "play the matching track".

### Other route weak spots
- **Unconfirmed rooms on level 5.** 12 of the 31 level-5 tracks cue only by theme: 027, 028, 034, 035, 036, 039, 040, 043, 049, 050, 051, 052 (053 also gives no reliable room number). The stretch 026 → 027 → 028 → (031) → (032–033) → 034 → 035 → 036 → (037) zigzags between numbered and unnumbered rooms. If Bazille hangs downstairs in room 18, as the PLAN coordinator suspects, I will hunt for it on level 5 and give up. **Fix:** say it once, in 024: "If you can't find a work within a minute, play its track anyway, or skip it. The next cue will get you back on track."
- **"Back of the nave" vs "end of the nave".** 017 → 018 ("back of the nave"), 020 ("end of the nave") and 021 → 022 ("At the back of the nave") use different words for what seems to be the same area. To a first-timer, "back" could mean the entrance end. Pick one term ("the far end of the nave, by the towers") and use it everywhere.
- **"Seine gallery" (014 → 015)** is never explained in 002, which only names the "Seine side".
- **Level 2, 061 → 062 "From here, find room 57"** gives no direction. 057 → 058 → 059 are good ("next door, in room 71"), but 059 → 060 jumps from room 72 to room 64 without a direction either.
- **016 Déjeuner hedge.** The room-29 hedge is only in @where. That's fine if the app shows @where; if the app is audio-only, it isn't. Confirm on site.

## 2. Live guide or lecture?

**Mostly a live guide.** The house style matches the Orangerie reference: a one-line hook ("Jane Avril discovered she could dance in a hospital", "someone threw a bottle of ink at this sculpture"), then the story, then "now look", then a closing line. Dialogue-like asides ("Draw your own conclusions", "See if you can find her") are well placed. The emotional range is real: the MNR room, Claudel and Bazille are handled gravely and without sensationalism.

**Where it slides into lecture:**
- **"The museum says / calls / points out / in the museum's words": 92 occurrences, in 56 of 67 tracks.** The Orangerie reference uses it once. Repeated this often, it sounds like footnotes read aloud and takes the warmth out of a friend's voice. Keep it for the 10–15 genuinely striking quotes ("Venus has become a prostitute", "a certain alcoholic imbalance") and paraphrase the rest.
- **The fact-checker's voice leaks into the script.** 026 ("Not four, as is sometimes said, but five"), 035 ("Older guides gave her name as Marguerite Legrand"), 042 ("guides told a touching story… It belongs to a different picture"), 045 ("Some guides have called them black"), 052 ("rests on very little evidence, so hold it lightly"), 053 ("rather than put words in his mouth"). A first-time visitor never heard the wrong version, so correcting it wastes the hook. 042 gets away with it because it turns the correction into a story; the others don't.
- **Provenance endings.** A dozen tracks end, or nearly end, on who bequeathed what to whom: 004, 007, 008, 017, 026, 036, 037, 039, 040, 045, 047, 052, 066. Twice in a row (039 → 040, both "the bequest of Isaac de Camondo, 1911") it really drags.
- **Statistics by ear.** 018 gives seven numbers in a row (100,000 / 60,000 / 45,000 / 13,000 / 2,200 / 225 / 15). Nobody can hold that through earphones. Keep three.

**Flat or dry tracks:** 020, 021, 022 (three low-energy "city plan" tracks in a row), 026, 036, 038, 040, 052, 053, 054, 060, 066. See section 5.

**Same openers, jokes and formulas**
- "Here's the twist / a nice twist / one small twist / here's the turn / here's the nice part": 006, 015, 016, 026, 051.
- "The part most people miss / never learn": 019, 032.
- "It's hard to argue / It's hard to disagree" as a closing wink: 013, 042.
- "Now look…" opens a paragraph 14 times; "So look…" 7 times.
- "Ask yourself…" appears 4 times (016, 052, 053, 064).
- The three-part list tagline ("Three women, one field, ten years of looking", "Six paintings. One jug. And no hurry at all", "A severed head, a girl and a lyre…") is used often enough to become audible as a device.
- Every cue ends "Play its track there" (57 times). That is acceptable as a functional signal and is the house style, but about once every 10 tracks a variation would help ("…and press play", "it's waiting for you there").
- Delivery tags are well spread (warmly 14, amused 12, lightly 11, quietly 10, curious 9, wry 5). No monotony there.

**Style consistency:** 001–012 spell years out ("eighteen forty-seven"). From 013 on, numerals are mixed in ("1876", "2026 to 2027"). Pick one convention for TTS. Date-bound phrases will age: "This year" (035, twice), "This is its fortieth year" (067), "In early 2026" (015). Fine for 2026–27, but log them for the next revision.

## 4. Fatigue: order and length for about 2 h 30

| Level | Tracks | Spoken words | Audio at ~130 wpm | Realistic time with walking and looking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 001–023 | 6,489 | ~50 min | ~1 h 15 |
| 5 | 024–054 | 8,273 | ~64 min | ~1 h 40 (crowded rooms) |
| 2 | 055–067 | 3,631 | ~28 min | ~40 min |
| **Total** | 67 | **18,393** | **~2 h 20** | **~3 h 30** |

All tracks are within the target bands (R 224–279, W 262–352, ANCHOR 351, CLOSE 311). That isn't the problem. **The problem is 67 stops.** The 2 h 30 figure is the audio time, not the visit time.

- **Level 0 ends on a low-energy run.** 018 (MNR, heavy) → 019 → 020 → 021 → 022 → 023 means three architecture or city-plan tracks back to back. After about an hour on foot, these are the ones people will skip. Merge 020 and 021 into one Opéra-models track, and mark 022 as optional.
- **Level 5 is the marathon.** It has 31 tracks, the most crowded rooms (Renoir, Van Gogh), and it comes when the visitor has already been in the building for over an hour. Fatigue peaks at 047–053: Seurat, Signac, Bernard, then four Gauguin and Pont-Aven tracks in a row. Consider making 036 (Sisley), 040 (Cézanne still life), 048 (Signac) and 052 (Cheval blanc) optional, or cutting them.
- **The breaks are in the right places.** 024 (clock window) and 054 (café) are well placed. But 054 says "a good moment to breathe" without saying you may sit down or have a coffee. Say it plainly; the plan forbids prices, not the invitation.
- **Level 2 is the right length** and ends on a high note (Claudel → Pompon → Balzac → farewell). 067's recap could be shorter so the goodbye lands while legs still work.
- **Recommended:** a "short route" set of about 45 tracks, announced in 002 ("tracks marked optional can be skipped if you're short of time"). This brings the visit down to roughly 2 h 30.

## Summary of fixes, by priority
1. Remove the duplicated clock passage from 054, and add level-2 orientation and the Lautrec-level check before the descent.
2. Remove the items the PLAN says must go: the pharmacist story (052) and the Renoir story (029).
3. Cut "the museum says/calls" from 92 to about 15, and delete the "older guides said…" corrections (026, 035, 045, 052, 053).
4. De-duplicate Bazille (027), Gauguin's age and masses (053), the wedding-portrait reveal (057), the 067 recap, and the Camondo and Faure chains.
5. Add a "can't find it? play or skip" rule in 024 and an "optional tracks" rule in 002, and mark about 20 tracks optional to fit a real 2 h 30 visit.
6. Tighten the 008 and 044 fallbacks so their "look at…" lines are conditional.

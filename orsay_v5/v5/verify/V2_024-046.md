## 2) Problems only

### 024. WEAK
- **Quote:** "You're looking at the dial from behind, so the numbers read backwards and the hands seem to turn the wrong way."
- **Why:** No fact sheet describes the dial seen from inside. R3 024 has only the "transparent dial" view, graded WEAK. PLAN row 024 allows "time running backwards in the glass" as something visible, but this needs an on-site check.
- **Closest evidence:** R3 024: "No official text on the clock window itself (size, mechanism, the view)". PLAN 024: "keep to what you can see".
- **Fix:** Keep the sentence only if the site check confirms it. Otherwise use "From in here, you see the clock from the wrong side."

### 025. NOTFOUND
- **Quote:** "the young Claude Monet started the biggest painting of his life."
- **Why:** R3 says only "the largest canvas of his youth". "Of his life" is an overclaim.
- **Closest evidence:** R3 (Monet fragments): "Hooks: (2) the largest canvas of his youth, now in pieces."
- **Fix:** "In the spring of eighteen sixty-five, the young Claude Monet started the biggest painting of his youth."
- **Low note:** "Manet's Déjeuner sur l'herbe, the painting you met earlier on this tour" is fine for the main route. If the site check moves Manet's painting to room 29, as the hedge in 016 allows, it will hang in this same room. Consider "the painting by Manet you have already met".

### 029. CONTRA (breaks a Coordinator decision)
- **Quote:** "Renoir later told a colourful story about how Monet talked his way in. [pause] Nobody has ever been able to document it. What we do know is that he got in, and that he came back again and again."
- **Why:** The PLAN Coordinator decision says the undocumented Renoir story "must not be mentioned at all (not even as 'undocumented'). Remove it."
- **Closest evidence:** PLAN, Coordinator decisions, 029. A3 22.2.
- **Fix:** Cut the first two sentences. Replace the third with "He got in, and he came back again and again."

### 032. WEAK (wording could mislead)
- **Quote:** "Everything else here is a careful copy. The tutu and the ribbon are the real thing."
- **Why:** The sheets say only that the cast has a tulle tutu and a satin ribbon. "The real thing" suggests they are Degas's own 1881 fabric, and no source supports that.
- **Closest evidence:** R3 (Petite danseuse): "Patinated bronze with a tulle tutu, satin ribbon and wooden base".
- **Fix:** "Everything else here is a careful copy. The tutu and the ribbon are real cloth."

### 035. NOTFOUND
- **Quote:** "and his friends called her Margot."
- **Why:** The sheet gives "Margot, Henriette-Anna Leboeuf". It does not say who used the name.
- **Closest evidence:** R3 (Bal): "Models: Margot, Henriette-Anna Leboeuf (1856–1879), a model very close to Renoir from 1875 to 1879."
- **Fix:** "She was Henriette-Anna Leboeuf, a model very close to Renoir, known as Margot."

### 036. NOTFOUND (placement cue)

- **Closest evidence:** R3 (Sisley): "Room: NONE. The search model's 'Salle Camondo' is an inference."
- **Fix:** "You'll meet his name again with the cathedrals."

### 042. NOTFOUND
- **Quote:** "So the portrait you're looking at spent its first sixty years not with his mother, but in the doctor's collection at Auvers-sur-Oise."
- **Why:** The painting dates from 1889 (Saint-Rémy). Van Gogh reached Auvers only on 21 May 1890, so Gachet cannot have owned it from the start. No source dates Gachet's acquisition.
- **Closest evidence:** R4 044: "Provenance: collection of Dr Paul Gachet, Auvers-sur-Oise. Accepted by the State in 1949". R4 043: "he arrived on 21 May 1890".
- **Fix:** "So the portrait you're looking at stayed for decades in the doctor's family at Auvers-sur-Oise, not with his mother."

### 043. CONTRA (speech and @what)
- **Quotes:**
  - Speech: "This canvas is not that first one. The original is in Chicago."
  - @what: "A smaller repeat of the 1888 original, now in the Art Institute of Chicago."
- **Why:** R4 separates the 1888 first canvas, which is in the Van Gogh Museum, from "the larger version", which is in Chicago. The track calls the Chicago painting the original.
- **Closest evidence:** R4 (Bedroom): "letter 705 describes the 1888 canvas (Van Gogh Museum), not the Orsay copy". Also R4: "The larger version is in the Art Institute of Chicago."
- **Fix, speech:** "This canvas is not that first one, and a larger repeat hangs in Chicago."
- **Fix, @what:** "A smaller version painted about a year after the 1888 original. A larger version is in the Art Institute of Chicago."

### 044. Rule: loan fallback only partly works
- **Problem:** After the fallback line, the speech keeps telling the listener to look at the empty wall: "So look. … The blues do most of the work", "Now look right down at the bottom. There's a couple". A visitor who has gone back to the Bedroom or the self-portrait cannot follow these lines.
- **Fix:** Extend the fallback sentence to "If the wall is empty, go back to the Bedroom, or to the self-portrait, and listen there while I describe what usually hangs here."

### 044. Rule: closing cue fails in the fallback case
- **Quote:** "In this room, find the church at Auvers"
- **Why:** A fallback listener may be standing at the Bedroom, whose room is not confirmed.
- **Fix:** "In room 36, find the church at Auvers, its windows deep blue under a cobalt sky."

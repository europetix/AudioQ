[warmly] Imagine floating over Paris in a balloon, with the whole city spread out below you. That is the view this painting offers.

It is by Victor Navlet, and its title is a small adventure in itself. Vue générale de Paris, prise de l'Observatoire en ballon. A general view of Paris, from the Observatory, in a balloon. It is dated eighteen fifty-five.

[pause] Not a street corner, not a single monument, but the whole city at once. Take a moment with it, and see how much of Paris you can recognise from the air.

These rooms carry a big name. Paris, capitale d'une nation moderne. Paris, capital of a modern nation. They opened in May twenty twenty-two, and they tell one story, the transformation of Paris under the Second Empire, in paintings, drawings, models and sculpture.

[curious] The rooms are centred on two things. One is the city seen whole, from above, in Navlet's view. The other is a single great building, Garnier's Opéra. You've met it twice already. Carpeaux's dancers came from its façade, and the model under the floor showed its quarter, stopped in nineteen fourteen.

[quietly] Put the two together and you have a portrait of a capital rebuilding itself. The view from the sky, and one building on the ground.

One last stop on this floor. At the very end of the nave, between the two towers, Rodin's Gates of Hell face back down the whole aisle. Go and stand in front of them for the last story of the ground floor.

[warmly] This room announces whose it is before you've seen a single painting. The picture rails, the thin rails the paintings hang from, are painted blue. When the museum rehung its Van Gogh paintings in 2024, it chose that colour to echo the colours Van Gogh used on his canvases. It's a small touch, and a lovely one.

Room 36 is called Vincent van Gogh in France, and it's laid out like a journey. It starts in Paris. Then it goes south, to Arles, and the sun. Then to the hospital at Saint-Rémy-de-Provence, where he still kept painting. And it ends in Auvers-sur-Oise, where he went on Theo's advice to be near a doctor named Paul Gachet.

[pause] The doctor has his place here too. Several works on these walls come from the collection Gachet built, which his children later gave to the nation.

[quietly] Keep one number in mind as you walk. Van Gogh arrived in Auvers on the twenty-first of May, 1890. He died there on the twenty-ninth of July. In those two months, he made about seventy paintings. Roughly one a day.

So as you move along these walls, remember that the last stretch of the journey is the shortest in time, and one of the fullest in paint.

Start in room 36, with a self-portrait. He wears a suit, the background swirls in pale green and turquoise, and his hair and beard are bright orange. Play its track in front of him.

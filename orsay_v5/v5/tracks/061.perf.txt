[lightly] At the start of this floor you looked out through one great clock. Here is the second. And behind it, someone has put the sea.

This is the Café Campana. It opened in 2011, when these rooms were renovated, and its designers were two Brazilian brothers, the Campanas. [amused] They imagined it as an underwater world. Look in from the threshold. Curving, organic seats. Golden chandeliers like fish scales. Hints of orange coral. A sea floor, at the top of a railway station.

[warmly] The whole room is a homage to the glassmaker Émile Gallé. Keep his name in mind, because on level two you'll meet his own glass.

[quietly] This is also a good moment to stop. Since that first clock you've walked from the first Impressionists to Van Gogh, Seurat, Gauguin and the Nabis. That's a great deal of looking. If you'd like to sit down for a few minutes, this is the place to do it. [long pause]

When you're ready, take the lift or the stairs down to level two, and make for its west end, near room 72. Find room 59, the Symbolism room, and look for an upright painting on wood of a girl in rich Oriental dress, holding a man's head that rests on a lyre. That's Moreau's Orphée, and its track begins there.

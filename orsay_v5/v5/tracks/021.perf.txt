[quietly] Nineteen fourteen is the year the war began. This little Paris never sees it.

Under your feet, a whole quarter of the city has stopped. The streets around the Opéra are frozen here as they stood that year. The model's full title says exactly that. Maquette du quartier de l'Opéra, arrêtée en l'état en 1914. The Opéra quarter, stopped as it was in 1914.

It was made between nineteen eighty-four and eighty-six, as the museum was being prepared, by Rémi Munier and a small team. It's resin, under plexiglass, six and a half metres on each side. The whole space around it was laid out by Richard Peduzzi.

[curious] Why build Paris in miniature inside an art museum? To show the Opéra and its quarter, in the museum's words a part of the city "profoundly transformed by the prefect Haussmann's major public works." You've just seen Carpeaux's dancers from its façade. Here is the street plan the building belonged to.

[lightly] So look down and find Garnier's Opéra among the blocks. Then follow the streets out from it, one by one. From up here, the plan of the quarter is easier to read than it ever is on foot.

In the same space, the Opéra appears again, this time sliced open from front to back. Find that second model nearby, and let its track walk you through it.

[wry] Some critics are remembered only for what they got wrong. Louis Leroy is one of them. His mocking review of a Monet called Impression, Sunrise is where the Impressionists got their name. And he had something to say about this painting too.

It was eighteen seventy-four, at the group's first exhibition. Camille Pissarro, the eldest of the group, showed five works, and this was one of them. Leroy looked at these frozen furrows and called them "uniform palette scrapings on a dirty canvas". Then he went further, with "neither head nor tail, neither top nor bottom, neither front nor back."

[pause] It's a good line, and it's also a confession that he didn't know how to look at it.

So give it the look he didn't. Pissarro painted this field the year before, at Pontoise. Find the furrows, and the frost lying white along them. Those are the very things Leroy called scrapings. Then follow the path, where a man carries a load of dead wood. It's ordinary winter work, and Pissarro thought it worth a canvas almost a metre wide.

[quietly] Then look down in the lower left corner. Pissarro signed it there and dated it. He wanted his name on exactly this.

What Leroy saw as mess is the frost itself. And this painting is still here, on a museum wall, a century and a half after his review.

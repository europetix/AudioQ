[warmly] In eighteen seventy-four, a group of painters set up a company of their own and hung an exhibition in Nadar's studio in Paris. In the catalogue, this little picture was number ninety-five.

After that giant picnic, Monet's Coquelicots is almost pocket-sized, about half a metre high. He painted it the year before the show, at Argenteuil.

Look at the two figures in the foreground, a woman under a sunshade and a child beside her. They're probably Camille and their son Jean. So this is not just a field of poppies. It's very likely a family walk.

Monet would go on to show at five of the group's exhibitions. [pause] Not four, as is sometimes said. Five.

[lightly] The dealer Paul Durand-Ruel bought the painting the year it was made. Then it belonged to Jean-Baptiste Faure, the opera baritone. Then to the collector Étienne Moreau-Nélaton, who gave it to the nation in nineteen oh six.

[long pause] Here's the nice part. Faure also owned Manet's Déjeuner sur l'herbe. And Moreau-Nélaton gave that to the nation as well, in the same year. So the picnic Monet tried to rival and his own little field of poppies passed through the same two collections, and became public property in the same year.

[lightly] To reach the top of this canvas, Monet lowered it into a trench in the garden, using a pulley. It's a slightly comic picture. A painter, a hole in the ground, and a canvas going up and down like a bucket in a well.

There was a good reason for it. In eighteen sixty-six Monet was painting in the garden of a house he rented in the suburbs of Paris. It was a twofold challenge. First, he painted outdoors, in the light itself. Second, he chose a huge format, the kind usually kept for history painting, for scenes of gods and heroes. Monet gave that size to a few women in a garden.

[curious] Now look at the three figures on the left. Three women. [pause] And one model. Camille posed for all three.

In nineteen twenty-one, the State bought this painting from Monet himself, for the Musée du Luxembourg. He had painted it more than half a century before.

[warmly] Much later, it needed care. Old repairs had deteriorated and the varnish had yellowed, so the museum and the national restoration centre for French museums cleaned the painting. The museum says the cleaning brought back "dazzling whites" against bluish-grey shadows. Let your eye move between the bright fabric and the cool shade. That light is what the trench was for.

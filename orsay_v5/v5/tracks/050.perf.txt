[amused] Gauguin offered this woman her portrait as a gift. She said no.

Her name was Marie-Angélique Satre. She kept a hotel at Pont-Aven and was thought one of the most beautiful women in the area. Gauguin painted her in 1889 as La Belle Angèle, The Beautiful Angèle. He has set her face inside a circle, on a mostly decorative background, with its forms outlined in a dark line, a method he took from Japanese prints. Beside her stands a pot in Peruvian style, which the museum calls an exotic version of a Breton idol.

[pause] But another painter saw a masterpiece. Edgar Degas bought it, and kept it for more than a quarter of a century, until his studio sale.

Now Les Alyscamps, painted a year earlier. In October 1888 Gauguin answered Van Gogh's invitation to Arles, and this is probably one of his first pictures there, in brilliant autumn colours. The Alyscamps was the old Roman burial ground of Arles, later made Christian by its first bishop, Saint Trophime. Gauguin almost leaves the history out. Fields, woods, a canal, and three walkers, two women of Arles and a man.

[wry] Gauguin found little charm in the women of Arles. So when he sent this canvas to Van Gogh's brother Theo, he titled it, with some irony, Landscape, or Three Graces at the Temple of Venus.

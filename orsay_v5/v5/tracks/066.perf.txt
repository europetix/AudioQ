[warmly] In 1900, when this building was brand new, the painter Édouard Detaille wrote a line about it. "The station is superb and looks like a Palais des beaux-arts…" He meant a palace of fine arts. It took most of the century for him to be proved right.

Look down from the terrace. Down there, where the sculptures stand along the central aisle, the trains once pulled in. And look along the terraces themselves. On both sides of the nave, they hold sculpture from about 1880 to 1910.

[pause] And now you know all three floors. You began down there, on the ground floor, with the academic painters, Millet and Courbet, and Manet's Olympia. You climbed to the top, under the roof, to the Impressionists, Van Gogh and Gauguin. And you came down here, to the Nabis, Art Nouveau, Claudel and Rodin's Balzac.

[quietly] Before you go, look all the way to the far end of the nave. Between the two towers stands Rodin's Gates of Hell, facing the whole length of the old station. You stood in front of it hours ago, at the end of the ground floor. From up here, you can take it in with everything else at once. [pause] Detaille only saw a station that looked like a palace. You've just walked through the palace.

[warmly] Thank you for spending these hours with me. I hope a few of these faces come home with you: a black cat, a little dancer, a man in a sack of plaster, a girl holding a poet's head.

When you're ready, take the escalators or the stairs down to level 0. Walk back along the nave to the clock end, where you came in, and follow the SORTIE signs out into Paris. Goodbye, and enjoy the city.

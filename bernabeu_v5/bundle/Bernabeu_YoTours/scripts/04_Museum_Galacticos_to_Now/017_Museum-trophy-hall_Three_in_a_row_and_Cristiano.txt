The old European Cup became the Champions League in the early nineties. For the next quarter of a century, not one club managed to win it twice in a row.

Then Real Madrid won it three times running, one final straight after another.

The man in charge was Zinedine Zidane, the hero of the Glasgow volley, now the coach. In his very first season, in 2016, the final in Milan was a rematch with Atlético. It finished one all and went to penalties. Juanfran hit the post for Atlético, and Cristiano Ronaldo stepped up for the last kick and scored. Cup eleven.

A year later in Cardiff, against Juventus, Ronaldo scored twice in a four goals to one win. As UEFA noted, that made Madrid the first team to win the Champions League two years running.

And in 2018 came Kyiv, against Liverpool, where Karim Benzema scored first. Then Gareth Bale scored with a bicycle kick, back to goal, feet over his head. He added another later on, for a three goals to one win. Cup thirteen, and three in a row.

Running through all of it was Cristiano Ronaldo. He arrived in 2009 and stayed nine seasons. In that time he scored four hundred and fifty-one goals in four hundred and thirty-eight matches. Let that sink in, because that's more goals than games. No one in the club's history has scored more.

He won four Champions Leagues here. Then, in the same year as Kyiv, he left. The next cup would take four years to arrive.

Look at these three cups side by side. It was the first hat-trick of Champions League titles.

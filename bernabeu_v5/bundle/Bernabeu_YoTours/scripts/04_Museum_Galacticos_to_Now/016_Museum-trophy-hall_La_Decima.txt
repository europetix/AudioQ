Ninety-two minutes gone. One goal down. Twelve years of waiting about to become thirteen.

After Glasgow in 2002, Real Madrid could not win this cup again, and season after season they went out. The tenth European Cup, La Décima, became the club's great unfinished business. Everyone in Madrid knew exactly what those two words meant.

Then, in 2014, they reached the final in Lisbon, and the opponents were their own neighbours from across the city, Atlético de Madrid. Two Madrid clubs met in a European final.

In the thirty-sixth minute, Diego Godín put Atlético in front, and then Atlético defended, and defended, as the minutes went by. Ninety came and went.

With ninety-two minutes and forty-eight seconds on the clock, Luka Modrić swung in a corner. Sergio Ramos met it with his head, and the ball went in. One all.

Spanish fans still have a name for that moment. They call it "minuto noventa y Ramos", minute ninety-and-Ramos.

In extra time, Madrid ran away with it. Gareth Bale scored, then Marcelo, then Cristiano Ronaldo from the penalty spot, to make it four goals to one for Carlo Ancelotti's team.

And here's the detail many people get wrong: Ramos was not the captain that night. The captain who lifted this cup was Iker Casillas, the same goalkeeper who had come on as a twenty-year-old substitute in Glasgow, twelve years before.

So when you look at cup number ten, you're looking at the one that unlocked everything after it. Within four years, three more followed.

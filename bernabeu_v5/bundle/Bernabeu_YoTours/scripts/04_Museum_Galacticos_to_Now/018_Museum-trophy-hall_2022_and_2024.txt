In the spring of 2022, Real Madrid kept heading out of the Champions League. And then, somehow, they kept staying in.

Against Paris Saint-Germain, against Chelsea, against Manchester City, they were on their way out, and each time they turned it round late, here in this stadium. Two of those nights get their own story down at the benches, where they happened.

By the time they reached the final, in Paris, against Liverpool, this team looked capable of coming back from anything. In the end, it didn't need to. Vinícius Júnior scored just before the hour, and the goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois made save after save, enough to be named player of the match. One goal to nothing. Cup fourteen.

Two years later came Wembley, against Borussia Dortmund. Two late goals, from Dani Carvajal and Vinícius again, and cup fifteen. As of autumn 2026, it's still the most recent.

Behind both of these cups stood the same coach, Carlo Ancelotti. In Paris he became the first coach ever to win the European Cup four times. At Wembley he made it five. Across his two spells he won fifteen titles with Real Madrid, more than any other coach in the club's history. He left in May 2025.

Look at these last two cups. They belong to a team that made a habit of the impossible, and to the most successful coach the club has ever had.

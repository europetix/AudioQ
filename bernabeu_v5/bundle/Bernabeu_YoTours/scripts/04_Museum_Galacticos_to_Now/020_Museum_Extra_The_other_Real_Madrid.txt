One of the most dramatic European Cups this club has ever won was settled with three seconds left on the clock. And it wasn't won with a football.

It was May 2023, in Kaunas, in Lithuania. Real Madrid's basketball team was playing Olympiacos in the EuroLeague final. Sergio Llull let go of his shot with three point one seconds to play. Seventy-nine points to seventy-eight. It was the basketball section's eleventh European Cup, and the first had come back in 1964. This year's final went the other way, to Olympiacos, so as of autumn 2026 the count stays at eleven. If you pass any basketball trophies, that's the story behind them.

The women's team is a much younger story. For well over a century, Real Madrid had no women's side at all. The club took over an existing Madrid team, CD Tacón, and on the first of July 2020 it became Real Madrid's own. The museum gives the women's first team an area of its own, where you can get to know the players.

If you find it, give it a minute. Most stories in this building look a long way back. This one is still near the beginning.

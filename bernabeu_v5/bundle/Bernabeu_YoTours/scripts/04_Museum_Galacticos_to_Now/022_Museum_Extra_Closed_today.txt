On a match day, and on the day before one, two doors on this tour stay shut. The club closes the dressing room and the benches. On those days they go back to being working rooms. Here is what lies behind them.

The dressing room is the first team's, the home side's room. The players change there before a game, and they come back to it at half-time. The Laws of the Game give them no more than fifteen minutes for that break. Whatever is said in that quarter of an hour stays in the room. The club doesn't publish it, and this guide won't invent it.

The benches are down at pitch level, beside the playing area. That's where the coach and the substitutes sit. From there, the stands don't fall away beneath you, as they do from the top tier. They rise over you, tier upon tier, up to the roof and the long ring of screen that hangs from it.

On another day, you can stand down there yourself.

If you haven't been up to the top tier yet, go there now, and play number four. If you have, and the dressing room and benches are closed today, the route goes on to the store: play number twenty-seven. Otherwise, carry on to the dressing room.

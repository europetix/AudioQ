For more than a century, this club has played in white. And the club's own history of its early years doesn't say why.

The story most often told goes like this. In the early days of football in Madrid, the people behind the club admired Corinthian, an amateur team from London. Corinthian were famous for playing in the right spirit, and they played in white shirts. Madrid, the story goes, took the colour in their honour. It's a lovely story. Just remember that it is a story, and the club itself doesn't tell it.

Whatever the reason, white became the name. Around the world, the team is simply Los Blancos, the Whites.

Look around this store, and you'll see the other half of what that colour means. The shirt is identity, and it's also business. Real Madrid's accounts for the season that ended in June 2026 show revenue of just over one point two billion euros, the most in its history. The club says no other sports organisation in the world has reached that figure. Deloitte's annual ranking of football's biggest earners has put Real Madrid at the top three years running, as of January 2026. Of that, the stadium alone brought in three hundred and sixty-three million euros.

And here's the part that surprises people. All that money belongs to a club owned by its members, the socios. There are no shareholders. Every shirt on these walls, in a sense, is sold on their behalf.

A colour chosen, the story goes, out of admiration for a team of English amateurs. Today it dresses one of the richest clubs in the world.

When you're ready, follow the way out of the store to the exit, and the way home.

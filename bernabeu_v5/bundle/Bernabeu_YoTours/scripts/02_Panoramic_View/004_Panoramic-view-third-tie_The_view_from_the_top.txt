Here's a question the club has never answered in public. How many people does this bowl hold? Real Madrid has not published an official capacity for the rebuilt stadium.

That hasn't stopped people guessing. The architects say the new stadium can take up to about eighty-three thousand spectators at major events. The league has quoted more than one figure over the years. And the president, speaking to the club's members in 2023, put it at around eighty thousand, give or take a thousand. So around eighty thousand is the honest answer, and the exact number stays in the club's drawer.

For comparison, think back to the story outside. By 1954 this same stadium could hold a hundred and twenty-five thousand people. Fewer fit in today, and every one of them has a seat.

Now take in the view. You're up in the third tier, high above the playing area. Look at how steeply the seats fall away beneath you, row after row, down towards the floor of the stadium. Then look across the bowl. No pillar breaks the view anywhere. Every seat you can see looks straight down at the same rectangle.

Above you is the roof, and hanging below its edge, a band of screens that runs all the way around. Both get their own stop in a moment. And down in the middle, the floor is most likely bare. That is not a mistake. The club keeps the grass underground except for matches, and you'll hear exactly how.

For now, just let it fill your eyes. Imagine every one of those seats taken, on a night when the result really matters.

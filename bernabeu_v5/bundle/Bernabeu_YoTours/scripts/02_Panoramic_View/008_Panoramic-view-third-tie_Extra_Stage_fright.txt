Spanish football has a name for what this stadium does to visiting teams. Miedo escénico. Stage fright.

The man who made the phrase famous was Jorge Valdano, an Argentine forward playing for Real Madrid in the 1980s. He didn't invent it. He borrowed it from the novelist Gabriel García Márquez, who had used it about the ordeal of speaking in public. In 1986, while still a Madrid player, Valdano wrote an essay with that title for the Spanish magazine Revista de Occidente.

He knew what he was describing, because he had been on the pitch for it. In December 1985, Real Madrid lost five goals to one away to Borussia Mönchengladbach in the UEFA Cup. Back here, Valdano scored twice with his head inside the first twenty minutes. Santillana added two more, the last in the eighty-ninth minute. Four goals to nil, and Madrid went through on away goals.

Look at those steep stands, and picture them full and roaring, from the other team's side. That was Valdano's point.

The route now leads down to the museum. If you've already seen the museum today, go on to the dressing room instead, and play number twenty-three.

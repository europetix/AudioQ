This stadium has staged a World Cup final, a European Championship final and four finals of the European Cup. In only one of them were Real Madrid actually playing.

That was in 1957. Real Madrid had home advantage, and beat Fiorentina two goals to nil. Alfredo Di Stéfano scored a penalty, and Paco Gento added the second. It was the second of the club's fifteen European Cups, won right here.

The other great nights belonged to other people. Spain beat the Soviet Union here to win its first European Championship. Three more European Cup finals were played here, all by other clubs. At the World Cup final, Italy beat West Germany three goals to one. And more recently, the biggest club final in South America was moved across the Atlantic to this stadium. It was River Plate against Boca Juniors, two rivals from Buenos Aires. River won.

One of those European Cup finals came in 2010. Inter Milan beat Bayern Munich here, two goals to nil, to win the Champions League. Inter's coach that night was José Mourinho. He didn't fly home with his team. He stayed in Madrid, and a few days later he was unveiled as Real Madrid's new coach. It's one way to get to know a stadium.

Now find the scale model, and the screens beside it. The club calls this its audiovisual show on how the stadium has changed since 1947. Use the model to get your bearings. It shows the whole stadium from outside in one glance, the steel skin you saw from the street wrapped around the bowl you're standing in. Then give the film a few minutes.

Every one of those finals was played before the rebuild began. The building has changed almost beyond recognition. The habit of hosting the biggest nights is still here.

Stay at this level, and look up, to the roof and the ring of screens that runs all the way around the stands.

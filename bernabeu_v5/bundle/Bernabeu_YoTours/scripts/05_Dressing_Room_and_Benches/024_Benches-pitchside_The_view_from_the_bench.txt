For fourteen years in a row, one man ran this club from the bench. Miguel Muñoz had already captained Real Madrid to the very first European Cup. Then, from 1960, he coached them. Six hundred and five matches, still a club record. Two more European Cups, nine league titles, fourteen trophies in all.

That total of fourteen stood as the club record for half a century. In August 2024, Carlo Ancelotti matched it. By the time he left the following May, he had fifteen, the most of any coach in Real Madrid's history.

Since then, the seat has been anything but settled. Xabi Alonso came in that summer and was gone by January. Álvaro Arbeloa took over for the rest of the season. And as of autumn 2026, the coach is José Mourinho, back since June 2026 for a second spell here. His first had ended more than a decade earlier.

Now look up. From the top tier, the stadium falls away beneath you. Down here it does the opposite. The stands climb over you, tier upon tier, to the roof, and the long ring of screen that hangs from it. This is the view a coach has for ninety minutes, and every one of those seats has a view of him.

Look at the floor in front of you too. It may be bare, or there may be equipment on it. The club keeps the grass underground in its own store, and brings it up only for matches. On match nights, a coach stands on this spot with the grass at his feet and the whole stadium above him.

It's a fine view from this bench. Keeping it is the hard part.

Stay here at the benches for the nights this stadium turned around at the very end.

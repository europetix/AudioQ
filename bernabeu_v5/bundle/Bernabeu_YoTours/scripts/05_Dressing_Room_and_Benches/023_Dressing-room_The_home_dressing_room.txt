Of all the rooms on this tour, this is the one that still has a job to do. That's why it closes. On match days, and on the day before, the club shuts it to visitors, and it belongs to the team again.

The club calls it the dressing room of the first team. This is where Real Madrid's players change into the white shirt before a home game, and where they come back when the first half ends. You may find each player's place marked with his name and his shirt. If they're up today, look for the names you know, and see who sits next to whom.

Now think about half-time. The Laws of the Game allow no more than fifteen minutes between the two halves. In that time the players come in off the pitch, sit down in these seats, and listen. Then they go back out.

What gets said in here? Honestly, nobody outside knows. The club doesn't publish it, and this guide won't make it up. But sometimes the scoreboard tells you how a half-time went.

On the ninth of March 2022, Real Madrid came into this part of the stadium at half-time a goal down to Paris Saint-Germain. Kylian Mbappé had scored for Paris six minutes before the break. Over the two legs of the tie, Madrid were two goals behind, with forty-five minutes left to save their season in Europe. What happened next is a story for the benches, down at pitch level.

For now, look around the room as it is today. Picture every seat taken, the door shut, and fifteen minutes on the clock.

A quarter of an hour, and a room like this one. Some nights in this stadium, that has been enough.

From here, the route takes you down to pitch level and the benches.

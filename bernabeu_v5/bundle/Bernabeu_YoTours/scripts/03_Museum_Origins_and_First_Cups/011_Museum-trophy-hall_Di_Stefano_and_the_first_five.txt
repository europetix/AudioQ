When the European Cup began, Real Madrid won it. Then they won it again the next year, and the year after that. Five times in a row. No club has matched that run since.

At the centre of it all was Alfredo Di Stéfano. He was born in Buenos Aires and arrived in Madrid three years before that first final. He was known as the Saeta Rubia, the Blond Arrow, and the club is not shy about him: it calls him the best player of all time.

The numbers explain why. In eleven seasons here he scored three hundred and eight goals in three hundred and ninety-six matches. And he scored in every one of those five finals.

The second of them wasn't played in a far-off capital. It was played right here, in this stadium, in 1957, against Fiorentina. Di Stéfano scored from the penalty spot, Paco Gento added the second, and Madrid won two goals to nothing in front of their own crowd.

These were also the years of General Franco's dictatorship, and how close the club stood to the regime is a question people still argue about.

Then came Glasgow. The fifth final, at Hampden Park, against Eintracht Frankfurt, in front of a crowd of about a hundred and twenty-seven thousand. Real Madrid won seven goals to three. The Hungarian Ferenc Puskás scored four, and Di Stéfano scored the other three.

Now look at these five cups together. This is where the club's European legend begins.

Stay at the row, and find cup number six, from 1966. A new team won it, with one old face still in the side.

For fifty-eight years, one footballer stood alone in the record books. His name was Paco Gento, and he won the European Cup six times.

He played in all five of those first finals, and won every one. Then came the hard part. Madrid reached two more finals and lost them both, to Benfica and then to Inter. By the middle of the sixties, Di Stéfano had gone. Puskás was no longer in the team. Gento was now the most experienced man in the side.

The sixth cup was won in Brussels, at the Heysel stadium, against Partizan of Belgrade. Madrid went a goal down ten minutes after half-time. Then Amancio equalised, and Fernando Serena hit the winner. Two goals to one. It was Gento's eighth European Cup final, and his sixth win.

What made that team different was who was in it. Coach Miguel Muñoz picked eleven Spanish players for the final. Many of them belonged to a new generation, the one taking over from the legends.

And they got a nickname from pop music. That same year, several of the players posed for a photograph as the Beatles. From then on they were the Madrid Ye-yé, after the "yeah, yeah, yeah" in the chorus of "She Loves You".

Now look at this sixth cup. For a long time it was the one thing nobody could take from Gento. Then, at Wembley in 2024, four Real Madrid players finally caught him. Dani Carvajal, Luka Modrić and Nacho won their sixth, all with this club. Toni Kroos won his too, one of them with Bayern Munich.

Four men needed a whole modern dynasty to draw level with one man from the fifties.

Look along the row to cup number seven, and notice the long gap before it.

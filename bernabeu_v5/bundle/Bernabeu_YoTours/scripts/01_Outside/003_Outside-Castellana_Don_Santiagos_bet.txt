In the early 1940s, Real Madrid's ground held about twenty-five thousand people. The club's new president wanted one for a hundred thousand. The club's own history calls the plan pharaonic.

His name was Santiago Bernabéu. He had played for the club from boyhood until the late 1920s, and he became its president during the Second World War. Spain was only a few years out of its own civil war, in which he had fought on Franco's side. Even so, the first stone of a new stadium was laid here on the 27th of October, 1944.

It opened on the 14th of December, 1947, under the name Nuevo Chamartín, the New Chamartín. Real Madrid beat the champions of Portugal, Os Belenenses, three goals to one. The first goal ever scored in this stadium came from Sabino Barinaga, in the fifteenth minute.

And the dream of a hundred thousand? The stadium kept growing upwards. Less than seven years after it opened, the club says it could hold a hundred and twenty-five thousand people, more than it has ever held since. A year later, the club gave the stadium his name, while he was still in charge.

He stayed president for almost thirty-five years, until his death. In that time the club won six European Cups and sixteen Spanish league titles. You'll meet several of those cups in the museum.

One more thing about that name. Since late 2025, the club has branded the stadium with a single word. Not the full name. Just his surname, Bernabéu.

Head now for the tour entrance on Concha Espina. The route takes you up to the top tier, for the view over the whole stadium. If the staff send you into the museum first, play number nine instead.

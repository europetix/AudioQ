[curious] If you were hoping for a perfect green pitch down there, you're probably looking at a bare floor instead. [pause] The grass hasn't gone far. It's underneath it.

The club keeps the pitch in what it calls a great underground greenhouse. The club calls it the hipogeo, the hypogeum, borrowing the name of the chambers beneath the arena of the Colosseum in Rome. The grass is real. It grows in six huge trays, and each one can be moved out sideways and lowered into the store below. Down there it gets light, air, irrigation and a controlled climate. When a match is coming, the trays are brought back up into place. The whole change takes a few hours. The club says plainly that the grass stays underground except for matches.

Why go to all that trouble? To free up the floor. With the grass safely stored, the stadium can host other things without wrecking the turf. In November 2025, for instance, it staged the first regular-season NFL game ever played in Spain.

[pause] The other big plan was music. In the spring and summer of 2024, the new stadium hosted a run of huge concerts, with Taylor Swift and Karol G among them. Neighbours complained about the noise, and a residents' association logged around a hundred and fifty readings across the events. That September, Real Madrid suspended concerts here to improve the soundproofing.

The dispute went to court. In May 2026, a Madrid appeal court ruled that the club's stadium company bore no criminal responsibility. It found that keeping to the sound limits was mainly the promoters' job. As of autumn 2026, no return of the concerts has been announced.

[wry] So look at that floor again. It isn't missing something. It's waiting for a match.

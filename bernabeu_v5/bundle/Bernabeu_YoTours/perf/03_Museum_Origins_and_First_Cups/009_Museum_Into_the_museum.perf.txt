[curious] For its first eighteen years, this club was not "Real" at all. It was plain Madrid Football Club.

It was formally constituted on the sixth of March, 1902, with a board led by Juan Padrós. The royal title came later. On the twenty-ninth of June, 1920, King Alfonso the Thirteenth granted it by letter, and with it the right to put a royal crown on the badge. [pause] More than a century later, that crown still sits on top of the badge.

The second thing worth knowing is who owns all this. Not a billionaire, and not a company. In the club's own words, Real Madrid is a club owned by its members. They're called socios, and it is they who elect the president.

And the third thing is what kind of museum you're in. The club calls it an audiovisual and interactive journey through its trophies. In other words, this is Real Madrid telling its own story, proudly, on screens, in old film and in glass cases. It's well made, and it isn't neutral. [warmly] You don't need to read every panel. Let the pictures carry you, and I'll fill in the stories that matter, along with one or two the walls tend to skip.

Everything here is building towards one room. Walk on to the trophy hall, and look for the long row of European Cups.

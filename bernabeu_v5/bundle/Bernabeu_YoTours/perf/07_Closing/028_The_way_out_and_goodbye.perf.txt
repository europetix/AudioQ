[warmly] You began this visit outside, with a man and a bet. A first stone laid in 1944, and a stadium that took his name eleven years later. You end it here, at the door, with the white shirt still all around you.

First, the way home. Staff will show you the way out of the store. If the street sign outside says Calle Padre Damián, you're on the stadium's east side, the opposite side from where you came in.

As of autumn 2026, the stadium's own metro station is closed for rebuilding. Line 10 is suspended through this stretch until the end of the year, and the new station is due in early 2027. So there's no train from right outside.

Instead, walk round the stadium to the Castellana, the wide avenue on the side where your visit began. The S10 replacement bus, at no extra cost for Metro passengers, stops there by Plaza de Lima, on both sides of the avenue. Heading south, it takes you to Nuevos Ministerios. Heading north, to Plaza de Castilla. Or walk south down the Castellana to Nuevos Ministerios yourself, for the Cercanías local trains and Metro lines 6 and 8.

[pause] Before you go, think back over what you've seen. A pitch that stays underground except for matches. A room where fifteen minutes can turn a night around. A bench that few coaches keep for long. And fifteen European Cups, with a gap of thirty-two years in the middle.

[quietly] The next time this stadium is on your screen, on a big night, you'll see it differently. You'll know where that grass goes between matches, who's watching from the bench, and what the clock means at the end.

[warmly] Thank you for walking it with me. Hasta la próxima, until next time.

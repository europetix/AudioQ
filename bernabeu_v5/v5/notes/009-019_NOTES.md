## Per track
**009 Into the museum (R).** Founded 6 March 1902, board under Juan Padrós (FACTS A1, OFF). "Real" title granted 29 June 1920 by Alfonso XIII by letter, with the crown on the badge; the club was previously "Madrid Football Club" (OFF, realmadrid.com 106th-anniversary page). Owned by socios (FACTS A12, OFF). "Audiovisual and interactive journey" (OFF wording, TOUR_ROUTE). Old-guide claims dropped: 200,000 objects, "a million visitors a year".

**010 The fifteen.** Honours board: RM 15, Milan 7, Liverpool 6, Bayern 6, Barcelona 5, PSG 2; nine won in the Champions League era (OFF-UEFA, updated 30 May 2026). The original trophy was kept by Real Madrid after 1966, the first cup had much smaller handles, the nickname is "Old Big Ears", and today's winners get a full-size replica (OFF-UEFA trophy page). **Not confirmed:** whether the museum displays that original. The script does not say it does.

**011 Di Stéfano and the first five.** All five finals and his goal in each (FACTS D1/D2 + UEFA finals page). The 1957 final was at this stadium, 2–0 v Fiorentina, Di Stéfano penalty and Gento (OFF-UEFA). Di Stéfano: Buenos Aires, at the club 1953–64, 308 goals in 396 matches, "Saeta Rubia"; the club calls him the best of all time (OFF, centenary page 4 Jul 2026). The Franco era gets one neutral sentence. Dropped from the old guide: white boots, the standing ovation, "greatest match ever played".

**012 Gento's six and the Ye-yé.** Gento's six (1956–60, 1966) and his status as sole record holder 1966–2024; Carvajal, Modrić and Nacho equalled it in 2024, and so did Kroos with one of his at Bayern (OFF). 1966 final: 11 May, Heysel, 2–1 Partizan, Vasović 55', Amancio 70', Serena 76'; eleven Spanish players; coach Miguel Muñoz; it was Gento's eighth final (OFF). Ye-yé: named after the "She Loves You" chorus, and several players were photographed "emulating the Beatles" (OFF history 1961–70). The two lost finals, 1962 Benfica and 1964 Inter, are from UEFA. Gento's playing position ("winger") was left out as unsourced.

**013 Thirty-two years.** The only final in the gap was Paris 1981, lost 1–0 to Liverpool, Alan Kennedy late (OFF-UEFA). 1998: 20 May, Amsterdam ArenA, 1–0 Juventus, Mijatović 66'; Juventus in their third straight final (OFF); coach Heynckes (OFF-UEFA). **014 Zidane's volley.** 15 May 2002, Hampden, centenary year; Raúl 9', Lúcio 14', Zidane 44' volley from Roberto Carlos's looping cross from the left; France Football (2020) named it the most beautiful Champions League goal (OFF).

[curious] This room began as a small, forgotten corner. The design studio that remade it, Local Projects, says so plainly: "We transformed a small, underused lounge in the lobby into ZERO."

What they made, the museum calls "an introductory gallery and immersive optical device". A curved projection and a set of mirrors wrap around you, giving a view of three hundred degrees. The mirrors multiply the room fourteen times over. [pause] It's a kaleidoscope you stand inside.

The pictures are about this place. In the museum's words, they show "the history, cityscape, and industrial past of Bilbao and the region of Biscay". There's "a new approach to Frank O. Gehry's building", and there are images of works from the collection.

[lightly] Listen as well as look. The soundtrack draws on the txalaparta, a traditional percussion instrument of the Basque Country, mixed with electronic sound. It's an old local rhythm inside a very new kind of room.

ZERO opened in December 2018, and the museum suggests starting every visit here. Think of it as an overture: the city and its past first, then the building, before you meet the real thing.

After ZERO, walk on into the Atrium, the tall, light-filled heart of the building. Stand somewhere near the middle where you can look straight up: Atrium · The heart of the building.

[curious] He's more than twelve metres tall. He's alive. And there are two of him.

This is Puppy, by Jeff Koons, made in 1992 of stainless steel, soil and flowering plants. The museum calls him "a behemoth West Highland terrier carpeted in bedding plants". He nods to the formal gardens of eighteenth-century Europe, and Koons wanted him, in his own words, to instil "confidence and security". He stands guard at the museum.

The first Puppy grew at the baroque castle of Arolsen, in Germany. He had about 20,000 plants on a wooden structure, and the design didn't last. [pause] He was reborn in Sydney, with about 60,000 plants on a stainless-steel frame worked out by computer. As the Art Gallery of New South Wales records it, "The Sydney version now stands outside the Guggenheim Museum in Bilbao." He was unveiled here just before the opening, "just arrived from Australia", as one Spanish paper put it.

[wry] You may read that Bilbao kept him by popular demand. "There is an idea that Puppy stayed in Bilbao by popular acclaim," the same paper wrote. "It isn't so." The museum's art committee had decided to buy him before the opening. The museum acquired him.

And the twin? Koons's studio lists Puppy as an edition of one, plus an artist's proof. Bilbao has the proof. The other stood at Rockefeller Center in New York, and since 2002 has lived at the Brant Foundation in Connecticut.

[warmly] Now look at his coat. About 40,000 plants, changed twice a year, in May and October. A team of twenty gardeners spends nine days on each change, following the artist's original designs. Since 2022 he has had his own watering system, run remotely, with moisture sensors in six critical zones. In spring and summer he wears flowers like begonias and petunias. Look closely, and you may find a place where the steel shows through.

If he's half-dressed today, you've caught the gardeners at work.

Before you go in, one stop that matters. In the small square just in front of Puppy, on the city side, there's a plaque. Find it, and stand quietly beside it: Museum plaza · Txema Agirre.

[warmly] Frank Gehry had no patience for talk of a "Bilbao effect". But about the city itself, he said this: "I love going to Bilbao and people come out and hug me. We all need love." And then: "And it's nice to get it for doing things like that." He was talking about why he did the work at all.

Think back to the plaza, where this began: a town that said it was dying asked him for its own Sydney Opera House. What it got stands beside you, on what was once a working wharf.

[pause] On visitors, at least, the bet paid off. The feasibility study said the museum needed 400,000 visitors a year, and in its first full year about 1.3 million came. By its twenty-fifth birthday there had been almost twenty-five million, more than double the first estimates, as the Basque press put it.

And what you've seen here belongs here. Puppy, Serra's steel, Maman and the rest carry the credit line Guggenheim Bilbao Museoa. They are this museum's own.

You've walked the whole of it. A dog made of flowers guarding the plaza. A stairway that takes you down to go in. The Atrium, rising all around you. Steel you could walk through, and a flower of metal overhead. Then out to the water, a spider by the footbridge, and this bridge that the building reaches out to hold.

[quietly] Gehry died in December 2025, at ninety-six. A town that said it was dying gave him its hugs. He gave it this.

In October 2027, the museum turns thirty.

[warmly] Before you go: if you missed the fog, it comes back on the hour. The fire burns after dark, so the river walk is worth a second look tonight. And you can walk all the way round the building, or under the bridge, and see it from every side.

Thank you for walking the Guggenheim with Yo Tours. Goodbye.

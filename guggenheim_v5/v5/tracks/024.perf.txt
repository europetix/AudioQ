[conspiratorial] There's one more work in this pond, and by day you'd hardly know it's there. It only wakes up after dark.

In a lecture at the Sorbonne in Paris in 1959, the artist Yves Klein described, in the museum's English, a "project for a public square, of an area of water above which streams of fire would dance rather than streams of water."

Two years later, at his exhibition in Krefeld, in Germany, he came close. A "wall of fire" of about fifty Bunsen burners stood on the lawn in front of a villa by Mies van der Rohe.

Here, in this pond, the dream became "a row of five fire fountains". The work is dated 1961, and the museum calls the fountains here a "posthumous realization" of Klein's project. In English, his own words run: "Fire for me is the future without forgetting the past."

[pause] Fire answers fire on this side of the building. Up on the terrace, the museum describes Chillida's steel as "a void of fire and heat".

[warmly] Unless it's already dark, you won't see them lit. So if you're in Bilbao tonight, come back to the river walk after dark, and watch fire dance on water, the way Klein imagined it.

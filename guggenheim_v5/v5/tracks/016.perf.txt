[curious] Down there, the walls moved with you. From up here, they hold still.

The museum's own quickest route sends you here: "Go up to the second floor of the Museum and take a look from the balcony opposite gallery 204." It promises "a panoramic view of the eight monumental sculptures that make The Matter of Time".

Its guide for teachers adds that this balcony reveals "the exact location of the sculptures". Down on the floor, the same guide says, "the viewer loses all reference and does not know what lies ahead".

[pause] So use the view. Count the eight. Find the twisted ellipses, then Snake, then the spirals, then the two pieces cut from rings and spheres. Now look at the spaces between them. "The entire room is part of the sculptural field," in the museum's words, and from here the corridors you walked are lines drawn on the floor.

[amused] Wait for someone to walk into a spiral. Watch them vanish, then turn up again where you didn't expect.

Serra said of these pieces: "When you move they move too in such a way that you are always trying to get them." From up here, at last, you can get them. Or nearly. As Serra himself put it, "the way the pieces rise always remains partially hidden".

Notice, too, what isn't there: a single column. This is the gallery that runs under the bridge, and the museum calls it "an enormous, column-free space".

Next, up to the third floor. Take a glass lift, or walk the curved walkways and stairs, and start the next stop as you go. Lifts and walkways · Every piece different.

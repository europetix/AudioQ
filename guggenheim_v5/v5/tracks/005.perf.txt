[warmly] Most grand museums make you climb a flight of steps to reach the art. This one sends you down. In the museum's own words, "visitors access the Hall by making their way down a broad stairway", "an unusual feature that successfully overcomes the height difference". The plaza sits a whole floor above the Hall, so you go in by going down.

The building you're walking into took four years to build, from October 1993 to October 1997. And it was built on a tight rein. The executive architects were IDOM, a firm from Bilbao, and they priced every change to the design. [pause] Three times, design work was stopped for a complete re-estimate of the cost. The project's director, Juan Ignacio Vidarte, had a simple rule. Nothing was approved unless it could be built within the budget and the schedule.

It cost about 100 million dollars to build. And this is where the story usually goes wrong. You'll often read that it came in under budget. None of the sources we trust says so. What they say is better than a boast: it came in on time and on budget.

[curious] The builder was Ferrovial. Its works manager, Juan Ramón Pérez, gave the building a famous compliment. He called it "twenty years ahead of its time". He also called the project "financially incredibly costly". Both things, it seems, were true.

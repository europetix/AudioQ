[warmly] Before there was a museum here, this riverbank worked for a living. In the museum's own account, the building went up "on a former wharf with port and industrial use on a curve of the Nervión".

Keep walking while you listen. You're heading for the river walk, the promenade along the water, and for the pond on the river side of the building. It's possible to walk all the way round the museum.

For most of the last century, this stretch of the river, Abandoibarra, was heavy industry. There were shipyards and container yards, roads, railway lines and docks. Among them was the Euskalduna shipyard, founded in 1900.

[pause] Then came a deep crisis in the steel sector, and in 1988 the shipyard closed.

[curious] So who brought the riverbank back? Not the museum on its own. In 1992 a public company, Bilbao Ría 2000, was set up to recover degraded and declining industrial land along the river.

The museum went up in the middle of that change. In its own words, building it "represented recovery of the banks of the river for the city, redeveloping them for culture and leisure." Today it calls the riverside avenue "an open-air leisure area", and "an emblematic example of the urban regeneration of Bilbao".

[lightly] Shipyards and containers then. A promenade now.

If you came out onto the plaza, make your way down to the river walk. If you came out on the river side, you're nearly there. At the water, find the pond beside the building, where a tall tree of silver balls stands in the water: The pond · Tall Tree & the Eye.

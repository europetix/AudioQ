[quietly] This small square carries the name of a police officer. His full name was José María Aguirre Larraona. Here, he is Txema Agirre.

He was an officer of the Ertzaintza, the Basque police. He was thirty-five. He had a newborn son.

On Monday 13 October 1997, five days before the museum opened, men dressed as gardeners were unloading flower planters from a van beside the building. Two Ertzaintza officers went to check the van. Txema Agirre was one of them.

He was shot. He died of his wounds.

[pause] The planters hid grenades, meant for the opening. ETA's target was the inauguration, attended by King Juan Carlos, the Spanish prime minister and the Basque president. The Basque president, José Antonio Ardanza, called Txema Agirre a hero.

[reverent] He is remembered here, in the square that bears his name. There is a plaque, and there is a monolith. The plaque took fourteen years to be put up. Every October, flowers are laid for him. On the twenty-fifth anniversary, in 2022, a tribute was held at the monolith.

If you'd like to, take a moment here before you go on.

[long pause] When you're ready, find a place on the plaza where you can see the whole front of the museum, with the long straight street into the city, Calle Iparragirre, at your back: Museum plaza · Our town is dying.

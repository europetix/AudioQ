[curious] For eight years, this long gallery held just one work. The rest of the room was waiting.

This is Snake, by Richard Serra. He made it between 1994 and 1997, for the museum's opening. When the doors first opened, it stood here alone. The other seven pieces came later, and the complete Matter of Time opened in June 2005.

The museum's director then, Juan Ignacio Vidarte, said: "This huge gallery was envisioned to hold art of the 21st century." The finished work, he said, "makes the museum a pilgrimage site for anyone interested in the present and future state of sculpture".

[pause] The critic Robert Hughes summed up the room as "one old and seven new rolled steel sculptures". Then he went further. On the strength of them, he called Serra the only great sculptor "at work anywhere in the early 21st century".

Snake is the old one. The museum's guide for teachers says it is built from the conical section, "the basic constructive unit": its walls are slices of cones, cut in steel.

[curious] So walk its length. Watch how its walls lean, and notice whether the lean stays the same as you go, or slowly changes. Then compare it with the twisted pieces on either side of it. Cones here. Twisting forms there. One steady idea, and the restless ones that followed.

[warmly] It was the first. For eight years it was the only one. Everything else in this room came after it.

Walk on along the gallery to the spirals: forms you walk into that keep closing in as you go. Gallery 104 · Lost on purpose.

Before there was a museum here, this riverbank worked for a living.

Keep walking while you listen. You're heading for the river walk, the promenade along the water. If you've come out onto the plaza, take the stairs that lead down from the plaza toward the park and the river walk, on the side away from the big road bridge. If you've come out on the river side, you're nearly there.

For most of the last century, this stretch of the river, Abandoibarra, was heavy industry. There were shipyards and container yards, roads, railway lines and docks. Among them was the Euskalduna shipyard, founded in 1900.

Then came a deep industrial crisis, and in 1988 the shipyard closed.

So who brought the riverbank back? Not the museum on its own. In 1992 a public company, Bilbao Ría 2000, was set up to recover degraded and declining industrial land along the river.

The museum went up in the middle of that change. In its own words, building it "represented recovery of the banks of the river for the city, redeveloping them for culture and leisure." Today it calls the riverside avenue "an open-air leisure area", and "an emblematic example of the urban regeneration of Bilbao".

Shipyards and containers then. A promenade now.

At the water, walk along the river toward the big road bridge until you reach the pond beside the building, where a tall tree of silver balls stands in the water: The pond · Tall Tree & the Eye.

"The Spider is an ode to my mother. She was my best friend." Those are Louise Bourgeois's words, and here is the ode: almost nine metres of bronze legs. Her name is Maman, French for "Mum".

Bourgeois was a French-American artist, and she made Maman in 1999. The spider is bronze, marble and stainless steel, one of a whole series of spiders Bourgeois made, and one of the most ambitious, in the museum's words.

The story starts in a family workshop. "My family was in the business of tapestry restoration," Bourgeois said, "and my mother was in charge of the workshop." So when she said, "Like a spider, my mother was a weaver," she meant it almost literally. And: "Like spiders, my mother was very clever."

And the spider itself? "Spiders are friendly presences that eat mosquitoes," she said. Then: "So, spiders are helpful and protective, just like my mother."

The museum reads her a little less sweetly. Its text says the spiders "suggest both protector and predator", because a spider's silk is used "both to construct cocoons and to bind prey". And it reminds us that her work keeps returning to "painful childhood memories of an unfaithful father and a loving but complicit mother".

Now look at her legs. The museum calls them "legs like Gothic arches that act at once as a cage and as a protective lair". Follow one leg up to the body. Slung beneath it, if you can make it out, is a sac of white marble eggs, "perilously attached to her undercarriage". Then look at the ridged bronze itself.

Maman was first conceived for London, as the opening commission for Tate Modern's Turbine Hall, in May 2000. Bilbao's is one of an edition of six, plus an artist's proof, and another stands in Ottawa, at the National Gallery of Canada.

Strength and fragility, the museum says, in one body. A mother who guards and a creature who traps. Bourgeois let her be both.

Now walk on along the river walk toward the big road bridge, to where you can get close to the museum's titanium skin: River walk · Titanium, stone and glass.

In 1991 there were three names in the running for this museum. Only one of them gets the photographs.

The competition was by invitation. Alongside Frank Gehry, the museum's own timeline names the other two: the architect Arata Isozaki, and the studio Coop Himmelblau.

It wasn't a walkover. Thomas Krens, who ran the Guggenheim Foundation, later said the final choice came down to Gehry and Coop Himmelblau, after, in his words, "two arduous days of deliberation".

But Isozaki didn't leave empty-handed. The city has towers that carry his name. The museum's own architecture route recommends "the stairs between the Isozaki Towers for brand-new views of the city".

And the same route lists the city's contributions "by internationally renowned architects". It names "not only the Museum building by Frank Gehry, but also those by Santiago Calatrava, Arata Isozaki, Rafael Moneo, and Álvaro Siza, among others."

So the man who lost the competition ended up in the same sentence as the man who won it. In a city that remade its riverbank, there was room for more than one winner.

Seventy-three mirrors, and no two of them show you quite the same Bilbao.

This is Tall Tree and the Eye, by Anish Kapoor, from 2009. Kapoor was born in Bombay in 1954. The tree stands about thirteen metres tall, in stainless steel and carbon steel. In the museum's words, it "consists of 73 reflective spheres anchored around three axes".

Every ball is a mirror that curves away from you, so each one bends the world around itself. And each sits at its own angle, so each catches a different piece of the city. The museum lists what ends up in them. "Images of the surrounding city, including the Nervión river, Buren's sculptural intervention on La Salve Bridge, and the museum itself, are cast into dynamic suspension."

So here's a game. Pick one sphere and find the river in it. Then the titanium. Then a flash of red: Buren's arches on the bridge, if the angle is with you. Then find yourself, very small, standing at the edge of the water. Take a few steps along the path and watch everything slide from ball to ball.

The spheres reflect each other, too, and that's the point. The museum says the work ends up "simultaneously creating and dissolving form and space". And the museum draws the lesson: he "reminds us of the instability and ephemerality of our vision, and by extension of our world."

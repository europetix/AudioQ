Down there, the walls moved with you. From up here, they hold still.

The museum's own quickest route sends you here: "Go up to the second floor of the Museum and take a look from the balcony opposite gallery 204." It promises "a panoramic view of the eight monumental sculptures that make The Matter of Time".

The museum's guide for teachers adds that this balcony reveals "the exact location of the sculptures". Down on the floor, as you found among the spirals, you lose all reference.

So use the view. Count the eight. Find the first spiral and the twisted ellipses, then Snake, then the other spirals, then the two pieces cut from rings and spheres. Now look at the spaces between them. "The entire room is part of the sculptural field," in the museum's words, and from here the corridors you walked are lines drawn on the floor.

Wait for someone to walk into a spiral, and see how long it takes them to come out.

Remember Serra's line, that when you move, they move too, and you're always trying to get them? From up here, at last, you can get them. Or nearly. As Serra himself put it, "the way the pieces rise always remains partially hidden".

Notice, too, what isn't there: a single column.

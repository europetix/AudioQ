For years, a bouquet of giant, shiny Tulips by Jeff Koons stood on this terrace. Then, in October 2022, for the museum's twenty-fifth birthday, a pair of cranes swung two steel sculptures into their place. The museum lists Tulips as not on view.

The new arrivals are by Eduardo Chillida, the Basque sculptor from San Sebastián, who died in 2002. The taller one is Advice to Space Five, about three metres high. In the museum's words, it is "constructed from enormous sheets of steel", and it "combines an organic quality with a lightness". It suggests "a kind of chimney, a void of fire and heat that infuses the surrounding space with vital energy".

The other is Embrace Eleven. In Basque its title is Besarkada, which means "embrace". Its steel was forged, "brought forth in a struggle with the material placed in fire". Its parts rise like plants, then fold in on themselves, the museum says, "as they tightly embrace".

Look into the hollow at the heart of the tall one, the chimney, the void. Then find the place on the other where the steel folds against itself. Chillida had a phrase for it: "To construct is to build in space."

Now look out to the pond. The museum's architecture route says it "pays tribute to the Nervión estuary and its fundamental role in the development of the city of Bilbao". It "makes the perfect setting" for Anish Kapoor's tall tree of silver balls, and for Yves Klein's fire fountains, which burn only in the evening. Around you are the building's three materials, glass, titanium and limestone. If you can see the giant spider by the river, that's Louise Bourgeois's Maman.

And why this riverbank? The Basques first offered an old wine warehouse in town, and Thomas Krens said no. The story goes that he found this site while jogging, and that Gehry, separately, picked it out from Artxanda, the hill across the river.

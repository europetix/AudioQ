A wall leans over you. A few steps on, it's falling away from you. And you can't quite say where the change happened.

The pieces at this end of the gallery are the twisted ones. There's Torqued Spiral, with a subtitle you can almost walk: Closed, Open, Closed, Open, Closed. There's Torqued Ellipse, and Double Torqued Ellipse. Serra made them in 2003 and 2004. "Torqued" simply means twisted. You'll feel the twist before you can describe it.

Serra explained it himself, in words the museum prints in English: "They are torqued shapes which, when entering them, allow you to move to understand their torsion." In other words, you don't understand these by looking from outside. You understand them by walking. And he added: "When you move they move too in such a way that you are always trying to get them."

Between the two torqued ellipses there's a gap of about two metres, and that gap makes a corridor. Find it, and walk it slowly. The museum treats the whole room as part of the sculpture, so this corridor is part of the work, just as much as the steel on either side.

Now go inside one of them. As you follow the wall, notice the moment it stops leaning in and starts leaning out. Look up at the rim, where the steel meets the light of the room. And notice whether your body adjusts before your eyes do: a shoulder drops, a step shortens.

You're not looking at a sculpture now. You're inside one, and, just as Serra said, it moves when you move.

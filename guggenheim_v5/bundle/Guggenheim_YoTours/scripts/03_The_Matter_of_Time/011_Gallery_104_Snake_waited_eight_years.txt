For eight years, Snake was the only part of The Matter of Time in this room. The rest was still to come.

This is Snake, by Richard Serra, the oldest work in this room. He made it between 1994 and 1997 for the museum's opening. It didn't have the gallery to itself, though. At the opening it shared the room with other works, among them two Lichtensteins and a Warhol, as an Irish critic noted. The other seven Serras came later, and the complete Matter of Time opened in June 2005.

The museum's long-time director, Juan Ignacio Vidarte, said: "This huge gallery was envisioned to hold art of the 21st century." The finished work, he said, "makes the museum a pilgrimage site for anyone interested in the present and future state of sculpture".

The critic Robert Hughes described the room as "one old and seven new rolled steel sculptures".

Snake is the old one. The museum's guide for teachers says it is built from the conical section, "the basic constructive unit": its walls are slices of cones, cut in steel.

So walk its length. Watch how its walls lean, and notice whether the lean stays the same as you go, or slowly changes. Then compare it with the twisted pieces you've just walked through. Cones here. Twisting forms there. One steady idea, and the restless ones that followed.

It was the first. Everything else in this installation came after it.

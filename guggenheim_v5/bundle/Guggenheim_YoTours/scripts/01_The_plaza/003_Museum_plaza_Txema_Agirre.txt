This small square is named after a police officer. His full name was José María Aguirre Larraona. He is remembered by the Basque form of his name, Txema Agirre.

He was an officer of the Ertzaintza, the Basque police. He was thirty-five. He had a newborn son.

On Monday 13 October 1997, five days before the museum opened, men dressed as gardeners were unloading flower planters from a van beside the building. Ertzaintza officers went to check the van. Txema Agirre was one of them.

He was shot. He died of his wounds.

The planters hid grenades, meant for the opening. ETA's target was the inauguration, which King Juan Carlos, the Spanish prime minister and the Basque president were to attend. The Basque president, José Antonio Ardanza, called Txema Agirre a hero.

In the square that bears his name, there is a plaque, and there is a monolith. The plaque took fourteen years to be put up. On anniversaries, flowers are laid here for him. On the twenty-fifth anniversary, in 2022, a tribute was held at the monolith.

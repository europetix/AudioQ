The ground under this plaza was once a working riverbank. The museum describes its site as "a former wharf with port and industrial use", on a curve of the river Nervión. In the early 1990s the Basques decided to build something very different here.

Welcome to the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, in Basque, Guggenheim Bilbao Museoa. It's one of the Guggenheim museums, with New York and Venice. But it's run from here, by Basque institutions with the New York foundation. It opened in October 1997. Its architect was Frank Gehry, who died in December 2025, at ninety-six.

This guide is about the building, inside and out, and the works of art that live here. On the full tour, you'll meet them all. Puppy. Richard Serra's steel in the long gallery. Jenny Holzer's columns of light. Two sculptures by Eduardo Chillida. A spider called Maman, a tree of silver balls by Anish Kapoor, a sculpture made of fog, fountains of fire, and Daniel Buren's red arches. The changing exhibitions aren't part of this guide. You'll walk past some of them.

The route: the plaza first. Then down the broad stairway, through the Atrium to Serra's long gallery, and up to the balconies. Then out and all the way round to the river, along the water to the bridge.

There are three lengths, on one route. The full tour takes about an hour and forty minutes. The essential takes about an hour and a quarter, and the highlights about three quarters of an hour, walking included. Play the stops on your phone in order. In the shorter versions the track numbers may jump, and every stop tells you where to go next.

The fog in the pond by the river comes on the hour. The fire fountains burn only in the evening. And for step-free access, there's a lift for wheelchair users from the plaza, a ramp on the river side, and lifts between all the floors.

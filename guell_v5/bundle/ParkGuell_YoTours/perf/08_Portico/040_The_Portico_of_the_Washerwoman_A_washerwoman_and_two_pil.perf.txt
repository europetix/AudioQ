Somewhere along this row of stone, one column turns into a woman. The park's printed guide describes her, in Catalan, as a column "in the form of a caryatid that represents a washerwoman", one that "carries the tools of her trade".

A caryatid is a column carved in the shape of a woman, an idea that goes back to the temples of ancient Greece. This one is no temple maiden. She is a working woman, a laundress, taking her share of the weight.

[curious] Look at her closely, and at what she carries. The park's guide doesn't list her tools, so see for yourself what she holds.

[lightly] Not everyone sees only a washerwoman here. One researcher, writing in 2020, argued that the park hides a picture of the Temple of Jerusalem, worked out in light and geometry. The study even looks for Solomon's two pillars, Jachin and Boaz, in this portico. It's one reader's theory.

Carry on along the path to its end, where a spiral ramp winds down toward the old farmhouse, Casa Larrard, which is a school today. Stop at the foot of the ramp with the house in view, and keep to the path: Casa Larrard · Güell's farmhouse.

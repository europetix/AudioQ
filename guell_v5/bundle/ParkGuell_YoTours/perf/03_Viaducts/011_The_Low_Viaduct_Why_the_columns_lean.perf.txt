This bridge leans on purpose. From here you can see it for yourself. In the park's words, it "draws a curved line and is supported by two rows of inclined columns forming a parabola that includes the central vault".

In 2011 an engineering student at Barcelona's technical university, Antigoni Kopsida, made this viaduct the subject of her master's thesis. She measured about fifty metres of stone columns, up to six metres high. Her verdict was dry and clear. "The inclination of the columns apart from impressing aesthetically has also a structural meaning."

[pause] Here is what that means. The columns lean because the weight leans. The push of the road and its vaults doesn't run straight down. So each column is tilted along the path the load wants to take, and the thrust stays inside the stone, all the way to the ground. Her analysis found the vault stable.

Follow one column with your eyes, from its foot up into the vault, and see how the slope carries on into the curve above it.

Gaudí kept the idea. The La Pedrera foundation notes that he later used the same inclined columns in the crypt of the Colònia Güell, the church of Güell's industrial village.

A bridge that leans the way its load leans.

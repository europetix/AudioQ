[curious] Here is a road that was bent round a tree. The park calls the Middle Viaduct, the Pont del Mig, "the carob bridge, where the natural growth of this interesting tree is respected". A photo caption in the city history museum's dossier for schools puts it, in Catalan, as "the middle viaduct with the carob trunk".

Find the trunk. Rather than lose the tree, the bridge makes room for it.

Before the park, this hill was farmland: vineyards, olive groves and fruit orchards. The park says the farms were given up: hard to reach, hard to work, poor in yield, and, for the vines, struck by the plague of phylloxera.

The city's history museum says Gaudí respected the vegetation that was already here. So this carob is a survivor, perhaps, of the farm that came before.

For now, beyond Casa Trias, the path on to the top bridge is closed for works.

[lightly] A tree that got its own bridge.

Go back down past the Low Viaduct. Then take the steps and ramps down to the esplanade inside the Carrer d'Olot gate, to the foot of the great stairway and the second ticket check. If there's a queue, join it, and play the next stop while you wait: The foot of the stairway · The healthy quarter.

The estate's first buyer was Güell's own lawyer. The park's history calls him simply "a friend of Güell". In the summer of 1902, Bassegoda recorded, a plot on this hill was sold to Martí Trias i Domènech, the lawyer of the Güell family.

His house was designed by the architect Juli Batllevell, and it went up over the next few years. The magazine Time Out describes it in Spanish as "true to the style of his master", the modernista architect Domènech i Montaner.

[warmly] And it was a real home. The same magazine says that the lawyer and his wife, Anna Maxenchs, lived there for the first half of the twentieth century. Of all the buyers Güell hoped for, they were among the very few who came, and stayed.

Today, in the park's words, the house "continues to be a private residence and is still owned by the same family". The city, though, has moved to buy it compulsorily.

[pause] Look up through the trees. Somewhere above these gardens stands Casa Trias, the house of the estate's first buyer.

It's a private home, so we leave it in peace, and look at it only from here.

The most famous man who ever lived on this estate lived in a house he didn't design.

The house began as an advertisement. Remember the show house, put up to tempt buyers? This is it. A few years into the works, the contractor, Josep Pardo i Casanovas, built it as a model home. In the park's words, it was "built according to the plans of Gaudí's friend and collaborator, Francesc Berenguer i Mestres". The Sagrada Família's pages on the house go further, and call Berenguer Gaudí's friend and right-hand man.

But Berenguer had no architect's title. So Gaudí signed the plans. As Bassegoda wrote, in Catalan: "plans by Francesc Berenguer, signed Gaudí".

[amused] So if anyone tells you that Gaudí designed his own house here, it isn't so. Berenguer drew it. Gaudí signed it.

[pause] And the advertisement didn't work. No buyer came for it. In 1906 the Gaudí family bought it themselves, and Gaudí lived here for nearly twenty years, until 1925.

Later, a couple who admired him, the Chiappos, made it known as the Casa Gaudí. Thanks to a society called the Friends of Gaudí, it opened as a museum, the Casa Museu Gaudí, in 1963. Its garden holds iron railings from two of Gaudí's town houses, Casa Vicens and Casa Milà. It's salvaged Gaudí, like the palm-leaf iron gates in the wall at the park's main entrance, which came from Casa Vicens too.

[warmly] Now find it among the trees: the pink stucco walls, and the pointed tower. It's a museum today, and this guide stays outside.

[wry] This estate very nearly became a palace. After Güell's death, his heirs offered the park, together with their estate at Pedralbes, to King Alfonso the Thirteenth, as a site for a new royal palace. One review of the park's history gives the answer in a line of Catalan: "in 1921 the king chose Pedralbes."

The park's historians say the heirs had offered it to the city as well. And the city bought it, slowly. The council agreed to buy at a full meeting in May 1922, and the purchase took a few more years to complete.

The city's director of parks was Nicolau Maria Rubió i Tudurí. A columnist in the newspaper Ara has called him "the inventor of green Barcelona". He promoted the city's purchase of land for green space, and Park Güell was on his list.

[pause] The farmhouse went with the park. In 1931, in the park's words, it "became a municipal public school", named after Baldiri Reixac. He was an eighteenth-century parish priest and teacher. As the Catalan encyclopedia sums up his method, education should begin in the mother tongue, and not in Latin.

Look at the old house once more, and picture a royal palace on this hill instead.

[warmly] Nearly a king's palace. Instead, a school named for a man who wanted children taught in their own language.

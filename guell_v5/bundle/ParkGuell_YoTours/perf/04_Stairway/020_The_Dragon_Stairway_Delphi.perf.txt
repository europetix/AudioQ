[curious] At the top of the steps, sheltered under the hall, the park describes an odeon: a stone bench curved like a little Greek theatre. Its flyer adds, in Spanish, "a tripod that seems made of three snakes". See if you can find it.

Put these together, and some writers on the park see ancient Greece. At Delphi, the god Apollo killed the serpent Python, and buried it under his temple, where it guarded the waters under the earth. His priestess gave her oracles seated on a tripod. And the temple was Doric. Here: a dragon guarding water, a tripod, and Doric-style columns above.

Bassegoda took it seriously. He was reported as saying: "The Delphi temple was Doric, and that is why Eusebio Güell wanted the columns of the park he commissioned from Gaudí to be of the Doric order."

[pause] Not everyone agrees. In 2023 the scholar María-Belén García Ávila wrote that "several authors claim it comes from the image of the temple dedicated to Apollo at Delphi". And she warned: "There is a lot of literature written, above all commercial, speculating about the inspiration of the hypostyle hall." She suggested the hall's first plan may have followed another temple of Apollo, at Cyrene, in North Africa.

[wry] As you heard beside the dragon, Gaudí never explained it. Delphi is a scholar's reading, and a beautiful one.

Step to where you can see the front of the hall above the stairway. Its outer row of fat, fluted columns is on the left as you arrive at the top: The Hypostyle Room · A market hall in Greek dress.

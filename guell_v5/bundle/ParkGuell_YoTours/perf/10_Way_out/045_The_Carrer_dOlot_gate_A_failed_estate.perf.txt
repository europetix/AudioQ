[warmly] A failed housing estate that became a triumph of a park. That's the shortest honest history of this hill. Sixty plots, and only two houses ever built. A market hall for an estate that never filled. A theatre on its roof, built for a town that never came.

The man who paid for it stayed to the end, in the old farmhouse on the way down, Casa Larrard, until 1918. Then the city bought the park from his heirs and opened it to everyone in April 1926. [quietly] That June, Gaudí died.

[pause] What was left behind is what you've walked through today. The city's history museum puts the idea in one line. "Gaudí's idea was a perfect symbiosis between urban planning, architecture and nature." Rain led down through columns, a square made into a roof, a hillside held up by a wave of stone.

And it was never one man's work. The colour on the bench and on the ceiling of the hall is largely Josep Maria Jujol's. Francesc Berenguer, Gaudí's right hand, drew the show house where Gaudí lived. The builders were family, an uncle and his nephew, Josep Pardo i Casanovas and Josep Berdié i Pardo. A caretaker kept the gate. And the lawyer Martí Trias bought the very first plot.

[warmly] Remember the name on the wall, Park, with a k. Güell spelt it in English, for a British-style estate for the few. It became Barcelona's park instead, for everyone.

Take one last look up the stairway, to the columns of the hall. Thank you for walking the hill with us.

The main gate on Carrer d'Olot is the way out. Once you've left the park, you can't come back in, so go when you're ready.

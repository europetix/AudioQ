It began with a shop window. At the Paris Exhibition of 1878, Eusebi Güell saw a display case Gaudí had made for a glove shop, Esteve Comella's. The park's own history says the partnership "began when Güell saw a window display that Gaudí had planned" for that glove shop.

Güell was born in Barcelona, the son of Joan Güell i Ferrer. He studied there, in France and in England. With Ferran Alsina he built up the cloth works that became the Colònia Güell, his industrial village. Then came cement. His Asland company opened some of the first Portland cement works in Catalonia. Güell's cement helped build the twentieth-century city.

He was a city councillor, then a senator, and president of the Centre Català. And this textile baron also wrote a book on microbiology, in French, published in Paris.

In 1886 Güell asked Gaudí to build his new house in the old city, the Palau Güell. In 1900 he gave him this hill to plan. The same history calls the bond between them "not simply that of an artist and his patron, but a real story of friendship".

Now look at the gates beside you. Their iron is shaped like palm leaves, and they weren't made for the park at all. The park's page on this entrance says it plainly. They "do not form part of the original plan, but came from Casa Vicens". That was a house Gaudí had built in the city years earlier.

Find a single leaf in the ironwork, and follow it from its stem out to its tips. That iron was shaped for another house, and it ended up here.

A partnership that began in a shop window, and a gate that began somewhere else. Even the way in is a transplant.

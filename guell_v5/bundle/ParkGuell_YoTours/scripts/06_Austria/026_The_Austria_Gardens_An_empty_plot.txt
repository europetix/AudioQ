This garden was meant to be somebody's house. What we now call the Austria Gardens, the park says, "was one of the zones to be used as plots in the estate". Remember the sixty plots? This is what one of them looks like with no house on it.

No house ever came. When the estate became a public park, the city used this ground as a municipal plant nursery. Its name, the Jardins d'Àustria, came from "a donation of trees from Austria in 1977".

Those trees had a hard time. The park's own nature pages explain that many of them died. They were mountain trees, from high up, and they could not adapt to the Mediterranean climate. So in 2012 the lawns were taken out, and replaced with flowering and aromatic plants.

Look at the beds around you. They're from 2012, not 1977. The park's page on these gardens says this corner has "a completely different look to the rest of the park". It feels more like a garden than a hillside.

From the middle of these gardens, the same page adds, "the two houses that were built in Eusebi Güell's time can be seen". Two houses, from all those plots.

Before you walk on, picture a house right here among the trees, with its own garden round it, waiting for neighbours who never came.

A botanist once gave this park a double title. In 2011 the director of Barcelona's botanic garden, Josep Maria Montserrat, gave a talk about it. The talk's summary calls Park Güell "the last modernist garden, and one of the first Mediterranean gardens in Barcelona".

Why Mediterranean? Apart from the great square, the talk explained, the aim was to keep the "pre-existing agricultural landscape", mainly carob and olive trees. Not a lawn and a flower bed, but the hillside as it was.

The city history museum says much the same of Gaudí. Its guide says, in Catalan, that he "respected the existing vegetation and planted Mediterranean species". The museum's story for schoolchildren goes a step further. In it, Güell has the hill replanted "with pines, holm oaks, palms and olives".

The garden is still changing. In 2009 high winds blew down a number of trees, and in 2010 snow damaged a great deal more. Since then, the park says, it has favoured native species that need little water.

Now look up into the trees around you, and listen. The park's own list of the birds here runs to twenty-five kinds, among them the hoopoe. You may not see one. You may well hear something.

A garden made of what already grew on the hill.

Follow the upper path on to the Nature Square, the great open square on top of the hall of columns. Stop out in the open, away from the bench: The Nature Square · You're standing on the roof.

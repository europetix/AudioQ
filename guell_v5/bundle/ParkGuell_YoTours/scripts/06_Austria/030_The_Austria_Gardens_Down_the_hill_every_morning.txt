How did Gaudí spend his days, in the years he lived up here? The Sagrada Família's own blog has pieced it together.

In the morning, it says, "he walked down to the Sant Joan de Gràcia parish church, where he normally worshipped in those years". After mass he walked on to the Sagrada Família, the church he had been commissioned to build back in 1883, and worked there. In the evening, after work, he went to the church of Sant Felip Neri, to pray and to take part in vespers.

Then he came home, back up this hill. He came "on foot or by tram, with the evening edition of the newspaper La Veu de Catalunya under his arm". The title means the Voice of Catalonia.

The blog does the sum for us: "Gaudí walked between eight and ten kilometres a day through the streets of Barcelona." And the way home always ended uphill.

Now look out over the city below you. Somewhere down there ran his route: down in the morning, through the streets, and back up at night to the quiet of this hill. The park's page on these gardens says simply that they have "good views".

The hill was his home. The city below was his work.

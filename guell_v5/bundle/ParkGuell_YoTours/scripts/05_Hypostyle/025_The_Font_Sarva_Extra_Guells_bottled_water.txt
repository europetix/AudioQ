The man who paid for this park also tried to sell its water. In 1913 Eusebi Güell had this spring analysed, and found magnesium in it. He decided to bottle it and sell it as medicinal water. For about a decade, says Barcelona's local broadcaster, betevé, you could buy it in chemists' shops.

The spring is older than anything Gaudí built here. Betevé calls it, in Catalan, "this feature, older than the modernista complex". Its water comes from mines deep in the hill. A city works notice places it "in the enclosing wall of the Hypostyle Room".

Long before the park, people from La Salut, the neighbourhood around the hill, came up with jugs to fetch its water. It was also piped to the well of the old Larrard farmhouse, which Güell later made his home.

Then for years it lay hidden in the hall's basement. The works that ended in 2020 opened up its little room, a space that, in the park's words, "until now had not been accessible". Visitors have been able to see it since September 2020.

As you look in, picture the neighbours with their jugs, and Güell's bottles lined up on a chemist's shelf.

Then follow the one-way route out and up the hall's right-hand side to the Austria Gardens. Stop as you come into the gardens: The Austria Gardens · An empty plot.

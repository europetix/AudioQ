The colour over your head is not Gaudí's. It belongs to his young assistant, Josep Maria Jujol. Jujol was born in Tarragona in 1879, twenty-seven years after Gaudí. A study of him by the historian Joan Duch dates their years of full collaboration from 1904 to 1914.

So who did what? Gaudí built the vault. In the park's words, the ceiling "is formed of small domes constructed using the traditional technique of clay bricks". They were "decorated with original tile-shard mosaics made by Josep M. Jujol". The archive of the Càtedra Gaudí, at the city's technical university, puts it in Catalan: Jujol "designs the colourful trencadís of the undulating bench and the panels of the hypostyle hall". Trencadís is broken-tile mosaic. And he made them, it adds, "with recycled material".

Now look up at the small domes nearest you, and at the broken-tile panels set into them. Follow the colour from one dome to the next.

Jujol's own son, writing about his father, put the partnership in two halves. As Duch quotes him, in building Gaudí influenced Jujol, and in decoration Jujol influenced Gaudí. Gaudí went on trusting him. At Casa Milà he trusted Jujol with the interior decoration and, the archive says, the running of the building site.

Take Jujol's name with you. You'll find his hand again on the bench.

This is the creature everybody queues to photograph. The park calls it "the dragon, or salamander, covered with decorative tile-shard mosaic". And it admits the creature "has become the most popular image of the park".

It's also a drain. At the top of these steps stands the hall that was meant to be Güell's market, and under that hall is a large tank for rainwater. This fountain was fed from it. Its mouth was the tank's overflow. Barcelona's water museum says: "The fountain acted as a spillway when the cistern was full." And today, "It is currently connected to the mains."

What is it meant to be? A little below the dragon, on this same stairway, are Catalonia's red and yellow stripes, under a serpent's head. And some writers, among them the Gaudí scholar Joan Bassegoda, see in this creature the Python of Delphi, the serpent that guarded hidden water. Gaudí never explained it.

The creature has had a rough time. In February 2007 vandals with an iron rod seriously damaged it. Separately, in 2025, its front right foot was taken away and studied for over a year by the sculptor Abel Vallhonesta, then put back. The restoration was finished on 22 April 2026.

It has had its moment in the cinema, too. In Woody Allen's Vicky Cristina Barcelona, Vicky bumps into Juan Antonio right here, in front of this fountain.

Now find the creature's own front right foot, the one that came back from the sculptor's workshop. Then look up to its open mouth, where the spare water poured out when the tank below was full.

The most famous dragon in Barcelona was built to be a drain.

You're standing on a roof. "This large square is artificial," the park admits. Part of it is dug into the rock of the hill. The rest is "held up on top of the hypostyle room". Below your feet is the market hall, with its forest of columns.

On the first plans this was the Greek Theatre. Later it was "rechristened as Plaça de la Natura", the Nature Square. It was "planned for staging large open-air shows that could be watched from the surrounding terraces". A theatre, then, with the terraces around it for seats.

Now look down at the ground. It's sand and gravel, and that's on purpose. This is where the rain starts its trip down those drainpipe columns. Between 2017 and 2019 the square's waterproofing, and its link to the tank, were redone.

Some people think the idea went further. One architect's doctoral thesis argues that the whole park is a rain machine, built to stop the hill washing away and to bring the trees back to it. Its author admits that no document proves it. It's an argument, not a fact, but stand here in a downpour and you might be persuaded.

Then find the long retaining wall that holds back the hill. The park says it's finished with "large capitals made to look like palm trees". See if you can pick one out.

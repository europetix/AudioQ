This bench prays. Josep Maria Jujol engraved prayers to the Virgin Mary into its tiles. One study calls their meaning an open question.

A study of his life says that crosses, Latin phrases, prayers and psalms run all through his work. In 1986 his son wrote about Jujol's work here, and the critic Ronald Christ about the inscriptions.

The restorers of the late 1980s and early 1990s wrote that its condition was "very precarious". They found that the rounded top pieces carried an original design, probably Jujol's, with engravings, inscriptions and symbols. Where new tiles were needed, made with the ceramist Toni Cumella, they refused to fake his hand. The inscriptions and symbols were left out of the new pieces, and each new piece was engraved with the year it was made. Run your eye along the top: you're reading two hands, his and theirs.

How much is original? In 2016 some experts claimed only a fifth of the original mosaic survives. The organiser of a Gaudí congress blamed, in Catalan, "neglect, or bad restorations, or rather than restorations, reconstructions". The city answered that it replaces only tiles that can't be restored, and that in the 1980s the white tiles were renewed and the coloured ones restored.

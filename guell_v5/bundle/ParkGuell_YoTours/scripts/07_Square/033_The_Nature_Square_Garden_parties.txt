In October 1906 the park hosted a party with an English name. The delegates of the first international congress of the Catalan language came up the hill for a Garden Party. The park's historians point out that it was called just that, in English. It was held as part of the congress. A press photographer, Frederic Ballell, was there.

Why throw a party on an estate that was still half built? The same historians say Güell's aim was to promote his estate. The parties were also advertisements.

And people kept coming. Within a year, the park's history says, events were being held in this square. In Güell's lifetime there were Catalanist gatherings here, and sardana dancing, the Catalan dance in a ring. Unfinished as it was, the park was already "one of Barcelona's great tourist attractions".

In 1908 there was even a race, for charity. It started at the gate on Carrer d'Olot, and finished on top of the hill of the three crosses, the highest point of the park.

In April 2026, a hundred years after it became a public park, the neighbours were invited to a community dinner in the park, in the spirit of those early parties.

Look round the square and fill it in your mind: long tables, a press photographer, dancers in a ring.

A square built for an estate's parties, and still throwing them.

Now go to the edge of the square on the stairway side, where the long tiled bench runs. Sit if there's room: The Nature Square · A hundred metres of broken tile.

The stone over your head is holding up a hillside. The park describes this portico as one that "adopts the shape of a great wave atop slanting columns". Underneath the wave it is "a double colonnade that acts as a buttress", set against "a retaining wall made from unworked stone".

In Catalan it's the Pòrtic de la Bugadera, the Portico of the Washerwoman, though the park's signs may call it the Laundry Room Portico. It takes its name from one of its columns, carved in the shape of a washerwoman.

Think for a moment about what a buttress does. Behind that rough wall is the hill itself, its weight pressing outward. The double row of columns braces the wall against it. The portico stands on leaning columns, as Gaudí's road bridges in the park do. Shelter for walkers, and a prop for the hillside, in one piece of building.

The park calls it "one of the finest examples of the organic architecture upheld by Gaudí". That means building that takes its shapes from nature, and here the shape is easy to read. A wave rolls along the side of the hill, and breaks over your head.

Look along the slanting columns, and then at the wall behind them. The stone was left unworked: rough pieces, never cut into neat blocks. Nothing here was carved smooth, except one column.

This is one of the few modest homes Gaudí ever built. The city's history museum describes it as "a small space, designed to be used by a humble family". It was built for the man who kept the gate, right beside it.

The study written for its restoration lists what this floor held: a vestibule, a kitchen, a pantry and a living room. Everyday rooms, for everyday life.

Look at the openings in the thick walls between the rooms. The same study describes them as two parabolic arches, the shape of a hanging chain turned upside down.

Then look at the colour of the walls. They are lime plaster, painted with natural pigments. When the house was restored in the late 1990s, the restorers found each room's original colour under the later paint, and brought it back.

The museum's theme for this floor is the house itself, and what it shows of Gaudí's architectural uniqueness.

The cleverest house on the hill was built for its humblest family.

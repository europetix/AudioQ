A house designed for a humble family needed bedrooms, and up here they had a whole floor of them. This level held four bedrooms, with an attic space above them.

Look at what holds the floor up. Downstairs Gaudí opened the walls with arches. Up here he used beams of steel and ceramic instead.

The exhibition's theme on this floor is the park: how the estate began, and how, in 1926, it became a park for the whole city. Why the estate failed is told at the foot of the great stairway.

The historian who curated this exhibition, Mireia Freixa, put Güell's part in one line when the house reopened. As an English press note reported her words, "behind every great artist is an ambitious promoter".

It's a fair point in this house. The museum's own visits here stress two relationships: the one between Gaudí and Güell, and the one between the park and the city below.

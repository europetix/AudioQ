This is how a private estate became everyone's park. In the early 1920s the city bought the park from Güell's heirs. It opened as a municipal park on 26 April 1926. As the park's historians put it, it was now open to all the city's people. The estate built for sixty rich families belonged to everyone.

Six weeks later, on an early June evening, Gaudí was hit by a tram at the corner of Bailèn and the Gran Via, in the city below. He was taken to the Hospital de la Santa Creu. He died there three days later.

He had moved out of his house here the year before, into his workshop at the Sagrada Família. So he knew this hill as a public park for only a few weeks, and by then he no longer lived on it.

The hill went on without him. Thirty years after the opening, Salvador Dalí gave one of his eccentric lectures here, and it was filmed. The Spanish state declared the park a monument, and in 1984 UNESCO made it World Heritage. Today it is one of seven works by Gaudí on that list.

2026 was named Gaudí Year, for the hundredth anniversary of his death.

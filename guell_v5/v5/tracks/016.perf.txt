Under the terraces of this stairway are two grottos, and one of them was built for waiting in. The park says it was used as a waiting shelter, "supported by a magnificent conical central column". [wry] A bus shelter, before there were buses.

The stair itself is a show. "From the entrance esplanade a twin flight of steps soars up", in the park's words, "flanked by two walls with merlons that form terraces under which there are two grottos". Merlons are the square teeth along the top of a castle wall. The park's works pages call it the Monumental Staircase. It's a double stair, set out in three flights, mixing rock and planting, with water from a fountain running down them.

If you can see into the grottos, look for the one held up by a thick, cone-shaped column. That's the shelter.

[curious] Now look up the first flight. On the first landing are what the park calls "capricious shapes like goblins". Take a moment to pick one out among the rocks.

In 2021 the stair's first basin was cleared of plants and silt, and its broken tile recovered.

A stair built to wait under, and to be looked at.

Climb the first flight, past the goblins, to the red and yellow stripes of Catalonia with a snake's head above them. Stop to one side: The Dragon Stairway · A flag on the stairs.

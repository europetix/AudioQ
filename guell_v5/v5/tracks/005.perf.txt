A gated estate needed a front desk. In the story the city's history museum tells schoolchildren in Catalan, this house held "a room for visitors, with telephones and armchairs". A study written for its restoration found two rooms of modest size: a reception room downstairs, and an office upstairs.

This was the estate's business end, built for work rather than for living, and its story is best told from out here.

Look up the tower. It stands about thirteen metres high. The restoration study describes it as a hyperbolic paraboloid that narrows as it rises, built of two layers of thin brick, wound with twisted metal straps in a double spiral.

On top is Gaudí's cross, with arms pointing four ways. [quietly] It's the third cross on this tower. The original was clad in pieces of porcelain, plates and cups and bowls, and it was destroyed in 1936, at the start of the Civil War. A cross put up after the war stood until 1996. The one you see was made then, from photographs of the original.

Look, too, at the windows, for the round bullseye panes of moulded, blown and tinted glass. They were made again when this pavilion was restored in the 1990s.

A telephone room for an estate that had no residents yet, under a tower of thin brick.

Now cross the esplanade to the other gatehouse, the one without the tower, and stand outside its door: Casa del Guarda · A modest dwelling.

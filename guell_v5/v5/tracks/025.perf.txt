[warmly] A failed housing estate that became a triumph of a park. That's the shortest honest history of this hill. Sixty plots, and only two houses ever built. A market hall for an estate that never filled. A theatre on its roof, built for a town that never came.

The man who paid for it stayed to the end. Eusebi Güell lived and died, in 1918, in the old farmhouse on the way down, Casa Larrard. In the early 1920s the city bought the park from his heirs, and in April 1926 it opened to everyone.

[quietly] Six weeks later, Gaudí was dead, run over by a tram in the city below.

[pause] What was left behind is what you've walked through today. The city's history museum puts the idea in a single line: "Gaudí's idea was a perfect symbiosis between urban planning, architecture and nature." Roads laid across the hill, rain led down through columns, a square made into a roof.

And it was never one man's work. The colour on the bench and on the ceiling of the hall is largely Josep Maria Jujol's. Francesc Berenguer, Gaudí's right hand, designed the show house where Gaudí lived. The builders were family, an uncle and his nephew, Josep Pardo i Casanovas and Josep Berdié i Pardo. A caretaker kept the gate. And the lawyer Martí Trias bought the very first plot.

[warmly] Remember the name on the wall, Park, with a k. Güell spelt it in English for a British-style estate for the few. It became Barcelona's park instead, for everyone.

In 2026 it turned a hundred, as a public park.

Take one last look up the stairway, to the columns of the hall. Thank you for walking the hill with us.

The main gate on Carrer d'Olot is the way out. Once you've left the park, you can't come back in, so go when you're ready.

[warmly] Park Güell was never meant to be a park. The city's history museum calls it "a residential condominium for sixty well-heeled families in the purest British style". Eusebi Güell wanted to recreate the British residential parks, and that's why it's *Park*, with a k.

Its makers were Güell, one of the great industrialists of his city, and Antoni Gaudí, his architect for over twenty years. Work began in 1900. This is how their private estate became everyone's park.

We begin at the gate and its gatehouses. The longer tours then climb to the roads and bridges Gaudí built first. Then come the great stairway and its dragon, the hall of columns, the Austria Gardens, the Nature Square and its bench, and the Portico of the Washerwoman. We end past Güell's own house, at the main gate on Carrer d'Olot, the way out.

[pause] Please listen on headphones: the park doesn't allow speakers. Partway up there's a second ticket check, and beyond it the route runs one way, so you can't go back. Once you leave the park, you can't come back in. The paths are steep, with many steps. The park publishes routes for people with reduced mobility. Everything is already on your phone, so you won't need a signal.

The full tour takes about an hour and a half, a little longer if you go into the Casa del Guarda. The essential takes about an hour and a quarter, the highlights about three quarters of an hour, walking and a short wait included. Play the stops in order. The numbers on your phone may jump, but every stop tells you where to go next.

From the Marianao entrance, on the Carrer d'Olot side, the start is close by. From the Carretera del Carmel, or the escalators at Sant Josep de la Muntanya, follow the signs down to the main gate on Carrer d'Olot, without going through the second ticket check.

Start on the entrance esplanade, just inside the main gate on Carrer d'Olot, between the two little gatehouses with their tiled roofs. Turn to face the gate and the wall: The entrance esplanade · Park with a k.

[curious] In the summer of 1899, the Marquis of Marianao sold Eusebi Güell the top of a hill. It was called the Muntanya Pelada, the Bald Mountain. It rose above the old town of Gràcia, in a quarter called La Salut, which means health. While you wait, here's what Güell meant to build up there.

In 1904 he applied to the city for permission. His application covered two old farms, Can Muntaner de Dalt and Can Coll i Pujol. With it went a memorandum signed by Güell himself. In Spanish, it praised the land's "excellent hygienic conditions", which had earned it the name "barrio de la salud", the healthy quarter. And it offered "villas where the people of Barcelona can live comfortably and independently, without losing sight of their usual business".

That is the pitch in one line. Not a public park. A healthy suburb for businessmen, high above the city, but close enough to keep an eye on the office.

Health was a real selling point. The city's history museum tells this as a water story. Down below, Barcelona was crowded, and its household wells were being fouled. Up here, Gaudí and Güell had to work out a water system of their own, for a whole estate. You'll meet a piece of it on the way up.

[wry] The paperwork, meanwhile, took its time. The plans in the city archive carry both signatures, Güell's and Gaudí's, and they are dated 26 October 1904. The permit came on 30 January 1906. By then the digging on this hill had been going on for more than five years. Roads, walls and gatehouses were already up before anyone had officially said yes.

[pause] So that was the dream: clean air, clean water, and a quiet villa for a man of business. Whether anyone would buy it was another question.

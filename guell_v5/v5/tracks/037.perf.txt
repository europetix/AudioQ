[curious] The hill you're looking up at is the park's highest point, about a hundred and eighty metres up: the Turó de les Tres Creus, the hill of the three crosses. The park says it was once called "Ore Hill or the Hill of Mines and Caves".

A chapel for the estate was meant to stand up there. On a plan of 1903 its outline survives, labelled "Capelya", lobed like a six-petalled flower. That same year an architect of the time, Bonaventura Bassegoda, wrote in a Barcelona newspaper, the Diario de Barcelona, that the chapel would protect the whole colony, looking down on it.

[pause] It was never begun: the plots didn't sell. One history of the hill says, in Catalan: "then it was decided to build the Calvary instead of the chapel." By 1905 three crosses of rough stone stood up there on a mound, reached by eighteen symbolic steps. Find them now against the sky.

In July 1936, as the Civil War began, the crosses were destroyed. After the war they were rebuilt, not quite as before.

[warmly] From a glazed window in the pink house, Gaudí could look up to them. The same history calls it the hill "which Gaudí wanted always to have in view".

Where the chapel should have been, three crosses.

Take a last look: once you leave the square, you can't come back. Find the old iron door at the corner of the square on your right as you look out over the bench. It once led into the gardens of Casa Larrard. Go through it and stop among the pines on the path beyond: The path above Casa Larrard · Two thousand pines.

[quietly] For a few years, this hill was almost bare again. During the Civil War, nearly every tree in the park was cut down for firewood. When the war was over, one history of the hill records, the city planted two thousand conifers and other trees. Much of the park's pinewood is younger than the war.

You came through an original iron door, the park says, one that leads "to where there used to be the gardens of Casa Larrard". This path runs above that house, through a pine grove. It was the way down to Güell's own home.

[pause] The trees have had a hard century. In 2020 Storm Glòria brought down two old trees close to the portico ahead. As they fell, their roots broke the ceramic roof of an old water mine, a narrow tunnel that carried water to Casa Larrard. It was never meant for visitors, and it stays closed. The paving round the portico was restored two years later.

Look up into the pines around you, at the light coming through them. On a hill that a war once stripped, they are the patient part of the story.

[lightly] A war took almost every tree on this hill. The pines came back.

Follow the path on to a portico of stone shaped like a breaking wave, and stand under it, to one side, so the one-way flow can pass: The Portico of the Washerwoman · The wave.

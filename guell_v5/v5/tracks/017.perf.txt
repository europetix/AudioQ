[curious] Halfway up this stair, most people are already looking past it, at the dragon. Stop for a moment on what's here. The park's Spanish flyer describes "a spout in the shape of a serpent's head over the coat of arms of Catalonia". Find the red and yellow stripes, and the snake's head above them.

Why put a flag on a garden stair? Look at the man who paid for it. Eusebi Güell was president of the Centre Català, a Catalanist association. And in 1900, the year the works began on this hill, he presided over the Jocs Florals, the Catalan poetry festival.

His architect shared the outlook. A scholar who studied this park wrote in 2023 that it's well known Güell and Gaudí thought alike, "since both were very much Catalanists".

[pause] The stripes fit that picture. For Güell's circle, the park was also a stage for Catalan identity. Anyone climbing these steps to the estate's market would pass Catalonia's colours on the way.

Climb on to the dragon, just above, the creature in broken tile that everyone stops for. Find a place beside it where you're not blocking the steps: The Dragon Stairway · The dragon is a drain.

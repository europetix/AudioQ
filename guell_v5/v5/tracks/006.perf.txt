[curious] Of everything Gaudí built on this hill, this is the one house made for a working man. The city's history museum calls it "a modest dwelling". It was to be the home of the estate's caretaker, right beside the gate he kept.

Look at its shape. The plan is an oval: an ellipse, closed at each end by a half-circle. On the roof sits what the La Pedrera foundation calls a "mushroom-shaped dome", the round white cap you can see from here.

The house is built of the hill itself. The study written for its restoration found the ground-floor walls half a metre thick, made of stone from the park's own old quarries.

The estate it guarded never filled up. From the 1920s to the 1980s both gatehouses were private homes, and they "suffered many changes and mutilations", in the Spanish words of that same study. Later the other one even served as a builders' hut. This house was restored at the end of the 1990s, and since 2010 it has been a small branch of the city's history museum.

[pause] Inside, the museum's exhibition is called "Güell, Gaudí and Barcelona, the expression of an urban ideal". There's one theme on each floor: the house, the park and the city.

[warmly] The caretaker lived inside that curve, keeping the gate of an estate that was still waiting for its buyers.

If you're on the full tour and can go inside, there are three short Extras in the house, one for each floor. Start on the ground floor with: Casa del Guarda · Extra · The caretaker's rooms. Otherwise, the next main stop is up among the roads Gaudí built first. Walk along the inside of the wall, past this house, to the Marianao entrance, without going out. From just inside it, steps and ramps climb to the Low Viaduct, the Pont de Baix. Stop on it: The Low Viaduct · Roads before houses.

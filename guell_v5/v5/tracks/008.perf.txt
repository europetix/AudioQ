[curious] The park's own description of this stairway sounds almost out of breath. "From the entrance esplanade a twin flight of steps soars up", it says, "flanked by two walls with merlons that form terraces under which there are two grottos". Its formal name is the *Escalinata Monumental*. On the park's map it's the Monumental Staircase.

It's a double stair, set out in three flights, with water from a fountain running down them. One of the two grottos was a waiting shelter, "supported by a magnificent conical central column". [wry] A bus shelter, before there were buses.

Now look up the first flight. On the first landing are what the park calls "capricious shapes like goblins". Halfway up, in the words of the park's Spanish flyer, is "a spout in the shape of a serpent's head over the coat of arms of Catalonia". Find the red and yellow stripes, and the snake's head above them.

[pause] Why put Catalonia's arms on a garden stair? Güell was president of the Centre Català, and in 1900 he presided over the Jocs Florals, the Catalan poetry festival. In his time, the park says, the great square at the top was often let for "Catalanist events, traditional Catalan sardana dancing". A scholar wrote in 2023, in Spanish, that it is well known Güell and Gaudí shared an outlook, "since both were very much Catalanists". For Güell's circle, the park was also a stage for Catalan identity.

In 2021 the stair's first basin was cleared of plants and silt, and its broken tile recovered.

[warmly] So the way up to Güell's market and square is also a flag, with goblins on the first landing and a snake for company.

Climb on to the dragon, halfway up, the creature in broken tile that everyone stops for. Find a place beside it where you're not blocking the steps: The Dragon Stairway · The dragon is a drain.

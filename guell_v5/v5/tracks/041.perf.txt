The man who paid for all this never built himself a new house. Eusebi Güell moved into one that was already here: "the stately farmhouse of the old Can Muntaner de Dalt estate", as the park calls it.

Its other name comes from an earlier owner. Bassegoda described the Larrard family as "a rich family of French origin, made rich by trade and banking". The Larrards sold the property to the Marquis of Marianao, the same man who sold the top of this hill to Güell.

[curious] So why would one of the great industrialists of his city leave a palace for a farmhouse? Güell had a palace in the old city, one that Gaudí had built for him. The city history museum's guide gives two reasons. The streets around the palace had decayed, and the political and social situation was, in its Catalan words, "molt tensa", very tense. So in 1907 he moved up the hill, to his own estate.

The old farmhouse was adapted for him by Gaudí. Patron and architect were now neighbours on the same hill: Gaudí in the pink show house, Güell down here. In the park's words, Güell "lived there until his death". Eusebi Güell died in this house on 9 July 1918.

[pause] Look at it from here: an old country house, older than the park around it. Of the three homes on the estate in Güell's time, the one he chose for himself was the oldest. It's a working school today, so this guide stays outside, on the path.

A farmhouse for the man who built a garden city.

Walk on a few steps along the path, with the house still in view, and stop out of the way: Casa Larrard · A palace for a king.

[curious] This is the creature everybody queues to photograph. The park's own description is careful with its name: "the dragon, or salamander, covered with decorative tile-shard mosaic". It admits that the creature "has become the most popular image of the park".

[pause] It's also a drain. This fountain was once fed from a large tank under the hall of columns at the top of the steps. The park's own text names the dragon's mouth as the tank's overflow. Barcelona's water museum puts it plainly: "The fountain acted as a spillway when the cistern was full." And today, "It is currently connected to the mains."

So what is it? In 2015 a zoologist, Bayard Brattstrom, wrote to the New York Review of Books. He thought it looked most like a garden lizard, or a North African rainbow lizard. "It is not a crocodile as some have said." [amused] The critic Martin Filler replied that Gaudí's power lay in "his inventive imagination, rather than any strict fidelity to this or that prototype".

Whose ideas does it carry? The Gaudí scholar Joan Bassegoda said that Gaudí's dragons "are taken from mythology and history, and reflect the ideas of Count Güell": for him, Catalanism, mythology and religion. A little below the dragon, on this same stairway, are Catalonia's red and yellow stripes, under a serpent's head. And some scholars see in this creature the Python of Delphi, the serpent that guarded hidden water. Gaudí never explained it.

In February 2007 vandals with an iron rod seriously damaged it. Its front right foot has since been taken away, studied by the sculptor Abel Vallhonesta, and restored. The park says that work was finished on 22 April 2026.

[warmly] Find that front right foot. Then look up to its open mouth, where the spare water poured out. The most famous dragon in Barcelona was built to be a drain.

Climb to the top of the stairway, where the last flight runs in under the hall of columns. Stop by the curved stone bench there, shaped like a little Greek theatre, and look for a tripod that seems made of snakes: The Dragon Stairway · Delphi?

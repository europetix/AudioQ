[curious] The stone over your head is holding up a hillside. The park describes this portico as one that "adopts the shape of a great wave atop slanting columns". It is "a double colonnade that acts as a buttress", leaning against "a retaining wall made from unworked stone". In Catalan it's the Pòrtic de la Bugadera, the Portico of the Washerwoman, though the park's signs may call it the Laundry Room Portico.

It isn't only a shelter. The portico carries a path on its back, raised on leaning columns, the same trick Gaudí used for the park's viaducts. The park calls it "one of the finest examples of the organic architecture upheld by Gaudí".

[pause] Now look along the columns for the one that gives the portico its name. The park's printed guide describes one of them, in Catalan, as "shaped as a caryatid that represents a washerwoman". A column carved as a woman, "carrying the tools of her trade". Find her, and see what she holds.

This was the way down to Güell's own house. From an old iron door on the square, the path led into what were the gardens of Casa Larrard, running above the house through a pine grove. In 2020 Storm Glòria brought down two old trees close by. Their roots tore up the ceramic roof of an old water mine that carried water to the house. The paving around the portico was restored in 2022.

[lightly] Not everyone sees only a washerwoman here. One researcher, writing in 2020, argued that the whole park hides a picture of the Temple of Jerusalem, worked out in light and geometry. The study even looks for Solomon's two pillars, Jachin and Boaz, in this portico. It's a theory, not a fact.

[warmly] A wave of stone, holding up a hill, with a washerwoman at work inside it.

Carry on along the path to its end, where a spiral ramp winds down toward the old farmhouse, Casa Larrard, which is a school today. Stop at the foot of the ramp with the house in view, and keep to the path: Casa Larrard · Güell's farmhouse.

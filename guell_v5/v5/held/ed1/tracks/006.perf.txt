[curious] Here is a road that was bent round a tree. The park calls the Middle Viaduct "the carob bridge, where the natural growth of this interesting tree is respected". A photo caption in the city history museum's dossier for schools puts it in Catalan: "the middle viaduct with the carob trunk".

Find the trunk. Rather than cut the tree down, Gaudí let the bridge make room for it. And the stonework below is meant to be wild: the park says the viaducts "simulate natural caves".

[pause] Above this one, the top bridge, the Pont de Dalt, climbs to the highest part of the estate. A photograph from 1927 in the archive of Barcelona's technical university shows a "path with stone planters" along its top.

For now the path on to the top bridge and to Casa Trias is closed for works.

[warmly] So the carob has the last word here: a tree that got its own bridge.

Go back down past the Low Viaduct and down the steps and ramps to the esplanade inside the Carrer d'Olot gate, to the foot of the great stairway and the second ticket check. If there's a queue, join it, and play the next stop while you wait: The foot of the stairway · Sixty plots, two houses.

[conspiratorial] This bench prays. Josep Maria Jujol engraved prayers to the Virgin Mary into its tiles. A 2020 study calls their meaning an open question.

It was his habit. A study of his life says, in Catalan, that in all Jujol's work "crosses, Latin phrases, invocations to Mary and psalms interweave with colour and form". In 1986 his son and the writer Ronald Christ published studies of them.

The restorers of around 1990 found the rounded top pieces carried an original design, "probably by Jujol", with engravings, inscriptions and symbols. Where tiles were replaced, they refused to fake his hand. "The most unique elements," they wrote, "such as inscriptions or symbols, were left out." Each new piece got a small engraved mark, so it can't pass for an original. Run your eye along the rounded top: you're reading two hands, his and theirs.

[pause] How much is original? In 2016 some experts claimed that only a fifth of the original pieces survive. One organiser of a Gaudí congress blamed, in Catalan, "neglect, or bad restorations, or rather than restorations, reconstructions". The city answered that it replaces only tiles that can't be restored, and that in that restoration the white tiles were renewed and the coloured ones restored.

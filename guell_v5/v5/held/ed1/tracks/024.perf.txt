[curious] The estate nobody wanted to buy became a place almost everybody wanted to see. Before the limits came in, the city estimated about nine million visits a year to the heart of this park. Nine million, onto the steps, the square and the bench you've just walked.

So the city started counting. "Access to Park Güell has been regulated since 2013 to prevent tourist overcrowding," the park says. The aim, in the operator's own words, is that "the park has to remain an urban park, a place for the community and even a place for individual and collective memories."

Since the summer of 2020 the controls have covered the whole twelve hectares of the historic park.

People from the neighbourhoods around it still come in free, every day of the year.

In 2025 the park counted more than four million paying visitors, and more than half a million neighbours.

[pause] Where does that leave the stones? The park's press release on one big round of works said, in Catalan, that they "have been funded by the return from the park's tourist regulation".

[warmly] And the repairs are slow, patient handwork. For their restoration, each of the four Suns on the ceiling of the hall was divided into twenty-four pieces. Conservators have worked on the bench for years, and before one campaign every backrest was photographed, one by one.

The sculptor Abel Vallhonesta restored the dragon's front right foot, and the park says that work was finished in April 2026.

[wry] Barcelona still employs people to glue Gaudí back together, piece by piece.

Take in the gate and the wall once more before you go. A failed estate, now a park so loved it has to be protected from its own success.

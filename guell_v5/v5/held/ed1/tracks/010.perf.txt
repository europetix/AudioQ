[curious] At the top of the steps, sheltered under the hall, the park describes an odeon: a stone bench curved like a Greek theatre. The park's flyer adds, in Spanish, "a tripod that seems made of three snakes". See if you can find it.

Put these together, and some writers on the park see ancient Greece. At Delphi, the god Apollo killed the serpent Python, and buried it under his temple, where it guarded the waters under the earth. His priestess gave her oracles seated on a tripod. And the temple was Doric. Here: a dragon guarding water, a tripod, and Doric-style columns above.

The Gaudí scholar Joan Bassegoda took it seriously. As reported in English, he said: "The Delphi temple was Doric, and that is why Eusebio Güell wanted the columns of the park."

[pause] Not everyone agrees. In 2023 the scholar María-Belén García Ávila studied this hall closely. She wrote, in Spanish, that "several authors claim it comes from the image of the temple dedicated to Apollo at Delphi". She named none of them. And she added a warning: "There is a lot of literature written, above all commercial, speculating about the inspiration of the hypostyle hall." Her own candidate was another temple of Apollo, at Cyrene, in North Africa.

[wry] As you heard beside the dragon, Gaudí never explained it. That's why everyone has a theory. Delphi is a scholar's reading, and a beautiful one. Nobody has found it in Gaudí's own words.

Step to where you can see the front of the hall above the stairway, its outer row of fat, fluted columns: The Hypostyle Room · A market hall in Greek dress.

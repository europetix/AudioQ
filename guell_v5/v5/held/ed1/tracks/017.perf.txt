[curious] The park's own description of this place begins with a confession: "this large square is artificial." Part of it is cut into the rock of the hill. The rest is "held up on top of the hypostyle room". So the open ground under your feet is a roof. Below are the eighty-six columns of the market hall.

On the first plans this was the Greek Theatre, later "rechristened as Plaça de la Natura", the Nature Square. It was "planned for staging large open-air shows".

People came long before it was a public park. The park's historians, Mireia Freixa and Mar Leniz, date the first public event they can document in the park to 20 October 1906. It was a Garden Party, during the first congress of the Catalan language. [amused] Called that, they note, in English. By 1907 events were being held in this square, and in Güell's lifetime it hosted Catalanist gatherings and sardana dancing. The park was already "one of Barcelona's great tourist attractions". The same historians say Güell's aim was to promote his estate. Every party was also an advertisement.

[pause] Now look down. The ground is sand and gravel, on purpose. Rain sinks through it, runs down inside the columns of the hall, and into the tank below. The square is part of the plumbing. Then find the long retaining wall against the hill, finished, as the park puts it, with "large capitals made to look like palm trees".

[warmly] Remember the rules for buyers: no house could block the view of the sea. The view from the edge of this square is the one those rules protected.

In April 2026, for the park's hundredth year as a public park, the neighbours were invited to a community dinner in the park, in the spirit of those garden parties.

A theatre on the roof of a market, and a drain for the rain. On this hill, almost nothing does only one job.

Now go to the edge of the square on the stairway side, where the long tiled bench runs. Sit if there's room: The Nature Square · A hundred metres of broken tile.

[warmly] The man who paid for all this never built himself a new house. Eusebi Güell moved into the one already here: "the stately farmhouse of the old Can Muntaner de Dalt estate", as the park calls it.

Its other name comes from an earlier owner. The Gaudí scholar Joan Bassegoda describes the Larrard family, in Catalan, as "a rich family of French origin, made rich by trade and banking". The Larrards sold the property to Salvador de Samà, the Marquis of Marianao, the same man who sold the top of this hill to Güell.

Güell "converted the old mansion", the park says, around the time Gaudí moved into the show house. He already had a palace in the old city, on Carrer Nou de la Rambla, which Gaudí had built for him. But the park's historians point out that it was here, on his own estate, that he lived. He "lived there until his death". Eusebi Güell died in this house on 9 July 1918.

[pause] Then the estate nearly became something else entirely. After his death, his heirs offered the park, together with their estate at Pedralbes, to King Alfonso XIII, as a site for a royal palace. One review of the park's history gives the answer in a line of Catalan: "in 1921 the king chose Pedralbes." The heirs had offered it to the city as well. The king took Pedralbes instead, and the city bought the park.

The farmhouse went with it. In 1931, in the park's words, it "became a municipal public school", named after Baldiri Reixac, and it is a school today.

[quietly] Look at it from here: an old country house, older than the park around it, made over for one of the great industrialists of his city. It's a working school, so this guide stays outside, on the path.

Güell lived and died in the old farmhouse. Children learn in it now.

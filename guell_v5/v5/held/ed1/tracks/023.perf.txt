[warmly] This is how a private estate became everyone's park. In the early 1920s, the city of Barcelona bought it from Güell's heirs. It wasn't quick. The city itself describes a long purchase from the Güell family. The council voted to buy at a full meeting, and even then the deal took a few more years to complete.

One man pushed hard for it: Nicolau Maria Rubió i Tudurí, the city's director of parks, whom one newspaper has called "the inventor of green Barcelona". He promoted the city's purchase of land for green space, and Park Güell was on his list.

The park opened as a municipal park on 26 April 1926. In the words of the park's historians, it became a place of free access for the citizens. The estate built for sixty rich families now belonged to everyone.

[quietly] Six weeks later, on an early June evening, Gaudí was hit by a tram at the corner of Bailèn and the Gran Via. He was taken to the Hospital de la Santa Creu. He died there three days later. An encyclopedia records it, in Catalan, in a few plain words: "run over by a tram".

[pause] He had moved out of his house here the year before, into his workshop at the Sagrada Família. The park had been everyone's for six weeks.

[warmly] The hill went on without him. Thirty years after the opening, Salvador Dalí gave a talk here in the park. The Spanish state declared it a monument, and in 1984 UNESCO made it World Heritage. Today it is one of seven works by Gaudí on that list.

2026 was named Gaudí Year, for the hundredth anniversary of his death.

Opened in April. Its maker gone by June.

There's one more story before the last. Stop just inside the Carrer d'Olot gate, out of the way of people leaving: The Carrer d'Olot gate · Nine million and a ticket.

You leave this cathedral the way the faithful once came in. More than eight hundred years ago, this was the front gate of the great mosque.

This is the Puerta del Perdón, the Gate of Forgiveness. It's an original Almohad gate, the main entrance to the mosque's courtyard. And it was set on one line with the mosque's central nave and its mihrab, the niche that showed the direction of prayer. Picture that line. Through this arch, across the courtyard, into the prayer hall, all the way to the mihrab. Today the same line runs straight into the walls of a Gothic cathedral.

Inside the arch, look at the carved plaster. In 2025 the Factum Foundation, working with the Cathedral, recorded it in three dimensions, detail by detail.

Christian Seville did not pull this gate down. It dressed it. In 1520 Miguel Florentín added terracotta figures: Saint Peter and Saint Paul at the sides, and above them the Virgin and the Archangel Gabriel. Two years later Bartolomé López added Plateresque plasterwork. And just above the arch is a large relief of Christ driving the merchants out of the Temple. A pointed choice, for a gate between a holy place and a busy city.

So think back over what you have seen. From the mosque: the minaret, the courtyard and this gate. From the Gothic church: the vast naves that replaced its prayer hall. From the Renaissance: the belfry on the old minaret, and, if your route took you there, the sacristies and the chapter house. Three buildings, in one.

If you'd like to see it all from above, the roofs can be walked on a separate visit.

Once you pass through this gate, your visit is over, and there's no way back in. So take one last look at the Giralda, if you can see it from where you stand.

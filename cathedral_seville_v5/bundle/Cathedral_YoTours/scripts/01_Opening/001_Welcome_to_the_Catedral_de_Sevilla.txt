This cathedral stands where the Almohads built a great mosque in the twelfth century. Its bell tower was the mosque's minaret. After Fernando the Third took Seville in 1248, the mosque became the cathedral. In 1401 the canons decided to replace it with this Gothic church. Since 1987 it has been a World Heritage Site, with the Alcázar and the Archive of the Indies.

This guide is one route in three lengths. The full tour takes just over an hour and a half, the essential an hour and a quarter, the highlights forty-five minutes, all with the Giralda. Whichever is on your phone, play it in order. Every stop names the next place in Spanish, as on the signs, with something to look for. On a shorter tour the numbers jump, and any Extra that's announced isn't on your phone: just go on to the next main stop.

The cathedral's own visit begins at the Giralda, so ours does too. The Capilla Real, the Royal Chapel, is kept for prayer and seen only through its grille, and other chapels may close for Mass. The way out is at the end, through the courtyard of orange trees, and once you're out, you can't come back in.

If you came in by the Puerta del Lagarto, the Lizard Gate, you may have passed under a hanging crocodile. Its story comes near the end of the full tour. Head for the tower door, signed Giralda.

If you came in by the Puerta del Príncipe, also called the Puerta de San Cristóbal, the monument near you, four heralds carrying a coffin, is the tomb of Columbus. You'll come back to it. Cross the middle of the church, between the gilded altarpiece and the choir, into the opposite arm of the transept, and follow the Giralda signs to the tower door in the corner.

Not climbing? Go to the tower door anyway and skip ahead to stop seven, at the foot of the Giralda. On the highlights tour, start at the Capilla Mayor, the gilded altarpiece in the middle of the church.

Climbing? Start the next stop on the first ramps of the Giralda.

Look at the figures at the cross, taking the body down. That moment caught Bartolomé Esteban Murillo. An eighteenth-century writer, Antonio Ponz, tells how Murillo would stand in that chapel looking at it, again and again. Late in life, he told the sacristan he was waiting until they finished taking Our Lord down from the cross.

It's a lovely story, and we have it secondhand. But it tells you what painters thought of this picture, more than a century after it was made.

Murillo asked to be buried in that same church, and he was. Then, during the French occupation of Seville, the church of Santa Cruz was pulled down. The painting survived, and in time it came to the cathedral. Murillo's grave was lost with the building. Today a plaque in the square of Santa Cruz marks only roughly where he lies.

So the painting he loved hangs in the cathedral, and the man who loved it lies somewhere under a square nearby.

Above your head, the end of the world is already under way, and not everyone is going to like the verdict.

The whole dome of the Sacristía Mayor is one great scene of the Last Judgment, carved in three rings. Read it from the bottom. The lowest ring holds the damned. Above them, the rings rise toward the Heavenly Court. Down in the vaults of the short arms are apostles and bishops. For almost two hundred years this room has also been a place to show the cathedral's treasures.

Now find Murillo's two saints, painted in 1655. They are San Isidoro and San Leandro, Saint Isidore and Saint Leander, both archbishops of Seville long ago. Isidore, the great scholar, is absorbed in his reading. For Leander, tradition says Murillo borrowed a face from the cathedral itself: Alonso de Herrera, who worked in its choir.

Look too at the pillars at the head of the room. Beside one stands a statue of King Fernando the Third, made when he was declared a saint in 1671. On the pillar opposite is the Immaculate Conception.

Stay in the Sacristía Mayor for the Custodia de Arfe, the tall silver monstrance.

Two paintings by Francisco de Zurbarán hang here, in the Sala del Pabellón, the exhibition room. That's where the Cathedral's own list of its paintings places them. The list gives no titles, only dates: one was painted between the mid-1630s and the mid-1640s, the other in the later 1640s.

They share the walls with other painters of Seville's great century. There are two works by Juan de Valdés Leal, painted within a year or two of each other in the later 1650s. And there is one by Francisco Pacheco, from 1620, the oldest picture of the group. He is less famous today than the others, but he taught the young Diego Velázquez. Velázquez later married his daughter, so Pacheco became his father-in-law as well. Read the labels for their subjects.

Five pictures by three painters, spanning close to forty years of one city's art.

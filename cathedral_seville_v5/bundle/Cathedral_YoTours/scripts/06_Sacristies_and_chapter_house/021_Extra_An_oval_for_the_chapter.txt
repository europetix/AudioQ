Most rooms in a cathedral are built for looking at something. This one was built so that people could see and hear each other.

You came through the Antecabildo, the antechamber, and along a curved corridor. This is the Sala Capitular, the chapter house, where the canons who ran the cathedral met. It was this chapter that decided, in 1401, to build a new cathedral where the mosque stood.

The room is an oval, begun in the mid-sixteenth century under Hernán Ruiz the Younger, the architect of the Giralda's belfry, and completed by Asensio de Maeda. The Cathedral says the oval let every member see every other, and gave the room exceptional acoustics. So keep your voice low. In here, words carry.

A learned canon, Francisco Pacheco, not the painter of the same name, planned the decoration: the virtues the chapter should live by, with Latin inscriptions to drive the lesson home. Pablo de Céspedes painted the Virtues in 1592. The carved reliefs are by Juan Bautista Vázquez the Elder, Diego de Velasco and Marcos Cabrera. Velasco also carved the mahogany armchair that presides over the room.

Stay here in the Sala Capitular for the main stop: Murillo's Immaculate, high in the vault above the ring of round paintings.

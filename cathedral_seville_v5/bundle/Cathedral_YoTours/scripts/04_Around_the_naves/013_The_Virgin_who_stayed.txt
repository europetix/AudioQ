This painting is older than the cathedral around it. When the builders arrived, she was already here.

This is the Capilla de la Antigua, the chapel of the Virgin of La Antigua. Above the altar is a fourteenth-century wall painting of the Virgin and Child. Nobody knows who painted it. Experts see in it the influence of the painters of Siena. It belonged to the old cathedral, the converted mosque. When that building came down to make way for this one, the image was kept and given a chapel of its own. Work to enlarge this chapel began in 1504.

Legend says the Virgin appeared to King Fernando the Third during his siege of Seville and promised him the city under her protection. It's a story, not a record. But it tells you how much she has meant here.

The chapel also holds the tomb of Cardinal Diego Hurtado de Mendoza, placed here a few years after the enlargement began. And in 1929, the year of the Ibero-American Exposition, the Virgin was crowned.

Her name travelled far. In 1493 Christopher Columbus came upon an island in the Caribbean and named it Antigua, after the Virgin venerated here. The island still carries her name. Copies of this painting spread across Andalusia, and her devotion went on to Mexico, Peru, Panama and Colombia.

This is still a place of prayer. If a Mass is on, listen from outside the grille.

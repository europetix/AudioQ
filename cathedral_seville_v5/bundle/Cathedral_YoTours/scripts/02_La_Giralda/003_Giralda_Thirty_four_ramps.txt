The ramps you're climbing were made wide enough for a rider on horseback. Thirty-four of them wind up through the Almohad tower, which also holds seven vaulted chambers. Up and down share the same ramps, so keep to the right, as the cathedral asks.

The design is simple and very strong. One wall runs round the outside, a central core rises in the middle, with the seven chambers stacked inside it, and the ramps turn between the two. Scholars call it a double-walled tube. It has carried the weight of the Renaissance belfry on top for more than four centuries.

The builders were thrifty, too. More than a hundred and forty capitals and column bases, taken from older buildings, have been found reused in the tower and recorded one by one.

Then came a real surprise. Restorers working on the tower's four outer faces from 2017 found sketches left in the brick by the men who built it: lettering, geometric patterns, plants and figures. One scholar suggests they served as a drawing book for the craftsmen, a kind of medieval sketchbook. The findings were presented at a congress in 2025.

So the tower isn't only a monument. It's a record of the people who built it, written into its own bricks.

At the top, walk out onto the bell level of the Giralda. Go round to the side that looks down on the courtyard of orange trees, the Patio de los Naranjos.

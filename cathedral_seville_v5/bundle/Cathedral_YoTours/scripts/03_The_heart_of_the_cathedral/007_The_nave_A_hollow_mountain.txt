People in Seville have a name for this building. They call it a hollow mountain. Give your eyes a moment to adjust, and you'll see why.

UNESCO calls it the largest Gothic building in Europe, with its five naves: a central nave, and two aisles on each side. Guinness lists it as the cathedral covering the greatest area of any in the world. Notice how wide it is. That's because it was built on the rectangle of the old mosque, and closely follows its footprint.

Here is how it fits together. The Giralda, the tower, stands in this corner of the church. In the middle of the central nave, two great enclosures face each other. One is the Capilla Mayor, the main chapel, with its huge gilded altarpiece. The other is the Coro, the choir. Behind the Capilla Mayor, closing this end of the church, is the Capilla Real, the Royal Chapel. And the sacristies and the chapter house fill the corner on the far side of this same end.

This guide takes you to the middle first. Then it goes round the church, and out at the end through the courtyard of orange trees.

Walk to the middle of the church, where the transept crosses the nave, and face the huge gilded altarpiece behind the iron grille: the Capilla Mayor.

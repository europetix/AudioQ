It took eighty-two years to make what you're looking at, from the first cut of the chisel to the last of the gilding. This is the Retablo Mayor, the high altarpiece of the Capilla Mayor, the main chapel.

The work began in 1482, under a Flemish sculptor, Pieter Dancart. After him came another Flemish master, then Pedro Millán, then two brothers, Jorge and Alejo Fernández. They finished the first phase almost half a century after it began.

Then the chapter wanted more. A generation later, sculptors including Roque Balduque and Juan Bautista Vázquez added two side sections, set at right angles to the front. The whole thing was finished in 1564.

The result is nearly thirty metres high and almost twenty wide, all carved and painted wood. It's often called the largest altarpiece in the Christian world. Count, if you like. There are forty-four carved scenes, and more than two hundred figures of saints on the framing pillars. Eight more reliefs, on the pillars around it, show the kings of Israel and Judah.

Now let your eye travel upward, scene by scene, to the very top. A Gothic Calvary crowns it all, older than the altarpiece itself. Sevillians call its crucifix the Christ of the Million, for the million graces the city believed it received in times of epidemic.

A two-year restoration of the altarpiece was completed in 2014.

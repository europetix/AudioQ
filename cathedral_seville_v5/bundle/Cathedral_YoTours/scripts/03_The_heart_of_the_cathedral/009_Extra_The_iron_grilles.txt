The man behind much of this ironwork was a friar who, the story goes, between commissions also mended the Giralda's clock.

Fray Francisco de Salamanca was a Dominican. In 1518 the cathedral summoned him to Seville to make its rejas, its iron grilles. The great screens in front of you were made largely by him and other Seville ironworkers, over the next two decades. The pulpits of the high altar are his work too, finished in 1531.

An old history of Spanish crafts, drawing on earlier records, fills in the human side. Sometimes he was paid in ducats. Sometimes he was paid in a bushel or two of corn. Besides putting the tower clock in order, he built an alarm to wake the cathedral's bell-ringer in the early morning.

Look at what iron can do in skilled hands. It is hammered and shaped into a screen you can see straight through, so the altarpiece is guarded without being hidden.

He also made the grille of the choir, between 1518 and 1523. It's the one you'll face when you turn round.

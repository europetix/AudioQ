The most famous tower in Seville is named after the statue on top of it.

Look up to the very top of the Giralda, close to a hundred metres up. That bronze figure is the Giraldillo, the little thing that turns. She is Faith Victorious, about four metres tall and around one and a half tonnes, holding a palm and a banner. She turns with the wind, a weathervane, and the whole tower took its name from her.

The bronze founder Bartolomé Morel cast her. She was probably designed by the painter Luis de Vargas, and her pose is thought to come from a print of Pallas Athena. A pagan goddess of war and wisdom, converted into Christian Faith. She went up on the thirteenth of August 1568, two years after the contract was signed. Morel also cast the bronzes of the great lectern in the Coro, so if you stopped at the choir, you have already seen his work.

Life at the top is hard. A gale once tore off her forearm, and it had to be remade. Later she came down for study and restoration, and a replica stood in for almost seven years. The restorers argued that she belonged on the tower, not in a museum. On the twenty-second of July 2005, the original went back up.

New works on the belfry below her began in spring 2026. She is not part of them, though experts are studying her again.

So the figure turning up there is the one Morel cast.

Now to the covered gallery along the side of the courtyard nearest the tower: the Nave del Lagarto, with a crocodile hanging from its ceiling.

[warmly] You've climbed an Almohad minaret. Now you're standing in a Renaissance belfry, set on top of it almost four centuries later.

Its architect was Hernán Ruiz the Younger. The cathedral chapter held a competition for a new top to the tower, and his design won. He signed his contract in December 1557. The old Almohad top had decayed, so he replaced it with a Renaissance one, and the work was crowned in 1568.

Now find the side that looks straight down into the Patio de los Naranjos, the courtyard of orange trees. [pause] Those rows of trees stand in what was the mosque's courtyard, where worshippers washed before prayer. That's where your visit ends, and you'll leave through its gate.

Then look out over the cathedral's roofs and domes. A building this size has had its crises. In 1511, just four years after the cathedral opened, the dome over the crossing collapsed and had to be rebuilt.

You're up here at a time of works. Since the spring of 2026 the bell chamber and the Renaissance crown above it have been under restoration, and the cathedral says visits are not interrupted.

The weather has had its say too. In February 2026 a storm brought down one of the four bronze vases of lilies that stood at the corners of the belfry. The other three were lifted down by crane about two weeks later, to be studied.

[quietly] Under your feet, an Almohad minaret. Around you, a Renaissance belfry full of Christian bells. The whole story of this place is stacked up in one tower.

There's a short Extra here on the bells. Then go back down the same ramps of the Giralda, keeping to the right. The next main stop plays on the way down.

# Catedral de Sevilla + Giralda: visit route, spaces and notices (research as of 30 Oct 2026)

Yo Tours audio guide v5. Purpose: get the route and the placement of works right, and know what is closed.

---

## 1. Method

- Tools: WebSearch and WebFetch only, one official page at a time with no bursts. No curl, no browser, no CAPTCHA.
- Grades used below:
  - **OFF**: an official Cabildo page (catedraldesevilla.es) read directly with WebFetch. WebFetch returns a model summary, not raw HTML, so quotes are as relayed.
  - **SEC**: a secondary source read directly (press, academic, tourism board, foundation catalogue, reputable travel site).
  - **SNIP**: search-result snippet only.
  - **WIKI**: Wikipedia. It is never used alone; it is always paired with another grade.
- **Blocked URLs.** These were not answered by the permission system, so they are unread and were not retried:
  - `https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/` (Spanish home)
  - `https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/visita-cultural/`
  - `https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/visita-cultural/horarios-y-tarifas/`
  - `https://www.seville-tickets.com/seville-cathedral/entrance/` (third party)
  - The Spanish official pages could therefore only be reached through their English twins.
- **Caching caveat.** Several official `?p=` URLs return older cached versions. One example is `/en/?p=53271`, which shows "rates effective 1 Jan 2025". Where two versions disagree, the URL with a 2026 date or 2026 content was preferred.
- **Coverage gap.** The newest official news item I could list is dated **4 May 2026**. For May–Oct 2026, official notices were found only through a secondary source (deeparrival.com). **This is a key CHECK.**

## 2. Official route (what the Cabildo itself says)

### Entrances

| Who | Door | Grade / source |
|---|---|---|
| Online ticket holders | **Puerta del Lagarto**. Street access is from C/ Cardenal Amigo Vallejo, on the east side of the Patio de los Naranjos next to the Giralda. | OFF: https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/cultural-visit/schedules-and-rates/ (2026 rates page) and https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/plan-your-visit-to-seville-cathedral-during-high-season-ticket-types-discounts-and-free-admission/ (17 Apr 2026) |
| Ticket-office buyers | **Puerta del Príncipe**, also called **Puerta de San Cristóbal** in the south transept. Ticket offices are there. The 17 Apr 2026 page says "Puerta del Lagarto or the Puerta de San Cristóbal, depending on the type of ticket purchased". | OFF, same two pages. Building page: https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/the-cathedral/building/the-gothic-cathedral-main-altarpiece-choir-side-chapels-and-doorways ("the Prince's Gate … south arm of the transept … where cultural visits are currently accessed individually") |
| Special case, 4–7 Dec (2025 Brotherhoods Congress) | Online entry via Puerta del Perdón; ticket office at Puerta del Lagarto | OFF (stale notice still shown on several pages in 2026) |

**Puerta del Príncipe = Puerta de San Cristóbal.** The official pages use both names for the same south-transept door. The script should use one name and give the other once.

### Order

- **"The visit begins at the Giralda tower, which has its own access control."** Source: OFF, schedules-and-rates page.
  - The tower is entered from inside the cathedral, at its north-east corner.
  - Practical implication: the guide's **first stop should be the Giralda climb**, then the interior.
  - CHECK: whether visitors entering at Príncipe are also sent to the Giralda first, or may go in any order.
- Tower closing time: the "Access and the Giralda tower close" one hour before closing, at 17:00 most of the year (OFF). A late arrival therefore loses the climb.

### Exit

- **Puerta del Perdón**, out of the Patio de los Naranjos onto C/ Alemanes.
  - OFF: building page, "The Door of Forgiveness serves as the exit for cultural and artistic visitors". Also https://catedraldesevilla.es/en/?p=53369.
  - SEC: trvlogue.com, updated 1 Oct 2026.
- Implied flow: Giralda, then the interior (naves, Capilla Mayor, coro, chapels, Columbus, the sacristies and Sala Capitular in the SE block), then out through the **Puerta de la Concepción** (north transept, "opening onto the Orange Tree Courtyard", OFF) into the **Patio de los Naranjos**, then exit at the Perdón.
  - The official site does not publish a step-by-step order for the interior. Order **inside** is free and self-guided.
- **No re-entry**: "Una vez que se haya validado el billete en los accesos no se podrá salir del recinto para volver a entrar." Source: OFF PDF, https://catedraldesevilla.es/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Normas-generales-catedral-descargable-actualizado.pdf

**Giralda works, 2026–2028**

- **Phase VI started after Holy Week 2026** (news dated 14 Apr 2026). It covers the inside of the bell chamber and the whole Renaissance crown up to the base of the Giraldillo. The Giraldillo itself is excluded and is under separate study. Duration is about 22 months, so to about early 2028. Six bell frames are being rebuilt with damping, and the 2021 shoring of the Santa María la Mayor bell is being replaced. "Cultural visits … will not be interrupted at any time"; bell-chamber work is to be scheduled around visitors. Source: OFF, https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/phase-vi-of-the-giralda-intervention-begins/
- Earlier phases: W face 2017, S 2018, E 2019, N 2023, openings and balconies 2025. The end of the four-faces restoration was announced in a press conference, OFF https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/?p=51887
- Summer 2026: the hours were adapted for "improvement works on the Giralda bell tower".
  - Source: SEC only, deeparrival.com, which cites a June 2026 Cabildo notice I could not open.
  - CHECK whether the belfry viewing level is partly screened or closed in Oct 2026.
- **Storm Leonardo, 5 Feb 2026.** One of the four Renaissance bronze **jarras de azucenas** (lily vases) at the belfry corners fell at 06:15.
  - The other three were **removed by crane on 21 Feb 2026** and placed temporarily, on purpose-built supports, **in the Patio de los Naranjos** for metallographic study.
  - Sources: SEC, El Español 21 Feb 2026, https://www.elespanol.com/sevilla/20260221/catedral-sevilla-desmonta-azucenas-giralda-caer-suelo-primeros-febrero/1003744140101_0.html. OFF, https://www.catedraldesevilla.es/en/the-cathedral-maintains-its-cultural-visits-during-regular-hours/ (9 Feb 2026), which says visits were unaffected and only the surroundings were cordoned.
  - **The Giralda's four corner lily vases are currently NOT on the tower** (as of Feb 2026).
  - **CHECK**: whether they are still in the Patio or visible to visitors in Oct 2026.

## 5. Columbus remains: what is actually established

- **2003 onward**: a Univ. of Granada team led by **José Antonio Lorente** studied the Seville bones.
  - Results in 2003–2005 showed only "compatibilidad posible", because there was too little DNA (SEC Público, 10 Oct 2024, https://www.publico.es/culturas/confirman-huesos-enterrados-catedral-sevilla-son-cristobal-colon.html).
- **10 Oct 2024** (documentary *Colón ADN. Su verdadero origen*, RTVE, aired 12 Oct 2024):
  - Lorente stated the Seville bones **are Christopher Columbus**, with reliability "prácticamente absoluta". The comparison was with remains of his son **Hernando** (also buried in this cathedral) and his brother **Diego** (SEC Público).
  - The Seville skeleton is **incomplete**. The Santo Domingo remains have **never been tested** (SEC Público).
- **Origin claim** ("Sephardic Jew, western-Mediterranean/Iberian"): **not peer-reviewed and data not published** at the time.
  - Experts urged caution (SEC Al Jazeera 27 Oct 2024, https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2024/10/27/what-is-the-science-behind-the-christopher-columbus-origins-discovery; also El Debate 16 Oct 2024, SNIP).
  - I found **no peer-reviewed publication** as of Oct 2026 (searches only, CHECK).
- **Safe script line**: "DNA comparisons with his son Hernando, announced in 2024 by the University of Granada team, support that the bones in this tomb are at least partly Columbus's. Claims about his origins have not been scientifically published." Avoid "proved Jewish" or "proved Spanish".

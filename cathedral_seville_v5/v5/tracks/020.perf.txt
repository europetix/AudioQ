[warmly] Every year, at the feast of Corpus Christi, this silver tower leaves the cathedral and goes out into the streets of Seville.

This is the Custodia de Arfe, the great monstrance of the cathedral, made to carry the consecrated Host in procession. Its designer was the goldsmith Juan de Arfe y Villafañe. In 1580 the cathedral chapter accepted his model. That model still survives. The finished monstrance was completed in 1587, and in its day it was considered the best of its kind.

[pause] Look at how it's built. It rises in storeys, like a little building, each ring of columns narrower than the one below, up to a lantern at the top. It is taller than a person. And it carries scenes and figures from Scripture and from the history of the Church. Take a moment to find a few.

[lightly] On Corpus Christi you'd see it moving slowly through the city, carrying the Host. And behind it, in the procession, come images of Seville's own saints. Isidore and Leander, whose portraits by Murillo hang in this room. Justa and Rufina, from Goya's canvas in the chalice sacristy. It's as if the people in these paintings go out for a walk with it.

This is only one piece of a treasury of about nine hundred works in gold and silver. But it is the one that still goes out to meet the city.

There's a short Extra on the chapter house itself first. Then the next main stop, in the same room, is Murillo's Immaculate. Through the Antecabildo, the antechamber, and along the curved corridor to the Sala Capitular: the oval chapter house, with Murillo's Virgin presiding over a ring of round paintings.

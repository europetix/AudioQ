[curious] The Gothic cathedral was finished in 1506. This doorway had to wait a great deal longer.

It's the Puerta de la Concepción, the Door of the Conception, at the end of this arm of the transept. It doesn't lead to the street. It leads into the Patio de los Naranjos, the old courtyard of the mosque.

For centuries this front stood unfinished. It was completed only in modern times, by the architect Adolfo Fernández Casanova, the same man who built the Puerta del Príncipe at the opposite end of the transept. He worked in the Gothic style, to match the rest. [wry] So this is one of the youngest parts of the building, dressed as one of the oldest.

[pause] Now look up above the door, at the great window of the Ascension. That glass is genuinely old. It was made around 1540 by Arnao de Flandes, Arnold of Flanders, a glass painter documented in this cathedral for more than twenty years. The Cathedral credits him with thirteen windows of saints here in the transept as well.

So the door is new, and the light above it is Renaissance. And the space behind it is older than both. When you step through, you leave the Gothic church and walk back more than eight hundred years, into the courtyard where the mosque's worshippers once gathered.

Out through the Puerta de la Concepción, then, into the Patio de los Naranjos: rows of orange trees around a fountain.

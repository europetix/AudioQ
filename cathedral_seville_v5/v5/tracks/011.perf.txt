[curious] Late in 1874, someone came into this chapel with a knife. When the theft was discovered, Saint Anthony was gone.

This is the Capilla de San Antonio, the chapel of Saint Anthony, and it is also the cathedral's baptistery. The huge canvas above the altar is by Bartolomé Esteban Murillo, Seville's own painter, and it was set here in 1656. Saint Anthony of Padua kneels and looks up, as the Christ Child appears to him among a crowd of angels. Eleven years later Murillo painted the smaller picture above it, the Baptism of Christ.

[pause] The thief cut out only the kneeling saint, a piece of canvas taller than a man, and left the angels behind. Rewards were offered. Then, early in 1875, two Spanish strangers walked into the New York gallery of an art dealer, William Schaus, and offered him an old painting. He had a good idea which one it was. He called the Spanish consul, who checked it against a photograph of the altarpiece. Schaus paid 250 dollars, the seller was arrested as the sale went through, and the dealer handed the saint over, asking only for his money back.

[wry] The saint sailed home by way of Havana and Cádiz, and the painting was restored that same year. One New York report of the time ended on a careful note. All was well, it said, if there is no counterfeiting in the case.

[quietly] So there he is, still on his knees, still looking up. A saint who went missing, crossed an ocean, and came back to his chapel.

The stained glass of this chapel also holds two of Seville's own saints, Justa and Rufina. Remember them. You'll meet them again in the sacristies, painted by Goya.

Walk on to the far end of the church, where the great central doors stand: the Puerta de la Asunción, seen from inside.

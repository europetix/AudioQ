[wry] The cathedral was finished in 1506. Its main doorway was not. For centuries, the great central door of the church stood unfinished.

You're at the Puerta de la Asunción, the Door of the Assumption, at the centre of the cathedral's main front. A cardinal finally decided to complete it in 1827, and the architect Fernando Rosales kept to the Gothic style of the rest. The carved figures outside came years later, by the sculptor Ricardo Bellver. Today these doors open for great ceremonies, not for visitors.

[pause] Now look at the light. The windows above the side chapels and along this end of the central nave belong to the oldest phase of the building. The earliest glass here is the work of a master called Enrique Alemán, who was working in Seville around 1480.

The tall window high above the doors is later, with the four Evangelists. It is one of three on this front by Vicente Menardo, a glass painter of the sixteenth century.

[lightly] Look back down the church from here. The whole cathedral is some 126 metres long, and almost all of it lies in front of you.

Along the far side of the church from the tower, to the Capilla de la Antigua: the largest chapel on that side, its medieval painted Virgin above the altar. The chapel names are over the grilles.

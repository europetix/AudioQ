[reverent] Most paintings you look at once. One of Seville's best-loved painters kept coming back to this one for years.

You're in the Sacristía Mayor, the great sacristy, and in front of its central chapel hangs the Descent from the Cross by Pedro de Campaña. He was a Flemish painter working in Seville, and he painted it in 1547. It was made for a chapel in one of the city's parish churches, Santa Cruz, the church of the Holy Cross.

[pause] Look at the figures at the cross, taking the body down. That moment caught Bartolomé Esteban Murillo. An eighteenth-century writer, Antonio Ponz, tells how Murillo would stand in that chapel looking at it, again and again. Late in life, he told the sacristan he was waiting until they finished taking Our Lord down from the cross.

[quietly] It's a lovely story, and we have it secondhand. But it tells you what painters thought of this picture, more than a century after it was made.

Murillo asked to be buried in that same church, and he was. Then, during the French occupation of Seville, the church of Santa Cruz was pulled down. The painting was saved and came here. Murillo's grave was lost with the building. Today a plaque in the square of Santa Cruz marks only roughly where he lies.

[warmly] So the painting he loved hangs in the cathedral, and the man who loved it lies somewhere under a square nearby.

There's a short Extra here on the dome and two bishops by Murillo. The next main stop is in this room too, here in the Sacristía Mayor: the great silver monstrance, the Custodia de Arfe.

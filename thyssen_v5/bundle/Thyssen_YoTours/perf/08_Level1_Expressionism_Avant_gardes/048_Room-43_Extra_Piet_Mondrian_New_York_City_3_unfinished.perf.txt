[lightly] The lines in this painting are not painted. Lean in and you will see why: they are strips of coloured adhesive tape.

Piet Mondrian had lived in Paris for nearly twenty years. In 1938, as war came closer, he moved to London. Two years later came the air raids on London and the German occupation of Paris. He crossed the Atlantic and settled in New York, where he spent the last years of his life.

New York loosened him up. He loved the city's grid of streets and its towers, which he called "the furthest from nature." He loved jazz and boogie-woogie. In his last studio he worked with tape, because tape can be moved. He shifted the strips again and again, looking for the right balance of line and colour. Only one picture in this group ever had its tape replaced with paint. This one was left as it was, unfinished, and you can still see how he was thinking.

[pause] In the same room hangs a very different Mondrian, Composition Number Thirteen, from his first months in Paris before the First World War. It seems to grow out of the branches of a tree. Years later, Mondrian called it a eucalyptus. Between the two, you can follow a painter from a tree to a city.

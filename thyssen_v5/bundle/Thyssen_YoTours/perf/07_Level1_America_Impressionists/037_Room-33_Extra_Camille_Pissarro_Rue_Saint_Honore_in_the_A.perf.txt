[lightly] Camille Pissarro spent most of his life in the countryside, painting landscapes. Then, late in life and for the sake of his health, he moved into the city and started painting out of windows.

In the winter of 1897, he took a hotel room on a square in the heart of Paris. His dealer, Durand-Ruel, promised to show whatever came of it. Pissarro painted every view the room could give him: the avenue de l'Opéra, the square beside the hotel, and this street, the rue Saint-Honoré. He made fifteen pictures that winter.

[pause] He painted this exact view three times, in three kinds of light. One has bright morning sun. One has the dim light of dusk. This one is early afternoon, and it has just stopped raining. Look at the street after the rain, the carriages, and the people picked out one by one, some still holding their umbrellas. You are looking down at them from high up, the way Monet had looked down on a Paris boulevard back at the first Impressionist exhibition.

[amused] Pissarro knew some people found these streets ugly. He wrote that perhaps it was not aesthetic, but he was delighted to paint them anyway.

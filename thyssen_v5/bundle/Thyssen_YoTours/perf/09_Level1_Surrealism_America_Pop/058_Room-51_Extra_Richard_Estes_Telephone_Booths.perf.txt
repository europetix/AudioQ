[curious] Nothing in this picture is quite where your eye first puts it. That's deliberate.

Richard Estes is one of the leading names of American Photorealism. But he didn't copy a single photograph. For Telephone Booths he took several pictures of a row of phone booths where Broadway, Sixth Avenue and 34th Street meet, and then combined them into one scene.

Now try to read it. The glass is see-through and a mirror at the same time. Some reflections are crisp on the glass, others warp across the metal. You can see the street in front of the booths and the buildings behind you, all at once. [amused] Look for the signs of Macy's and Woolworth, caught among the reflections.

And there's something unusual here. Estes's cities are nearly always empty. In this one, every booth is taken, each person with their back to you, talking to someone you'll never hear.

The tall canvas is his too. People's Flowers belongs to the Carmen Thyssen collection, which you'll see on the ground floor, but it hangs up here. Follow the frames and rectangles inward, and they lead you to the neon sign in the middle. Keep Estes in mind. He comes back in the last room of this guide.

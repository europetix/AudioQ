[warmly] Juan Gris was born here in Madrid. When this was painted, in the spring of 1917, he was in Paris, in the middle of a war that wasn't his.

As a Spaniard he didn't have to fight. But life was hard. Foreigners were under suspicion, and food and fuel came and went. His dealer, Kahnweiler, was in exile, and for a while Gris was really poor. Then a new dealer, Léonce Rosenberg, signed him up. This painting went to Rosenberg for three hundred and fifty francs.

The woman is probably Josette, his partner. No one has proved it, but this picture is the pair to a portrait of her from the year before. [conspiratorial] She's dressed up here, in a party dress. Gris once traded a small painting for a dress made for her by the sister of the couturier Paul Poiret. Is this that dress? It's a lovely thought. Nobody knows.

[pause] Now look at how he builds her. Overlapping triangles, a pleated cloak, oblique lines all leaning against each other. From above, the way Cézanne looked down on his seated women. But there's an older master in here too. Gris had been studying Corot, and making paintings after him. The play of curves against straight edges, the soft dark and light, comes from Corot. This was a time when he and Picasso were especially close, and Cubism was learning to look back.

You met Gris upstairs in Room 41, with The Smoker. Here, four years on, he is moving towards a calmer, purer Cubism. [warmly] A Madrid painter, far from home, painting the woman he lived with in her best dress.

Room J, the last room, has a short Extra on Charles Bell's giant pinball machine. After that, the way out.

[curious] When this little panel was finished, a procession of dignitaries walked it through the streets of Siena. Well, not this panel alone. It was one small piece of an enormous altarpiece, the Maestà, carried from Duccio's workshop to the cathedral in 1311.

Welcome to Level 2 and the old masters. The walls up here keep their classic salmon colour, and the rooms run in order of time, so as you walk you move forward through the centuries. You start in the 1300s.

The Maestà was huge, around five metres tall, and painted on both sides. This scene came from the back, from the predella, the strip of small pictures along the base. The altarpiece was taken apart around 1771, and its panels scattered. This one passed through collections in London and New York before it came here.

[pause] The story is from the Gospel of John. Christ, tired from the road, sits on the edge of Jacob's well outside the Samaritan city of Sychar. A woman arrives with a pitcher balanced on her head, and he asks her for a drink. Now watch the hands. There's no speech here, so the whole conversation is carried by gestures. On the right, four apostles come out through the city gate carrying food, and a rocky road runs down to the well.

The background is still pure gold, the timeless space of heaven. But look at the fine gold lines tracing the folds of Christ's cloak, and the soft light and shade on the faces. Duccio is giving his figures weight, and his town real depth.

[warmly] Before you move on, find the tall Virgin and Child enthroned between Saint Dominic and Saint Martin, still in its original stuccoed frame. It was painted around 1290, and it's the oldest painting in the whole collection. Seven centuries start right here.

Room 2 has a short Extra on a 15th-century birth scene full of busy women. The next main stop is Room 3, for Gerard David's Crucifixion.

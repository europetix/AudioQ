In the autumn of 1908, Kandinsky moved into a flat and studio in Schwabing, the artists' quarter of Munich. A few metres away stood the church of Saint Ludwig. When a religious procession passed, he sketched it in a notebook.

The sketch survives, covered in colour notes. Red and rose pink above the porch. Green, violet and blue under the arches. He wanted the contrasts clear in his head before he painted.

Now look at what he did with them. The sun is so bright that the porch falls into deep shadow. Against that darkness, the crowd becomes small, glowing patches of colour, banners and priests in yellow vestments among them. Many of the patches aren't anything at all. They're just colour. That was something new for him.

Upstairs, in Room 37, you saw a bright Murnau street from the same year. And in Room 39, years later, the pure abstraction of In the Bright Oval. Here you can watch the first step from one to the other.

The quieter beach nearby is by Paul Signac, whose dotted technique had fascinated Kandinsky for years. Here Signac is young and still painting like Monet. It even carries the wrong year beside his signature.

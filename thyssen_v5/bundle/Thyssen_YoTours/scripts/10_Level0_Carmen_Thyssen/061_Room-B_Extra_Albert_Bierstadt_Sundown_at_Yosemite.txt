You've just left one Garden of Eden in Room A. Here's another. In 1863 the writer Fitz Hugh Ludlow travelled west with Albert Bierstadt to Yosemite. If the reports were true, he wrote, they were heading for the original site of the Garden of Eden.

They spent seven weeks in the valley. Bierstadt worked fast, making small, quick oil sketches on the spot. This is one of them. Look at the sun breaking through between the granite walls. He used that effect again, much bigger, in later pictures built from these studies.

For Americans at the time, a sunset over the West was a symbol of the country's destiny. And the valley was revealed to the country in the dark years of the Civil War, which gave that symbol extra force. You met this American wilderness upstairs, in Room 31.

Nearby, a pink orchid and a hummingbird. Martin Johnson Heade painted it in 1902, when he was eighty-three, living in Florida. He kept hummingbirds as pets there, and you can see how closely he had watched them.

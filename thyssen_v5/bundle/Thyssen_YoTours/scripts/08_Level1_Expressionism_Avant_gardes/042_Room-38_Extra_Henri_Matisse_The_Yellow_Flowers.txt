In 1902, Henri Matisse was having a terrible year. His wife's parents, who had been his main financial support, went bankrupt in a public scandal. His painting turned sombre, and he made a series of modest flower pieces, what his biographer called small, inexpensive bunches of chrysanthemums, yellow ranunculus or ragged sunflowers.

This is one of them. The colours are muted, almost drab. But look at the mirror. The reflection of the yellow flowers glows with an energy the rest of the picture holds back. Within a few years Matisse would be painting in the pure, blazing colour of the Fauves, and you can feel it starting here.

Now find the tall still life of yellow roses nearby. Max Beckmann painted it in 1937 in Amsterdam. He had fled Berlin the day after Hitler opened the Nazi exhibition mocking modern art as "degenerate". In exile he painted big allegorical pictures about the horror of the regime. And alongside them, as a kind of relief, still lifes like this one, with no politics at all. Follow the yellow strips up from the roses to the ears of corn in the corner.

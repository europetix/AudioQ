Francesco Venier ruled Venice as its doge for just two years. He was so unwell that he needed help to move about.

Look at how Titian shows that. The body under the gold brocade seems fragile, almost propped up. But the eyes are sharp. Nothing gets past this man.

As official painter to the Venetian Republic, Titian painted each new doge for a long frieze of portraits in the great council hall of the Doge's Palace. Then, in 1577, fire swept through the palace, and that gallery of faces was lost, Venier's official portrait with it.

This canvas survived because it was never there. It's Titian's own replica of the lost image, perhaps made for the doge's brother, or for one of the city's offices. So this is now the face of Venier.

Through the window on the left, out on the water, a boat is burning. Nobody has identified the episode. It may simply stand for the power of the city.

If you saw Tintoretto's long Paradise in the central hall downstairs, that was part of the same story: the palace needed new paintings after that fire.

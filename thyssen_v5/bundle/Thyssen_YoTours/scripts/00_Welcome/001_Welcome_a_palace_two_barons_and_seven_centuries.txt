This museum began as a loan. In 1988, Baron Hans Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza lent Spain 775 works from his family's collection, with a view to selling them later. In 1993 the Spanish State bought them. They are the heart of what you'll see today, gathered over two generations, by his father Heinrich and then by Hans Heinrich himself.

To house the collection, the architect Rafael Moneo converted an old aristocratic palace on the Paseo del Prado, the Palacio de Villahermosa. The museum opened in the autumn of 1992. Today the ground floor also holds his wife Carmen Thyssen's collection.

We'll walk it from the top down. On Level 2 you start with gold-ground panels from medieval Italy and move forward in time, room by room. Level 1 carries the story on to Pop Art. Then the Carmen Thyssen collection on Level 0, and out onto the Paseo del Prado. Every stop gives the room number from the museum's map, often with the room's title.

This guide comes in three lengths. The full tour takes about two and a half hours, the essential version about an hour and a quarter, and the highlights about forty minutes. Whichever one is on your phone, play it in order. The numbers may jump, and every stop tells you where to go next.

Some stops are short Extras, to skip whenever you like. And one honest warning. This museum lends a great deal, and the map itself warns that works are often out on loan. Now and then you may find a note where a painting should be. The temporary exhibitions are not part of this guide.

On your way, look out in this hall for a long, crowded canvas of heaven. It's Tintoretto's Paradise, his proposal for the great council hall of the Doge's Palace in Venice after a fire. That palace comes back upstairs.

Walk to the far end of the central hall, away from the Paseo del Prado doors, and take the stairs or the lift up to Level 2. Room 1 is where the map marks "Recommended start of the visit". Look for small gold-ground panels from 14th-century Siena.

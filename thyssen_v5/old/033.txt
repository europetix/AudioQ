 Down a floor, across an ocean: America
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Rooms 30–32 — one floor down.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    The Hudson River School and John Singer Sargent.
    LISTEN FOR
    How American art found its own subject — first the wilderness, then high society.


 You've come down one floor and forward about a hundred years — and the first thing the museum does
 on this level is spring a genuine surprise: America.

 This is one of the Thyssen's best-kept secrets. It holds one of the finest collections of nineteenth-century
 American painting anywhere outside the United States — a whole story most European museums skip
 entirely. The younger Baron, Hans Heinrich, fell in love with American art when almost no one else in
 Europe collected it, and these rooms are the reward.

 What you'll see is a young nation painting itself into existence. Vast, untouched wilderness —
 mountains, forests, waterfalls — painted as a kind of national scripture, proof of America's God-given
 grandeur. Then the cities, the light on the water, the new urban life. It's landscape doing the work of
 identity: this is who we are. Watch for the sheer scale and optimism of it, so different from the older,
 more crowded European world upstairs. Then, through here, we cross back to France for the revolution
 everyone knows by name — Impressionism. America first, just beyond.

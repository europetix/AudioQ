 Out into the Paseo del Arte
 And there it is — seven centuries, one staircase, one afternoon. You've walked from a Sienese altarpiece
 glowing with gold leaf all the way down to Pop Art and a lonely woman in a hotel room, and if you did
 it top to bottom, you did it exactly as the family who built this collection intended: as one continuous
 story, with no chapter missing.

 That's the quiet genius of this place. The Prado will show you the summits of Spanish and royal
 painting; the Reina Sofía will show you what Spain did to the twentieth century. But only here can you
 stand in front of Van Eyck in the morning and Pollock by mid-afternoon and feel the through-line — the
 slow, restless, endlessly inventive business of human beings trying to put the world onto a flat surface.

 Step outside onto the Paseo del Prado. You're standing on the spine of the Golden Triangle: the Prado is
 just up the boulevard, the Reina Sofía down toward Atocha, and the great green lung of the Retiro is a
 short walk east. If your feet are tired, the Thyssen's own café in the courtyard is one of the calmer spots
 in the city to sit and let it all settle.

 Whatever you do next, you've just done something rare — you've seen the whole arc, start to finish.
 Take it with you. Safe travels, and enjoy Madrid.

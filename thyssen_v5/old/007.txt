 Vittore Carpaccio, Young Knight in a Landscape
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Room 5 — a young knight in armour in a teeming landscape.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Vittore Carpaccio, Young Knight in a Landscape, 1505 — oil on canvas.
    LISTEN FOR
    One of the first full-length portraits in Western art, and the puzzle of who he is.


 A striking full-length figure — a young man in gleaming armour, standing in a crowded landscape
 teeming with plants, birds and animals.

 Young Knight in a Landscape, painted around 1505 by the Venetian Vittore Carpaccio. It's one of the
 earliest full-length portraits in all of Western art — at a time when portraits were almost always just the
 head and shoulders, Carpaccio dared to paint a whole standing man, life-size, head to armoured foot.

 And he filled the world around the knight with meaning. Look closely and the landscape is alive with
 symbols — an ermine (a creature said to die rather than dirty itself, an emblem of purity), birds, flowers,
 a distant town, even a scroll with a Latin motto that translates as something like "better death than
 dishonour." Scholars still argue over exactly who the young man is, but the message is unmistakable:
 this is a portrait of virtue, of chivalric honour made visible. Carpaccio gives you a person and, at the
 same time, an entire moral world for them to stand in. After this, full-length portraiture was on the table
 for everyone. And right beside it hangs one of the sharpest faces in the building — just here.

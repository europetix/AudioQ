 Albrecht Dürer, Jesus Among the Doctors
    WHERE YOU ARE

    Albrecht Dürer, Jesus Among the Doctors, 1506 — oil on panel.
    LISTEN FOR
    The painting Dürer says he made in just five days — and the war of hands at its centre.


 A claustrophobic, almost airless painting — a swarm of heads and grasping hands pressed tight to the
 surface, a calm young boy at the centre surrounded by grotesque old faces.

 Jesus Among the Doctors, painted by Albrecht Dürer in 1506, while he was in Venice. It shows the boy
 Jesus, having slipped away from his parents, debating scripture with the learned elders in the Temple —
 and astonishing them. Dürer famously noted that he painted it in just five days, an almost boastful
 speed.

 It's built on a brutal contrast. The boy is serene, smooth, luminous, his delicate fingers calmly counting
 off the points of his argument. Around him press the doctors — wrinkled, hook-nosed, gnarled, almost
 caricatures, their old hands tangling with his young ones at the very centre of the picture. Dürer crops in
 tight, with almost no background, so the heads crowd the surface and crowd you. He'd been studying
 Leonardo's grotesque-head drawings in Italy, and you can feel him testing them out — youth against
 age, clarity against confusion, faith against mere learning. It's unsettling on purpose. There's nowhere
 for your eye to rest except that calm young face. Beside this northern intensity hangs something far
 cooler and more seductive — Cranach, a little further on.

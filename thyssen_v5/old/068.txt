 A luminous English landscape — green countryside under a big, restless sky full of moving cloud, a
 river or a mill, painted with fresh, flickering touches of light.

 A landscape by John Constable, one of the jewels of the Carmen Thyssen Collection and the heart of the
 Baroness's love of landscape painting. Constable, working in early-nineteenth-century England, is one
 of the two great English landscape masters alongside Turner.

 Constable did something quietly revolutionary: he painted the ordinary working countryside of his
 native Suffolk — the River Stour, its locks and mills and hay wagons — with a truthfulness and
 freshness nobody had brought to it before. He studied clouds like a scientist, filling the sky with weather
 you can almost feel, and he built his greens and lights from broken, flickering touches of paint that
 seemed crude to his contemporaries and prophetic to ours. When his work was shown in Paris it stunned
 the French and helped light the way toward the Impressionists you saw upstairs. This whole floor
 doubles down on the Thyssen's quiet strength — landscape — chosen by the Baroness for its light and
 loveliness rather than to fill a textbook. Let your eye rest in it. One last jewel, and then out into Madrid
 — a little further on.

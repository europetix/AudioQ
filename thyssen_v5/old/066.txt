TRACK 066· ROOMS A–J                               R-INTRO                            196 WORDS · ~2 MIN



 A second collection, a second eye
    WHERE YOU ARE
    The Carmen Thyssen rooms (A onward).
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Gauguin, Constable, Goncharova and the Baroness's own collection.
    LISTEN FOR
    How a second collection, with a different taste, rounds out the museum's story.


 One more floor — and a lovely surprise to end on. Level 0 holds a separate collection with its own
 personality: the Carmen Thyssen Collection, assembled by Carmen "Tita" Cervera, the Spanish former
 beauty queen who married the last Baron and became the great champion of keeping all this art in
 Madrid.

 It began as an extension of the family collection and grew into something distinctly hers. Where the
 Baron's taste ranged encyclopaedically across all of art history, the Baroness followed her heart —
 toward landscape, toward colour, toward beauty and pleasure. So this floor is gentler and sunnier than
 the rooms above: arcadian landscapes, glowing Impressionist and Post-Impressionist scenes,
 nineteenth-century American vistas, early modern colour.

 Think of it as a second, more personal walk through some of the same centuries you've just travelled —
 a coda in a warmer key. It's smaller and easier on tired feet, and it holds at least one undisputed
 masterpiece that many visitors rank among their favourites in the whole museum. After the long descent
 through the history of everything, this is the part you can simply enjoy. The jewel of it — a Gauguin —
 is just along here.

 Franz Marc, The Dream
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Room 34 — animals and figures dissolving into colour.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Franz Marc, The Dream, 1912 — oil on canvas.
    LISTEN FOR
    Marc's belief that colour itself carried meaning — and the dream-world he built from it.


 A glowing, dreamlike scene in pure colour — a seated nude woman with her eyes closed, surrounded by
 animals: horses, a lion, a quiet menagerie in flat zones of blue, red and yellow.

 The Dream, painted in 1912 by Franz Marc, one of the founders, with Kandinsky, of Der Blaue Reiter
 — "the Blue Rider" — the more lyrical, spiritual wing of German Expressionism in Munich.

 Marc loved animals above all subjects. He believed they were purer, more innocent, more in tune with
 the rhythms of creation than corrupted modern humans, and he painted them in symbolic colours —
 blue for the spiritual and masculine, yellow for joy, red for the earthly and violent. Here the sleeping
 woman and her calm animals seem to dissolve into a single harmonious dream, the boundary between
 human, beast and world melting away in colour. It's a vision of a lost paradise, an inner Eden — exactly
 the kind of spiritual renewal the Blue Rider artists hoped art could bring. Tragically, Marc was killed at
 Verdun in 1916, one of the great talents lost to the First World War. Now the same liberation of colour,
 in a sunnier French key — Derain, just beyond.

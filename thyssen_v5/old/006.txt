 Petrus Christus, Our Lady of the Dry Tree
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Room 3 — a miniature Madonna in a dead, thorny tree hung with letters.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Petrus Christus, Our Lady of the Dry Tree, c. 1465 — oil on panel.
    LISTEN FOR
    The strange medieval symbol behind this tiny picture, and the gold letters in the branches.


 A very small panel — easy to walk past — of the Virgin and Child standing inside the branches of a
 bare, thorny, leafless tree, with tiny golden letters hanging from the twigs.

 Our Lady of the Dry Tree, painted around 1465 by Petrus Christus, who took over as Bruges's leading
 painter after van Eyck. Those fifteen little golden objects dangling in the branches are the letter "A" —
 for Ave, the opening of the prayer Ave Maria — so the whole image is a visual rosary, a prayer you look
 at rather than say.

 The dead, thorny tree is the key. It comes from a vision in the Old Testament and stood for fallen, barren
 humanity — the world after Eden, dry and cursed. And in the middle of it, blooming where nothing
 should grow, is Mary with Christ: new life, redemption, the dead wood made to flower. It's a complete
 theology compressed into something the size of a paperback, made for private devotion in someone's
 hands. Northern painters loved this — enormous meaning packed into tiny, perfect objects. Now Italy
 answers, with two of the first true portraits in Western art, just along here.

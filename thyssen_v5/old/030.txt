 1800: the world cracks open
    WHERE YOU ARE
    Room 29.
    WHAT YOU SEE
    Goya and Caspar David Friedrich — the threshold of the modern.
    LISTEN FOR
    How two very different painters drag art across the line into the modern world.


 You've reached the end of Level 2, and the end of the old world. Around 1800 everything changes —
 revolutions, Napoleon, the collapse of the old certainties — and painting changes with it. The age of
 elegant surfaces gives way to something turbulent, emotional, and recognisably modern.

 Two figures here mark the threshold. Francisco de Goya — court painter turned bitter witness, who
 started in the Rococo world and ended up painting nightmares, war and human cruelty with a raw
 modern honesty that makes him feel like our contemporary. And the German Caspar David Friedrich,
 the foremost painter of Romanticism, who turned landscape into a mirror of the soul — lonely figures
 before infinite skies, mountains and seas, the vast and the sublime.

 Between them, the door to the modern age swings open: the self, the unconscious, doubt, awe, terror.
 When you walk downstairs, you'll walk straight into the nineteenth and twentieth centuries that grew
 from this rupture. Goya and Friedrich, a short way on — then down we go.

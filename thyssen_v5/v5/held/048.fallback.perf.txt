[curious] Before the famous Mondrian of red, yellow and blue squares, there was this one. And there is a tree hidden in it.

In 1911, in Amsterdam, Piet Mondrian saw paintings by Cézanne, Picasso and Braque. Cubism hit him so hard that he moved to Paris. This canvas comes from his first months there, just before the First World War.

[pause] Look at it from a little way back. Short lines and small planes crowd together across the canvas. There is no object you can name. But years later, on the back of a photograph of this picture, Mondrian wrote a title: The Eucalyptus Tree. When it was shown in New York in 1949, it carried that name.

So somewhere in here are branches. Mondrian had been taking trees apart, simplifying their shapes again and again, until only rhythm and structure were left. He believed painting should leave the limits of what the eye sees and move towards something more like music.

[lightly] Follow that road a few more years and you arrive at his grids.

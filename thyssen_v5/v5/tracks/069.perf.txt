[amused] The last painting in this guide is a pinball machine. After seven centuries, that seems fair.

Charles Bell was one of the pioneers of Photorealism. In the late 1960s he painted gumball machines, which reflect the light. Then, in 1976, he turned to pinball machines, which *throw* light out at you. Here you get both at once. A panel glowing from behind, and a metal ball that mirrors everything around it.

The panel speaks the language of comics: white and yellow stars, a crescent moon, thunderbolts and explosions. Where did all that come from? The museum suggests two sparks. The first colour photographs of the surface of Mars, sent back by the Viking 1 probe. And the premiere of Star Wars, in 1977, the year of this painting.

[warmly] And here's Richard Estes again, whom you met in Room 51. Nedick's was a turning point for him. For the first time his view goes deep into the shop, to mirrors twenty feet back, and out through the windows to a distant city on the left.

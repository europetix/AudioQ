[quietly] There's a moon in this painting, but you can't see it. It's hidden behind the thick tree trunks, and still it lights up the night sky.

Titian painted this in his last years, around 1575. Twenty years earlier he had painted the same saint for a church in Venice, surrounded by symbols: a skull, an hourglass, books, ivy on the rocks. Here he lets almost all of that go. What remains is an old man, a stone in his right hand to beat his breast, his eyes on a crucifix set on a long branch.

[pause] Look at the colour. It's nearly all browns and earth, and in places the bare canvas shows through. The only strong colour is the red of his tunic, flicked quickly across his leg. And down in the lower right corner, a lion, its head almost lost in the dark.

The story goes that in his final years Titian painted more with his fingers than with his brushes. Whether or not that's true, you can see why people said it.

[reverent] He died in 1576, during an outbreak of plague in Venice. This is the work of a man who no longer needed to explain anything.

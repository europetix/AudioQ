[warmly] In 1961, Baron Hans Heinrich Thyssen-Bornemisza wanted to leave his own mark on a family collection built on old masters. So he bought modern art for the first time: German Expressionists, Kirchner, Nolde, Heckel and their friends. These rooms grew out of that decision, and from here on the walls get louder.

This room is called "Expressionist Landscapes". And it starts with the painter many of them saw as a forerunner, Vincent van Gogh.

He arrived in Arles, in the south of France, in February 1888, looking for light. That August he wrote to his brother Theo about an evening by the Rhône. A big boat loaded with coal was moored at the quay. Workmen in dirty blue and white carried the cargo ashore. The sky was lilac with an orange streak in the west, and the town was violet. [pause] "It was pure Hokusai," he wrote.

That evening became three paintings. This is one of the two sunset versions, painted later from memory in his studio. Look at the dark boats and the tiny workers standing out against that hot orange air. The black of the coal, and the coming night.

[curious] Now find the large London view nearby, Derain's Waterloo Bridge. A Paris dealer bought everything Derain painted and sent him to London three times, to answer Monet's famous views of the Thames. Derain answered in pure colour. He called colours "cartridges of dynamite whose purpose is to discharge light."

One more Van Gogh is on show today, painted years before Arles. You will meet it on Level 0, in Room C.

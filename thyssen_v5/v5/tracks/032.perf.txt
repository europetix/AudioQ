[curious] In 1832, Delacroix spent six months travelling with a French diplomatic mission to Morocco. He made sketch after sketch, and he lived off that journey for the rest of his career.

This little panel came more than twenty years later. On the twenty-third of April 1854 he noted in his diary "a small Arab seated and his horse behind him". That's what you see. A man rests on the ground by a fire, a sword at his belt. Behind him stands a fiery black horse with a long mane. Delacroix loved horses for their energy and spirit, and he cared less about describing them than about making you feel them.

[wry] The other Delacroix in this room is much earlier, and much more scandalous. A medieval duke lifts a sheet to show a visitor the body of his lover, keeping her face hidden. The visitor is her husband. He doesn't recognise her.

Take the lift beside this room, or the stairs at the east end of the Room 24 gallery, down one floor to Level 1. Room 30, "Transatlantic relations", is right by the lift: look for portraits from colonial America.

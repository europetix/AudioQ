[lightly] It's one of the smallest paintings in this gallery, and it travels further than you'd think.

Watteau painted Pierrot Content around 1712. He had learned the world of the Italian comedy in Paris, from his master Claude Gillot. In a corner of a garden, under dense trees and a statue of Pan, Pierrot sits in the middle between two men and two women. One woman plays a guitar, the other holds a fan, and the man on the left is dressed as Mezzetin, another stock character. Nobody quite looks at anybody. Everyone seems lost in thought.

[conspiratorial] Look into the dark leaves behind them. An engraving made in 1728 shows two more figures peeping out of the trees. They're still there, but the paint has darkened and they have almost vanished. The canvas has also been cut down on both sides.

[warmly] More than two and a half centuries later, Lucian Freud chose this little picture for the background of his portrait of Baron Thyssen. It also inspired a large painting of his own. You'll meet the Baron in Room 49, on Level 1.

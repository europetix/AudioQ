[conspiratorial] James Ensor grew up surrounded by masks. His parents kept a shop in Ostend, on the Belgian coast, and every year they sold masks for the town's carnival.

He never let them go. Masks run through his whole career, sometimes funny, sometimes diabolic, and he used them to say something about modern life, the loneliness of the individual in the new society of the masses. Look at this stage. The actors under the lights wear masks, and so does the audience, and the two crowds melt into one. Who is performing, and who is watching?

[wry] Even the frame joins in. It is gilded and shaped like a stage, and according to a previous owner it was made to Ensor's own instructions. A Belgian poet compared these masked theatres to Watteau's elegant parties, the kind you saw upstairs in Room 24.

On another wall, Mikhail Larionov's Blue Nude was a young Russian's answer to the Gauguins he saw in a Moscow collector's house. His companion, later his wife, was the painter Natalia Goncharova. You will meet her work on Level 0.

Keep straight on into Room 37. The opening you pass into Room 56 belongs to the temporary Kenny Scharf exhibition, until 17 January 2027, and is not part of this guide. In Room 37, look for Grosz's red Metropolis.

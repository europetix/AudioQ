[amused] In Ovid's poem, the god Apollo kills the boy he loves with a discus. Tiepolo has swapped the discus for a tennis ball.

He had a reason. An Italian translation of Ovid from 1561 had already turned the discus into a ball, and the court game was still the height of fashion among the nobility. Tiepolo painted this huge canvas around 1752, during his years in Würzburg, in Germany.

[pause] Look down at the floor. There's the racquet with its balls, and a third ball has rolled away to the left. Behind the onlookers you can see part of the net. Hyacinthus lies with a bruise on his cheek, looking up at Apollo. And the stone god Pan seems to have turned his head, smiling, so as not to miss a thing. In the right-hand corner, the flowers are already growing.

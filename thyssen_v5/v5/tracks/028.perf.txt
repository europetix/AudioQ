This church no longer exists. And the painting doesn't show it quite as it was, either.

Pieter Saenredam was the first painter to make true portraits of real buildings. A biography published in his lifetime called him "the first architectural portraitist". He measured on site, drew, went home, made careful construction drawings, and then sometimes waited years before he painted.

That's what happened here. In the summer of 1636 he spent four months in Utrecht, drawing its churches. His drawing of this one, the Romanesque church of Saint Mary, is dated the thirtieth of August. He painted this panel twenty-six years later. The church itself was demolished in the nineteenth century.

[conspiratorial] Now compare it with reality, because he improved it. At each end of the façade there used to be a ruined structure. He took both away. He changed the size of the round window in the middle. And at the foot of the building he added three small figures. [pause] Find them. They give you the scale, and they make that pale stone feel enormous.

Until 10 January 2027, the visiting Mauritshuis exhibition in the Classical rooms includes another Saenredam of this same church.

[warmly] For a complete change of mood, look for Aelbert Cuyp's Landscape with a Sunset. A cowherd brings his cattle home at the end of the day, along a path towards a wooden bridge, under a pink evening sky. Cuyp probably never went to Italy. He learned that golden Mediterranean light from painters who had, and British collectors in the eighteenth century couldn't get enough of it.

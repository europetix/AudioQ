[warmly] This young man is praying to someone who isn't here anymore.

The pose gives it away: his hands are joined, and his eyes are fixed on something beyond the frame. The museum thinks he was one wing of a small triptych. In the centre, a Virgin and Child. On the other wing, probably a woman donor, praying too. Only his panel is here, and nobody knows his name. He may have belonged to the Italian community in Bruges, where Memling worked, though some scholars have suggested a Spanish client.

Look at what's around him. A loggia opens onto a soft landscape, and there's an oriental rug on the ledge. Memling didn't invent this kind of open background, but he did a great deal to spread it.

[lightly] Now the surprise. On the back of this panel Memling painted a jug of flowers, marked with Christ's monogram. Lilies for Mary's purity, irises for her as Queen of Heaven, and aquilegias for the Holy Spirit. It's one of the first independent still lifes known in art. If the panel is shown so that you can see its back, walk round and look.

Step into the long Villahermosa Gallery, Room 6: look for a Venetian woman with roses and a monkey on a chain.
